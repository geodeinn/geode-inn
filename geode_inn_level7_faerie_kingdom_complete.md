# Geode Inn — Level 7: FAERIE KINGDOM
## The Complete Level Design Document

---

## Zone Overview

The Faerie Kingdom is the Inn's ROOTS — the deep nature, the oldest magic, the world before civilization. Where the Catacombs hold human history, the Faerie Kingdom holds the history before humans — the nature that was here first.

The Faerie Kingdom is the Inn's most EXPLORABLE zone — a full MMO-style space with isometric/top-down exploration, NPCs, quests, faction reputation, and creature encounters. Not a single building or a linear path — a WORLD.

The Vanir are the original Fae. Freyr's golden light feeds the Seelie Court. Freyja's silver feeds the Unseelie Court. The Fae are the descendants of the Vanir — the nature powers that predated the tree, the Inn, the staircase. The Fae Kingdom was here before everything else. The Inn grew from it.

The Faerie Kingdom is the Inn's WILDNESS — the untamed, the pre-civilized, the raw. Every other zone is built, shaped, organized. The Faerie Kingdom GROWS. The Fae don't build; they BECOME. Their architecture is alive — walls of thorn, floors of moss, ceilings of canopy. The Fae Kingdom is the Inn's unconscious — the dream beneath the waking structure.

---

## Entry Requirement

**Win Standing Stones (Tetris) on the Main Floor.** Completing the Stonehenge game activates the first stone circle portal. The portal opens on the world map — a glowing circle at the Stonehenge location. The player steps through.

The portal system connects four real-world stone circles to four locations in the Faerie Kingdom:

| Stone Circle (World Map) | Faerie Kingdom Location | Access |
|---|---|---|
| Stonehenge | The Threshold (entry) | Unlocked by Standing Stones |
| Avebury | The Summerlands (Seelie) | Unlocked by Seelie reputation 10+ |
| Callanish | The Hollow (Unseelie) | Unlocked by Unseelie reputation 10+ |
| Ring of Brodgar | The Stone Circle Chamber (deepest) | Unlocked by Neutral reputation 10+ (both courts simultaneously) |

Each portal is a one-way entry — the player arrives in the Faerie Kingdom through the portal, but returns to the Inn through the Fae Archive Key (Catacombs connection) or through the Threshold's return portal.

## Visual Language

**Linework:** Mixed, by court:
- **Seelie (Summerlands):** Soft flowing lines, warm golden-green palette. The Seelie are rendered in the lore style — soft, organic, flowing. Everything curves. Nothing is sharp.
- **Unseelie (The Hollow):** Sharp angular lines, cold shadow-ice palette. The Unseelie are rendered in the guardian style — sharp, crystalline, hostile geometry. Everything cuts.
- **The Between:** Both styles simultaneously, shifting. The linework changes as the power balance shifts between courts. One moment soft, the next sharp — the visual representation of instability.
- **Crystal Gardens (Neutral):** No-outline (cosmological style) — the Crystal Gardens are rendered like the Cosmology level. Light IS the form. The Crystal Gardens are the Faerie Kingdom's connection to the divine — the place where the Fae's nature magic meets the Vanir's original power.
- **Stone Circle Chamber:** No-outline, deep silver-gold. The deepest point in the Inn. The fourth Brísingamen ring's frequency (84Hz) defines the visual — a deep, sub-audible glow that the player FEELS as much as sees.

**Color Palette:**
- Seelie: gold, green, amber, sunlight, wildflower colors
- Unseelie: silver, blue-black, ice-white, shadow-purple
- The Between: shifting between the two — never stable
- Crystal Gardens: crystal-clear, prismatic, every color at once
- Stone Circle Chamber: deep silver-gold, the Brísingamen's 84Hz glow

**Architecture:** Alive. The Fae Kingdom is grown, not built:
- Seelie: living walls of thorn and flower, floors of moss, ceilings of woven branches
- Unseelie: frozen walls of ice and shadow, floors of black stone, ceilings of stalactite
- The Between: walls that shift between thorn and ice — sometimes mid-step
- Crystal Gardens: walls of crystal that grow from the floor like trees, transparent, prismatic
- Stone Circle Chamber: standing stones — the original stone circle, the archetype of every stone circle in the world. The stones are alive. They hum.

## Spaces

### 1. The Threshold
The entry point. Where the player arrives from the Stonehenge portal. A liminal space — half light, half dark. The Threshold is the Faerie Kingdom's version of the Staircase: the in-between, the place of choice.

**The Threshold contains:**
- **The Faction Stone** — a standing stone at the center, split down the middle: one half golden, one half silver. The player touches the stone to choose which court to approach first. The choice is not permanent — the player can switch courts — but the first choice determines which guide the player gets.
- **The Threshold Keeper** — a chibi Fae NPC who stands at the Faction Stone and explains the court system. The Threshold Keeper is neither Seelie nor Unseelie — the Keeper is the Fae Kingdom's equivalent of the Wanderer: the one who stands at the door and welcomes.
- **The Return Portal** — the way back to the Inn. The return portal is always open — the Faerie Kingdom doesn't trap anyone. (The folklore of being trapped in Faerie is acknowledged but inverted: the Fae Kingdom is a CHOICE, not a prison.)

### 2. The Summerlands (Seelie Court)
The warm half. Golden light, green growth, wildflowers, living architecture. The Seelie are the Fae who chose warmth — Freyr's light, the golden hour made into a kingdom.

The Summerlands are the Faerie Kingdom's GARDEN — everything grows, everything blooms, everything is alive. The Seelie aesthetic derives from Freyr's Garden in Cosmology — the Summerlands are Freyr's light, filtered through the earth, made into a civilization of nature.

**The Summerlands contain:**
- **The Seelie Court** — the Queen's throne room, grown from a single ancient oak. The throne is a living seat — flowers bloom where the Queen sits. The Seelie Queen rules through growth: the Summerlands flourish when the Queen is pleased and wilt when the Queen is neglected.
- **The Riddle Gardens** — hedge mazes where riddle-quests are given. The hedges grow and reshape themselves — the maze is never the same twice. Each riddle is a test of wit, not knowledge. The Seelie value cleverness over scholarship.
- **The Sunlit Ponds** — warm pools where Fae creatures drink and bathe. The water connects to the Water Zone — the ponds are the Fae Kingdom's surface water, and the depths connect to the Water Zone's 7-layer system. The Mermaid lives here — the Fae Kingdom's link to the deep water.
- **The Fae Village** — the Seelie residents' homes, grown from flowers and trees. Each home is a bloom — a rose house, a lily house, a sunflower tower. The village is the Seelie community — social, warm, welcoming to those who prove their wit.

### 3. The Hollow (Unseelie Court)
The cold half. Shadow, ice, black stone, frozen architecture. The Unseelie are the Fae who chose cold — Freyja's silver, the deep night made into a kingdom.

The Hollow is the Faerie Kingdom's FORTRESS — everything is hardened, sharpened, preserved. The Unseelie aesthetic derives from Freyja's silver half in the Threshold Chamber — the Hollow is Freyja's death aspect, made into a civilization of endurance.

**The Hollow contains:**
- **The Unseelie Court** — the Queen's throne room, carved from a single black crystal. The throne is a frozen seat — ice crystallizes where the Queen sits. The Unseelie Queen rules through permanence: the Hollow doesn't change, doesn't grow, doesn't wilt. It ENDURES.
- **The Trial Pits** — combat and survival challenges. The pits are frozen arenas where the player faces Unseelie challenges: combat encounters, endurance tests, survival puzzles. The Unseelie value strength over wit.
- **The Frozen Lake** — a vast underground lake, frozen solid. Beneath the ice, the deep water flows — the Frozen Lake connects to the Water Zone's deepest layer. The Unseelie who fall through the ice don't die; they descend. The Frozen Lake is the Unseelie version of the Sunlit Ponds — the cold-water connection to the deep.
- **The Shadow Village** — the Unseelie residents' homes, carved from ice and obsidian. Each home is a crystal — a geode cut open, the interior livable, the exterior impenetrable. The village is the Unseelie community — sparse, independent, respecting of privacy. The Unseelie don't socialize; they COEXIST.

### 4. The Between
The shifting borderlands. The space where the Seelie and Unseelie meet. Not neutral — CHAOTIC. The Between changes with the tide of power between the courts.

The Between is the Faerie Kingdom's most dangerous space — not because of enemies, but because of INSTABILITY. The ground shifts. The light changes. The architecture grows and freezes alternately. The player who enters the Between must adapt constantly.

**The Between contains:**
- **The Morrigan's Perch** — a dead tree at the center of the Between, where the Morrigan sits as a crow. The Morrigan is the Between's only constant — the one thing that doesn't shift. The Morrigan is the power between the courts — the fate that chooses who wins and who falls.
- **The Shifting Market** — a marketplace that appears and disappears. The Shifting Market is where Seelie and Unseelie traders meet on neutral ground — the only place where goods from both courts can be exchanged. The Fae Merchant runs the Shifting Market. The market's location changes every visit — the player must find it.
- **The Lost Fae's Clearing** — a small space where the Lost Fae sits, undecided. The Lost Fae wandered from the Seelie to the Unseelie and doesn't know which court to return to. The clearing is the Between's emotional center — the place where the Fae Kingdom's duality becomes personal.


### 4b. The Mycorrhizal Network (The Between's Hidden System)

Beneath The Between, visible only in Form Sight, the Mycorrhizal Network pulses — a living web of fungal threads connecting every zone at the root level. The network is the Fae Kingdom's nervous system: information, resources, and frequencies travel through the mycelium the way nerve signals travel through a body.

The Mycorrhizal Network is the Inn's DEEPEST trade system — deeper than the Artisan Market (surface trade), deeper than the Bead Composer (craft trade), deeper than the Caravan Master's routes (cultural trade). This is ROOT trade — the system that runs beneath everything, connecting zones at the root level.

**The Hub:** The Between is the network's center. The mycelium is visible in Form Sight here — glowing white threads beneath the ground, pulsing at 42Hz. When the player enters The Between with Form Sight active, the network resolves like a constellation map underground: nodes and threads spreading outward toward every earth-connected zone.

**How It Works:**
- The player places an item on any mycelium node (a glowing white point on the ground, visible in Form Sight)
- The item is absorbed into the network and appears at a DIFFERENT node — one the player has visited before
- Items travel at the speed of GROWTH (a few real hours, not instant). The network doesn't rush. The network is a tree, and trees don't rush.

**What Can Be Sent:**
- **Stones** — arrive at destination with a slight frequency shift (the mycelium adds its own 42Hz hum, creating a harmonic). Stones sent through the network are unique — no surface trade or gathering produces this harmonic.
- **Messages** — short text messages appear on leaves at the destination node. NPCs can read and respond.
- **Frequencies** — played on an instrument, the frequency travels the threads and plays at the destination. A musical message. A root-level serenade.

**NPC Sending:** NPCs use the network too. The player who has a mycelium node near their favorite nook will occasionally find items left there — a stone from an unvisited zone, a note from an NPC who remembered the player's favorite frequency, a charm from the Fae.

**Network Connections:**
| Zone | Node Location | Connection Character |
|------|--------------|---------------------|
| The Between (Hub) | Center of the borderlands | The network's heart — all threads radiate from here |
| The Catacombs | Underground river bank | Water harmonic — items carry the river's frequency |
| The Norse Zone | Frozen lake's center | Ice harmonic — 42Hz carries through frozen water, clear and crystalline |
| The Mediterranean | Olive Grove's oldest tree | Root harmonic — the tree's roots ARE the network here |
| The Redwood Cathedral | Cathedral floor | The Queen of Roots' personal node — items sent here reach her directly |
| The Bayou | Cypress roots | Wetland harmonic — items carry bay water's mineral signature |
| The Poison Garden | Beneath the poisonous plants | Toxicity tag — items sent through this node arrive altered (not damaged, changed) |

**Form Sight Integration:**
The Mycorrhizal Network adds a layer to Form Sight (the Inn's ability to see hidden frequencies). When Form Sight is active, the player sees:
- Mycelium threads as glowing white lines beneath the ground
- Nodes as brighter glowing points where threads converge
- Item flow as small motes of light traveling along the threads (in real time — the player can watch their sent items traveling)

### 5. The Crystal Gardens
Neutral ground. Beautiful, dangerous. The only place in the Faerie Kingdom where neither court has advantage. The Crystal Gardens are the Faerie Kingdom's connection to the divine — the place where the Fae's nature magic meets the Vanir's original power.

The Crystal Gardens are rendered in no-outline style (cosmological) — the Crystal Gardens are the Faerie Kingdom's COSMOLOGY. The Fae who come here are touching the divine source of their own magic. The Crystal Gardens are where the Fae remember they are Vanir descendants.

**The Crystal Gardens contain:**
- **The Crystal Trees** — trees made of living crystal, growing from the floor like stalagmites. Each crystal tree resonates at a frequency — the Crystal Trees are the Fae Kingdom's version of the Sonic Mineral Codex. The crystals ARE stones, growing.
- **The Griffin's Nest** — at the center of the Crystal Gardens, the Griffin's nest. The Griffin is the Crystal Gardens' guardian — the boss that tests the player's readiness for the Stone Circle Chamber.
- **The Neutral Altar** — a stone altar where Seelie and Unseelie can meet on equal terms. The Neutral Altar is the faction-quest turn-in point — the player who follows the Neutral path makes offerings here.

### 6. The Stone Circle Chamber
The deepest point in the Inn. The Faerie Kingdom's foundation. The fourth Brísingamen ring (84Hz, death/octave) is embedded here — the ring that Freyja gave to the Fae Kingdom. The ring has been here so long the stones have grown around it.

The Stone Circle Chamber is the archetype of every stone circle in the world — Stonehenge, Avebury, Callanish, Ring of Brodgar are all SHADOWS of this original circle. The stones here are alive. They hum. The 84Hz tone is the deepest note the Inn produces — the octave of the 42Hz Song.

The Stone Circle Chamber connects:
- **DOWN to the Catacombs (Fae Layer)** — through the Fae Archive Key. The archive's deepest section opens into the Stone Circle Chamber. The passage is sealed until the player unlocks it through "The Deep Read" Level 5.
- **UP to Cosmology** — through the 84Hz octave. The Stone Circle Chamber's 84Hz is the octave of the 42Hz foundation. The two frequencies are the same note, one register apart. The Stone Circle Chamber and the Cosmology level are connected by frequency — the deepest point and the highest point sing the same note in different octaves.

## Character Roster & Quests

### 1. The Seelie Queen
**Station:** The Seelie Court (the living oak throne)
**Title:** "The One Who Grows"

The Seelie Queen rules through growth. The Summerlands flourish when the Queen is pleased and wilt when neglected. The Seelie Queen's power is Freyr's light, filtered through earth — the golden hour made into sovereignty.

**Audio Profile:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | ~125Hz (warm, musical — the voice of growth) |
| Text Reveal Speed | ~55ms (flowing — the Seelie Queen talks like a vine grows) |
| Pause | ~500ms (comfortable — the pause is a breath between thoughts) |
| Expressiveness | 0.55 (moderate-high — the Seelie Queen's warmth is genuine) |
| Instrument | Wind through leaves — the sound of the Summerlands breathing. A living instrument that changes with the Queen's mood: gentle breeze (content), rustling (amused), howling (angry — the trees shake). |

**The Quest: "The Riddle That Grows"**

The Seelie Queen gives the player a series of riddle-quests in the Riddle Gardens. Each riddle, when solved, grows a new section of the Summerlands — the hedge maze expands, new paths open, new creatures appear. The riddles are tests of wit that reference the Inn's other zones:

1. **The Stone Riddle** — "I am hardest when I'm cold, softest when I'm warm. I sing at 637Hz but I'm not one color. What am I?" (Answer: Quartz — which includes amethyst, citrine, rose, smoky. The riddle teaches the player about the quartz family's frequency.)

2. **The Water Riddle** — "I flow in seven layers but never mix. The deepest layer has never seen light. The surface layer has never seen dark. What am I?" (Answer: The Water Zone's 7-layer system.)

3. **The Stair Riddle** — "I rise as you climb and fall as you descend. I am never the same twice, but I am always the same note. What am I?" (Answer: The Staircase's ascending-key music.)

4. **The God Riddle** — "I am two and I am one. I gave away my sword and I was stronger. I gave away my chord and I was richer. My twin is my other half and my other half is myself. What am I?" (Answer: The Vanir — Freyr and Freyja, the twin gods who gave away and were more.)

5. **The Final Riddle** — "I am the place between rooms. I am the refuge that became a home. I am the sound a child heard when the rooms were too much. What am I?" (Answer: The Inn — the Staircase, the origin, the 42Hz lullaby.)

Each solved riddle grows the Summerlands. The final riddle's solution grows a path to the Crystal Gardens — the Seelie Queen's test complete, the player has proven their wit and earned the right to approach the Griffin.

**Reward:** The Seelie Bloom — a charm that grows. The Seelie Bloom is a living charm — a small flower that the player carries. The flower changes with the player's Seelie reputation: healthy and blooming when reputation is high, wilting when neglected. The Seelie Bloom grants the player access to Seelie-only areas and provides a "Growth" buff in the Bead Board (designs created with the Seelie Bloom active grow more beautiful over time — the arrangement subtly shifts, becoming more balanced, more organic).

### 2. The Unseelie Queen
**Station:** The Unseelie Court (the black crystal throne)
**Title:** "The One Who Endures"

The Unseelie Queen rules through permanence. The Hollow doesn't change, doesn't grow, doesn't wilt. It ENDURES. The Unseelie Queen's power is Freyja's silver — the deep night made into sovereignty.

**Audio Profile:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | ~85Hz (cold, deep — the voice of permanence) |
| Text Reveal Speed | ~65ms (measured — the Unseelie Queen speaks with the weight of ice) |
| Pause | ~1000ms (long — the Unseelie Queen's silence is the Hollow's silence) |
| Expressiveness | 0.20 (low — the emotion is in the endurance, not the expression) |
| Instrument | Ice cracking — the sound of the Hollow settling. Deep, resonant, the sound of something that has been cold for a very long time and will be cold for a long time yet. |

**The Quest: "The Trial That Endures"**

The Unseelie Queen gives the player a series of survival challenges in the Trial Pits. Each challenge is a test of strength, endurance, and will:

1. **The Cold Trial** — the player must survive in the Hollow's deepest cold for a set time. No combat — just endurance. The cold drains the player's stamina; the player must manage resources (warmth charms, the Firekeeper's Stone) to survive. The Cold Trial teaches resource management.

2. **The Shadow Trial** — the player must navigate the Hollow's darkest section without light. The player must rely on the Stone Vibration Touch System — identifying stones by frequency, navigating by sound. The Shadow Trial is the Inn's accessibility system turned into a gameplay challenge. (If the player has the Allfather's Gaze, the trial is slightly easier — but the Gaze is "seeing," and the Shadow Trial tests NOT seeing.)

3. **The Combat Trial** — the player must defeat three Unseelie champions in the Trial Pits. The champions are: an Orc (brute force), a Shadow Fae (speed and stealth), and an Ice Troll (endurance). The combat is not lethal — the Fae don't kill. The combat is a TEST: can the player hold their ground?

4. **The Solitude Trial** — the player must spend time alone in the Hollow. No NPCs, no quests, no objectives. Just the cold and the dark and the silence. The Solitude Trial is a test of the player's relationship with stillness. After a set time, the Unseelie Queen speaks: "You stayed. That is enough."

**Reward:** The Unseelie Mark — a permanent charm. The Unseelie Mark is a scar that the player carries — not a wound, but a badge. The Mark grants the player access to Unseelie-only areas and provides an "Endurance" buff: the player's stamina drains more slowly in all zones. The Unseelie Mark is permanent — once earned, it doesn't fade. The Unseelie respect what you've proven.

### 3. The Morrigan
**Station:** The Morrigan's Perch (the dead tree in the Between)
**Title:** "The One Who Chooses"

The Morrigan is the Celtic goddess of war, fate, and death. A Full Presence character — one of the most powerful beings in the Inn. The Morrigan's domain is the Between — the shifting borderlands. The Morrigan chooses who wins and who falls in the Fae conflicts. The Morrigan is the Neutral path — not Seelie, not Unseelie, but the power that exists between them.

**Audio Profile:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | ~55Hz (the deepest voice in the Inn — deeper than the Allfather's 64Hz. The Morrigan's voice is the sound of fate, and fate is heavier than observation) |
| Text Reveal Speed | ~120ms (very slow — slower than the Allfather. Each word is a choice that can't be unchosen) |
| Pause | ~2000ms (the longest pause in the Inn — the silence of someone who decides who lives and who dies) |
| Expressiveness | 0.05 (almost zero — the Morrigan's emotion is in the choice, not the expression) |
| Instrument | A crow's call — sharp, single, final. The Morrigan speaks rarely; when the Morrigan speaks, a crow calls first. The call IS the announcement: the Morrigan is about to choose. The crow's call is the last sound some Fae ever hear. |

**Visual Design:**
- Full Presence — the Morrigan is rendered in full, not chibi
- The Morrigan appears as both a woman and a crow — the form shifts. Sometimes the woman sits in the dead tree; sometimes the crow perches. The player never sees the transition.
- Dark feathers, dark hair, dark eyes. The Morrigan's color is the absence of color — the space between the Seelie's gold and the Unseelie's silver.
- The Morrigan's linework: fragmented (crisis style). The Morrigan is the Inn's most fragmented character — the one who exists in the space between states. The Morrigan is the crisis. The Morrigan IS the choice.

**The Quest: "The Balance That Costs"**

The Neutral path. The hardest. The deepest reward.

**Phase 1: The Observation**
The player approaches the Morrigan's Perch. The Morrigan doesn't speak — the crow calls once. The player must WAIT. The Morrigan tests patience: the player must stand at the Perch for a set time without speaking, without acting, without leaving. Just observing. The Between shifts around the player — light to dark, dark to light, thorn to ice, ice to thorn. The player watches the duality. The Morrigan watches the player watch.

**Phase 2: The Question**
After the observation, the Morrigan speaks. One question: "Why do the courts fight?"

The player must answer. Not with information — with understanding. The game presents dialogue options, but the "correct" answer is not the one the Morrigan wants to hear. The "correct" answer is the one the player BELIEVES. The Morrigan doesn't grade the answer; the Morrigan WITNESSES it.

The possible answers:
- "Because they are different." (The Morrigan: "No. They are the same.")
- "Because they want power." (The Morrigan: "No. They want to BE.")
- "Because they are afraid." (The Morrigan: "Closer.")
- "Because they don't know they need each other." (The Morrigan: "Yes.")

The last answer — "Because they don't know they need each other" — is the one that opens the Neutral path. The Morrigan says: "The Seelie grow. The Unseelie endure. Growth without endurance is fragile. Endurance without growth is stagnant. They need each other. They don't know it. Show them."

**Phase 3: The Proof**
The player must maintain positive reputation with BOTH courts simultaneously. This is the Neutral path's challenge: the Seelie reputation grows with attention and drops when neglected; the Unseelie reputation is permanent but hard to earn. Balancing both requires constant effort — attending to the Seelie's needs while enduring the Unseelie's trials. The player must reach reputation 10+ with both courts at the same time.

**Phase 4: The Offering**
At the Neutral Altar in the Crystal Gardens, the player makes an offering: one item from the Seelie (a Seelie Bloom) and one from the Unseelie (an Unseelie Mark). The two items combine — the bloom and the scar merge into something new: The Morrigan's Feather. The feather is both warm and cold, both growth and endurance. The duality, unified.

**Phase 5: The Choice**
The Morrigan appears. The crow calls. The Morrigan says: "The courts will not unify. They should not. The duality is the point. The Seelie grow; the Unseelie endures. The Between shifts. I choose who wins and who falls, and I choose... both. Always both. The fight is the life. The balance is the peace."

The Morrigan gives the player the Feather. The Morrigan says: "You will not end the fight. You will hold the balance. This is harder than ending it. This is the Neutral path."

**Reward:** The Morrigan's Feather — the Fae Kingdom's deepest reward. The Feather grants:
- Access to the Stone Circle Chamber (the final portal)
- The "Balance" buff: the player's reputation with both courts no longer degrades. The Seelie don't wilt; the Unseelie don't forget. The balance is held.
- The Morrigan's Voice: the player can call the Morrigan in the Between for guidance. The crow will call, and the Morrigan will speak — one line, always the right line, always the line the player needs.

### 4. The Griffin Boss — "The Winged Threshold"
**Station:** The Griffin's Nest (Crystal Gardens)

The Griffin is the Crystal Gardens' guardian. The Griffin tests the player's readiness for the Stone Circle Chamber. The Griffin is the Faerie Kingdom's BOSS — the most powerful creature, the guardian of the deepest point.

**The Encounter:**
The Griffin is not a combat boss in the traditional sense. The Griffin tests the player's UNDERSTANDING, not their combat skill. The Griffin asks three questions — not riddles (that's the Seelie) and not trials (that's the Unseelie). The Griffin asks QUESTIONS that require both wit AND endurance:

1. **The Wing Question:** "I am the creature that lives between earth and sky. I have the body of a lion and the wings of an eagle. I guard the deepest point because I am the only creature that can see both the highest and the lowest at once. What do I see when I look down?" (Answer: "Everything I protect." The Griffin guards not out of aggression but out of love.)

2. **The Stone Question:** "The stones in the Chamber sing at 84Hz. This is the octave of 42Hz. One is life, one is death. But which is which?" (Answer: "Neither. The octave is the same note. Life and death are the same song, one register apart." The answer echoes Freyja's teaching — death is the octave of life.)

3. **The Player Question:** "You have grown (Seelie) and endured (Unseelie). You have balanced both (Neutral). What are you now?" (Answer: The player must answer in their own words — the Griffin doesn't grade the answer. The Griffin WITNESSES it, like the Morrigan and the Questioner. The answer is the player's self-definition. The Griffin accepts whatever the player says, because the answer IS the readiness.)

After the three questions, the Griffin steps aside. The path to the Stone Circle Chamber opens. The Griffin says: "You are ready. Not because you are strong. Because you know what you are."

**Reward:** The Griffin's Feather — a charm that grants flight within the Faerie Kingdom. The player can fly between zones (Threshold, Summerlands, Hollow, Between, Crystal Gardens, Stone Circle Chamber) without walking. The Griffin's Feather is the Faerie Kingdom's FAST TRAVEL system. The player who has earned the Griffin's respect can move freely through the Fae world.

### 5. The Chibi Fae NPCs

**The Threshold Keeper** — stands at the Faction Stone. Explains the court system. Neither Seelie nor Unseelie. The Threshold Keeper is the Fae Kingdom's version of the Wanderer — the one who stands at the door and welcomes. The Threshold Keeper's quest is a TUTORIAL: explaining the faction system, the reputation mechanics, and the portal system. No reward except understanding.

**The Seelie Guide** — a small, bright Fae who guides the player through the Summerlands. Gives riddle-quest hints and introduces Seelie NPCs. The Seelie Guide is the Summerlands' version of Beverley — the one who helps you FIND things. The Seelie Guide's quest: "The Lost Bloom" — find a specific flower that has stopped growing. The flower is in the Between, where the duality has stunted it. The player brings it back to the Summerlands and tends it. Reward: a Seelie reputation boost.

**The Unseelie Guide** — a small, sharp Fae who guides the player through the Hollow. Gives survival tips and introduces Unseelie NPCs. The Unseelie Guide is the Hollow's version of the Clockmaker — the one who MAINTAINS things. The Unseelie Guide's quest: "The Frozen Path" — clear a blocked passage in the Hollow. The ice has grown too thick. The player must use fire charms (the Firekeeper's Stone) to melt the passage. Reward: an Unseelie reputation boost.

**The Between Dweller** — a Fae who lives in the shifting borderlands. Gives quests about balance. The Between Dweller is the only Fae who is comfortable in the Between — everyone else finds it disorienting. The Between Dweller's quest: "The Shifting Map" — create a map of the Between. The player must navigate the shifting landscape and mark stable points. The map is a tool the player can use to navigate the Between more easily in future visits. Reward: the Between Map — a navigation item that shows the Between's current configuration.

**The Crystal Gardener** — tends the Crystal Gardens. Gives quests about the Griffin and the Stone Circle Chamber. The Crystal Gardener is the only Fae who lives in the Crystal Gardens full-time — the Gardens are too intense for most Fae. The Crystal Gardener's quest: "The Crystal Song" — tune the Crystal Trees to a specific chord. The player uses the Alchemist's Scale knowledge (from the Staircase) to combine crystal frequencies. Reward: a rare crystal stone, available only from the Crystal Gardens.

**The Stone Circle Keeper** — guards the Stone Circle Chamber. Gives the endgame quest. The Stone Circle Keeper is the oldest Fae — the one who remembers the Vanir. The Stone Circle Keeper's quest: "The Deepest Note" — the player must sit in the Stone Circle Chamber and listen to the 84Hz tone until the tone reveals its secret. The secret: 84Hz is the octave of 42Hz. Death is the octave of life. The Stone Circle Chamber's stones sing the same note as the 42Hz Song, one register down. The two deepest points in the Inn — the Stone Circle Chamber and the 42Hz Foundation — are the same note in different octaves. Reward: the Octave Key — the understanding that the Inn's highest and lowest points are connected by frequency. The Octave Key completes the Inn's sonic architecture: the player can now hear the full range, from 42Hz (foundation) to 84Hz (octave) and every frequency in between, as ONE SONG.

**The Lost Fae** — a child Fae who wandered from the Seelie to the Unseelie and doesn't know which court to return to. The Lost Fae sits in the Between's clearing, undecided. The Lost Fae's quest is the Faerie Kingdom's most EMOTIONAL quest: "The Home That Isn't a Place." The player helps the Lost Fae understand that the court doesn't matter — the Fae's family is in BOTH courts. The Seelie family misses the Fae; the Unseelie family noticed the Fae's absence. The Lost Fae doesn't have to choose. The Lost Fae can live in the Between — the place between courts, the place that is both and neither. The Between Dweller offers to share their home. The Lost Fae accepts. Reward: the Lost Fae's Trust — a charm that grants a small reputation bonus with BOTH courts whenever the player helps any Fae. The kindness given to one Fae echoes in both courts, because both courts are the same family.

**The Fae Merchant** — runs the Shifting Market in the Between. Sells Fae-specific items: Fae stones (Labradorite, Fluorite, Rainbow Moonstone, Preseli Blue Stone — the rare stones not available from surface games), Fae potions, Fae charms. The Fae Merchant's goods change location with the Shifting Market — the player must find the market each visit. The Fae Merchant's quest: "The Trade Route" — establish a stable trade route between the Shifting Market and the Main Floor market. The player must navigate the Between's shifting landscape enough times to create a reliable path. Reward: the Fae Merchant becomes a permanent vendor on the Main Floor market — Fae stones available without navigating the Between.

## Game Templates

### 1. The Riddle Gardens (Seelie)
A maze puzzle game in the Seelie Court's hedge mazes. The mazes grow and reshape themselves — each visit produces a new layout. The player navigates the maze, finds riddle stones, and solves riddles to open paths. The mazes are timed — the hedges grow behind the player, so the player must solve each riddle before the path closes.

### 2. The Trial Pits (Unseelie)
A combat/survival game in the Unseelie Court's frozen arenas. The player faces waves of Unseelie challenges: combat encounters (Orcs, Ice Trolls), endurance tests (surviving cold, darkness, solitude), and resource management (using charms and stones strategically). The trials escalate in difficulty; the final trial is the Solitude Trial.

### 3. The Between Run
A navigation/challenge game in the shifting borderlands. The player must cross the Between from one court to the other while the landscape shifts. The player adapts to changing terrain (thorn to ice, light to dark) and avoids instability zones (areas where the Between is actively reshaping). The Between Run is the Faerie Kingdom's most replayable game — the landscape is different every time.

### 4. The Crystal Harmony
A puzzle game in the Crystal Gardens. The player tunes the Crystal Trees to specific frequencies, creating chords. The game uses the Alchemist's Scale mechanic (from the Staircase) but with crystal frequencies instead of stone frequencies. The Crystal Harmony is the Seelie version of the Alchemist's Scale — growth-based instead of transformation-based.

### The 84Hz Tone

The Stone Circle Chamber's 84Hz is the Faerie Kingdom's defining sound. It's the octave of 42Hz — the same note, one register down. Death is the octave of life. The 84Hz tone is:
- Sub-audible — felt more than heard, like the 42Hz Song but deeper
- Constant — the standing stones never stop singing
- Resonant — the player's collected stones vibrate in sympathy when the player enters the Chamber
- Revealing — with the Octave Key, the player hears the 84Hz as the SAME note as the 42Hz, confirming the Inn's deepest sonic truth: the highest and lowest points sing the same song in different octaves
