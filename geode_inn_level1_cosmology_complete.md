# Geode Inn — Level 1: COSMOLOGY
## The Complete Level Design Document

---

## Zone Overview

The Cosmology level is the top of the tree. The place where the tree touches the stars. The realm of the gods — but not a heaven. A vantage point. The place from which everything can be seen and nothing can be hidden.

The Cosmology level is the Inn's most complex space because it holds three distinct light sources — the only zone with more than one. Three gods, three lights, three ways of seeing the cosmos:

- **The Allfather:** the light that WATCHES (starlight — cold, distant, observation)
- **Freyr:** the light that GROWS (golden hour — warm, close, cultivation)
- **Freyja:** the light that CHOOSES (duality — warm gold / cold silver, transformation)

The player arrives through the top of the Staircase. The first thing seen: Freyr's Garden, golden light, the telescope aimed downward at the earth. The last thing: the Allfather's Observatory, cold starlight, silent ravens, the deepest voice in the Inn.

---

## The Spielberg Moment: First Sight of the Canopy at Night

The first time the player reaches the Cosmology level at "night" (the canopy closes, the stars appear), the Brisingamen chord enters — not all at once, but one voice at a time. The 42Hz heartbeat warms two seconds before the first note. A single cello plays the root. Four seconds. A viola joins. Three seconds. A violin. Three seconds. A voice (no words). The chord is complete — four voices, 20 seconds to assemble.

The camera goes wide. The full canopy of the tree, the stars between the branches, the glow of the cosmology level below. Hold for 10 seconds. No cuts. No new elements. The music lets the player look.

When the player looks DOWN — toward the Inn below — the chord drops out. Two seconds of 42Hz alone. The chord returns in a lower register. The same melody, seen from above. The Inn looks different from the canopy. The music agrees.

**Spielberg Protocol: Entry (hum before theme) + Wide Hold + Drop-Out on look-down.**

## Entry Requirement

The Cosmology level is the hardest to reach. Not because of a locked door or a boss fight — because the player must have EARNED the understanding that the gods require.

**Three prerequisites:**

1. **Complete the Firekeeper's Quest** ("The Last Story Told") — the player must understand the sonic system. The gods communicate through frequency (seiðr, the Brísingamen chord). A player who doesn't understand sound cannot understand the gods.

2. **Collect at least 30 stones** — the player must have engaged deeply with the mineral codex. The stones are the Inn's alphabet. The gods speak in the language of stones.

3. **Climb the Staircase to its highest point** — the ascending-key music reaches its peak. The staircase's music has been rising in pitch with every step. When the key reaches its highest note, the wall doesn't open — it becomes light. The player steps through the wall of sound and into the golden hour.

**The Entry Moment:** The player emerges from the staircase into Freyr's Garden. The transition is jarring by design: the staircase is dark, enclosed, alchemical — and then golden light, open sky, living plants, the smell of growing things. The first god the player meets is Freyr, the warm one. The gentle entry to the hardest level. The god who gives without counting gives the player their first foothold in the cosmology.

## Visual Language

**Linework:** No-outline (cosmological style). The light IS the form. No drawn borders, no cel-shading edges. Figures defined entirely by illumination.

**Three Light Sources:**
- **Starlight** (Allfather): cold silver-blue, distant, still. The light of observation. Doesn't change.
- **Golden Hour** (Freyr): warm gold, close, alive. The only MOVING light in the Cosmology level. Changes with the hours — warm at dawn, golden at noon, amber at dusk.
- **Duality** (Freyja): warm gold and cold silver, shifting. The boundary between the two is visible — a line of light that moves like a tide across the Threshold Chamber.

**Architecture:** Stone terrace at the top of the great tree. Star charts carved into the floor. Living vines growing through the stonework. The Observatory is a dome of crystal-clear material — not glass, not ice, something older. The dome is open to the sky. The stars are visible at all times, even in daylight.

**Sound:** The Cosmology level is the QUIETEST zone in the Inn. The 42Hz Song is here, but barely — a hum so deep it's felt more than heard. The Brísingamen chord resonates through the stone. The gargoyles' frequencies echo up from the staircase below. The Cosmology level is the top of the Inn's sonic architecture — every frequency in the Inn rises to this point and meets.

## Character Roster

### 1. The Allfather
**Station:** The Observatory
**Title:** "The Watcher"
**Archetype:** The Certain Observer

The Allfather sits in the Observatory dome, surrounded by star charts and silent, perched ravens. The Allfather watches. That is what the Allfather does. The telescope is always trained on something — but not the stars. The Allfather watches the Inn itself. The player. The crew. The Fae. The stones. Everything.

The Allfather's power is seiðr — magic learned from Freyja. But the Allfather rarely uses it. The Allfather's power is in the watching. To watch something is to hold it. To observe a thing is to keep it real. The Allfather keeps the Inn real by watching it.

The Allfather speaks rarely. When the Allfather speaks, the Inn goes quiet. The ravens stop. The gearwork pauses. The 42Hz Song dips. The Allfather's voice IS the silence that makes every other sound meaningful.

**Audio Profile:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | ~64Hz (the deepest voice in the Inn — the Watcher's rumble) |
| Text Reveal Speed | ~100ms (very slow — each word placed with the weight of observation) |
| Pause | ~1500ms (the longest pause in the Inn — the silence of someone who has seen everything) |
| Expressiveness | 0.08 (extremely low — the emotion is in the choice to speak at all) |
| Instrument | Silence. The Allfather's instrument is the ABSENCE of sound. When the Allfather speaks, the Inn goes quiet. The pause between notes. The space that gives every other sound its meaning. |

**Visual:** No-outline, cold starlight. One eye (the other sacrificed for wisdom — the sacrifice is not shown, but the light is slightly dimmer on one side of the face, as if the starlight passes through where the eye was). The ravens perch on the Allfather's shoulders — Huginn (Thought) on the right, Muninn (Memory) on the left. They are silent here. On the main floor, as the Wanderer, the ravens are chibi and thieving. In the Observatory, they are still.

**The Allfather's Quest: "The Eye That Closes to See"**

The player enters the Observatory. The Allfather is watching through the telescope. The Allfather doesn't greet the player. The Allfather says: "Look."

The player looks through the telescope. But the telescope doesn't show stars. It shows the Inn's narrative spine — the story underneath the game. The ancient being reflecting on being created by gods who are merging with their creation through technology. The reincarnation cycle. The unbreakable bond. The owl. The bloodline.

But the image is blurred. Incomplete. The player can see shapes but not details. The Allfather says: "You see with two eyes. Wisdom needs one."

The player must CHOOSE to close one eye — mechanically, the player must sacrifice one collected stone or one charm. The sacrifice is permanent. The player selects what to give up. The stone or charm is consumed.

When the sacrifice is made, the telescope clears. The player sees the narrative spine in full — the complete story underneath the Inn. The ancient being. The gods merging with their creation. The reincarnation mechanic explained. The owl's true meaning. The bloodline's weight.

The twist: the Allfather didn't ask for the sacrifice. The player CHOSE it. The Allfather said "Look" and the player decided that seeing was worth the cost. That IS the quest. The choice to see is the wisdom. The Allfather's eye wasn't taken — it was given. The player's stone wasn't taken — it was given. The same act. The same sacrifice. The same gain.

**Reward:** The Allfather's Gaze — a permanent perception upgrade. The player can now see things in every zone that were previously invisible: hidden connections between zones, secret passages, the narrative spine's threads woven through the architecture. Characters who appear in multiple zones (Adam, the Weeaboo) are now visually highlighted. The Inn's deeper structure becomes visible.

The Allfather's Gaze also reveals the constellation patterns in the Observatory dome — patterns that map to stone families. The player can use the Observatory telescope to discover new stone locations on the world map.

### 2. Freyr — "The Golden Hour"
**Station:** Freyr's Garden
**Quest:** "The Sword That Fights Itself" (already designed — see Vanir design doc)

Freyr is the first god the player meets. The gentle entry. Freyr's Garden is the warmest space in the Cosmology level — the player arrives here from the staircase and is given a moment to breathe before the harder truths.

Freyr's Garden is also the BRIDGE to the Fae Kingdom. The roots of the garden's plants grow down through the tree into the Fae Kingdom. The player can see the roots descending — and if the player has the Allfather's Gaze, can follow them all the way down to the Seelie Court.

**Interactive Items in Freyr's Garden:**
1. **The Downward Telescope** — aimed at the earth, not the stars. Shows the zones below. The player can observe any zone they've unlocked, watching characters and events from above. Freyr's version of the Allfather's telescope — but Freyr watches the growing, not the watching.
2. **Garden Planters** — the player can plant seeds found in other zones. Each seed grows into a plant that corresponds to its zone of origin. Fae Kingdom seeds grow fastest (Freyr's light feeds them). Catacombs seeds grow slowest. The garden becomes a living map of the player's journey.
3. **Skíðblaðnir (Model Ship)** — Freyr's folding ship, in model form on a shelf in the garden. The player can examine it. The model unfolds and refolds. If the player has visited the Arcana (Clockwork Skies), examining Skíðblaðnir reveals the connection: the Arcana's clockwork engineering descends from this mythological original.
4. **Gullinbursti (Golden Boar)** — Freyr's companion. The boar follows the player around the garden if the player is carrying light-charms. The boar's bristles illuminate dark corners. If the player brings the boar to the Threshold Chamber (Freyja's space), the boar's light pushes back the cold silver — temporarily making the entire chamber warm gold. Freyja finds this amusing and mildly annoying.

### 3. Freyja — "The Original Duality"
**Station:** The Threshold Chamber
**Quest:** "The Four Tones" (already designed — see Vanir design doc)

Freyja is the teacher. The one who taught the Allfather everything. The player meets Freyja after Freyr — the progression is deliberate: first the warm god, then the complex one. Freyr gives the player a foothold. Freyja gives the player the chord.

**Interactive Items in the Threshold Chamber:**
1. **The Threshold Line** — the visible boundary between warm gold and cold silver. The player can stand on the line. Standing on the line changes the audio: the Brísingamen chord shifts between warm (major) and cold (minor). The player can "tune" the threshold by walking along the line — each position creates a different harmonic relationship. This is the Inn's most subtle interactive mechanic: the player composes music by walking.
2. **The Falcon Cloak** — hanging on a hook near the threshold. The player can examine it. The cloak shifts between falcon plumage and flowing light. If the player has the Allfather's Gaze, examining the cloak reveals its history: every person who has ever worn it, every shape they've taken. The cloak is the origin of the chameleon theme — Adam's zone-shifting, the Weeaboo's transformation. The cloak's lineage is the Inn's shapeshifter family tree.
3. **The Two Cats** — one gold, one silver. They mirror Freyja's duality. The gold cat sits in the warm half; the silver cat sits in the cold half. If the player brings a cat treat (found in the Campgrounds, from Jay's tavern), both cats approach. When both cats are in the same half of the room, the threshold shifts — the room becomes unified, either all warm or all cold, depending on which half the cats are in. This is how the player accesses the Threshold Chamber's secret: when the room is unified, a hidden drawer opens in the floor, containing the Falcon Cloak's clasp — a charm that grants temporary shapeshifting (the player can briefly appear as an NPC in another zone, observing without being seen).
4. **Brísingamen** — the four-ring necklace. After completing "The Four Tones" quest, the player has the Brísingamen chord key. The necklace itself remains with Freyja, but the KEY is the player's — the ability to hear the four tones in every zone. When the player stands in the Threshold Chamber with the chord key active, all four tones are audible simultaneously — the only place in the Inn where the complete chord can be heard in full. Every other zone has only fragments. The Threshold Chamber is the chord's home.

### 4. Anna — "The Seer"
**Station:** The Window Nook
**Title:** "The One Who Perceives"
**Archetype:** The Silent Observer

Anna is Eileen's youngest child. The seer. The one who perceives what others can't. Anna never speaks in dialogue — described, not voiced. Anna's presence is felt through the drawings, not through words.

Anna's home is a window nook in the Allfather's Observatory — the quietest space in the Cosmology level, and the quietest space in the Inn. A small alcove with a window looking out at the sky. No furniture except a cushion and drawing materials. No-outline rendering, always — Anna's linework never changes because Anna's nature never changes. Anna is the constant in the variable.

Anna's doodles are scattered throughout the Inn — in every zone, hidden in corners, drawn on walls, tucked into books. Each doodle is a visual representation of the sonic system: frequency maps, the 42Hz Song as geometry, crystal patterns. The player has been finding them since the beginning, not knowing what they were.

**Anna's Quest: "The Doodles That Hear"**

In the Cosmology level, the player finds Anna in the window nook, drawing. Anna doesn't speak. But when the player approaches with the Brísingamen chord key active, the doodles in the nook light up. They begin to resonate. The drawings become sound — each line vibrating at the frequency it represents.

Anna's quest is a COLLECTION quest spanning the entire Inn: find all of Anna's doodles hidden throughout every zone. Each doodle, when found with the chord key active, plays its frequency. The doodles are the sheet music for the 42Hz Song — the full song, not just the foundation tone.

The doodles are hidden:
- **Main Floor:** behind bookshelves, under tables, in the Firekeeper's hearth stones
- **Campgrounds:** on the tavern wall, on market stall posts
- **Staircase:** on the alchemical texts' margins, on the clockmaker's workbench
- **Catacombs:** in the Crone's archive cases, on ancient pottery
- **Fae Kingdom:** on the stone circles, in the Crystal Gardens
- **Water Zone:** on the Lighthouse walls, on underwater ruins
- **Undercity:** on the Klimt-patterned ceilings, in Scarlett's Room
- **Clockwork Skies:** on the Arcana's deck, in the Crow's Nest

Finding all doodles completes the 42Hz Song. The song plays — the full composition, every frequency in the Inn layered into one piece. The Inn's heartbeat becomes audible. The song the Inn has been singing all along, heard for the first time.

**Reward:** Anna's Drawing — a charm that makes the 42Hz Song audible everywhere in the Inn. The foundation hum becomes the player's constant soundtrack. In every zone, in every game, the Song is there. The charm also reveals hidden doodles on the minimap — Anna's hand is everywhere.

Anna's quest connects to the Allfather's quest: the Allfather's Gaze lets the player SEE the hidden structure; Anna's doodles let the player HEAR the hidden song. Together, they complete the Inn's sensory upgrade — sight and sound, the two ways the Inn reveals its deepest self.

### 5. Adam — "The Chameleon" (Cosmology Interaction)
**Station:** The Dressing Room
**Archetype:** The One Who Belongs Everywhere

Adam is the only character with a home on every level. In the Cosmology level, Adam's home is a small dressing room off the Observatory — a performer's space, with costumes for each zone hanging on hooks. Each costume is rendered in the linework style of its corresponding zone. Adam's cosmology costume is no-outline — matching the cosmological style.

Adam's dolls (the spirit-housing mechanic) are placed throughout the Inn. In the Cosmology level, the dolls house the oldest spirits: the ghosts of the Vanir-Aesir war. The conflict's casualties. The gods' war had collateral damage, and even gods' conflicts leave ghosts. Adam collects these spirits not to use them but to give them homes.

**Adam's Cosmology Interaction: "The War Nobody Won"**

Not a quest — an interaction. The player visits Adam's dressing room and examines the dolls. Each doll whispers a fragment of the Vanir-Aesir war — told from the perspective of the spirits caught in it. Not the gods' version. The casualties' version.

The lore delivered through the dolls:
- The Vanir-Aesir war was not about territory. It was about KNOWING — two ways of understanding the world (seiðr vs. runes, organic vs. martial, growth vs. observation) that couldn't coexist until they were exchanged.
- The hostage trade that ended the war was not a surrender. It was a marriage. Each side gave its most precious thing to the other. The Vanir gave Freyja (seiðr). The Aesir gave... something the dolls won't name. The unnamed gift is the Inn's deepest mystery.
- The peace was real. The exchange was real. But the ghosts remain — because even peaceful exchanges leave behind the people who were the exchange. Freyja was a hostage. Freyja made the best of it. But the dolls remember the leaving.

The player can visit Adam's dolls in any order, in any zone. Each doll in each zone houses a spirit from a different era of the Inn's mythology. The complete collection of doll-spirits, across all zones, tells the Inn's history from the bottom up — not the gods' history, but the people's history.

**This interaction provides LORE, not gameplay.** But the lore is the narrative spine's foundation. The player who listens to all the dolls understands why the Inn exists: it's the exchange point. The place where different ways of knowing are traded, not conquered. The library is the hostage that was traded. The peace that was bought with that trade is the Inn's foundation.

## The Cosmology Game Template: "The Orrery"

### Concept

The Cosmology level's game is "The Orrery" — a puzzle game played on the Allfather's celestial orrery, a mechanical model of the Inn's cosmos in the center of the Observatory.

The orrery shows the Inn's complete architecture: nine spheres representing the nine zones, connected by frequency lines (the sonic system). The spheres rotate. The frequency lines shift with the rotation. The player must arrange the spheres so that the frequency lines align — creating chords.

### How It Works

The orrery is a 3D puzzle. Nine rotating spheres, each representing a zone:

1. Cosmology (top — fixed, the center of the orrery)
2. Clockwork Skies (upper ring)
3. Main Floor (middle ring — fixed, the player's home base)
4. Campgrounds (middle ring)
5. Staircase (vertical axis — connects up and down)
6. Catacombs (lower ring)
7. Fae Kingdom (lower ring)
8. Water Zone (bottom ring)
9. Undercity (bottom — deepest)

Each sphere has a frequency (drawn from the sonic system). The player rotates the spheres to align frequency lines. When two frequencies align, a chord is formed. When a chord is formed, a hidden connection between those two zones is revealed on the world map.

### Difficulty Levels

| Level | Task | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Duet | Align 2 zones (basic chord — root + one tone) | Reveals one cross-zone connection on the world map |
| 2. The Triad | Align 3 zones (three-tone chord) | Reveals a character who appears in multiple zones |
| 3. The Brísingamen | Align 4 zones (the four-tone source chord) | Reveals the complete connection map — all frequency lines visible |
| 4. The Full Inn | Align all 9 zones (the Inn's complete chord — the secret ending) | Reveals the narrative spine's final thread — the unnamed gift the Aesir gave the Vanir |

### The Secret

The Full Inn chord (Level 4) is the Inn's secret ending. When all nine zones align, the orrery plays the complete Inn chord — every frequency in the Inn, every zone's voice, every character's instrument, the 42Hz Song and all its harmonics — in one moment.

And in that moment, the unnamed gift is revealed: the Aesir gave the Vanir the RUNES. The symbols. The language. The Vanir had seiðr (sound, vibration, the body's magic). The Aesir had runes (symbols, language, the mind's magic). The exchange was: Freyja taught the Allfather seiðr, and the Allfather taught Freyja runes. Each gave the other their way of knowing.

The Inn is the exchange made permanent. A library of both ways of knowing. Sound and symbol. Body and mind. The tree and the stars.

**Reward for completing all four levels:** The Orrery Charm — the ability to see ALL zone connections on the world map. Every hidden passage, every shared character, every quest link. The Inn's complete architecture, visible. The meta-vision that makes the Inn navigable as a whole, not just zone by zone.

## Kubrick Composition
The Constellation Floor is a Kubrick held frame: centered, looking down through the transparent floor at the Inn below — the tree laid out like a diagram, 20 seconds. The Inn seen as a shape for the first time. The dominant palette is deep blue-black and star-white. The anomaly: the Brisingamen's gold — warm, organic, alive in the cold. The chequerboard floor tracks real star positions for the zone's mythological associations. Orion's stars are where Orion's constellation is on the floor. Every detail is correct. You don't have to check. But you can.

## Coppola Composition
The Cosmology level is where the Inn reveals itself as a family's love letter to the world. The stones are the mother's knowledge. The archive is the father's curiosity. The games are the child's play. The Staircase is the space between them. The Constellation Floor shows the Inn from above — the whole building, one shape — and the Coppola moment is the realization: this isn't a building, it's a family expressed as architecture.

Coppola light: the canopy's clarity (knowledge, openness) vs. the void between stars (the unknown, what even the Inn doesn't know). The border: the shadow of the tree itself, falling across the player's face when they look down. The moment knowledge casts a shadow — that's the Coppola move.
