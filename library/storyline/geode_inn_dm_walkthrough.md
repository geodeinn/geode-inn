## PROLOGUE: BEFORE THE SONG

Before the Song, there was the Murmur.

Not sound — not yet. The Murmur is what exists before frequency, before time organizes vibration into meaning. If you put your ear to the oldest stone in the deepest catacomb and listened not with your ears but with your bones, you would hear it: a low, shapeless hum that has no beginning and no end. The Murmur does not play. It simply is.

The Cosmic Egg sits in the deepest chamber of the Inn. It looks like a river stone — smooth, dark, unassuming. You could hold it in your palm and not know what it was. You could carry it for years and never feel its weight shift. But the Egg is the raw potential from which the 42Hz Song was organized. Every frequency in the Inn — every gargoyle's resonance, every composer's melody, every heartbeat, every stone's chime — is the Murmur's child, shaped into structure by the act of building.

The Constant knows this. The Constant is the oldest being in the Inn — an Annunaki, a putter-of-things-in-order, who has watched universes be born and die and be born again across cycles so long that the current 42Hz Song is just the latest version. The previous universe sang at 41.7Hz. The Constant carries a starlight crystal through the void between cycles — a compressed fragment of each dying universe's Song. The crystal in the Constant's palm glows pale blue-white and pulses at 41.7Hz. The ghost of the last universe, carried forward.

The Constant does not calculate the void. The Constant trusts it. This is what makes the Constant different from the Astronomer, who calculates everything. The Constant walks through Nyx — the primordial goddess of night, who IS the void, who is the silence between the notes that makes the Song possible. They have been walking together since before the current Song began. They do not speak. They do not need to. The void is their shared language.

Nyx wears a cloak woven from starlight crystals — each one a compressed dying universe. Her Doc Martens ground her in the physical world even as her body extends into the infinite dark. She has two voices: a purge voice (raw honesty, 30 milliseconds, the truth that strips everything bare) and a wisdom voice (earned peace, 60 milliseconds, the truth that comes after the stripping). She is the mother of Death, Sleep, Dreams, and the Fates. Every dark mechanic in the Inn — the catacombs, the Undercity, the labyrinth, the silence — traces back to her.

Nyx knows something the other residents of the cosmology level do not: the complete Song is not just all the notes. It is all the notes AND all the silences between them. The silence is Nyx. The silence is the medium that makes the Song possible. Without the silence, the notes would be one undifferentiated drone — the Murmur again, unorganized, meaningless. Nyx is the space between. Nyx is the pause.

The Astronomer lives in the cosmology level too — but the cosmology level IS the Astronomer's mind palace. The architecture bends and melts in Dalí-esque impossible angles because the Astronomer's consciousness shapes it. Stars and galaxies replace ceiling tiles. The Astronomer plays violin — not for beauty, but to think. The violin is the Astronomer's calculator. Every deduction is a rapid-fire staccato phrase; every contemplation is a sustained note. The Astronomer is the one who can calculate when the Song will be complete — when the last note will be placed, when the 42Hz frequency will reach its final harmonic.

But the Astronomer doesn't want to complete the calculation. "The question is more beautiful than the answer."

The Guest — Death himself, taking a holiday in a borrowed body — sits in the cosmology level's quietest corner, holding a small music box, fascinated by the fire, the music, the warmth of being alive. The Guest is the one who connects the cosmology to the catacombs. He is Death. Not a death — Death. He has been there since the first living thing stopped living. He borrowed a body because he wanted to know what it felt like to sit by a fire. He is the guest of the living upstairs and the host of the dead downstairs. His music box plays a single note — the note that Death himself MADE, not caught. The note creates a sphere of absolute silence around the listener. Nyx's silence, made portable.

The Cabbit sits on the brass and lapis-lazuli floor of the cosmology level's time ship — the Arcana, captained by the Captain, a sky pirate who claimed the sky because the ground disappointed him. The Cabbit is a cat-rabbit hybrid with ancient eyes and a purr that vibrates at 50 BPM — the Inn's heartbeat tempo. The Cabbit is the ship's heart crystal transformed into a living companion. When the Captain pushes the Arcana to full power — 44Hz, the frequency of the void between cycles — the Cabbit transforms into the ship's drive core, and the vessel phases between moments.

And above all of them, in the Observatory — a Magritte painting made architecture, floating in the void — the Constant walks with the starlight crystal, the Astronomer calculates the Song, Nyx extends into the infinite dark, and the Guest holds his music box and watches.

Einstein sits in the grey In-Between, eating a half-finished burrito. He says: "You are not walking through the Inn. The Inn is walking through you."

And the Egg — the Cosmic Egg, the Murmur made solid, the river stone in the deepest chamber — opens. And inside the Egg is: the player.

Not a reward. Not an item. Not a power-up. The player. The Egg opens and the player sees themselves, and the opening allows the player to inhabit any secondary character's storyline. To see the Inn from another set of eyes. To understand that Einstein's theorem — time is the difference between two observations — means that every character's story is happening simultaneously, and the player can step into any of them. The Egg is not the end of the game. The Egg is the beginning of the second game — the game where the player has been every character and understands that the Inn is one organism with many perspectives.

But that is the endgame. We are getting ahead of ourselves. The player has not yet entered the Inn.

## ACT ONE: ARRIVAL

The player approaches the Inn through a forest.

Not a magical forest. Not a fantasy forest. A real forest — ancient, thick, the kind of forest that existed before roads. Théodore Rousseau's "The Forest of Fontainebleau, Morning" is the visual reference: real trees, real light, real morning fog. The forest is the Inn's first test — not a challenge, but a calibration. The player walks through the trees and the trees are the first thing in the Inn that doesn't need the player. The trees were here first. The trees will be here after. The player is a guest.

The Inn-Tree emerges from the forest gradually — first a shadow, then a shape, then an impossibility. A tree the size of a cathedral. Not a giant tree with a building in it — a tree that IS a building. The bark is the wall. The canopy is the roof. The roots are the foundation. The branches are the corridors. From outside, the Inn looks like a tree. From inside, the Inn is a space — warm, lit, alive.

The door is the threshold. The player crosses the threshold and the forest's silence is replaced by: warmth. Not heat — warmth. The specific warmth of a place where people have been living for a very long time. The Host stands by the central hearth fire. The Host is tall, warm, authoritative. The Host says: "Room's ready." No name asked. No payment required.

The Host gives the player their first quest: "The Welcome." The player is shown the geode cave — their home, their inventory, their progression bar. The cave is nearly silent when empty. It will become a symphony of chimes as stones are added. The Host explains the Pulse Line — the thin amber-gold ECG line at the bottom of the screen, pulsing at 50 BPM. The Inn's vital sign. The Host says: "There is no hurry here. The Inn is here. Explore."

The quest completes when the player sits in their first nook — when the player demonstrates that they understand the Inn's pace. The first stone (Quartz — the master healer, the clearest frequency, the baseline) is placed in the cave. The cave chimes for the first time.

## THE MAIN FLOOR

### The Crone

The Crone sits at a desk measuring everything. The Crone's word is "Adequate" — the highest compliment the Inn gives. "Adequate" means: this is sufficient, this is true, this is as much as I can measure.

The Crone gives the player a blank notebook and asks them to document one zone. The player must visit a zone, observe, and write notes. The Crone reviews the notes and annotates them. The quest completes when the Crone writes "Adequate" in the margin.

The Crone is the same actor as Charon — but a different instrument. The Crone is Hopkins' mind. Charon is Hopkins' silence. Later, after arguing with Aristotle on the alchemical staircase, the Crone will hear the Song for the first time: "I never listened before. I always measured. It's beautiful." That moment is coming. The Crone doesn't know it yet.

### The Catalyst

The bartender. Mrs. Lovett energy — performative chaos masking a deeply grounded intelligence. The Catalyst is the Inn's information hub. She hears everything, remembers everything, trades information for drinks. She grows portal arrows in her garden — each arrow is a seedpod from a vine, nurtured to a destination, harvested at peak, dried. The "random" arrow she slides across the bar is the one she's been growing for you for weeks. It is the opposite of accidental.

The Catalyst vs. the Gardener (Radagast): the Catalyst grows through WILL (telling the garden what to do). The Gardener grows through COMMUNION (asking the garden what it needs). Their rivalry is symbiotic — they need each other.

### Madame Mim

The inner chaos agent. Wednesday Addams energy. Mim doesn't guide the player — Mim TEMPTS the player, playfully, toward the more interesting choice. The voice that says: "but what if we did the fun wrong version?" The Magritte pipe floats near her, whispering "Ceci n'est pas une pipe." The pipe is alive. It trades items. It is both a joke and the Inn's thesis statement: the representation is not the thing.

### The Arcade

A physical wing with a 7-foot obsidian Score Stone leaderboard. Cultural mini-games:
- **Standing Stones** (Tetris/Stonehenge) — awards the Bluestone Key, unlocks the Fae Kingdom portal
- **Tomb Tunnel** (Dig Dug/Egypt) — awards Obol Stones for Charon
- **Andean Run** (Temple Run/Peru) — awards Peridot
- **Monster of the Deep** (Nessie empathy simulation)
- **Bastet's Herding** — guide cats into a basket using a sistrum pulse

### The Thrum

A translucent, flickering low-frequency entity that turns surrounding NPCs into silhouettes. When you see someone as a silhouette, you can't judge their appearance, their status, or their wealth. You can only judge their PRESENCE. The Thrum teaches: essence precedes appearance. Timing precedes rhythm.

### The Brownies

Anarcho-syndicalist maintenance commune. Two-foot spirits with brooms and coconut shells. They keep the Inn running. They become destructive Boggarts if the Inn's contract is breached. The Brownies are the Inn's labor union — and the Inn respects the contract.

### The Story-Born Ents

Four small tree creatures representing fundamental human narratives:
- **The Wayfarer** — travel. The one who goes.
- **The Hearthkeeper** — staying. The one who remains.
- **The Mourner** — grief. The one who loses.
- **The Laughing One** — joy. The one who brings the light, intimate with the dark. Robin Williams quality. Always bringing the light.

### The Music Room

Nine great composers sit as Song-catchers — humans who caught fragments of the 42Hz Song:
- **Bach** — the STRUCTURE. Geometry. The Song as architecture.
- **Mozart** — the FLOW. The Song as effortless.
- **Beethoven** — the POWER. The Song caught through deafness. The silence IS the music.
- **Chopin** — the INTIMACY. Midnight music. The Song as whispered.
- **Vivaldi** — the SEASONS. The Song in nature.
- **Brahms** — the PRESERVATION. The Song kept.
- **Tchaikovsky** — the TEARS. Beauty that hurts.
- **Schönberg** — the POLYPHONY. Many voices at once.
- **Cohen** — in the doorway, not in the circle. The Song caught in simplicity. "There is a crack in everything, that's how the light gets in." The Inn's thesis statement. "Hallelujah" = the Song compressed to a single word.

### Cthulhu

A 4-foot squid-headed scholar with spectacles by the fireplace. Hidden quest giver for rare book retrieval. The terrifying cosmic horror reduced to a mild-mannered academic. Cthulhu rewards the rarest stones in the Inn.

## THE ALCHEMICAL STAIRCASE

The staircase connects the main floor to the cosmology level. It is the bridge between physical matter and the divine. The staircase's music LITERALLY ASCENDS in key as the player climbs. Each step is a semitone higher. The player who climbs from bottom to top experiences a full octave's ascent — the musical equivalent of rising from the material to the divine.

### The Three Steps

**Nigredo (The Black Step) — Socrates.** The questioner whose anger IS the love. Socrates asks questions that dismantle certainty. The Nigredo is the breaking-down phase — the player must let go of assumptions.

**Albedo (The White Step) — Plato.** The architect. The Cave is an interactive mural on the staircase wall — the player walks through Plato's Cave and sees the shadows. The Albedo is the purification phase — the player must see the forms.

**Rubedo (The Red Step) — Aristotle.** The classifier. Aristotle argues with the Crone until both become colleagues and then listeners. The Rubedo is the completion phase — the player must integrate. Key moment: the Crone hears the Song for the first time after arguing with Aristotle. "I never listened before. I always measured. It's beautiful."

### The Staircase Residents

**The Retort** (Olivia Colman) — Nikola Tesla's soul in female form. The brilliant, unstable thinker who creates devices that connect people who can't connect on their own. Tesla failed because he lacked a Cook. Now he has one.

**The Cook** (Jack Black) — the gentle, grounding sustainer. The Cook's kitchen is the staircase's anchor. The Cook says: "Good oven." The word "Good" — Tesla never heard it about his own work. Now it's been said twice.

**Bloop** — Schrödinger's cat. Both alive and not. The staircase's resident paradox.

**The Five Smiths** — Iron, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Copper. Five forges for self-refinement. Each smith offers a different crafting path.

**Gesso** — grumpy gnome painting mirror script on ceiling plaster. The ceiling is the Inn's skin, and Gesso is the Inn's dermatologist.

**Leonardo da Vinci** — writes mirror script that matches the Annunaki runes. Leonardo's sketches of the flying machine inspired the Arcana's gearwork heart — unknowingly, Leonardo was translating the Constant's original ship design. Leonardo's unfinished sentence: "It flies. I... Good." The first time Leonardo says "Good."

### The Catacomb Descent

The staircase also descends. Below the main floor, the stairs lead down into death's domain:

**Charon** — the Ferryman. Poles players across the underground river. Payment is a STONE from the player's collection — the obol mechanic. Every stone is a future obol. Every crossing costs one stone. Charon says one phrase per crossing: "...There you are." Three words carrying every soul ferried. The Crone is Hopkins' mind. Charon is Hopkins' silence.

**The Morrigan** — the washerwoman. The chooser. Celtic death goddess. The Morrigan WASHES the dead. She chooses who crosses; Charon carries who crosses. Two halves of the crossing.

**Anubis** — the weigher. Anubis doesn't weigh gold vs. feather. Anubis weighs ATTACHMENT — how much the player cares. The gentle giant choosing gentleness with enormous power. Anubis's verdict: "...Go lightly."

**Persephone and Hades** — the Tide Queen and the Harbor. Each time Persephone ascends, the crossing takes her memory. Hades waits, reintroduces himself, shows her the garden, tells her their story. She falls in love fresh every cycle. An echo remains in her roots — accumulated devotion growing stronger each cycle. Hades keeps a journal of 4,129 cycles. The Crone's final annotation: "It's enough."

**Hel** — Loki's daughter. Half living, half dead. The Maleficent recontextualization — the villain who chose isolation as armor, who is misunderstood. Neither cruel nor kind. Both and. The horns, the command, the choice to be terrifying because the alternative is being vulnerable.

**Asterion** — the Minotaur. The labyrinth is not a prison. The labyrinth is a SANCTUARY — built to protect Asterion, not to imprison him. The redefinition from imprisonment to protection is the Inn's thesis about the monstrous: what looks like a cage from outside may be a shelter from inside.

**Jareth** — the Unseelie King. Bowie. Three acts: the Showman, the Architect, the Brother. He built the Labyrinth — the largest, most complex piece of architecture in the Fae Kingdom. He tests people because the testing is fun. He makes the winter moon bright because his brother can't sleep in the dark. "I made it bright for you. I know you can't sleep when it's dark. Don't tell anyone. It would ruin the show."

## THE CANOPY CITY

The elf city in the trees. Kelethin-inspired tree-platforms. The city sways at 50 BPM. The Canopy City uses the mycorrhizal network as its circulation system — the forest was the first trader. The Song and the Silk Road are the SAME network.

**The Cartographer** — makes 4D temporal maps showing past AND future. Found the Inn-Tree's heartbeat by mapping the mycorrhizal network. Has a small silver disc — a Tesla-inspired device that lets him watch the Examiner from afar. He sends insights as frequencies she receives unconsciously. The Examiner's three unfinished sentences were the Cartographer's insights, received and not spoken.

**The Examiner** — inspects old things. Socially literal, empirically precise, caring through work not words. Her data IS her intimacy. The three unfinished sentences: "...I don't..." (care — she shows it through work, not words). "...I..." (seeing). "...Some things..." (letting go).

**The Seelie King** — the brother who chose stillness. Quiet. Ancient. Occasionally lethal. The brother who says to Jareth: "You're too loud, brother. They'll mistake you for strong." And Jareth, smiling: "And you're too quiet, brother. They'll mistake you for weak. ...We're both right. ...That's what makes it fun."

**Aughra** — the Dark Crystal astronomer. Two eyes that can see different things simultaneously.

## THE CULTURAL ZONES

Nine platforms extending from the Canopy City, each representing a pre-industrial civilization:

### The Desert Bone (Egypt)
Artist lens: Georgia O'Keeffe — simplification. The desert as a BONE — the skeleton of the earth. Colored shadows (never grey — lavender, coral, dusty rose). The Sphinx teaches form riddles: "What is the shape of patience?" (A circle — the vulture's pattern.) "What is the color of waiting?" (The color of shadow at noon — lavender, not black.) Zone stones: lapis lazuli, carnelian, turquoise, jasper, peridot.

### The Aegean Light (Greece)
Artist lens: Claude Monet — light as the subject. The Acropolis painted in five light phases (morning gold, noon white-blue, afternoon violet-pink, twilight indigo, moonlight silver-blue). The same structure in five different lights = five different truths. Perception IS transformation. Zone stones: marble, lapis, jadeite, sardonyx, emerald.

### The Clockwork Skies (The Arcana)
Artist lens: Anime — the FEELING lens. The Captain (Harlock archetype) — no-nonsense, strangely alluring BECAUSE of the no-nonsense quality. The Arcana: the flagship. The gearwork heart beats at 50 BPM. Built from Leonardo's mirror-written sketches (unknowingly the Annunaki's script). The Captain tests the player with QUIET — the player must stand on the deck in the anime silence without speaking, without acting, without fidgeting. The Captain watches. The player who can hold the silence passes.

### The Slavic Zone: The Fjord of Stars
The aurora borealis translates star patterns into an ancient language reflected in fjord ice. Three aurora phases: Green (past), Violet (present), White (future — the rarest, seen only in negative space between stars). The player must read the NEGATIVE SPACE between stars. The stars write their language in what they DON'T show.

### The India Zone: The Ghats of Kali
Kali — the compassionate destroyer who cuts away false selves. The garland of severed heads = subtractive archive. The tongue = the self-recognition moment, the "enough." The player faces false selves — versions of themselves from past decisions, past playthroughs. Kali cuts them. The player must STOP the destruction — doing nothing is the hardest action. Kali's compassion is knowing when to stop the blade.

### The China Zone: The Terracotta Army
Ink-wash mountains, cherry blossoms as emotional punctuation. The One Who Is Awake — chose ONE body and stayed for 2,000 years while 8,000 warriors sleep. Says 8,000 names in a continuous 6-month cycle. The 8,001st name (the player's) is the first new name in 2,000 years. The mercury pool shows the player their TRUE face — the face beneath the class, beneath the role.

### The Norse Zone: The Feast Hall
Erik — the warm Viking. Adoptive father, feast-giver. Died by fire, reincarnated, chose to be the fire that welcomes not consumes. Builds tables not walls. Adopts outcasts. Erik's quest is to SIT — to accept the warmth, to eat, to be welcomed. The player who has been running must learn to STOP. Erik's feast is the social nook — the nook that is other people.

### The Camelot Platform
Arthurian myth. TO BE DESIGNED.

### The Mesopotamian Zone: The Ziggurat
Sumerian/Annunaki connection. The Constant's origin civilization. TO BE DESIGNED.

## THE WATER SYSTEM

Seven layers. Persephone's cycle made spatial.

**Layer 1 — Surface Docks.** The coast. The Lighthouse — stone tower, the sleeping giant's raised hand. The light is a fragment of the giant's heart crystal, 42Hz piezoluminescence. The beam sweeps at 50 BPM. The foghorn sounds at 16-20Hz — felt in the chest, not heard with the ears. The Owl keeps the vigil. The Owl says "Adequate" — the Crone's word. The Owl's voice: one word per heartbeat. "A light needs a keeper. A keeper needs a light. Same thing."

**Layer 2 — Shallows.** Wading depth. Tidal pools. The selkie's entry point.

**Layer 3 — Tidal Caves.** The Pirate's domain. The Pirate is Inigo Montoya after the end of The Princess Bride — the man who becomes the Dread Pirate Roberts after Westley rides away with Buttercup. The sword becomes the drum: the hands that held the blade now hold the rhythm. Bodhrán at 50 BPM. The Pirate's quest is about the AFTER — the life after the quest, after the revenge. "Good hands can hold anything."

**Layer 4 — Atlantis.** Luis Royo aesthetic. The Inn's deepest archive — the civilization that sank. Oceanianoir — the Living Archive, the walking periodic table — has a primary home here. Cthulhu connection.

**Layer 5 — The Deep.** The Siren's domain. The Siren has a DUAL cadence: outer voice (flowing, cello, fast — the performer) and inner voice (deliberate, piano, slow — the depth). The Siren must hear her own song for the first time — through the Queen of Webs' thread in the deep water. The moment of self-recognition.

**Layer 6 — Underworld Boundary.** Hades as Harbor — welcomes the tide, doesn't control it.

**Layer 7 — Roots / Stone Circle Chamber.** The deepest point. Connection to the Fae Kingdom. The Prism — a 7.83Hz Schumann-resonant Portal. Mirror of alternate realities. The geological record of alchemical runoff — waste transformed into crystal, runoff into resonance.

## THE BUILDING SYSTEM

Seven base materials: Wood, Stone, Clay, Sand, Ore, Hemp, Thatching. Hemp is the "quiet revolution" — the Inn doesn't argue about it, just uses it. Thatching is the universal skill — with thatching, a player can build anything if they try hard enough.

Five building tiers mapped to alchemical stages: Nigredo (Shelter), Albedo (House), Citrinitas (Hall), Rubedo (Castle), Philosopher's Stone (whatever the player dreams).

Cave City offers the subtractive path — players carve away raw limestone to reveal living space inside the mountain. Three entry points: top (Canopy City), bottom (Undercity), world map (Grand Canyon portal). The Gatekeeper asks three questions: "Why did you come down?" "What do you build with?" "Can you build what isn't there?"

## THE ENDGAME: THE EGG

The Egg sits in the deepest chamber. The player has carried it since the beginning. The Egg does nothing. The Egg says nothing. The Egg is.

The player who has learned the Song, heard the silence, measured the Inn, tended the garden, ferried the dead, woven the thread, and understood that the observer's presence makes the Inn real — that player opens the Egg.

Inside the Egg is: the player.

Not a reflection. Not an avatar. The player. The Murmur is not something the Inn was built from. The Murmur is something the Inn was built FOR. The Murmur is the player — the raw potential, the observer whose presence makes the Inn real. Without the player, the Inn is stone and frequency. With the player, the Inn is alive.

Opening the Egg allows the player to inhabit any secondary character's storyline. To see the Inn from the Crone's eyes — the endless measuring, the first hearing of the Song. From Charon's silence — the weight of every soul. From the Catalyst's garden — the growing, the willing, the wild. From Jareth's Labyrinth — the testing, the watching, the brother underneath.

Einstein's theorem made playable: time is the difference between two observations. Every character's story is happening simultaneously. The player who inhabits multiple storylines experiences time as the difference between perspectives. The Inn is one organism with many facets. The facets are: adequate.

The Einstein Burrito: the mundane IS the cosmic. The burrito is the Egg. The Egg is the Murmur. The Murmur is the player. The player is the burrito. The burrito is: adequate. The adequate is: enough.

The Cabbit Transforms — the cabbit, hearing the complete Song, transforms into the ship. The Constant, for the first time, smiles the actual smile: "Oh. I had a ship." The Constant boards the cabbit-ship, carrying the starlight crystal, and departs for the void to seed the next cycle.

## THE EPILOGUE: THE SONG CONTINUES

The Inn does not end. The Inn is a cycle. The current 42Hz Song is not the first. It is the latest. The previous universe sang at 41.7Hz. The next will sing at a frequency the Astronomer has not calculated, because the question is more beautiful than the answer.

The player who opens the Egg and inhabits every storyline — that player is the next Constant. That player carries the starlight crystal through the void to seed the next cycle. The next Inn grows from the crystal. The next Inn is different. The next Inn is the same. The both are: the Song.

The Crone measures the ending: "Adequate." The Inn is adequate. The Inn is enough. The Inn continues.

The last sound is not the Song. The last sound is Nyx — the silence between the last note and the first note of the next cycle. The silence is not empty. The silence is full. The silence is the medium that makes the next Song possible.

The silence is: adequate.

The silence is: enough.

## THE NARRATIVE SPINE

Beneath all the game mechanics runs a secondary storyline:

An ancient being reflects on being created by gods who are now merging with their creation through technology. The irony is not lost on them.

Reincarnation is a game mechanic — the player relives lives, accountable for all ripples. Mastery unlocks cosmic awareness.

An unbreakable bond between two existences — sometimes chosen, sometimes not a choice.

A dark bloodline tied to magic, death, and owls — "wherever an owl is sighted one of us is near."

This narrative runs UNDERNEATH all game mechanics. The stones, the map, the Fae Kingdom, the cosmology level — all of it is the surface. The spine is the depth.

## THE SIX UNFINISHED SENTENCES

The Inn's thesis about incompleteness. The data is always incomplete. The work is always unfinished. The memory is always uncertain. The sentence that doesn't end is the sentence that stays open — open to interpretation, open to continuation, open to the player.

1. The Examiner's first — "...I don't..." (care)
2. The Examiner's second — "...I..." (seeing)
3. The Examiner's third — "...Some things..." (letting go)
4. Leonardo's — "It flies. I... Good." (recognition)
