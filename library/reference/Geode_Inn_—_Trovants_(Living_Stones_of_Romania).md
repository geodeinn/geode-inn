# Geode Inn — Trovants: The Living Stones of Romania

## Overview

Trovants are a real geological phenomenon found in Costesti, Wallachia, Romania — the same region as Vlad the Impaler's Poenari Castle. They are sandstone concretions that grow, move, and multiply when exposed to mineral-rich water. The Geode Inn treats trovants as the thesis creature of the Romania/Transylvania zone — a stone that is also a creature, a transformation that is also a music game, and a geological process that is also sacred geometry.

---

## Real Geology

- **What they are:** Sandstone concretions with a hard stone core surrounded by a shell of cemented sand. The name "trovant" is Romanian for "growing stone."
- **How they grow:** Rainwater carrying calcium carbonate and other minerals seeps into the stone. Minerals precipitate on the surface, creating new layers. Growth rate: ~4-5 centimeters per 1,000 years.
- **Tree rings:** When cut open, trovants show spherical and ellipsoidal rings — like tree trunk rings. The rings record geological history: every earthquake, every mineral deposit, every frequency shift.
- **Movement:** Trovants appear to move over time. Locals swear they've seen them walk — with feet. Science attributes this to soil heating/cooling and seismic activity. The folklore says: they go where they want.
- **Multiplication:** Uneven mineral secretion causes trovants to appear to multiply. Complex aggregates — two or more trovants fused together — are common.
- **Shapes:** Spheres, ellipsoids, mushrooms, and irregular organic forms. The perfect spheres are believed to result from the strongest ancient earthquakes.
- **Age:** Up to 6 million years old, formed during seismic activity in a sedimentary basin.
- **Location:** Trovants Museum Natural Reserve in Valcea County, protected by UNESCO since 2004.
