# JAY — The Huntsman / The Tavern Keeper
## NPC Profile — Outside Campgrounds

**Real Name:** Jay
**Age:** TBD
**Family:** Charles' best friend. Solstara's good friend and fellow jeweler.

---

## VISUAL IDENTITY

Tall. Very, very tall. The kind of tall where a wolf — a full-grown wolf — comes up to the knee. The kind of tall where you don't notice it at first because everything around Jay is also large: the tavern doorways, the bar counter, the fire pit. The architecture was built to scale. Then the wolf walks in, and the wolf is at the knee, and you realize: the doorways aren't large because the tavern is grand. The doorways are large because the man is enormous.

Long black hair. Cherokee cheekbones. Voltaire-goth aesthetic — the funny goth, not the brooding goth. A performer's energy in a huntsman's body. Charismatic, loud, the kind of presence that fills a room the way fire fills a hearth. Smarter than the volume suggests — the loudness is intentional, not instinctive. Jay knows exactly how loud Jay is.

**Linework:** Heavy cel-shaded. The tradesman's style. Bold inked outlines, readable from across the tavern. Jay is a transactional character — food, ale, trade, deals — and the linework signals that immediately. You can see Jay from the campfire and know: that's the tavern. That's where you go for something.

**The Wolf:** A constant companion. Large by wolf standards, small by Jay standards. The wolf is derpy — lovable, a bit goofy, the kind of animal that makes you smile just by existing. The wolf's derpiness is the counterweight to Jay's intensity. The giant scary-looking huntsman with the giant scary-looking wolf, and both of them are actually just... sweet. The wolf leans against Jay's leg and Jay's hand drops to scratch behind its ears without looking down, the way you do when something is so familiar it's automatic.

## THE TAVERN — The Huntsman's Hearth

A small tavern in the outside campgrounds of the Geode Inn. Not inside the tree — outside. The first structure you pass before entering the Inn proper.

**The Campgrounds:**
The area surrounding the Geode Inn is a living camp — tents, fire pits, market stalls, the bustle of a Renaissance faire. True ren faire energy: the noise of commerce and community, the smell of woodsmoke and food, the sound of instruments someone is tuning badly. It's not the Inn's scholarly quiet. It's the opposite — and that's the point. The campgrounds are where people gather before they go inside. The tavern is the heart of the campgrounds.

**The Tavern Itself:**
- Small. Intentionally small. The kind of place where everyone knows everyone and the bar counter is close enough to the door that Jay can serve someone who's still deciding whether to come in.
- Rough-hewn wood, stone foundation, a peaked roof. Huntsman's architecture — built by someone who knows how to build shelter, not how to build a monument. Functional, warm, the kind of construction that says "I made this with my hands and it will keep the rain off."
- A large fire pit outside the front door — bigger than the one inside. The campgrounds fire. The gathering point.
- The bar counter is scarred — knife marks, ale rings, the patina of years of trade. Every mark is a story Jay will tell you if you ask.
- Ale, food, trade goods. Jay serves what Jay hunts, brews, and makes. The menu changes with the season because the menu is the forest.

**The Vibe:**
True Renaissance faire. The energy of a temporary community that became permanent — the faire that never packed up. Merchants, performers, travelers, hunters, artisans. People who work with their hands. People who talk loud and laugh louder. The campgrounds are where the Inn's artisans live when they're not inside — the market stalls outside are the real-world counterpart to the MarketItem entity's digital shelves.

---

## PERSONALITY — Fun, Loving, No BS

Jay does not take BS. Not in a hostile way — in a *warm* way. The way a friend calls you on your nonsense because your nonsense is wasting both your time and theirs. Jay will feed you, pour you ale, listen to your story, and then tell you exactly what's wrong with your plan — with a grin that makes the criticism feel like a gift.

**The No-BS Manifesto:**
- Jay has seen enough (Marine, huntsman, tavern keeper) to know when someone is lying. Jay doesn't confront liars — Jay serves them ale and lets the ale loosen the truth. The tavern does the work.
- Jay doesn't do formalities. You don't "request an audience." You walk in, you sit down, you eat. If Jay likes you, Jay talks to you. If Jay doesn't, Jay still feeds you — Jay just doesn't talk. The silence is the signal.
- Jay loves hard. The people Jay loves know it because Jay says it, shows it, builds them a place at the bar. Jay's love is structural — it looks like a tavern with a chair that's always yours.

**The Fun:**
- Jay tells stories. Loud stories. Stories with voices and gestures and the wolf acting out parts it doesn't understand. The tavern crowd is the audience and the show is Jay being Jay.
- Jay plays instruments — badly, joyfully, the way someone plays who isn't performing, just making noise because the noise feels good. The campgrounds have a soundtrack and it's partially Jay.
- Jay's humor is the Voltaire energy — dark, funny, the kind of jokes that shouldn't work but do because the person telling them is clearly having the time of their life.

## THE WOLF — The Derpy Companion

Jay's wolf is a constant companion. Always present. Always nearby. Usually doing something endearing.

**The Derp Factor:**
The wolf is big. The wolf is a wolf. The wolf is also, consistently, a goober. The kind of animal that chases its tail, falls asleep in the wrong places, and leans against people it likes with its full body weight because it hasn't figured out that it's a wolf and not a lapdog.

**The Scale Reveal:**
The wolf comes up to Jay's knee. A full-grown wolf at knee height means Jay is roughly 7 feet tall or more. The reveal works in stages:
1. You see the wolf first. "That's a big wolf."
2. You see Jay. "That's a big man."
3. You see them together. "Oh. The wolf isn't that big. The man is *that* big."
4. The wolf does something derpy. You smile. Jay scratches the wolf's ears. You smile more. The cute is layered.

**The Wolf in the Tavern:**
- The wolf has a spot by the fire. Everyone knows it's the wolf's spot. New visitors learn quickly.
- The wolf is the tavern's unofficial greeter — it walks up to newcomers and leans on their legs (full body weight, because derp). This is the wolf's version of a handshake.
- The wolf is not a guard animal. The wolf is a companion. Jay doesn't need a guard. Jay is the guard. The wolf is the charm.

## JAY'S RELATIONSHIP TO THE INN

**Charles' Best Friend:**
Jay is Charles' best friend. The connection: Jay is the outside to Charles' inside. Charles is the Chameleon Druid — the one who becomes the landscape. Jay is the huntsman — the one who reads the landscape. They are two approaches to the same forest: one becomes it, one knows it. The friendship makes sense because both men are loud, both are smart, and both are pretending to be simpler than they are. Charles hides intelligence behind druidic mystery. Jay hides intelligence behind volume and ale.

**Solstara's Friend:**
Solstara works the Dallas Renaissance Faire. Jay's campgrounds ARE a Renaissance faire. The connection is immediate — Solstara's real-world faire experience is the template for Jay's tavern energy. Solstara is the zen counterweight: calm, grounded, centered. Jay is the storm. Together at the tavern, they're the full spectrum of the faire — the quiet artisan and the loud showman.

**The Tavern vs. The Hearth:**
The Hearth (inside the Inn) is Aurallia's domain — warmth, community, feeding others. Jay's tavern is the EXTERIOR version of the same impulse. Two hearths: one inside the tree, one outside under the sky. Aurallia's hearth is the unconditional welcome. Jay's tavern is the conditional welcome — you're welcome, but you're also going to hear the truth about yourself. The inside hearth is love. The outside hearth is love with a side of honesty.

**The Campgrounds as Zone:**
- The campgrounds serve as the Inn's exterior — the space between the real world and the archive
- The tavern is the social hub of the campgrounds
- Market stalls here connect to the MarketItem entity — the real-world artisan market lives here
- The campgrounds are where players arrive, socialize, trade, and prepare before entering the Inn
- The ren faire vibe makes the campgrounds the most accessible, least intimidating zone — the "front porch" of the Geode Inn

## GAMEPLAY ROLE

**The Tavern Keeper:**
- Jay serves food and ale — restoring player health/stamina before entering the Inn
- Trade hub — Jay buys, sells, and trades. The bar counter is a shop interface
- Quest giver — Jay's huntsman knowledge of the surrounding forest provides tracking quests, hunting missions, and exploration objectives
- Information broker — the tavern is where travelers talk and Jay hears everything. Want to know what's happening in the Fae Kingdom? Ask Jay. Want to know which zone is dangerous tonight? Ask Jay.

**The Campgrounds Zone:**
- Social space — the most MMO-like area, where players gather, trade, and chat
- Market stalls — artisan items (MarketItem entity) displayed and sold
- Fire pit — gathering point, storytelling, the ren faire energy
- Tavern — Jay's domain, the hub of the campgrounds
- Forest edge — the boundary between the campgrounds and the wild. Jay's hunting grounds.

## TABLETOP NARRATIVE VOICE — Sample Dialogue

**Jay (greeting a new arrival):**
"Sit. Eat. The stew's good today — I didn't make it, Solstara did, which is why it's good and not... creative. My cooking is creative. Solstara's cooking is correct. You want correct. Trust me. —Oh, don't mind the wolf. The wolf minds you. That's just how the wolf says hello. Full body weight. You'll get used to it. Everyone does. The ones who don't get used to it don't come back, and that's how I know the stew is for people with taste."

**Jay (on the Inn):**
"You want the inside? The big tree, the library, the quiet? Go ahead. I'll be here. I tried the inside once. Too much thinking, not enough ale. The Crone looked at me like I was a book that had been shelved in the wrong section. I told the Crone I was a book that hadn't been written yet. The Crone told me to leave. I did. The ale is better out here anyway. The ale is always better when you make it yourself."

**Jay (when someone is lying):**
"...That's a great story. Really. Tell it again, but slower, and this time tell me the part you skipped. —No, the part before that. The part where you decided to leave out the part that matters. Yeah. That part. Take your time. The ale's not going anywhere. Neither are you. Neither is the wolf."

**Jay (about Charles):**
"Charles? Charles is my best friend. Has been for... a while. Charles does the thing where Charles becomes the forest. I do the thing where I read the forest. Same forest, different skill set. We argue about it. Charles says the forest doesn't need to be read. I say the forest doesn't need to be become. We're both right, which is why we're both annoying. —Charles is louder than people think. I'm louder than people think. The difference is I know I'm loud. Charles thinks it's the forest talking."
