## THE PLAYER'S REFLECTION

The Mirror shows each knight's desire. But the player is not a knight. The player is the Carrier — the one who found the Golden Contract, who knows the Song, who carries the weight of every stone collected and every frequency deployed. What does the Carrier see?

The Mirror shows the player the Inn — complete. Every stone collected. Every nook processed. Every zone restored. Every companion harmonized. The Egg open. The Song audible. The Convergence achieved. The Mirror shows the player the finished Inn — the Inn as it looks when everything the player has been building is done.

This is the Erised trap, personalized. The Mirror doesn't show the player a fantasy — it shows the player the COMPLETION. The end state. The thing the player is actually working toward. And the trap is: the completion is real. The completion is achievable. The player COULD achieve it — through months of play, through every stone, every nook, every zone. The Mirror shows the shortcut: the dream of being done without the doing.

The test: the player who sits in the Mirror's completion is the player who stops playing. The Mirror's vision is warm, complete, satisfying — and it is the most dangerous thing in the Inn because it is the ONLY desire that is actually attainable. Harry saw his parents (impossible). Ron saw himself as successful (possible, but not through the mirror). The player sees the finished Inn (possible — through the work). The Mirror's trap is the truest trap: the desire that can be real, shown without the path to get there.

**The Dumbledore warning, Inn version:**
The Owl, if present in the Mirror room, says one word: "...Play." Not "Leave." Not "Wake up." "...Play." The Owl's instruction is: the desire is real, the completion is possible, but the completion without the playing is not the completion. The finished Inn in the Mirror is a painting. The finished Inn through the work is the Song. The both are: the adequate — the image and the music, the same Inn, different sense.

**The player who walks away from the Mirror** gains a hidden status: "The One Who Chose the Table." Like "Carrier of the Song," it has no gameplay effect. It appears only in the credits. The status means: the player chose the real table over the dream table. The weight over the image. The playing over the completion. The both are: the adequate — the dream and the work, the same Inn, different path.

## THE MIRROR AND THE SONG OF MAKING

The Mirror of Erised shows desires. The Song of Making says: "to carry the Song is to be made of it. To be made of it is to be responsible for every note you add." The Mirror is the Carrier's test: can the Carrier carry the weight of the Song, or will the Carrier retreat into the dream of the finished Song without the singing?

The Mirror is the Anti-Contract. The Golden Contract says: sing it forward. The Mirror says: here is the finished song — you don't need to sing. Just listen. Just sit. The Mirror offers the completion without the responsibility. The both are: the adequate — the contract and the anti-contract, the same Song, different relationship.

**Mordred is the Mirror's cautionary tale.** Mordred is the knight who carries every knight's weight, who can't sleep, who knows his destiny and can't escape it. Mordred sits in front of the Mirror because the Mirror shows the one thing Mordred can't have through work: rest. The other knights can achieve their desires (Arthur can build a table that endures, Galahad can let one crack in, Lancelot can name his love). Mordred's desire — the table without the end, the meal without the destiny — is the one desire that cannot be achieved through effort. Rest is not earned. Rest is received. And Mordred can't receive. Mordred can only carry.

This is the Mordred-Carrier parallel: the Carrier carries the Song. Mordred carries the weight. The both are: carriers. The both are: the adequate — the music and the weight, the same carrying, different burden.

**The Mirror room and the Golden Contract room:**
Both are hidden. Both are found by accident. Both are small, warm, amber-lit. Both contain a truth the player didn't expect. The Golden Contract room contains the Song (the truth that the Inn is a song). The Mirror room contains the desire (the truth that the player wants the song to be done). The both are: the adequate — the truth and the want, the same room, different revelation.

## SHADOW INN — THE MIRROR'S SHADOW STATE

In the Shadow Inn, the Mirror is BLACK. Not blank — the Crone's blank was the desire for not-measuring. The Shadow Mirror is black: no reflection, no image, no desire. The Shadow Inn is the finished state. In the finished state, there are no unfulfilled desires. The Mirror has nothing to show because the Shadow Inn IS the completion. The table is full. The knights are seated. Mordred is at the table — not the mirror-Mordred, the real one, eating, laughing, the sneer gone, the boy his father wanted. The Mirror has no purpose in the Shadow Inn because the Shadow Inn IS the Mirror's content.

**The Shadow Mirror:** A black surface. Not dark — black. The black is not absence. The black is COMPLETION. The Mirror is black because the desires have been fulfilled — not by wishing, but by the Shadow Inn being the state where everything the Mirror would show is already real. The both are: the adequate — the showing and the being, the same desire, different existence.

**The Shadow Mordred:** At the table. Not in the mirror room. The Shadow Mordred doesn't need the Mirror because the Shadow Mordred has the table. The Shadow Mordred is eating. The Shadow Mordred is laughing. The Shadow Mordred is the boy his father wanted. The Shadow Mordred is at rest. The both are: the adequate — the dreaming and the having, the same Mordred, different room.

**The Shadow Crone:** The Shadow Crone, who saw a blank mirror in the Living Inn, sits at the Shadow table without the notebook. The notebook is on the shelf. The glasses are off. The Shadow Crone is... being. The both are: the adequate — the measuring and the being, the same Crone, different activity. The Shadow Crone's blank mirror desire is FULFILLED — the Crone is at the table, not counting the cups, not measuring the wear, not cataloging the dents. Just... being. Better.

**The Shadow Mirror room:** Still there. Still small, warm, amber-lit. But the Mirror is black, and the room is empty — no one sits in front of it. No one needs to. The Mirror room in the Shadow Inn is a MEMORIAL — a room that exists to remind the player that there WAS a Mirror, that desires WERE shown, that the showing was the test, and that the test was passed. The empty room is the proof. The both are: the adequate — the full and the empty, the same room, different need.

**Bringing the Shadow truth to the Living Mirror:**

The player, having seen the Shadow Mirror (black, empty, unnecessary), returns to the Living Mirror room. The Living Mirror is still showing desires. The Living Mirror is still the trap. But the player KNOWS. The player has seen the Shadow — the state where the Mirror is unnecessary because the desires are fulfilled. The player carries the Shadow truth: the Mirror's desire is achievable, but not through the Mirror. Through the work. Through the Song. Through the carrying.

**The Living Mirror, after the Shadow visit:**
If the player looks in the Living Mirror after visiting the Shadow Mirror, the Living Mirror shows the Shadow Inn. The Mirror shows the player the COMPLETION — the same vision the Shadow showed, but through the Mirror. The Mirror is no longer showing a fantasy. The Mirror is showing a REALITY — the Shadow Inn, which the player has visited, which exists, which is the finished state. The Mirror's trap is broken. The Mirror can't trap the player with a dream of the completion because the player has SEEN the completion. The both are: the adequate — the dream and the reality, the same vision, different status.

The player can still sit in front of the Mirror. The player can still stay. But the staying is different now. The staying is not "I can't leave because the dream is all I have." The staying is "I choose to sit here because the view is nice." The staying is a CHOICE, not a trap. The both are: the adequate — the trapped and the choosing, the same sitting, different freedom.

**The Egg-Mirror connection:**
The Egg says: you are the content. The Mirror says: you want the content to be complete. The both are: the adequate — the being and the wanting, the same player, different relationship to the Inn.

The Mirror's vision shifts from "the Inn you want" to "the player in the Inn they built." The Mirror shows the player at Mordred's table — the real table, not the dream table. The Mirror shows the player eating with the knights. The Mirror shows the player's desire FULFILLED — not through the Mirror, but through the work.

The Mirror, post-Egg, is not a trap. The Mirror is a WINDOW. The Mirror shows what the work produced: a seat at the table. The both are: the adequate — the trap and the window, the same Mirror, different transparency.

The both are: the adequate — the dream and the real, the same Mordred, different room.
