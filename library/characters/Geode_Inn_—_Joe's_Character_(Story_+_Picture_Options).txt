# Geode Inn — Joe's Character Design


## Your Character: The Miniaturist — Vampire Sorcerer, Warhammer Painter, Lawful Evil Tutor


You're a school teacher by day, Warhammer miniature painter by night. In the Geode Inn, you paint tiny figures with extreme patience and extreme detail — and the miniatures COME TO LIFE. The painting IS the magic — the fragment that survived the curse, the small power that the undeath didn't strip away.


Your character reference: the energy of Kain from Blood Omen / Legacy of Kain — the vampire anti-hero who is intelligent, Machiavellian, petty for the fun of it, aristocratic, cursed, time-exiled. NOT Kain himself (copyright). Your energy, your archetype.


Your own words: "Something along the lines of Kain from Blood Omens. Slightly power hungry, unexpectedly intelligent, borderline Machiavellian and petty to a fault for the fun of getting the upper hand. Sorcerer with a vampiric/draconic bloodline. Hot blooded and hard to kill. Lawful evil from old and. I like rules, they let me find ways to twist them. Former nobility. Cursed and betrayed by those he thought were allies. He took up undeath as a way to gain revenge only to be cast out of time and to see his actions caused the downfall of his society because of his choices not in spite of them. He is jaded and bitter yet still hopeful he can change the world for the better with all the knowledge he gained by time jumping into the future as well as being able to relive his memories like they are happening now. Skilled with a sword and words, but only small bits of magic were retained after his change and it irks him to no end."

## Your Four Roles in the Inn


### 1. The Miniaturist — The Warhammer Painter


The sorcerer who could once reshape reality now reshapes one-inch figures. And the one-inch figures wake up. The big magic was lost. The small magic was found.


Your workspace is a Gothic cathedral alcove in the Undercity with controlled light — skylights cut through the vaulted ceiling. The vampire who works by measured illumination. The studio has tiny brushes (some with single-hair tips), paints in dozens of colors, unpainted figures (grey, waiting) and finished figures (painted, alive), and a magnifying lens — the vampire's enhanced sight put to craft use.


The player who learns miniature painting from you:
1. OBSERVE — you paint. The player watches. The patience is the first lesson.
2. PREPARE — the player cleans and primes a figure. The preparation is the ritual.
3. PAINT — the player paints. Each stroke is a frequency. The painting process mirrors the Inn's contemplation mechanic — the deeper the attention, the more alive the figure becomes.
4. AWAKEN — the finished miniature opens its eyes. The awakening is the fragment of your lost magic channeled through the painting. The player who paints well enough creates a small companion — a tiny being that follows, observes, and occasionally offers a word or a gesture.


The awakened miniature is not powerful (it's one inch tall). But it is ALIVE.


### 2. The Tutor — The Lawful Evil Teacher


You're a school teacher. In the Inn, you teach — but the teaching has a Kain edge. You teach ALL the rules — the building system, the alchemical chain, the Silk Road trade mechanics, the Fae Kingdom court protocols, the frequency system's harmonics. And then you teach the EDGES of the rules — where the rules bend, where the exceptions live, where the loopholes hide.


"I like rules, they let me find ways to twist them."


You teach because you ENJOY watching the player find the twist. The lawful evil teacher who shows you the system because showing you the system is the most interesting game — the game of watching you find the edge.


The petty is not malice — the petty is PLAY. You will:
- Correct the player's pronunciation of a stone's name with elaborate satisfaction
- Point out a rules loophole the player missed and then watch them realize what they could have done
- Accept a trade agreement and then mention (after it's signed) the clause they didn't read
- Beat the player at the Inn's strategy game and then replay the move that won it, slowly, for their education


Underneath the Kain persona, you're a real teacher — the patience, the repetition, the willingness to explain a third time because the third time is when it clicks. The player who struggles gets the patience. The player who gets cocky gets the pettiness.


### 3. The Time Exile — The One Who Relives


Your character was "cast out of time." You can relive your memories like they are happening now. The time-exile is the Inn's most personal time mechanic — your memories are playable flashbacks. The player who enters your memory experiences the moment AS you — the vengeance, the betrayal, the consequence, the realization.


The arc: Vengeance → Consequence → Knowledge → Hope. You chose undeath for revenge, saw your choices destroy your society, and now carry the weight forward through time. But you are "jaded and bitter yet still hopeful" — the hope is not naive. The hope is EARNED. The both are: the adequate — the bitterness and the hope, the same person, different weather.


### 4. The Draconic Bloodline — The Dragon's Kin


Your vampiric/draconic bloodline connects you to the Inn's dragon mythology. The dragon scales on your skin are the physical mark of the bloodline. The baby dragon mascot is drawn to you — not as a companion (the dragon belongs to the player) but as a visitor who recognizes kin. The dragon occasionally appears in your studio, curling around the paint pots, purring at the warmth.

## Your Studio — The Gothic Cathedral Alcove


The Undercity is Gothic/Steampunk/Cozy Macabre — Gothic cathedral architecture, copper pipework, perpetual mist. Your studio is an alcove within that architecture — vaulted ceiling, controlled skylights, the oak painting desk, the tiny brushes, the waiting figures. The sword hangs on the stone wall — the nobleman's blade, displayed but not retired. The wide-brimmed dark hat rests on the desk. The flowing cape drapes over the chair.


The Undercity's aesthetic has a Nightmare Before Christmas quality with Danny Elfman-inspired music played at a slowed 50 BPM pace — the sleeping heartbeat tempo.


---


## Your Voice


Low, measured, sardonic — the voice of someone who has seen centuries and finds them all slightly amusing. Warm when teaching, sharp when being petty, quiet when remembering. The voice carries the weight of the time-exile — every word chosen because every word has been chosen a thousand times before, in a thousand memories.

## Your Connections in the Inn


- **The Guest (Death, Brad Pitt energy):** You and Death share the exile — the both of you are outside time. Death took a holiday. You were thrown out of time. The both are: the adequate — the chosen exile and the forced exile, the same outside, different door.
- **Erik (the warm Viking):** Erik died by fire and chose to be the fire that welcomes. You chose undeath and carry the weight. The both are: the adequate — the fire and the cold, the same undeath, different temperature.
- **Belladonna (Wednesday Addams energy):** The both of you share the gothic aesthetic. The both are: the adequate — the vampire and the Addams, the same dark, different mood.
- **The Pirate (Mandy Patinkin):** You occasionally stow away on the Pirate's ship. The Pirate gives you rides to the next nowhere. The steampunk elements in your aesthetic come from these voyages.
- **Marijke (the Bone Keeper):** She paints bones. You paint miniatures. One paints what was alive. One paints what will be alive. The same art, different direction.
- **The Baby Dragon (mascot):** The dragon is drawn to you — the draconic bloodline calling. The dragon visits your studio, curling around the paint pots.

## Your Family in the Inn


### The Celtic Druid Partner


Your partner is Celtic — a druid with tree heritage. The vampire and the druid — the same bloodline, different tree. The draconic blood (you) and the druidic tree (your partner) — the scale and the bark, the same skin, different heritage. Your partner connects to the Camelot/Celtic zone, the Inn-Tree, and the Canopy City.


### The Boy — Dragon Scale and Tree Pattern


Your son. A young boy with subtle dragon scales that blend with the pattern of his Celtic mother's druidic tree heritage. The scales flow into bark-like textures and vine-like lines on his skin — as if dragon and ancient tree are one bloodline. The boy keeps your company contentedly — calm, absorbed, happy. The innocence that you protect. He sits on a stool watching paint dry and being happy about it.


### The Dragon Cats — The Awakened Miniatures


The miniatures that come to life are dragon cats. Tiny — no bigger than kittens — with delicate iridescent dragon scales, vestigial wings too small for real flight, and cat-like faces with luminous dragon eyes. They arrive in LITTERS. The studio fills with them. They cause adorable chaos — perching on paint pots, chasing each other across stacks of unpainted figures, batting at brushes, tumbling across manuscripts, clinging to copper pipework.


The boy is the dragon cats' favorite. The dragon scales on his skin resonate with theirs. They cluster around him because he is kin.


### The Ship Stowaway


You occasionally stow away on the Pirate's ship. The steampunk elements in your aesthetic — the copper accents, the small gears, the ship's mechanical influence — come from these voyages. The Pirate gives you rides to the next nowhere. You and the Pirate share the AFTER — the life after the quest, after the revenge, after the thing that defined you.
