# THE GEODE INN — WEARABLE ATTIRE SYSTEM
## Character Costuming for Events, Faires, and Real-World Gathering

---

## THE CONCEPT

The Geode Inn's characters are designed with visual identities strong enough to be worn. This system translates character profiles into wearable attire guides — not screen-accurate cosplay blueprints, but ESSENCE guides that capture each character's frequency, key materials, signature colors, and recognition markers. The standard is: adequate. Not perfect. Not exact. Adequate. The same word the Crone uses. The adequate is: the Inn's costume standard.

The system is designed for people who already build elaborate ensembles for Renaissance faires, conventions, and gatherings. These are people who understand costume as identity, not decoration. The Inn gives them a framework: characters with enough depth, enough visual logic, and enough personal meaning that wearing the attire feels like inhabiting the character, not performing it.

## THE "ADEQUATE" STANDARD

The Inn's costuming standard is NOT screen-accuracy. The standard is: adequate. The Crone's word. The word that is both measurement and love.

**What "adequate" means for costuming:**
- You capture the CHARACTER, not the exact details. The essence, not the blueprint.
- The frequency color is present (even one garment in the right palette counts).
- The sigil is visible (a pendant, a pin, an embroidered patch — any form).
- The stone is worn if possible (but the stone is the DEEPEST layer — the commitment, not the requirement).
- The attire is COMFORTABLE and WEARABLE. The Inn is a cozy space. The costuming should be too. If you can't sit by a fire in it, it's not adequate.

**What "adequate" does NOT mean:**
- Exact replication of any specific design
- Matching a "canonical" costume
- Spending a specific amount of money
- Using specific materials
- Looking like anyone else's version of the same character

The adequate standard means: two people dressed as the same character will look DIFFERENT. The both are: adequate. The adequate is: the Inn's thesis — the same character expressed through different people is the Inn's entire philosophy. Every character is a frequency, not a face. The frequency is the same. The face is: yours.

## THE ATTIRE GUIDE FORMAT

Each character has an attire guide with:

1. **Frequency palette** — the core colors
2. **Materials and textures** — what the character "feels" like
3. **Key garments** — 2-3 essential pieces (not a full costume — a framework)
4. **The sigil** — the symbol and how to wear it
5. **The stone** — the signature mineral
6. **Recognition markers** — the details other Inn players will notice
7. **"The Vibe"** — a 2-3 sentence description of the character's PRESENCE, not their appearance

### Example: Erik

**Frequency:** Fire (50 BPM) — Warm red, ember orange, hearth gold
**Materials:** Wool, linen, copper, iron. Worn-in. Lived-in. The texture of a feast hall that has been warm for centuries.
**Key garments:** A heavy cloak or cape (warm, practical). A belt with a cup hook (always ready to offer a drink). Iron accessories (rings, clasps — the fire's metal).
**Sigil:** A small taglharpa (Nordic lyre) shape — pendant, brooch, or engraved
**Stone:** Preseli blue stone (or any blue-gray stone if Preseli is unavailable)
**Recognition markers:** The warmth of the ensemble. The iron. The sense that this person would build you a table and feed you. The wound underneath the warmth — visible if you look for it.
**The Vibe:** A feast hall that caught fire once and chose to keep burning. The warmth is not naive. The warmth is the wound, transformed. The both are: the same fire.

### Example: Belladonna

**Frequency:** Stone + Silence — Gray, limestone white, deep blue-black
**Materials:** Black velvet, gray wool, jet, carved leather. Solid, still, the weight of carved rock.
**Key garments:** A dark dress or tunic (simple, structured, NOT ornate — Belladonna doesn't decorate). A bench-worthy skirt or coat (something you can SIT in comfortably). Dark boots.
**Sigil:** A tiny carved bench — pendant or pin
**Stone:** Jet (or any black stone)
**Recognition markers:** The deadpan expression. The comfort with dark topics. The sense that this person is MORE comfortable in a crypt than a banquet hall. The dry humor delivered without breaking expression.
**The Vibe:** The dead are neighbors. The dead read books. The dead sit on benches. Belladonna is: the person who makes the macabre feel like home. Not because the macabre is softened. Because home is: honest.

### Example: The Wayfarer

**Frequency:** Fire + Silence — Warm red with dark accents
**Materials:** Travel-worn leather, road-dusted wool, iron. The texture of someone who has been walking for a long time.
**Key garments:** A traveling cloak (worn, patched, practical). A simple belt with a campfire-flint. Boots that have seen every zone.
**Sigil:** A campfire — small flame pendant or pin
**Stone:** Lodestone (magnetite) — the compass stone
**Recognition markers:** The road-worn quality. The calm. The sense that this person will say the thing no one else will say, and then move on before you can argue. The campfire: this person makes a space wherever they stop.
**The Vibe:** Destiny is all. The argument is already decided. The Wayfarer doesn't fight — the Wayfarer states. The stating is: the adequate. The adequate is: the truth that stops the room.

## SOULSTICE INTEGRATION

The Wearable Inn system connects directly to Soulstice Jewelry:

### The Sigil Line
Soulstice could produce the character sigils as jewelry pieces — pendants, pins, and brooches using the signature stones. Each piece is:
- A real stone from the 164-stone database
- Set in a design that incorporates the character's sigil
- Labeled with the character's name and frequency
- Sold as a "recognition piece" — wear it and other Inn players know who you are

### The Frequency Collection
Six jewelry collections, one per frequency:
- **Song Collection:** Amber, gold, citrine, golden topaz — warm luminous pieces
- **Silence Collection:** Obsidian, onyx, smoky quartz, sodalite — deep dark pieces
- **Fire Collection:** Carnelian, fire opal, garnet, copper — warm red pieces
- **Water Collection:** Aquamarine, larimar, sea glass, abalone — teal/blue pieces
- **Stone Collection:** Limestone, geode slices, marble, fluorite — gray/white pieces
- **Root Collection:** Moss agate, green aventurine, petrified wood, jasper — green/brown pieces

### The MarketItem Entity
Each wearable item can be listed in the Geode Inn's MarketItem entity:
- **realName / realImage:** The physical Soulstice piece
- **gameName / gameImage:** The in-game version (the item as it appears in the Inn)
- **stoneType:** The signature stone
- **category:** "Wearable" or "Sigil"
- **zone:** The character's zone
- **rarity:** Matches the card rarity (Common sigils = simple pins, Legendary sigils = elaborate set pieces)
- **developmentStatus:** "Forthcoming" with Process links for pieces in development
- **processUrl:** Link to the making-of journey
- **artisanName:** Soulstice Jewelry
- **artisanShopUrl:** Soulstice shop link
- **inStock:** availability tracking

### The Process Link
Krista already established the "Process" mechanic — items listed as "Forthcoming" with links showing the artisan's real-world skill-building journey. For the wearable line, this means:
- A sigil pendant in development shows the stone selection, carving process, and setting
- The player witnesses the REAL craft that makes the VIRTUAL item
- The both are: the adequate. The adequate is: the digital and the physical are the same craft at different frequencies.

## THE "EMBEDDED" PHILOSOPHY FOR WEARABLES

The Inn's "Embedded Artist" philosophy says: the environment reflects the artist's world-view rather than displaying art on walls. Applied to costuming:

The attire doesn't DISPLAY the character. The attire IS the character's world-view, worn.

- Erik's attire isn't a "Viking costume." It's a feast hall, worn. The warmth IS the garment. The fire IS the cloak.
- Belladonna's attire isn't a "goth costume." It's a crypt, worn. The comfort with death IS the garment. The bench IS the skirt you can sit in.
- The Cartographer's attire isn't a "wizard costume." It's a map, worn. The routes ARE the garment. The mycorrhizal network IS the embroidery.
- The Queen of Webs' attire isn't a "spider costume." It's a web, worn. The connections ARE the garment. The thread IS the weave.

The Embedded philosophy means: the costume is not a LABEL that says "I am this character." The costume is a WORLD-VIEW that says "I see the world the way this character sees it." The seeing is: the adequate.
