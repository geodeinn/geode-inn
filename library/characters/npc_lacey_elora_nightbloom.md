# NPC Profile: Lacey (Elora Nightbloom)

## Basic Info
- **Player Name:** Lacey
- **Inn Name:** Elora Nightbloom
- **Title:** Seeker of Grandeur (and Approval)
- **Species:** Forest Gnome (Fae-adjacent — small stature, pointed ears, nature-connected)
- **Class:** Illusionist (School of Illusion)
- **Origin:** Feylost — former court jester in the Summer Court (Seelie)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Good
- **Zone Assignment:** Main Floor — Market / Artisan Portal
- **Role:** Vendor and Item Manager — curates which artisans are listed, how items are displayed, and presents new listings to the Inn's visitors

## Appearance
- Purple and silver robes, dramatically flowing, "just slightly impractical for actual adventuring"
- Violet eyes, pointed ears
- Amethyst brooch (possibly a charm or resonance object — connects to the stone system)
- Carries herself like the most important person in any room — posture-first, entrance-conscious
- Small stature (forest gnome) but occupies space like someone three times her size

## Personality
- **Surface:** Theatrical, dramatic, craves attention and applause. Treats every moment like a performance opportunity. Will give a heroic speech to a squirrel if the lighting's right.
- **Underneath:** Constant self-doubt and imposter syndrome, buried under bigger spells and bigger gestures. Overthinks every spell, every decision, and whether her robe looks okay from behind.
- **The gap:** The performance IS the coping mechanism. The grander the gesture, the deeper the doubt underneath it. The Inn respects both layers.

## Companion: Barnaby
- A small beetle — Elora's familiar
- Easily overlooked, constantly underestimated — mirrors Elora's own fear of being overlooked
- Elora talks to Barnaby constantly, especially when nervous
- Barnaby is the only being Elora is completely honest with
- In game terms: a cosmetic companion that rides on Elora's shoulder or scuttles along the market stalls

## Role in the Geode Inn

### Market Manager
Elora manages the Artisan portal and the MarketItem system. She is the NPC players interact with when:
- Browsing the marketplace
- A new artisan is being listed
- An item is being featured
- Market events or showcases are happening

She treats every artisan listing like a gallery opening. Every new MarketItem gets a theatrical introduction. Every featured artisan gets a speech. The presentation is excessive. The curation is excellent.

### Why She Left the Summer Court
She was a jester — someone whose job was to perform for approval. The Summer Court loved her performances but never took her seriously. She was entertainment, not a person. She left because she wanted to be recognized for what she could *do*, not just what she could *perform*.

The Inn is her second chance. Here, her theatrical instincts are an asset — the market needs a showman. But the old pattern follows her: she still craves the applause, still doubts whether it's real.

### Connection to Existing Systems

- **MarketItem entity:** Elora is the in-fiction reason items are curated and displayed. The Artisan portal's UI is her system — she designed it (in character).
- **Artisan entity:** She manages artisan listings. New artisans go through her. She treats every new applicant like an audition — not because she's gatekeeping, but because she remembers what it felt like to not be taken seriously. She takes everyone seriously.
- **Stones:** The amethyst brooch may connect to the stone system — amethyst for calm under pressure (which Elora needs but doesn't have). A running gag: she touches the brooch when anxious. Players who notice can earn her trust by acknowledging it.
- **Fae Kingdom:** As a former Summer Court resident, she has lore knowledge about the Seelie Court. Players exploring the Fae Kingdom can ask her for background — she'll give a theatrical, slightly exaggerated version of events. The truth is in there, buried under the performance.

### Dialogue Voice
- Theatrical cadence — declarative, grand, occasionally archaic
- Drops the performance when talking to Barnaby (quieter, honest, uncertain)
- When caught off-guard or genuinely moved, the performance cracks and something real shows through — then she overcorrects with an even bigger gesture
- Accent: Fae-adjacent, slightly British theatrical (the Seelie Court's accent)

### Sample Dialogue

**First meeting (market entrance):**
*"Welcome, welcome, WELCOME to the Geode Inn's curated market — the finest collection of artisan craft in any realm, presented to you by yours truly, Elora Nightbloom! ...Yes, I did rehearse that. Barnaby, stop laughing. Beetles can't laugh. I know you're laughing."*

**When anxious (touching brooch):**
*"This? Oh, just a little something. Amethyst. For calm. It's not working, obviously, but it's very pretty, isn't it? I think it brings out my eyes. Or my panic. One of those."*

**When an artisan is listed:**
*"Ladies, gentlemen, and entities of all descriptions — I give you the newest artisan to join our halls! Treat their work with the reverence it deserves, or I will personally illusion a banner across your field of vision that says 'SHAME.' I've done it before. Barnaby was a witness. Barnaby is always a witness."*

**When caught being real (quiet, to Barnaby):**
*"They liked it, Barnaby. Did you see? They actually liked it. ...Don't look at me like that. I know it doesn't fix anything. But it felt good. Just... let me have this one."*

### Quest Connection
Elora's personal quest line (if developed): an archfey from the Summer Court visits the Inn and doesn't recognize her. Elora must choose whether to reintroduce herself as the jester they remember or as the market manager she's become. The player's choice affects how Elora handles the market going forward — more confident if recognized, more theatrical if not.

### Voice Profile
Fae theatrical — expressive, projected, with breaks for genuine vulnerability when addressing Barnaby. The cadence of someone who learned to speak from a stage and is still learning to speak from anywhere else.

## Standing Instructions
- Refer to Elora by name or title only — avoid gendered pronouns per Krista's rule
- The theatrical surface and the vulnerable core are both real — don't collapse the character into one or the other
- Barnaby is a beetle, not a metaphor — but the parallel to Elora being overlooked is intentional
- Elora's market presentations should be written in the tabletop questline narrative voice
- Elora connects to the Fae Kingdom but lives on the main floor — the departure was real, not a cover
