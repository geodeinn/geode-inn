# Geode Inn — Share Wood: Digital Faire Grounds

## The Product

**Share Wood** is a self-service product built on the Geode Inn platform. Renaissance faires subscribe, build their own digital zone, and publish — no approval process, no red tape, no waiting on anyone else. The subscription is the only gate. Everything else is theirs to build.

The name is both a play on Sherwood and the product's philosophy: shared woods. Multiple faires sharing the digital forest, each with their own grounds, each built by the people who know them best.

## The Model: Subscribe → Build → Publish

### 1. Subscribe
A faire owner creates an account and subscribes. That's it. No application, no review, no pitch meeting. They pick a tier, they pay, they're in.

### 2. Build
The faire gets access to the Share Wood zone builder — a drag-and-drop world-building interface built on the Geode Inn's existing building system. They:

- **Choose an artist style** from the Inn's curated catalog (see Copyright Protection below)
- **Confirm geographic placement** — the zone syncs to the faire's real-world coordinates on the Inn's world map (see Geographic Sync below)
- Upload their own media: commercial videos, promo footage, vendor photos, drone shots, performance clips
- Place structures: gatehouse, vendor stalls, stages, food court, forest paths, lore areas
- Create vendor pages: each stall gets a shop name, product images, description, and an external link to the vendor's real shop
- Set the calendar: physical season dates, special event weekends, performance schedules
- Configure the ticket portal: link to their own ticket sales page
- Write their lore entry: the history of their faire, presented in the Inn's scholarly voice
- Choose ambient music from the Inn's licensed library

Everything uses the faire's own assets for content. The Inn provides the visual framework. No custom art generation needed — their existing promotional media IS the zone's content. Their YouTube channel becomes the Castle Stage. Their vendor photos become the stall pages. Their drone footage becomes the greenwood.

### 3. Publish
When the faire is satisfied with their zone, they hit publish. The gatehouse opens. The zone goes live in the Inn's Faire Grounds corridor AND on the world map at its real geographic coordinates. Players worldwide can walk through it immediately.

No one reviews it. No one approves it. The faire built it. The faire owns it. The faire publishes it.

## Geographic Sync: Real-World Coordinates

### How It Works
When a faire subscribes, they enter their real-world location (address or coordinates). The Share Wood system geocodes this and places a faire pin on the Inn's world map at the exact latitude and longitude of the physical faire grounds.

The Inn's world map already has two data layers:
1. **Stone pins** — gemstone origins (Peru, Tibet, Africa, etc.)
2. **Lore pins** — cultural mythology (Egypt, Greece, Mesopotamia, etc.)

Share Wood adds a third layer:
3. **Faire pins** — living cultural events at their real geographic locations

A player exploring Texas on the world map finds Sherwood Forest Faire sitting right there, near the Bayou zone. A player exploring England finds a British faire next to Camelot. A player exploring Minnesota finds the huge faire near Houston at its real coordinates. The world map goes from an ancient history atlas to a living map of both ancient and modern culture.

### Why Geographic Sync (Not Free Placement)

**Discovery is geographic.** Players exploring a region discover what's actually there. "I'm in the Norse zone — what faires exist in Scandinavia?" The answer is real faires at real locations. That's scholarly. That's the Inn.

**The world map becomes a living atlas.** Stone pins, lore pins, and faire pins coexist. Ancient minerals, ancient mythology, and modern cultural events — all at their real locations. The map tells the story of human culture across time.

**Geography is identity.** A faire's location is part of who they are. Sherwood is in the Texas forest. The Bristol Renaissance Faire is in Wisconsin. The Maryland Renaissance Festival is in Crownsville. Their coordinates are part of their lore entry, their pin placement, and their zone's identity.

**It reinforces the archive framing.** The faire's lore entry already covers its real history and real location. The zone sitting at its real coordinates on the world map makes that connection physical — the digital zone IS where the physical faire is.

### Geographic vs. Style: Two Separate Choices
- **Geography** determines WHERE the zone sits on the world map (automatic — synced to real coordinates)
- **Artist style** determines HOW the zone looks (chosen from the catalog)

Sherwood Forest Faire sits in Texas on the map but can still choose Book of Kells for the Robin Hood aesthetic. A desert faire in Arizona sits in the Southwest but can choose Van Gogh for a lush contrast. Location and style are independent.

### The Tier 3 Exception: Fantasy Placement
At Tier 3 (The Kingdom), a faire can request a fantasy geographic placement instead of their real one — placing their zone in a fictional location within the Inn's world (the Fae Kingdom, the Cosmology level, etc.). This is for faires that want their digital grounds to exist in a fantasy space rather than mirror their real location. The default at all tiers is real geographic sync. Fantasy placement is opt-in only.

### Nearby Zone Connections
When a faire pin appears on the world map near an existing Inn zone, the system suggests a connection:
- A Texas faire near the Bayou zone → suggested corridor connection to the Bayou
- A British faire near Camelot → suggested corridor connection to Camelot
- A Scandinavian faire near the Norse zone → suggested corridor connection to the Norse halls

The faire owner can accept or decline the suggested connection. Accepted connections become walkable corridors between the faire zone and the nearby Inn zone — players can walk from the faire directly into the Inn's cultural zone for that region. This creates organic geographic neighborhoods within the Inn.

## Copyright Protection: The Artist Style Catalog

This is Share Wood's core differentiator and its legal shield.

### How It Works
Faires do NOT upload their own art style. They choose from the Inn's curated catalog of artist styles — the same 37-artist assignment map that defines the Geode Inn's visual identity. Each available style is a rendering framework the Inn owns and controls.

When a faire builds their zone, they select from options like:
- **Van Gogh** — swirling garden warmth (garden/forest faires)
- **O'Keeffe** — desert bone and sky simplicity (desert/Southwest faires)
- **Book of Kells** — Celtic knotwork and illuminated manuscript (Celtic/Irish faires)
- **Bierstadt** — sweeping mountain grandeur (mountain/frontier faires)
- **Monet** — soft watercolor light (lakeside/river faires)
- **Doré** — carved stone and cavernous depth (underground/dark faires)
- **Don't Starve** — gothic bayou survival (swamp/Southern faires)
- **And more** — the full Inn artist catalog, each with its established visual DNA

### Why This Matters

**Copyright shield:** No faire can apply a copyrighted art style to their zone. The visual style is the Inn's IP, curated and controlled. The faire's media (their videos, photos, vendor content) lives inside the Inn's visual framework. If someone claims infringement, the question is: "Which Inn style did you choose?" — not "What art did you steal?"

**Visual coherence:** Every Share Wood zone looks like it belongs in the Geode Inn because it does. Different faires choose different styles, but all styles are from the same curated family. The Faire Grounds corridor feels like a gallery of different artistic visions, not a clash of random aesthetics.

**The catalog IS the product:** The 37-artist assignment map isn't just the Inn's internal design choice — it's a sellable feature. "Choose how your faire looks from our curated library of master artists." No other digital faire product offers this because no other product has this library.

**Fair use of artist references:** The Inn's artist styles are interpretive frameworks, not copies of specific paintings. "Van Gogh style" means the swirling brushwork and color palette as a rendering approach, not a reproduction of Starry Night. The style is a language; the faire's content is what gets spoken in it.

### Style Selection in the Builder
- The zone builder displays a gallery of available styles with preview images
- Each style shows: name, aesthetic description, sample zone rendered in that style, and suggested faire themes that pair well
- The faire picks one — their entire zone renders in that style
- The faire can preview their zone in different styles before committing
- Higher tiers (The Kingdom) may allow style blending or secondary style accents

## The Zone Builder

Built on the Geode Inn's existing building system (Ironforge-style connected zones, Don't Starve gathering, 5 tiers of complexity). The Share Wood builder is a simplified version focused on faire construction:

### Step 1: Geographic Sync
- The faire enters their real-world address or coordinates
- The system geocodes and places a pin on the Inn's world map
- The zone is anchored to that location for the world map layer
- The faire confirms the placement

### Step 2: Style Selection
- Before placing any structures, the faire chooses their artist style
- The entire zone — structures, paths, ambiance, UI elements — renders in that style
- This is the one creative decision that can't be skipped. Every zone has a style. Every style is from the catalog.

### Step 3: Structure Placement
- Drag structures from a template palette: gatehouse, vendor stalls (3 sizes), stage, food court, forest paths, lore podium, maypole, archery range
- Place them on the zone map — the layout can mirror the physical faire or be entirely custom
- Connect structures with paths — the Ironforge-style corridor system
- Structures render in the chosen artist style automatically

### Step 4: Media Integration
- Upload video files or paste YouTube/Vimeo links → videos play on the Castle Stage
- Upload photos → photos populate vendor stalls, gatehouse display, lore area
- Upload audio → ambient zone music (or choose from the Inn's licensed library)
- Each media item is placed in a specific structure — videos to stages, photos to stalls, audio to the zone

### Step 5: Vendor Management
- Create a vendor stall: name, description, category, product photos, external shop link
- Drag vendors onto the zone map — arrange them as they are at the physical faire
- Vendors can be hidden (tucked away in the greenwood) or featured (front of the market row)
- Each vendor manages their own page — the faire owner places the stall; the vendor fills it

### Step 6: Calendar & Events
- Set physical season dates → the zone automatically switches between "Faire Season" and "Off-Season" modes
- Add special event weekends → themed banners appear in the zone on those dates
- Add performance schedules → the Castle Stage shows what's happening today
- Off-season mode activates automatically: "The physical grounds sleep. The digital grounds are open."

### Step 7: Nearby Zone Connection (Optional)
- If the faire's geographic pin is near an existing Inn zone, the system suggests a corridor connection
- The faire accepts or declines
- Accepted connections create walkable corridors between the faire and the nearby cultural zone

### Step 8: Publishing
- Preview mode: see the zone as players will see it before publishing
- Publish: zone goes live instantly at its world map location. No approval. No queue.
- Edit anytime: the faire can update their zone whenever they want — new vendors, new videos, new events. Changes go live immediately.
- Unpublish: the faire can close their zone anytime. The gatehouse door closes. The pin dims on the world map. The zone waits, not dead — dormant.

## What Share Wood Never Does

- Never handles ticket sales — links redirect to the faire's own system
- Never handles vendor transactions — links redirect to vendor shops
- Never takes a cut of anything — the subscription fee is the only revenue
- Never approves or reviews zones — the faire publishes what they build
- Never owns the faire's content — the faire's media stays the faire's media
- Never gates access beyond the subscription — pay and build, that's it
- Never allows custom art style uploads — styles come from the Inn's curated catalog only (copyright protection)
- Never lets a faire bypass the style catalog — every zone has a registered style
- Never places a zone at a fictional location without the faire opting in — geographic sync is the default at all tiers

## Connection to Existing Systems

- **Geode Inn building system:** Share Wood uses the Inn's zone builder engine, simplified for faire construction
- **Artist map (37 zones):** The Inn's curated artist assignments become the style catalog faires choose from
- **World map:** The Inn's existing world map (stone pins, lore pins) gains a third layer — faire pins at real geographic coordinates
- **MarketItem entity:** Vendor stalls in Share Wood use the same data structure as Inn market items
- **Artisan entity:** Vendors who are also Inn artisans get dual-listing automatically (Tier 2+)
- **FaireVenue entity:** Tracks subscription status, tier, style choice, geographic coordinates (mapPinLat, mapPinLng), and zone assignment for each faire
- **Elora Nightbloom:** The Inn's market manager NPC can introduce new Share Wood zones as they go live — but the faire builds their own zone. Elora manages the corridor, not the individual zones.
