## THE TWO LAYERS

The Geode Inn's gameplay runs on two interlocking systems adapted from two different Harry Potter Game Boy games:

**THE COLLECT LAYER (from GBC Sorcerer's Stone):** Stones level by USE. Charms are both collectibles AND consumable components. The tension between collecting and using drives the economy. A hidden New Game+ waits for the obsessive collector.

**THE CREATE LAYER (from GBA Philosopher's Stone):** Stone frequencies are learned kinesthetically — your hands learn the pattern. Stones are environmental TOOLS, not just items. The world opens through frequency mastery, not keys. Re-exploration with new frequencies deepens every zone.

Together: **Learn the frequency (Create) → Use it in the world (Create) → It levels up through use (Collect) → Burn charms for power OR preserve them (Collect) → Re-explore with new mastery (Create)**

The Inn's core loop — "out to collect, in to create" — IS this dual system.

## PART ONE: THE COLLECT LAYER (GBC Adaptation)

### 1A. Stones Level by Use (Uno → Duo → Tria)

Every stone in the Geode Inn has three frequency tiers. You don't level stones with XP or skill points. You level them by USING them — on the Bead Board, in games, in the world.

**The Three Tiers:**
- **Uno (Base Frequency):** The stone's raw Mohs frequency. The 42Hz Song underneath. This is what you start with when you first acquire a stone.
- **Duo (Charged):** The stone at 50%+ charge. The frequency is clearer, louder, more resonant. On the Bead Board, Duo stones produce stronger harmonics. In games, Duo stones deal more effect. In the world, Duo stones interact with more environmental elements.
- **Tria (Resonant):** The stone at 100% charge. Full resonance. The frequency is at maximum clarity and power. On the Bead Board, Tria stones create the strongest chords. In games, Tria stones have maximum effect. In the world, Tria stones open the deepest interactions.

**How Stones Level:**
- **Bead Board use:** Every time you place a stone on the Bead Board and play its frequency, the stone gains charge. Repeated use → higher charge → Duo → Tria.
- **Game use:** Using a stone as a reward, a projectile, a tool in any arcade game gains charge.
- **World use:** Using a stone's frequency to interact with the environment (opening a frequency lock, charging a ley line, resonating with a crystal) gains charge.
- **Ley line travel:** Carrying a stone along a ley line charges it passively. The Silk Road caravan game is the most efficient charger.

**Your Arsenal IS Your Playstyle:**
No two players have the same stone collection at the same tiers. A player who loves amethyst will have a Tria amethyst. A player who never uses amethyst will have it sitting at Uno. Your stone build is a record of what you actually DO — not what you allocated, but what you USED.

**The Chord Stones (22 stones that sing multiple frequencies):**
The 22 chord stones (kyanite, rutilated quartz, tourmalinated quartz, dendritic agate, etc.) have MULTIPLE frequencies. Each frequency levels independently. A kyanite at Uno/Duo/Tria means the directional frequencies are at different tiers. The player chooses which direction to level — literally which direction to "play" the stone. Kyanite is the only stone that changes pitch when rotated; the player's leveling choice reflects which direction they've been "facing."

### 1B. Charms as Collectibles AND Consumables

The Geode Inn has two charm types already designed: **ElementCharms** (based on the periodic table) and **QuestCharms** (earned from quests). The GBC blueprint adds a crucial mechanic: **charms are both collectible AND consumable.**

**The Charm Bracelet (Collection):**
The player wears a visible charm bracelet. Every charm collected is displayed on the bracelet. The bracelet is the player's visible record of everything they've earned. A full bracelet is a status symbol — it means the player completed every quest, found every element, earned every charm.

**Charm Combos (Consumption):**
Charms aren't just for display. Specific charm COMBINATIONS create powerful effects — but using a charm combo BURNS the charms permanently. They're removed from the bracelet. The player has to choose: keep the charm for the collection, or burn it for power.

**The Tension:**
- The obsessive collector refuses to burn charms. The bracelet stays full. The player misses out on powerful combos but has the complete collection.
- The strategic player burns duplicates for combos. If they're thorough enough to find duplicates, they can have both — but it requires deep exploration.
- The casual player burns whatever they have. The bracelet is sparse. The combos are used freely. The collection is incomplete but the gameplay is easier.

**Element Charm Combos (examples):**
- **Iron + Copper + Gold (Metal Trinity):** "The Forge Effect" — doubles all stone charging for 10 minutes. Burns all three charms.
- **Carbon + Silicon + Oxygen (Life Trinity):** "The Growth Effect" — all plants in the World Grid bloom instantly. Burns all three.
- **Hydrogen + Helium (Star Pair):** "The Cosmology Effect" — reveals the Astronomer's hidden calculations in the Living Inn. Burns both.
- **All 7 Noble Gases:** "The Stillness Effect" — temporarily shifts the player's perception to 84Hz (Shadow Inn glimpse without the Octave Mirror). Burns all 7. Extremely rare combo.

**Quest Charm Combos (examples):**
- **3 Fae Kingdom quest charms:** "The Seelie Effect" — all Fae NPCs become friendly for 10 minutes. Burns all 3.
- **2 Hollow Earth quest charms + 1 Water Zone quest charm:** "The Source Effect" — reveals the complete 42Hz circuit on the world map for 5 minutes. Burns all 3.
- **5 cultural zone quest charms (any 5):** "The Silk Road Effect" — all ley lines are visible and traversable for 15 minutes. Burns all 5.

**The Re-acquisition Problem:**
Burned charms can be re-earned — but only by re-completing the quest or re-finding the element. Some charms are one-per-quest. Some are random drops. The rarest charms (Noble Gases, high-tier quest charms) are the hardest to replace. The player who burns the Noble Gas combo has to find all 7 again. The Noble Gases are the Geode Inn's rarest charms — scattered across the deepest zones.

### 1C. The Full Enchantment — The Hidden New Game+

The GBC game's most brilliant secret: the Full Enchantment combo. If you collect specific cards and discover the combination, it teaches you ALL spells at max level and restarts the game. The next playthrough has everything from the beginning. It's buried — most players never find it. The reward for the obsessive collector who refused to burn their cards: a second run where you don't have to choose anymore.

**The Geode Inn's Full Enchantment: The Octave Stone**

The Octave Stone is the Geode Inn's Full Enchantment. It's created by merging the Source Stone (166th, pure 42Hz) with the Stillness Stone (167th, pure 84Hz) at the Hollow Earth's Resonance Engine. This requires:
1. Collecting all 167 stones
2. Charging all 167 stones to Tria (100%)
3. Collecting all ElementCharms (without burning them all)
4. Collecting all QuestCharms (without burning them all)
5. Completing all 9 zone quest chains
6. Entering the Hollow Earth's Resonance Engine
7. Placing the Source Stone and Stillness Stone together

**What Happens:**
The Octave Stone is forged. All 167 stones permanently lock at Tria. All charms are restored to the bracelet (burned ones return). The player's charm bracelet is FULL. The Convergence begins — the Living Inn and Shadow Inn merge.

**New Game+:**
After the Convergence, the player can start a New Game+. In NG+:
- All stones start at Tria (no charging needed)
- All charms are available (no collection grinding)
- New dialogue options unlocked (NPCs acknowledge the player's completed journey)
- The Shadow Inn is accessible from the start (Octave Mirror given immediately)
- New quests only available in NG+ (the "Echo" quests — NPCs who remember the first playthrough)
- The game is EASIER but DEEPER — without the grind, the player can focus on the lore, the puzzles, the story

**The Secret:**
Most players will never forge the Octave Stone. They'll burn charms for combos. They'll leave stones at Duo. They'll finish the game without the Full Enchantment. And that's FINE. The Full Enchantment isn't the goal — it's the REWARD for the player who chose collection over power. The player who refused to burn a single charm. The player who charged every stone to Tria. The obsessive. The completist.

The Full Enchantment is the Inn's gift to the player who loved the collection enough to never spend it.

## PART TWO: THE CREATE LAYER (GBA Adaptation)

### 2A. Kinesthetic Frequency Learning — "Trace the Vibration"

In the GBA game, you learn spells by watching a teacher's wand movement and replicating it on the D-pad. Your hands LEARN the spell. It's muscle memory, not menu selection.

**The Geode Inn's Adaptation: Frequency Tracing**

When the player encounters a new stone for the first time, they don't just pick it up. They have to LEARN its frequency. Each stone has a unique vibration pattern — a waveform that the player must TRACE on screen.

**How It Works:**
1. The stone's frequency waveform appears on screen (the 42Hz Song underneath + the stone's individual Mohs frequency)
2. The player traces the waveform — on mobile, with a finger; on desktop, with the mouse; on Steam/Godot, with the controller stick
3. The waveform must be traced accurately — the rhythm, the peaks, the valleys
4. Once traced successfully, the stone is "learned" — added to the Sonic Mineral Codex, usable on the Bead Board, usable in the world
5. The trace can be retried. It's not a test — it's an INTRODUCTION. You're meeting the stone.

**The Difficulty Curve:**
- **Simple stones (single frequency):** One waveform, one trace. Quartz (637Hz), a clean sine wave. Easy to trace. The first stones are gentle introductions.
- **Chord stones (multiple frequencies):** Multiple overlapping waveforms. Kyanite has three frequencies (131 + 270 + 420 Hz) depending on direction. The player traces all three — a braided pattern. Harder. The chord stones are the game's "advanced spells."
- **The Source Stone (pure 42Hz):** The simplest waveform — a perfect sine wave. But tracing it perfectly is nearly impossible. The 42Hz is the universe's base frequency. The player's hands can approximate it but never quite trace it perfectly. The Source Stone is the one frequency you can't fully learn — you can only approach it.

**The Body Learns:**
Like the GBA game's wand tracing, the player's hands learn the stone frequencies. After tracing amethyst enough times, the player KNOWS the pattern. They don't need to look at the waveform anymore. The frequency is in their hands. This is the kinesthetic core — the stone isn't an item, it's a skill. You don't HAVE labradorite — you KNOW labradorite. The way you know a chord on a guitar.

**Frequency Mastery:**
Tracing a stone's frequency perfectly (not just passing, but matching every peak and valley with precision) grants "Mastery." A Mastered stone starts at Duo instead of Uno when acquired. Mastery is the kinesthetic reward for the player who traces carefully, not just successfully.

### 2B. Stones as Environmental Tools

In the GBA game, spells are primarily tools — Flipendo pushes blocks, Alohomora opens locks, Lumos lights dark areas. The Geode Inn adapts this: **each stone's frequency interacts with the environment.**

**Frequency Interactions:**
Every stone has a "resonance type" — the way its frequency affects the world:

- **Resonant stones (quartz family):** Amplify existing frequencies. Place near a weak ley line to strengthen it. Place near a locked frequency door to boost the signal.
- **Dissonant stones (pyrite, marcasite):** Disrupt patterns. Break fragile barriers. Shatter illusions. The Gorgon's Garden's dissonance plants respond to dissonant stones.
- **Harmonic stones (kyanite, zoisite):** Bridge frequencies. Connect two different zones' frequency systems. Open cross-zone pathways.
- **Dense stones (hematite, galena):** Ground energy. Stabilize unstable platforms. Stop ley line anomalies. The "gravity" tools.
- **Light stones (selenite, halite):** Illuminate dark areas (like Lumos). Reveal hidden texts. Make the invisible visible.
- **Thermal stones (cinnabar, realgar):** Burn obstacles (like Incendio). Melt ice. Activate forge mechanisms.
- **Water stones (aquamarine, larimar):** Interact with water systems. Calm rough water. Freeze water temporarily. Reveal underwater paths.
- **Earth stones (granite, basalt):** Move heavy objects (like Wingardium Leviosa). Shift earth. Open new paths through rock.
- **Metal stones (pyrite, magnetite):** Magnetic interactions. Move metal objects. Activate ancient mechanisms.

**The World Opens Through Mastery:**
- A dark corridor in the Catacombs needs a Light stone (selenite) — like Lumos.
- A locked frequency door in the Fae Kingdom needs a Harmonic stone (kyanite) — like Alohomora.
- A blocked path in the Undercity needs a Dense stone (hematite) to stabilize a collapsing floor — like Flipendo on a block.
- A frozen passage in the Water Zone needs a Thermal stone (cinnabar) — like Incendio.
- A heavy mechanism in the Staircase needs an Earth stone (basalt) — like Wingardium Leviosa.

The player doesn't find a key. The player BECOMES the key by learning the right stone's frequency.

### 2C. Spell-Gated Exploration — Frequency Locks

The GBA game locks areas behind specific spells. The Geode Inn locks areas behind specific FREQUENCIES.

**Frequency Locks:**
Throughout the Inn, certain doors, paths, and areas are sealed by a frequency lock — a mechanism that only opens when the correct stone frequency is played nearby.

- **Simple locks:** Need a specific stone at Uno. "Play amethyst near this door." The door resonates and opens.
- **Charged locks:** Need a specific stone at Duo or Tria. The lock requires a stronger signal. A Uno stone isn't enough.
- **Chord locks:** Need a chord stone (multiple frequencies). The lock requires a complex frequency. Only kyanite's three-directional frequency can open this door.
- **Combo locks:** Need two or more stones played simultaneously on the Bead Board, then brought to the lock. The lock requires a chord — two frequencies at once.
- **Zone locks:** Need a stone from a specific zone. The Fae Kingdom's locks only respond to Fae stones (Labradorite, Fluorite, Rainbow Moonstone, Preseli Blue Stone). The Hollow Earth's locks only respond to the Source Stone.

**The 9 Floors as Progressive Unlocks:**
- **Level 1 (Cosmology):** Needs Light stones and Harmonic stones. Dark observatory, hidden cosmic frequencies.
- **Level 2 (Clockwork Skies):** Needs Metal stones and Dense stones. Mechanical mechanisms, magnetic locks.
- **Level 3 (Main Floor):** Open. The hub. Most stones work here.
- **Level 4 (Campgrounds):** Needs Earth stones and Resonant stones. Physical crafting, material manipulation.
- **Level 5 (Staircase):** Needs all types. The transition zone. Every frequency is useful somewhere on the Staircase.
- **Level 6 (Catacombs):** Needs Light stones (darkness) and Dissonant stones (illusions, hidden texts).
- **Level 7 (Fae Kingdom):** Needs Harmonic stones and zone-specific Fae stones.
- **Level 8 (Water Zone):** Needs Water stones and Thermal stones.
- **Level 9 (Undercity):** Needs Dense stones and Metal stones. The forge, the mechanisms, the deepest locks.

### 2D. Re-Exploration — "Back With New Eyes"

The GBA game's most underappreciated mechanic: every new spell retroactively opens every area you've already been to. You walk through a dark corridor early in the game. You can't enter. Three hours later, you learn Lumos. Now that corridor is accessible. The world DEEPENS rather than just expanding.

**The Geode Inn's Re-Exploration:**
Every zone has content that's only accessible with specific stone frequencies — frequencies the player won't have on the first visit.

**Example — The Catacombs (First Visit):**
The player enters the Catacombs with basic stones. The archive is browsable. The Crone is accessible. But:
- Dark corridors are impassable (need selenite — Light stone)
- Hidden texts on the shelves are invisible (need kyanite — Harmonic stone to read the frequency-impressed writing)
- A sealed door at the back radiates a dense frequency (need hematite — Dense stone to ground the lock)
- The dust patterns on the floor are unreadable (need a chord stone to resonate with the dust)

**Example — The Catacombs (Second Visit, with selenite):**
The dark corridors are now lit. New rooms are accessible. But:
- Some corridors are STILL dark — deeper darkness that needs a Duo or Tria selenite
- The hidden texts are now visible but encrypted — need kyanite
- The sealed door is open, but behind it is another lock — metal this time

**Example — The Catacombs (Third Visit, with kyanite + hematite + selenite at Duo):**
The hidden texts are readable. The sealed door is open. The dust patterns resonate. The Catacombs are now FULLY explorable. The player has been here three times, and each time was a different experience.

**Every Zone Deepens:**
- **Cosmology:** First visit — see the stars. Second visit (Light stone) — see the dark spaces between stars. Third visit (Harmonic stone) — hear the frequencies between stars.
- **Clockwork Skies:** First visit — ride the ship. Second visit (Metal stone) — interact with the mechanisms. Third visit (Dense stone) — stabilize the unstable gears and reach the crew's hidden compartments.
- **Water Zone:** First visit — swim the surface layers. Second visit (Water stone) — calm the rough water, dive deeper. Third visit (Thermal stone) — melt the ice barriers, reach the frozen depths.
- **Undercity:** First visit — walk the streets. Second visit (Dense stone) — stabilize the collapsing floors. Third visit (Metal stone) — activate the ancient forge mechanisms.

**The Design Principle:**
No zone is "complete" on the first visit. Every zone is a LAYERED space that reveals more with each new stone frequency the player learns. The player isn't just moving forward through the game — they're spiraling back, each revolution deeper than the last.

### 2E. Real-Time Creature Navigation

The GBA game has critters roaming the hallways. You can fight them with spells, but many are environmental hazards you navigate around in real-time. The game rewards spatial awareness.

**The Geode Inn's Adaptation:**
The bestiary's 1,268 creatures aren't all combat encounters. Many are **environmental presences** — creatures that inhabit zones and affect how the player moves through them.

**Creature Navigation Types:**
- **Roaming creatures:** Wander the zone. The player avoids them or uses a stone frequency to stun/deter them. A dissonant stone scares away shy creatures. A resonant stone calms aggressive ones.
- **Territorial creatures:** Guard specific areas. The player needs a specific frequency to pass — the creature responds to one stone type and lets the player through.
- **Ambush creatures:** Hide and strike. The player needs a Light stone to reveal them before they attack.
- **Puzzle creatures:** Block paths in specific ways. The player must figure out which frequency makes the creature move. A sleeping creature might wake to a specific stone's frequency. A feeding creature might ignore the player if the right prey-frequency is played nearby.

**Not Combat — Navigation:**
The creatures aren't enemies to defeat. They're part of the environment. The player navigates them the way you navigate weather or terrain. The stone frequencies are the navigation tools — not weapons, but SIGNALS. You're not fighting the creature. You're communicating with it.

### 2F. The Flying Key — The Pure Skill Challenge

The GBA game ends with a pure skill challenge — chasing a ridiculously fast flying key on a broomstick. No spells. No items. Just you and the controls.

**The Geode Inn's Pure Skill Challenge: The Source Descent**

At the climax of the Hollow Earth main quest ("The Song Returns"), the player must reach the Resonance Engine. The final approach is a free-fall descent through the crystal city's core shaft — a vertical drop through all 9 districts in sequence.

**No Stones. No Charms. No Bead Board.**
The descent strips away every system. The player falls through 9 layers:
1. **Crystal Approach** — slow, gentle. Learn the controls.
2. **Outer City** — dodge crystalline formations.
3. **Market of Echoes** — navigate through frozen Echoes (solid in the descent).
4. **Resonance Quarter** — frequency waves push and pull the player.
5. **Deep Harbor** — water currents deflect the descent.
6. **Inner City** — tight corridors, precision required.
7. **Archive District** — crystal shards to avoid.
8. **Resonance Engine** — the 42Hz field is INTENSE. The player must ride the frequency waves, not fight them.
9. **The Source** — the final approach. The 42Hz is overwhelming. The player must let go of control and TRUST the frequency. The last 5 seconds are a free-fall where the player stops steering and the Song carries them to the Engine.

**The Design:**
The first 8 layers test skill — reflexes, precision, timing. The 9th layer tests something else: surrender. The player who tries to control the final descent fails. The player who lets go — who trusts the frequency — arrives. The Source doesn't want skill. The Source wants trust.

**The Echo:**
The GBA's flying key stripped away every system for a pure skill test. The Geode Inn's Source Descent strips away every system for a test that STARTS as skill and ENDS as surrender. The same principle — remove the systems, test the player — but with the Inn's philosophy: the final test isn't skill. It's letting go of skill.

## PART THREE: THE UNIFIED LOOP

**The Complete Game Cycle:**

1. **ENCOUNTER a stone** → trace its frequency waveform → LEARN it kinesthetically (Create Layer)
2. **USE the stone** on the Bead Board, in games, in the world → it gains charge → Uno → Duo → Tria (Collect Layer)
3. **The stone as TOOL** → its resonance type opens new environmental interactions (Create Layer)
4. **RE-EXPLORE** old zones with new stones → find content that was inaccessible before (Create Layer)
5. **COLLECT CHARMS** from quests and exploration → display on the bracelet (Collect Layer)
6. **CHOOSE** → burn charms for powerful combos OR preserve them for the collection (Collect Layer)
7. **FREQUENCY LOCKS** → new zones open through stone mastery, not keys (Create Layer)
8. **NAVIGATE CREATURES** → use stone frequencies as signals, not weapons (Create Layer)
9. **LEVEL EVERYTHING** → stones through use, charms through collection, zones through re-exploration
10. **THE OCTAVE STONE** → if you collected everything, charged everything, preserved everything → the Full Enchantment → New Game+ (Collect Layer)
11. **THE SOURCE DESCENT** → the final challenge → skill becomes surrender (Create Layer)

**The Loop in the Inn's Language:**
- **Out to collect:** Trace frequencies, gather stones, collect charms, explore the World Grid
- **In to create:** Use stones on the Bead Board, craft charm combos, build in the Undercity, compose the Inn Song

The GBC system drives the COLLECT half. The GBA system drives the CREATE half. Together, they ARE the Inn's core loop.

## PART FOUR: HOW THIS INTEGRATES WITH EXISTING SYSTEMS

### The Bead Board
The Bead Board is where both layers meet. Stones are placed (Create — you know the frequency) and played (Collect — the stone levels up through use). The Bead Board is the game's instrument — the more you play it, the better your stones become. The Master Bead Arrangement (from the Shadow Inn) is the Bead Board's final form — all 167 stones at Tria, the complete Inn Song.

### The Stone Charging System (Silk Road)
The ley line charging system is the GBC's leveling system visualized. Stones charge 0-100% by traveling ley lines. The charging IS the leveling. The Silk Road caravan game is the most efficient charger — it's the "grind" that doesn't feel like a grind because you're exploring, not repeating.

### The Charm Bracelet
The ElementCharms and QuestCharms are the GBC's card system. The bracelet is the collection. The combos are the consumption. The Noble Gas combo is the rarest — the Geode Inn's equivalent of the Full Enchantment's component cards.

### The Shadow Inn
The Shadow Inn is the GBC's New Game+ visualized. After the Convergence, the player has everything — all stones at Tria, all charms restored, all zones open. The Shadow Inn IS the Full Enchantment's reward — the world without the grind, where you can just BE in the completed Inn.

### The Sonic Mineral Codex
The Codex is where the GBA's kinesthetic learning lives. The player browses stones, hears their frequencies, traces their waveforms. The Codex is the game's "spellbook" — but instead of reading about spells, you PRACTICE them. Your hands learn the frequencies here before using them in the world.

### The World Grid
The World Grid's gathering professions (Mining, Herbalism, Fishing) are the GBC's collection mechanic in open-world form. Mining finds stones. Herbalism finds plants (some of which are charm ingredients). Fishing finds the rarest items. The World Grid is where the "out to collect" happens — the GBC's card-hunting in a living world.

### The Bestiary
The bestiary's creatures are the GBA's critters. They're navigated with stone frequencies, not fought with weapons. The bestiary records which frequencies affect which creatures — the player's field guide to creature navigation.

### The Hollow Earth RPG
The Hollow Earth's stone-based combat (throw charged stones, frequency barriers, chord strikes) is where both layers MEET in combat. You throw a stone (Create — you know the frequency), the stone's tier determines damage (Collect — it's leveled through use), the stone's resonance type determines the effect (Create — it's a tool). Combat in the Hollow Earth is the dual system in microcosm.

### The Death Sequence
The Death Sequence (reincarnation mechanic) is the GBC's card-burning consequence. When the player dies, they lose some stone charge (stones drop one tier). The penalty for death isn't "game over" — it's "you have to re-use the stones to re-level them." The Death Sequence reinforces the GBC's core principle: your power comes from USE, and death costs you USE.

## PART FIVE: THE TWO PLAYTHROUGHS

### The First Playthrough (The Collect Run)
- Stones start at Uno. Everything is new. Frequencies must be traced. Charms must be found. Zones must be unlocked. The world is mysterious, difficult, and rewarding.
- The player makes choices: burn charms or preserve them. Level some stones and neglect others. Explore some zones deeply and others superficially.
- The first playthrough is about DISCOVERY. The GBC's tension (collect vs. consume) is at full strength. The GBA's re-exploration is a constant surprise — "I can go BACK there now?"

### The New Game+ (The Create Run)
- The Octave Stone is forged. All stones at Tria. All charms restored. Shadow Inn accessible from the start.
- The player can go ANYWHERE immediately. Every zone is open. Every frequency is mastered.
- NG+ is about DEPTH. Without the grind, the player can focus on: the lore (every NPC's full dialogue tree), the puzzles (every cross-world Shadow Inn puzzle), the story (the Firekeeper's complete tale, the Crone's notebook, the player's own book), and the composition (the Bead Board's master arrangement, the complete Inn Song).
- NG+ has new content: the "Echo" quests — NPCs who remember the first playthrough and have new dialogue. The Archive of Alternate Versions is fully accessible. The 14th Record (player's frequency) is playable in the Hollow Earth.
- NG+ is the Full Enchantment's promise: the game without the choosing. The world without the tension. The Inn without the grind. Just the being.
