## PHILOSOPHY

These are personal additions. Each is an original adaptation of a beloved game mechanic, integrated into the Geode Inn's world, lore, and aesthetic. Not copies — adaptations. The gameplay DNA is preserved; the skin, sound, and soul are the Inn's.

Every game uses the Inn's Celtic knotwork frame (gold on dark stone, gothic arches). Every game plays at 50 BPM low-dub classical (classical instruments, deep sub-bass). Every game connects to the Inn's lore, stones, or zones.

### 1. THE DEEP WELL — (Adapted from Super Mario Bros: The Lost Levels)

**Mechanic preserved:** Brutal precision platforming. Unforgiving difficulty. Hidden traps. Inverted expectations.

**The Inn's Version:**
The Shadow Inn's version of Andean Run. The "Lost Levels" are the levels that were too difficult for the Living Inn — the Shadow Inn preserves them at their maximum difficulty. Every trap is visible because everything in the Shadow Inn is frozen — but the traps still kill.

**Design:**
- 10 levels (matching the Living Inn's Andean Run) but each is the HARDEST possible version
- Poison mushrooms become "Dissonant Stones" — stones that look like power-ups but drain charge on touch
- Backwards warp zones that send the player to earlier levels (the Shadow Inn's geometry is fixed — warps are the only "wrong" direction)
- The Invisible Floor: one level has platforms that only appear when the player stands still (84Hz = stillness reveals truth)
- The Reversed Gravity Level: the Shadow Inn's 84Hz inverts the 42Hz's natural order — gravity flips
- The One-Tile Gaps: pixel-perfect jumps. No margin. The Shadow Inn doesn't compromise
- Death penalty: lose 10% stone charge on every death (the GBC consequence system)
- Reward for completing all 10: the "Shadow Jumper" charm — grants a double jump in the Living Inn's Andean Run

**Aesthetic:** Klimt gold on black stone. The levels are the Shadow Inn's architecture — gold-veined platforms, dark voids, the 84Hz tone as ambient sound. No background scenery — just gold and black. The starkest game in the collection.

**Connection:** The Deep Well is accessed through the Shadow Inn's Undercity. The entrance is a well in the Blacksmith's district — the well goes DOWN, not up. The Blacksmith's forge is above the well. The completed Octave Key opens the well.

### 2. THE INN QUEST — (Adapted from Super Mario Bros)

**Mechanic preserved:** Side-scrolling platformer. Power-ups change the player's state. Boss at the end of each world. Secret areas in pipes.

**The Inn's Version:**
A side-scrolling adventure through all 9 levels of the Geode Inn. Each "world" is an Inn level. The player moves LEFT TO RIGHT through the level, jumping, collecting, and using stone frequencies as power-ups.

**Design:**
- 9 worlds (one per Inn level), each with 4 stages
- Power-ups are stone frequencies: grab a quartz crystal = Resonant state (bigger, can break certain blocks). Grab a selenite = Light state (illuminates dark areas of the level). Grab a cinnabar = Thermal state (can burn through ice/plant barriers)
- The "pipes" are the Inn's ley line portals — entering one takes the player to a sub-area (a mini-cave with extra stones or a shortcut)
- The "flagpole" at the end of each stage is a frequency tuner — the higher you grab, the more stone charge you gain
- Each world ends with a boss: the zone's signature creature or NPC challenge
  - World 1 (Cosmology): The Astronomer's Star Calc (dodge falling stars)
  - World 2 (Clockwork Skies): The Gear Guardian (avoid rotating mechanisms)
  - World 3 (Main Floor): The Firekeeper's Riddle (answer correctly to pass)
  - World 4 (Campgrounds): Jay's Bar Brawl (dodge thrown mugs)
  - World 5 (Staircase): The Stair Specter (the impossible geometry fights back)
  - World 6 (Catacombs): The Archive Wraith (dust storms and book avalanches)
  - World 7 (Fae Kingdom): The Griffin (aerial battle)
  - World 8 (Water Zone): The Kraken (underwater boss)
  - World 9 (Undercity): The First (the Hollow Earth boss — a frequency match, not a fight)
- The "princess" is the Inn Song — completing World 9 reveals the Song's first complete phrase
- Secret: if the player enters specific ley line portals in the right order, they access the "Negative Zone" — a glitchy, inverted version of the level (the In-Between)

**Aesthetic:** Each world uses its zone's assigned artist style as the background. Turner for Cosmology, Dalí for Clockwork, etc. The foreground is the Inn's Celtic frame. The player character is a simple silhouette — the Wanderer's shape.

### 3. THE DREAMER'S DESCENT — (Adapted from Super Mario Bros 2)

**Mechanic preserved:** Multiple characters with different abilities. Pick up and throw enemies/objects. Dig through sand. Vertical scrolling. Subcons/dream world.

**The Inn's Version:**
The player chooses from 4 Inn characters, each with different abilities:
- **Scarlett:** Fastest, can climb walls (reaches high areas). Lowest jump. The agile one.
- **The Pirate:** Highest jump, can dig through earth floors. Slowest. The powerful one.
- **The Hearth Tender:** Balanced. Can carry objects the longest (doesn't tire). The steady one.
- **Eileen:** Can float-glide (paintings on the wall become platforms for Eileen). The creative one.

**Design:**
- 7 "dream zones" — each is a cultural zone experienced as a dream (the In-Between's influence)
- Pick up and throw: enemies, stones, vegetables (the World Grid's crops). Throwing a stone at a barrier breaks it if the stone's resonance type matches
- Digging through sand: the Mesopotamian zone's desert. The Andean zone's gravel
- Vertical scrolling: zones scroll UP, not sideways. The player climbs through layered content
- The "boss" of each dream zone is a sleeping cultural figure who must be woken — not killed. Waking them by playing the right frequency
- The "vase" system: pick up potions (frequency vials) that create temporary doors to a shadow version of the screen (the Shadow Inn's influence — a brief glimpse of the petrified world)
- The "Subcon" equivalent: the In-Between. The dream zones are the In-Between's interpretation of the cultural zones — familiar but wrong, like a dream of a place you've been

**Aesthetic:** Bosch for the dream zones. The surreal, dreamlike quality of Bosch's impossible creatures and landscapes fits the dream-world mechanic. Each cultural zone is filtered through Bosch's lens — familiar but distorted.

**Mechanic preserved:** Carry a baby. Throwing eggs (aim with cursor). Flutter jump. Ground pound. Art style is hand-drawn/crayon. The baby is the goal — protect the baby.

**The Inn's Version:**
The player carries the baby dragon through the Inn's most dangerous zones. The baby dragon is the Inn's mascot — the player must protect it while navigating platforming challenges.

**Design:**
- The player is a Fae guardian creature (the chibi griffin from The Faerie Quest), carrying the baby dragon
- 6 worlds (one per dangerous zone: Catacombs, Fae Kingdom, Water Zone, Gorgon's Garden, Hollow Earth, In-Between)
- Egg-throwing: the guardian throws "frequency eggs" — stones charged with a frequency. Aim with a cursor, throw in an arc. The egg's frequency affects what it hits (a Light egg illuminates, a Thermal egg burns, etc.)
- Flutter jump: the guardian's mid-air hover
- Ground pound: crack through floors to find hidden areas
- The baby dragon mechanic: if the guardian is hit, the baby dragon floats free in a bubble. A timer counts down — if the baby is caught before the timer ends, no penalty. If the timer runs out, the baby is taken by the In-Between's silhouette figure (the "Toadies" equivalent)
- The art style: each world is rendered as a CAVE PAINTING — Eileen's art style. The hand-drawn, crayon/pastel aesthetic of Yoshi's Island becomes Eileen's prehistoric, primal art. The world looks like it was painted on a cave wall. The baby dragon is a cave-painting come to life

- Boss battles: each world's boss is fought as a giant creature that the guardian must hit with frequency eggs from multiple angles

**Aesthetic:** Eileen's cave painting style. The warm, primal, hand-drawn look. Each world is a different cave wall — different pigments, different textures. The baby dragon is rendered in the same warm gold as the Inn's Klimt patterns.

### 7. FREQUENCY DOCTOR — (Adapted from Dr. Mario)

**Mechanic preserved:** Falling capsules. Match colors to clear viruses. Puzzle game. Increasing speed.

**The Inn's Version:**
Falling mineral capsules. Match stone families to clear dissonant frequencies. The "viruses" are dissonance — wrong notes in the Inn's Song.

**Design:**
- Falling capsules are two-tone mineral pills (two stone families per capsule)
- The "viruses" are dissonance nodes — red (dissonant), blue (discordant), yellow (chaotic)
- Match 4 of the same stone family in a row/column to clear the dissonance
- Stone families: Quartz (clear), Feldspar (pink), Garnet (red), Beryl (green), etc. — 8 families, color-coded
- When a row is cleared, the cleared stones play their frequency — a satisfying audio chime
- Clearing the entire board plays a chord — the stone family's harmonic
- Difficulty levels:
  - Easy (Uno): slow capsules, few dissonance nodes
  - Medium (Duo): faster, more nodes, capsule orientation matters
  - Hard (Tria): very fast, many nodes, some capsules are "chord capsules" (3 colors, harder to place)
  - Shadow (84Hz): the board is inverted — you START with a full board and must clear it by REMOVING stones (reverse Dr. Mario)
- Multiplayer: two boards side by side. Clearing rows sends "dissonance" to the opponent's board. The player who survives longest wins
- Story mode: each level is a zone of the Inn that needs "tuning" — the dissonance is cleared and the zone's frequency is restored

**Aesthetic:** The Bead Board's visual language. The board looks like the Bead Board — wood tray, stone beads. The falling capsules are polished mineral pills. The background is the Inn's Celtic frame pattern. Low-dub classical music at 50 BPM.

### 8. THE MIRROR MAZE — (Adapted from Snow White: Happily Ever After)

**Mechanic preserved:** Side-scrolling platformer. Throw apples at enemies. Collect fruit and stars. Warp between spots. Find secret jumps. Choose between two characters. Difficulty levels.

**The Inn's Version:**
Snow White in the Gorgon's Garden. The fairest of them all in the 84Hz death garden. The mirror maze is the Gorgon's Garden's petrified labyrinth.

**Design:**
- Play as either **Persephone** (the spring/underworld dual character) or **The Owl** (the watcher)
- Persephone: can throw pomegranate seeds (the apple equivalent). Seeds grow into temporary platforms. Persephone can also "descend" — phase through floors into a shadow version of the level
- The Owl: can fly/glide. Cannot throw projectiles but can dive-bomb enemies. The Owl's sight reveals hidden paths (the watcher's perspective)
- Collect: pomegranate seeds (health), gold mirrors (stars — bonus points), bottled frequencies (power-ups)
- Warp spots: mirrors. Step into a mirror to warp to another mirror in the level (the Gorgon's Garden is full of reflective surfaces — each one a potential warp)
- Secret jumps: hidden behind petrified plants. The player must use a specific stone frequency to reveal the jump (Light reveals, Harmonic reveals mirror warps)
- Difficulty levels: Easy (Living — enemies are slow, warps are clearly marked), Medium (Twilight — enemies are faster, some warps are hidden), Hard (Shadow — everything is 84Hz, enemies are frozen but contact with them petrifies instantly)
- The "Maliss" equivalent: the Gorgon's Garden's guardian — a shadow version of Medusa. Not Medusa herself (Medusa is the architect, not the villain). The guardian is the garden's defense system — a living shadow that patrols the maze
- 8 levels (matching the 16 plants' quarters: 2 levels per quarter — Narcotic, Venomous, Petrifying, Cathartic)
- The "Happily Ever After" ending: completing all 8 levels reveals that the mirror maze was always the Shadow Inn's entry point — the Gorgon's Garden exists in both Inns simultaneously. The "happily ever after" is the completion: the player is now able to enter the Shadow Inn through any mirror

**Aesthetic:** Beardsley's gothic art nouveau for the Gorgon's Garden. Black and gold, sharp linework, ornamental detail. The mirrors are framed in Beardsley's flowing curves. The petrified plants are Beardsley's geometric vegetation. The most visually ornate game in the collection.

### 9. STANDING STONES — EXPANDED (Adapted from Tetris SNES Version)

**Mechanic preserved:** Tetris with multiple modes — classic, versus, puzzle, magic.

**The Inn's Version:**
The existing Standing Stones (Tetris) expanded with the SNES version's mode variety:

**Design:**
- **Classic Mode (already built):** Standard Tetris. Standing Stones fall, clear lines, Stonehenge theme. Low-dub classical.
- **Versus Mode:** Two boards. Clearing lines sends "rubble" to the opponent. The rubble is dissonant stones — blocks that can't be cleared normally, must be surrounded by matching stones to dissolve. Play against AI (zone NPCs as opponents) or local multiplayer.
- **Puzzle Mode:** Pre-set boards. The player is given a specific set of stones and must clear the board in a specific number of moves. Each puzzle is a "frequency equation" — the solution is the arrangement that creates a specific chord when cleared.
- **Magic Mode:** The existing game with magic blocks — special stones that clear entire rows regardless of color, or transform adjacent blocks. The magic stones are the 22 chord stones — each has a unique clearing effect:
  - Kyanite: clears in two directions (the directional stone)
  - Rutilated quartz: clears a diagonal line (the dual mineral)
  - Bloodstone: clears all metal-colored blocks (the metal inclusion)
- **Shadow Mode:** The Shadow Inn's version. The board is inverted — blocks rise from the bottom instead of falling from the top. The player must push blocks DOWN to clear lines at the top. 84Hz tone instead of 42Hz.
- **Endless Mode:** No levels. Just play until you die. The board background cycles through all 9 zone aesthetics as the speed increases. At maximum speed, the background is the Undercity at full Klimt gold.

**Aesthetic:** Each mode uses a different zone's stained-glass backdrop. Classic = Cosmology (Turner). Versus = Main Floor (Matisse). Puzzle = Catacombs (Goya). Magic = Fae Kingdom (Dalí). Shadow = Shadow Inn (Klimt gold). Endless = cycling.

### 13. STONE POPPER — (Adapted from Bust a Move)

**Mechanic preserved:** Shooter at bottom. Aim and fire bubbles upward. Match 3+ colors to pop. Ceiling descends. Puzzle layouts.

**The Inn's Version:**
A frequency cannon at the bottom of the screen. Aim and fire stone-frequency bubbles upward. Match 3+ stone families to pop them. The ceiling of stones descends.

**Design:**
- The cannon is the Inn's frequency projector
- The bubbles are stone spheres (the Bead Board's stone beads)
- 8 stone families (color-coded): Quartz (clear/white), Feldspar (pink), Garnet (red), Beryl (green), Tourmaline (blue), Topaz (yellow), Zoisite (purple), Metal (gold)
- Match 3+ of the same family: they pop. Each pop plays the family's frequency — a satisfying chime
- Chain reactions: if popping one group causes another group to disconnect from the ceiling, it falls (bonus points + a satisfying cascade of chimes)
- The ceiling descends every 30 seconds (the 50 BPM heartbeat — every 30 seconds at 50 BPM is 25 beats. The descent is on the beat)
- Stone types:
  - Normal: standard stone, matches by family
  - Chord stone: matches as ANY family (the wild card — it's a multi-frequency stone)
  - Dense stone: takes 2 hits to pop (must be matched twice)
  - Dissonant stone: doesn't match anything. Must be surrounded and cleared by popping adjacent stones
  - Shadow stone (84Hz): only appears in Shadow Mode. Matches with its OPPOSITE family (opposite on the color wheel). Upside-down matching.
- Puzzle mode: 100 pre-set puzzles. Each puzzle is a frequency pattern — the solution arrangement creates a specific chord when cleared. The puzzle mode is the Inn's "sheet music" — each solved puzzle is a melody.
- Versus mode: two boards. Popping stones sends "dissonance bubbles" to the opponent. The player who survives longest wins.
- Endless mode: the ceiling never stops descending. How long can you survive?

**Aesthetic:** The Bead Board's stone beads as the bubbles. The board is framed in Celtic knotwork. The background is the Inn's interior — the arcade room's Matisse-petroglyph walls glowing steadily. Each pop creates a ripple of gold light (Klimt gold).

### 14. FROZEN ASCENT — (Adapted from Ice Climber)

**Mechanic preserved:** Vertical platforming. Break through ice layers. Co-op. Collect vegetables at the top. Polar bears and yetis as enemies.

**The Inn's Version:**
Climb through the Shadow Inn's frozen Water Zone. All 7 layers, frozen solid. Break through each layer of ice to ascend.

**Design:**
- The player climbs UPWARD through 7 frozen layers of the Water Zone
- Each layer is a sheet of ice with breakable sections. The player uses a frequency hammer to smash through
- The frequency hammer: a stone-charged tool. Different stones break different ice:
  - Thermal stones (cinnabar) melt ice instantly
  - Dense stones (hematite) shatter thick ice
  - Resonant stones (quartz) crack ice in a pattern
  - The player switches stones (L/R) to match the ice type
- Enemies:
  - The "polar bear" equivalent: the Lighthouse Keeper's shadow, who patrols each layer. Contact = frozen (the Shadow Inn's petrification). The player must avoid or use a Dissonant stone to stun
  - The "yeti" equivalent: a frozen Source Creature that breaks free from the ice. Larger, more aggressive. Must be avoided or melted with a Thermal stone
  - The "bird" equivalent: a frozen owl (the Owl's Shadow form) that swoops down. Steals the player's current stone (forces a switch)
- Collectibles at the top of each layer: frozen artifacts (the Shadow Inn's items, preserved in ice). Reading them when collected reveals lore
- The "vegetable" at the top: the frozen Source Stone (the 166th stone, frozen in the ice at the top of Layer 7). Collecting it completes the ascent
- Co-op: two players climb simultaneously. One can break ice while the other avoids enemies. If one player reaches the top, they can throw down a rope to help the other
- 7 layers = 7 levels. Each layer is longer and harder. Layer 7 (the bottom = the top of the screen in the original, but here it's the DEEPEST = the Source) is the hardest — the ice is thickest, the enemies are most aggressive, the 84Hz is strongest
- Bonus stage: between layers, a "jumping stage" where the player jumps through falling ice crystals (the original's bonus stage, preserved)

**Aesthetic:** Vampire Hunter D gothic anime — frozen. The Water Zone's gothic architecture under ice. The ice is blue-black, the frozen creatures are visible inside. The background gets darker as the player descends (deeper = darker = closer to the Source). Layer 7 is pure black with gold veins (the Shadow Inn's 84Hz visible in the deepest ice).

### 15. ARCHIVE MAZE — (Adapted from Pac-Man)

**Mechanic preserved:** Maze navigation. Eat pellets. Power pellets make ghosts vulnerable. Four ghosts with different AI. Fruit bonuses. Increasing speed.

**The Inn's Version:**
Navigate the Catacombs' archive corridors. Collect knowledge fragments (pellets). The bestiary's creatures patrol the stacks. Power stones make the creatures vulnerable — briefly.

**Design:**
- The maze is the Catacombs' archive layout — bookshelf corridors, narrow passages, the Crone's reading nooks
- The "pellets" are knowledge fragments — small glowing dots in every corridor. Collecting them fills the archive's index (the completion meter)
- The "power pellets" are Resonant stones placed at corridor junctions. Collecting one makes all creatures vulnerable for 5 seconds. The creatures turn gold (Klimt gold) and can be "absorbed" (collected into the bestiary)
- The four "ghosts" are bestiary creatures with different AI:
  - **The Chaser (red):** a roaming creature that follows the player directly. Persistent. The most dangerous.
  - **The Ambusher (pink):** a creature that tries to cut the player off. Moves to where the player is HEADING, not where the player IS
  - **The Wanderer (orange):** a creature that wanders randomly. Unpredictable. Sometimes dangerous, sometimes not.
  - **The Sentinel (cyan):** a creature that patrols a fixed route. Predictable but blocks key corridors. The most frustrating.
- The "fruit" bonuses: rare books that appear in the center of the maze. Each book gives a lore drop. Different books per level:
  - Level 1: a small book (the Inn's founding story)
  - Level 2: a scroll (the 42Hz discovery)
  - Level 3: a grimoire (the alchemical texts)
  - Level 4: the Necronomicon (Naut's reading)
  - Level 5: the player's own book ("The Player Who Stayed")
- 255 levels (the original's 256th level is the kill screen — here, Level 256 is the Shadow Inn's Archive, where the maze is gold and the creatures are frozen. The "kill screen" becomes the Shadow Inn — the game completes itself)
- Speed: increases with each level. The Catacombs' 84hz heartbeat accelerates
- The "intermission" screens (between levels): short animations of the Firekeeper telling stories. The original Pac-Man intermissions become lore moments

**Aesthetic:** Goya's Catacombs. Dark, heavy, the weight of history. The corridors are lined with bookshelves. The knowledge fragments glow faintly (the only light source). The creatures are rendered in the bestiary's art style — gothic creatures from the 1,268-creature catalog. The Celtic frame is the maze's border.

### 16. UNDERCITY KNIGHT — (Adapted from Castlevania: Dracula X)

**Mechanic preserved:** Gothic action-platformer. Whip weapon. Sub-weapons. Stair climbing. Multiple paths. Backtracking. Boss battles. Gothic atmosphere.

**The Inn's Version:**
A gothic action-platformer through the Undercity. The player is the Undercity's knight — a guardian of the completed work. The "whip" is a frequency lash. The "Dracula" is the Shadow Inn's final challenge.

**Design:**
- The player explores the Undercity's districts: the Workshop District, the Bone Room, the Goldsmith's Studio, the Blacksmith's Forge, the Clockmaker's Workshop, the Archive of Alternate Versions
- Side-scrolling action with vertical elements (stairs, platforms, ropes)
- The "whip" is a frequency lash — a beam of concentrated stone frequency. It extends forward and strikes. The lash's frequency changes based on the equipped stone:
  - Quartz lash: standard, balanced range and power
  - Thermal lash (cinnabar): shorter range, burns enemies over time
  - Light lash (selenite): longer range, weaker damage, reveals hidden platforms
  - Dense lash (hematite): shortest range, highest damage, can break walls
  - Harmonic lash (kyanite): can change direction mid-swing (the directional stone)
- Sub-wearts (consumed hearts/ammo):
  - The Thrown Stone: throw a charged stone (projectile)
  - The Frequency Barrier: a temporary wall of frequency that blocks enemies
  - The Chord Strike: an AoE burst that hits all enemies on screen (uses a chord stone)
  - The Octave Mirror: a brief Shadow Inn shift — enemies freeze for 3 seconds
  - The Bead Board: drops a wall of beads that blocks the corridor
- Multiple paths: each stage has 2-3 routes through the Undercity. The "stairs" lead to alternate paths. Some paths require specific stone frequencies to access
- Backtracking: the player can return to earlier stages with new stones to find secrets
- Boss battles: each district has a guardian:
  - The Workshop District: the Goldsmith's shadow (a petrified artisan who attacks with thrown metal)
  - The Bone Room: the Bone Keeper (a skeleton that reassembles)
  - The Blacksmith's Forge: the Blacksmith's shadow (throws molten metal)
  - The Archive: the Alternate Version — a mirror version of the player (the Shadow Inn's version of the player, which uses the same moves)
  - The Clockmaker's Workshop: the Clock (a giant mechanism that attacks with gears)
  - Final: Medusa (the Shadow Inn's architect). Medusa doesn't want to fight. Medusa tests whether the player understands the Shadow Inn. The "battle" is a frequency match — the player must play the right frequencies at the right moments. Medusa's gaze petrifies if the player moves during the wrong beat. The player must learn the rhythm of the Shadow Inn — 84Hz — and move ONLY on the beat. A rhythm-game boss fight disguised as a platformer boss
- The "Dracula" elements preserved: the gothic atmosphere, the candle-holding enemies (here: the Inn's candle-holding gargoyles), the bone-throwing skeletons (here: the Catacombs' archive wraiths), the medusa heads (here: literal Medusa references — small gargoyle faces that float), the platforming precision

**Aesthetic:** Klimt's Undercity at maximum intensity. Gold-veined gothic stone. The most atmospheric game in the collection. The background is the Undercity's architecture — vaulted gold ceilings, narrow stone streets, the frozen fountain. The lighting is candle-gold (the hearth's frozen fire). Low-dub classical at its most dramatic — the 84hz tone is prominent.

## INTEGRATION WITH THE INN

### How These Games Connect to Existing Systems:

**The Octave System (GBC + GBA):**
- Every game uses the stone frequency system. Stones level up (Uno → Duo → Tria) by playing these games.
- Every game's difficulty modes mirror the stone tiers (Easy = Uno, Medium = Duo, Hard = Tria, Shadow = 84Hz)
- Charms earned from completing games can be burned for power-ups or preserved for the bracelet
- The "Shadow Mode" in each game is the Shadow Inn's version — accessed through the Octave Mirror

**The World Map:**
- Each game is accessible from a specific zone:
  - The Inn Quest → Main Floor (the primary adventure)
  - The Deep Well → Shadow Inn's Undercity
  - The Dreamer's Descent → In-Between
  - The World Map → World Grid (the overworld)
  - The Faerie Quest → Fae Kingdom
  - The Hatchling → Cave City (Eileen's domain)
  - Frequency Doctor → Main Floor Arcade
  - The Mirror Maze → Gorgon's Garden
  - Standing Stones Expanded → Main Floor Arcade
  - Ley Line Racing → Silk Road
  - Crystal Descent → Hollow Earth (polar entrance)
  - Resonance Break → Main Floor Arcade
  - Stone Popper → Main Floor Arcade
  - Frozen Ascent → Water Zone (Shadow)
  - Archive Maze → Catacombs
  - Undercity Knight → Undercity

**The Shadow Inn:**
- Every game has a Shadow Mode — the Shadow Inn's version
- Shadow Mode is harder, inverted, gold-on-black, 84Hz
- Completing Shadow Mode in every game is a requirement for the Octave Stone (the Full Enchantment)

**The Death Sequence:**
- Dying in any game costs stone charge (the GBC consequence)
- The Death Sequence briefly enters the Shadow Inn before respawning (the death screen is a Threshold)

**The Bead Board:**
- Stones earned in these games are usable on the Bead Board
- The Bead Board's master arrangement includes stones from every game

### Reward System:
- Each game rewards stones (specific stones per game, matching the zone)
- Each game has a unique charm reward for completion
- Completing all 16 games' main modes = the "Arcade Master" charm
- Completing all 16 games' Shadow Modes = the "Shadow Player" charm (extremely rare)
- Completing all 16 games at Tria difficulty = the "Octave Player" charm (the rarest charm in the Inn)
