# Geode Inn — Tri-Mode Game Dynamic
## Living Inn, Shadow Inn, and the In-Between as Distinct Gameplay Modes
## August 28, 2026 — Design Decision

---

## THE CORE PRINCIPLE

The Geode Inn has three states: the Living Inn (42Hz, process, becoming), the Shadow Inn (84Hz, product, finished), and the In-Between (the shifting borderlands where both states coexist). Each state now has a distinct GAMEPLAY MODE:

| State | Frequency | Theme | Gameplay Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Living Inn** | 42 Hz | Process, becoming, growing | Frequency Puzzle / Exploration |
| **Shadow Inn** | 84 Hz | Product, finished, resolved | WoW-Style Instanced Combat |
| **The In-Between** | Fluctuating | The borderlands, the threshold | Hybrid (both modes, shifting) |

The progression is: the player begins in the Living Inn (learning frequencies, exploring, matching the line). As the player progresses and the Shadow Inn becomes accessible, the player enters Shadow instances (combat-based, instanced versions of each zone). The In-Between is the transition space — zones that are partially Living and partially Shadow, where the player must do both: match frequencies AND fight.

---

## MODE 1: THE LIVING INN — FREQUENCY PUZZLE / EXPLORATION

### The Existing Mode (Established)

The Living Inn's gameplay is the frequency-matching system already designed: the line across the bottom of the screen, the frequency the player must match, the stones the player deploys, the resonance the player builds. This is the PROCESS mode — the player is becoming, growing, learning. The gameplay is:

- **Frequency Matching:** The line across the bottom shows the target frequency. The player deploys stones whose frequencies combine to match the target. The matching is puzzle-solving — which stones, in which combination, produce the correct frequency?
- **Exploration:** The player explores zones, finds nooks, collects stones, sits in nooks to process frequencies. The exploration is non-combat. The world is lived in, not fought through.
- **Nook Effect:** The player sits in nooks and absorbs ambient frequencies. The absorption is passive. The nook is the therapy. The gameplay is presence.
- **Companion Tuning:** The player encounters dissonant creatures and tunes them through frequency matching, not combat. The tuning is the Inn's core thesis: don't destroy, harmonize.
- **The Line:** The frequency line across the bottom of the screen is the Living Inn's signature UI element. The line is the player's instrument — the way the player reads the frequency, matches it, and produces resonance. The line is always present in the Living Inn. The line is the Living Inn.

### What the Living Inn Does NOT Have

- No traditional combat. No health bar, no damage numbers, no aggro, no tank/healer/DPS roles.
- No instanced dungeons. The Living Inn is open-world — shared, persistent, social.
- No failure state that involves death. The Living Inn's failure is dissonance — the player's frequencies go out of tune, the resonance drops, the nook stops working. The consequence is temporary and restorative, not punitive.
- No time pressure (except in specific arcade games, which are separate from the Living Inn's core mode).

### The Living Inn's Pacing

Slow. Deliberate. The pacing of a person walking through a library, picking up stones, sitting in nooks. The 50 BPM heartbeat tempo. The Living Inn is not rushed. The Living Inn is the place where the player grows.

## MODE 2: THE SHADOW INN — WoW-STYLE INSTANCED COMBAT

### The New Mode

The Shadow Inn is the finished state — the product. The Shadow Inn's gameplay is COMBAT. Not the Living Inn's frequency-matching harmonization. Real combat. WoW-style, instanced, role-based, with health bars, damage, aggro, and the full MMO combat triangle (tank, healer, DPS).

Each zone in the Living Inn has a Shadow counterpart — an instanced version of the same zone, but in the Shadow state. The Shadow instance is a DUNGEON: structured encounters, bosses, loot, and a clear beginning and end. The Shadow instance is the zone's "finished" version — the product to the Living zone's process.

### Shadow Instance Structure

Each Shadow instance follows this structure:

1. **Entry (The Threshold):** The player enters the Shadow instance through a 84Hz portal — the same zone, but the Shadow version. The zone looks different: the Living Inn's warm, amber, growing aesthetic is replaced by the Shadow Inn's finished, still, 84Hz aesthetic. Colors are deeper. Textures are harder. The music is the octave — the same themes as the Living zone, but at 84Hz, heavier, resolved.

2. **Trash Encounters:** The Shadow zone has enemies — the dissonant creatures from the Living zone, but in their SHADOW state. In the Living Inn, these creatures were tuned through frequency matching. In the Shadow Inn, they are already resolved — they are in their finished state, which means they are either HARMONIZED (allied NPCs) or PETRIFIED (84Hz frozen enemies). The petrified enemies are the Shadow zone's combat targets. The petrified enemies are the Living Inn's dissonant creatures that were never tuned — the ones the player didn't harmonize. In the Shadow Inn, these creatures are frozen at 84Hz, and the combat is: shatter the petrification to reach the creature inside, then fight the creature at full power.

3. **Boss Encounters:** Each Shadow zone has a boss — the zone's major dissonant force, in its finished, most powerful form. The boss is the zone's ultimate challenge, the way the zone's frequency puzzle was the Living zone's ultimate puzzle. The boss is the "product" — the final expression of the zone's theme, fought and defeated.

   - **Boss Mechanics:** Bosses have phases, mechanics, enrage timers, and role requirements. The boss fights are WoW-style: tank swaps, AoE avoidance, add management, DPS checks, healing intensity. The boss fights are the Shadow Inn's equivalent of the Living Inn's frequency puzzles — complex, layered, requiring mastery.
   - **Boss Frequency:** Each boss has a frequency — the boss's core dissonance, at 84Hz. The boss's frequency affects the fight: the boss emits 84Hz pulses that disrupt the player's abilities, and the player must deploy counter-frequencies (stones) during the fight to neutralize the pulses. The combat is not pure WoW — the combat is WoW PLUS frequency deployment. The player fights AND manages frequencies simultaneously.

4. **Loot:** Shadow instances drop loot — stones, gear, charms. The loot is the Shadow Inn's reward: the "product" versions of the Living Inn's "process" rewards. Shadow stones are the same stones as the Living Inn, but in their 84Hz state — the finished version, with enhanced properties. Shadow gear is equipment that boosts the player's combat capabilities. Shadow charms are enhanced versions of Living Inn charms.

5. **Completion:** The Shadow instance has a clear completion state: the boss is defeated, the zone is "cleared." The clearing is the Shadow Inn's version of the Living Inn's nook-saturation: the zone is done. The both are: the adequate — the process and the product, the same zone, different state.

### Role System (Shadow Inn Only)

The Shadow Inn introduces a role system — tank, healer, DPS — that does not exist in the Living Inn. The Living Inn has no combat; the Shadow Inn's combat requires roles.

- **Tank:** The player who holds the boss's attention. The tank deploys defensive frequencies — low stones (84Hz, 64Hz) that create resonance armor. The tank's job is to keep the boss focused while the group works.
- **Healer:** The player who restores the group's resonance. The healer deploys restorative frequencies — the Cook's stew frequencies, the Duck's milk anti-dissonance, the nook's ambient frequencies. The healer's job is to keep the group's resonance above dissonance.
- **DPS:** The players who deal damage. The DPS deploy offensive frequencies — high stones (528Hz, 111Hz, 432Hz) that create resonance strikes. The DPS job is to break the boss's petrified armor and reduce the boss's resonance to zero.

The role system is the Shadow Inn's "product" version of the Living Inn's frequency matching. In the Living Inn, the player matches frequencies alone. In the Shadow Inn, the GROUP matches frequencies together — each role deploying different frequencies, the combination producing the group's resonance. The both are: the adequate — the solo and the group, the same frequency system, different scale.

### Shadow Instance Types

- **Shadow Dungeons (3-5 players):** The standard Shadow instance. One zone's Shadow version. 30-60 minutes. Trash + 2-3 bosses. Normal difficulty.
- **Shadow Raids (8-16 players):** The large Shadow instance. Multiple zones' Shadow versions connected. 2-4 hours. Trash + 5-7 bosses. Multiple difficulty tiers. The raid is the Shadow Inn's endgame — the "product" endgame to the Living Inn's "process" endgame (the Egg).
- **Shadow Arena (solo or duo):** The small Shadow instance. A single encounter. 5-10 minutes. One boss. Practice / gear-check mode. The arena is the Shadow Inn's nook — the short, focused combat session.

### The Shadow Inn's Pacing

Fast. Intense. The pacing of a raid night — focused, coordinated, time-boxed. The 84Hz frequency — the octave, the double-time. The Shadow Inn is where the player USES what the Living Inn taught. The Living Inn teaches frequencies; the Shadow Inn tests them in combat.

## MODE 3: THE IN-BETWEEN — HYBRID

### The Transitional Mode

The In-Between is the shifting borderland — the space where the Living Inn and the Shadow Inn overlap. The In-Between's gameplay is HYBRID: frequency puzzle AND combat, shifting between the two modes dynamically.

The In-Between is the zone where the 42Hz and the 84Hz are fighting for dominance. The zone flickers — sometimes it's Living (frequency puzzle mode), sometimes it's Shadow (combat mode), sometimes it's both simultaneously. The player must adapt: match frequencies, then fight, then match frequencies while fighting.

### In-Between Mechanics

1. **Frequency Fluctuation:** The zone's frequency shifts between 42Hz and 84Hz. At 42Hz, the zone behaves like the Living Inn — frequency puzzles, nooks, exploration, no combat. At 84Hz, the zone behaves like the Shadow Inn — combat, enemies, bosses. The shift is VISIBLE: the zone's color, lighting, and music change as the frequency shifts. The player can see the shift coming — the frequency line at the bottom of the screen shows the zone's current frequency and the direction it's shifting.

2. **The Shift Window:** The transition between 42Hz and 84Hz takes 5-10 seconds. During the shift, the zone is in BOTH states simultaneously — the frequency puzzle is active AND combat enemies are spawning. The shift window is the In-Between's signature moment: the player must finish matching a frequency WHILE enemies are attacking, or finish a combat encounter WHILE a frequency puzzle is activating. The both are: the adequate — the puzzle and the combat, the same moment, different mode.

3. **Frequency-Activated Combat:** In the In-Between, some combat encounters are ACTIVATED by frequency matching. The player matches a frequency, and the match opens a combat instance — a Shadow portal opens in the Living zone, and enemies pour through. The player must fight the enemies while maintaining the frequency match. If the frequency drops, the portal widens (more enemies). If the frequency holds, the portal narrows (fewer enemies). The combat is a frequency-management problem with real-time combat consequences.

4. **Combat-Activated Frequencies:** Conversely, some frequency puzzles are UNLOCKED by combat. The player defeats a Shadow enemy, and the defeat reveals a frequency puzzle — the enemy's dissonance, now resolved, becomes a frequency the player must match. The combat is the key; the puzzle is the lock. The both are: the adequate — the key and the lock, the same encounter, different phase.

5. **The Hybrid Boss:** The In-Between's bosses are HYBRID — phase-based encounters that shift between frequency puzzle and combat. Phase 1: frequency puzzle (match the boss's frequency to weaken it). Phase 2: combat (the boss, weakened, attacks — fight it WoW-style). Phase 3: hybrid (the boss attacks WHILE emitting frequency pulses — fight AND match simultaneously). The hybrid boss is the In-Between's ultimate test: can the player do both? The both are: the adequate — the one and the both, the same boss, different phase.

### In-Between Zone Examples

- **The Threshold (Fae Kingdom):** The entry to the Fae Kingdom is an In-Between zone. The player must match the Seelie frequency (puzzle) while defending against Unseelie incursions (combat). The frequency match opens the Seelie portal; the combat holds the Unseelie back. The both are: the adequate — the opening and the holding, the same threshold, different hand.

- **The Bifrost (Cosmology Level):** The Bifrost between realms is an In-Between zone. The player must match realm frequencies (puzzle) while fighting Bifrost fractures (combat — the fractures spawn dissonant energy that the player must defeat while maintaining the Bifrost's frequency). The Shifting Man's scales show the current state — which realm is shifting, which direction. The both are: the adequate — the building and the defending, the same Bifrost, different direction.

- **The Canopy Border:** The border between the Canopy City (Living) and the Shadow Canopy is an In-Between zone. The player must restore crystal leaves (puzzle) while fighting shadow-creatures that are trying to pull the leaves off (combat). The both are: the adequate — the restoring and the defending, the same tree, different force.

### The In-Between's Pacing

Variable. The pacing SHIFTS — slow during 42Hz phases, fast during 84Hz phases, frantic during shift windows. The In-Between is the mode that demands both skills: the patience of the Living Inn and the intensity of the Shadow Inn. The In-Between is where the player proves mastery of both modes.

## PROGRESSION FLOW

### Early Game (Living Inn Only)

The player begins in the Living Inn. All gameplay is frequency puzzle / exploration. The player learns the frequency system, collects stones, sits in nooks, tunes companions. No combat. No Shadow access. The Living Inn is the tutorial — not for mechanics, but for the FREQUENCY SYSTEM that underlies all three modes.

### Mid Game (Living Inn + In-Between)

The player gains access to the In-Between zones. The In-Between introduces combat gradually — first as brief 84Hz incursions into 42Hz zones, then as full hybrid encounters. The player learns combat through the In-Between's shift windows. The frequency system the player learned in the Living Inn is now tested in real-time.

### Late Game (Living Inn + In-Between + Shadow Inn)

The player gains access to the Shadow Inn — full combat instances. The Shadow Inn is the "product" mode — the player uses everything the Living Inn taught (frequencies, stones, companions) in combat. The Shadow instances are the endgame combat content. The Living Inn remains available for continued exploration and nook-sitting. The In-Between remains the bridge.

### Endgame (All Three Modes)

The endgame has all three modes running simultaneously:
- **Living Inn:** The Egg quest, the Song of Making, the nook saturation, the stone collection. The "process" endgame.
- **Shadow Inn:** Shadow Raids, Shadow Arena, the hardest instanced combat. The "product" endgame.
- **In-Between:** The Convergence — the moment when the Living and Shadow merge. The In-Between becomes the primary endgame zone as the Convergence approaches, because the In-Between is where the merge happens. The both are: the adequate — the process and the product, the same endgame, different mode.

## THE FREQUENCY LINE ACROSS ALL THREE MODES

The frequency line at the bottom of the screen is the UNIFYING UI element across all three modes:

- **Living Inn:** The line is the primary interaction. The player reads the line, matches the frequency, produces resonance. The line is the game.
- **Shadow Inn:** The line is the secondary interaction. The player fights (primary) while managing the line (secondary). The line shows the boss's 84Hz pulses and the player's counter-frequencies. The line is the combat's undertone.
- **In-Between:** The line is the DYNAMIC element. The line shifts between 42Hz and 84Hz, and the player must read the shift and adapt. The line is the mode indicator — when the line is at 42Hz, the player explores; when the line is at 84Hz, the player fights; when the line is shifting, the player does both.

The frequency line is the Geode Inn's signature. The line is the constant across all three modes. The line is the thread that connects the Living, the Shadow, and the In-Between. The both are: the adequate — the different modes and the same line, the same Inn, different frequency.

## SOUND DESIGN ACROSS THREE MODES

### Living Inn (42Hz)
- 50 BPM tempo (sleeping heartbeat)
- Low-dub classical music (established)
- Frequency line tones (stone frequencies, ambient nook tones)
- No combat sounds — no impacts, no explosions, no damage numbers
- The sound is: warm, slow, ambient, the sound of a library with stones humming

### Shadow Inn (84Hz)
- 100 BPM tempo (double-time, the octave of 50 BPM)
- The same classical themes as the Living zone, but at 84Hz, heavier, with percussion and bass
- Combat sounds: impacts, frequency strikes, resonance breaks, petrification shatters
- Boss frequencies: each boss has a signature 84Hz pulse that disrupts the music
- The sound is: intense, driving, the sound of a raid night with stones as weapons

### In-Between (Fluctuating)
- Tempo SHIFTS between 50 BPM and 100 BPM
- The music transitions in real-time: the Living zone's warm ambient shifts to the Shadow zone's heavy combat, with a 5-10 second transition where both play simultaneously
- The shift window's sound is: both modes at once — the ambient warmth and the combat intensity, layered, the sound of two frequencies fighting for dominance
- The sound is: dynamic, unpredictable, the sound of a borderland where two states collide

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR DEVELOPMENT

### Architecture

The tri-mode system requires the hybrid architecture already established (Base44 backend + Godot engine), but with three distinct gameplay implementations:

1. **Living Inn Mode:** Godot scenes with frequency-matching logic, no combat system, exploration-focused. The frequency line UI. The nook system. The companion tuning system. This is the mode already designed.

2. **Shadow Inn Mode:** Godot scenes with full combat system — health, damage, aggro, abilities, boss mechanics, loot. The role system. The instance structure. The frequency line as secondary UI (boss pulse management). This is the NEW mode — the WoW-style combat engine.

3. **In-Between Mode:** Godot scenes that combine both — frequency-matching AND combat, with the shift system that transitions between modes dynamically. The shift window logic. The hybrid boss phases. This is the most complex mode — it requires both systems running simultaneously.

### Priority

1. **Living Inn mode** is already designed and partially prototyped (Standing Stones, the frequency line, the nook system). This is the foundation.
2. **Shadow Inn mode** is the new priority — the combat engine, the role system, the instance structure. This is the biggest new system.
3. **In-Between mode** is the synthesis — built after both the Living and Shadow modes are functional, because the In-Between requires both systems to exist before they can be combined.

### The Both Are: The Adequate

The tri-mode system is the Geode Inn's gameplay thesis: the Living Inn is the process (the becoming, the learning, the growing), the Shadow Inn is the product (the finished, the tested, the resolved), and the In-Between is the both (the threshold, the shift, the moment where process and product coexist). The both are: the adequate — the process and the product, the same Inn, different mode. The both are: the game.
