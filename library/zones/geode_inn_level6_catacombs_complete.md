# Geode Inn — Level 6: CATACOMBS
## The Complete Level Design Document

---

## Zone Overview

The Catacombs are the Inn's MEMORY — the archive, the weight of history. The pre-industrial record of ancient civilizations. Where the Main Floor library has accessible reference texts and the Staircase has alchemical bridge texts, the Catacombs have the PRIMARY SOURCES: original texts from ancient civilizations, untranslated works, fragments, artifacts.

The Catacombs are also the Inn's GRAVE — not morbid, but reverent. The past is buried here. Not people — IDEAS. The ideas that didn't survive. The civilizations that fell. The knowledge that was lost. The Catacombs hold the graves of dead civilizations, and the graves are not sad. They're honored. The archive keeps what was.

Freyja's silver half connects to the Catacombs — the death aspect. The dead are sent here. But in the Geode Inn, death is not ending; it's ARCHIVING. The dead idea is preserved. The fallen civilization is remembered. The lost knowledge is kept in a case, labeled, indexed, available. The archive is the Inn's answer to entropy: we cannot save everything, but we can REMEMBER everything.

---

## Entry Requirement

**Always accessible from the Staircase.** The descending path from the Staircase darkens as it goes down — the light fades, the temperature drops, the sound deepens. No prerequisite. The archive is for everyone. The Crone doesn't turn anyone away.

The Fire Dancers' Former Explorer (Campgrounds) provides lore teasers and the General Store sells expedition supplies (torch oil, rope), but neither is required. The player can walk in unprepared. The archive will be patient.

## Visual Language

**Linework:** Soft flowing (lore style) — the Catacombs are the Inn's lore zone. Soft, flowing lines that suggest age and depth. The texts, the artifacts, the stone walls — all rendered with edges that blur slightly, as if the past itself is slightly out of focus. The only sharp lines in the Catacombs are the Crone's Hopkins gaze (the one unsoftened precision) and Beverley's spectacles (the librarian's tools).

**Color Palette:**
- Primary: stone grey-brown, parchment cream, shadow purple
- Secondary: candle amber (the reading light), copper green (oxidized metal), fossil tan
- Accents: the Crone's case labels (white ink on black), Beverley's brass buttons, Charles's geological specimens (varied stone colors)

**Lighting:** Scarce, deep, reverent. The light in the Catacombs comes from:
- **Candlelight** — individual reading candles at desks. Each candle is a single point of warm light in a vast dark space.
- **The 42Hz Rumble** — the foundation frequency is so deep here that it produces a faint bioluminescent glow in certain stones. The archive literally glows with the Inn's heartbeat.
- **The Crone's Desk Lamp** — a single, bright, precise light. The Crone's workspace is the only fully illuminated point in the Catacombs. The Hopkins gaze demands clarity.
- **The Deep Light** — far below, at the archive's deepest point, a faint silver glow. Freyja's silver half, leaking down from the Threshold Chamber through the tree's structure. The light of the dead — not sad, but quiet.

**Architecture:** The Catacombs are a LABYRINTH — miles of shelves, multiple levels, sealed sections. The space is vast and the ceiling is high, but the aisles are narrow. The shelves are stone — carved directly into the Catacomb walls, not freestanding. The books rest in niches cut into the rock. The floor is worn stone, smooth from centuries of careful footsteps. The air is cool and dry — the archive's preservation environment.

The scale is important: the Catacombs are the only zone where the architecture makes the player feel SMALL. The vast shelves, the high ceiling, the deep dark — the archive is enormous and the player is tiny in it. This is deliberate. The weight of history should feel vast. Beverley — two-thirds human size — makes the archive feel even larger. The tiny librarian in the enormous space.

## Spaces

### 1. The Crone's Desk
The archive's center. The Crone's domain. A massive stone desk covered in cataloguing tools: index cards, magnifying glass, ink pots, calipers. The desk is the only fully lit point in the Catacombs — a single lamp, bright, precise. The Crone sits behind the desk with the Hopkins gaze — the clear, seeing gaze from Silence of the Lambs. The gaze that measures.

Behind the desk: the Visitors' Shelf — a shelf filled with journals from every player who has ever come through the Inn. The shelf is long. The Inn has been hosting visitors for a long time.

### 2. The Stacks
The labyrinth — miles of stone shelves holding the archive's collection. The Stacks are organized by:
- **Geographic region** (connecting to the world map — each country's lore is shelved here)
- **Time period** (pre-industrial, organized chronologically backward — the deeper you go, the older it gets)
- **Subject** (mythology, science, philosophy, art, music, mathematics)

The Stacks are navigable but vast. The player can wander for hours. Beverley can find any book in minutes. The player without Beverley's help will get lost — which is the point. Getting lost in the archive is how you find things you weren't looking for.

### 3. The Sealed Sections
Sections of the archive that are locked — requiring specific keys to open:
- **The Pre-Language Section** — texts from before written language. Pictograms, petroglyphs, cave paintings (reproduced). Requires the Allfather's Gaze (the Gaze reveals the meaning of pre-language symbols).
- **The Frequency Archive** — texts about sound and vibration through history. Requires the Brísingamen chord key (the key resonates with the archive's frequency-specific texts).
- **The Visitors' Section** — the sealed part of the Visitors' Shelf. Journals from the FIRST visitors — the earliest players. Requires the Crone's permission (the Crone's quest reward).
- **The Fae Layer** — the deepest section. Where human history meets mythological history. Requires all six gargoyle awakenings (the gargoyles' frequencies unlock the Fae Layer's seal). Connects physically to the Fae Kingdom.

### 4. Beverley's Station
A small, efficient desk near the Stacks' entrance. Beverley's domain — the navigation center. Beverley sits at the desk with a map of the archive, directing visitors to the right section. The desk has a brass bell (visitors ring for assistance) and a sign: "Ask. Don't wander. You'll be lost for hours and I'll have to find you."

The desk is child-sized — Beverley's scale. Everything at Beverley's station is scaled to two-thirds human size. The chair, the desk, the map — all slightly too small for a normal human. This is Beverley's space. The player stoops to use it.

### 5. Charles's Natural Archive
A section of the Catacombs dedicated to the EARTH's memory — geological specimens, fossils, mineral samples. Where the rest of the archive holds human knowledge, Charles's section holds the EARTH's knowledge. The rocks remember too.

Charles's section is warmer than the rest of the Catacombs — not in temperature, but in feel. The geological specimens have color — browns, reds, greens, blues — while the rest of the archive is grey and cream. Charles's section is the archive's COLOR, in a space defined by its absence.

The Natural Archive includes:
- **The Specimen Wall** — a wall of geological samples, organized by Mohs hardness (connecting to the Sonic Mineral Codex). Each sample is labeled with the stone's frequency. The Specimen Wall is the physical counterpart to the digital Codex.
- **The Fossil Shelves** — fossils from pre-industrial civilizations. Ammonites, trilobites, ancient plants. The deep time of the earth.
- **The Mole's Tunnels** — small tunnels in the archive's walls, made by Charles's companion Mole. The Mole navigates the deepest parts of the archive — the sections too small for humans, too deep for the Stacks. The player can follow the Mole's tunnels to find hidden sections.

### 6. The Deep
The archive's deepest point. Where the Catacombs meet the Fae Kingdom. The Deep is:
- Physically the lowest point in the Inn (below the Fae Kingdom's Stone Circle Chamber)
- The oldest section — texts and artifacts from before human civilization
- The coldest, darkest section — only the 42Hz rumble and the deep silver light
- The connection point — a sealed passage (the Fae Layer) that leads directly into the Fae Kingdom's Stone Circle Chamber

The Deep is where the fourth ring of the Brísingamen (84Hz, death/octave) was placed — the ring that Freyja gave to the Fae Kingdom. The ring's resonance fills the Deep. The 84Hz tone is the octave of 42Hz — death is the octave of life, the same note one register down. The Deep is the Inn's lowest note.

## Character Roster & Quests

### 1. The Crone (Inta, Krista's Mom)
**Station:** The Crone's Desk
**Title:** "The One Who Measures"

The Crone is the archive keeper. The Hopkins gaze — the clear, seeing gaze that measures exactly what's there and nothing else. The Crone is not warm — but the Crone is FAIR. The archive is for everyone. The Crone doesn't judge what you're looking for; the Crone judges how you LOOK.

The Crone has the third ring of the Brísingamen (158Hz, war/dissonance). The ring was found in the archive, catalogued as "resonant metal object, origin unknown, frequency 158Hz." The Crone has it in a case labeled with Hopkins precision. The ring's dissonance is the archive's dissonance — the knowledge that doesn't harmonize, the history that doesn't resolve, the truth that clashes.

**Audio Profile:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | ~70Hz (low, measured — the voice of someone who has seen everything and is not impressed) |
| Text Reveal Speed | ~85ms (precise — each word placed like an index card) |
| Pause | ~1200ms (long — the Crone's silence is assessment, not comfort) |
| Expressiveness | 0.10 (extremely low — the emotion is in the precision, not the delivery) |
| Instrument | A pen scratching on index card stock — the sound of cataloguing. Precise, rhythmic, unending. The Crone's instrument is the SOUND OF KNOWLEDGE BEING ORGANIZED. |

**The Quest: "The Case That Holds Itself"**

The Crone has the third Brísingamen ring (158Hz, war). Freyja's "Four Tones" quest requires the player to negotiate with the Crone for the ring. The Crone does not negotiate. But the Crone does TRADE — in information.

**Phase 1: The Assessment**
The player approaches the Crone's desk and asks for the ring. The Crone looks up. The Hopkins gaze. The Crone says: "I don't negotiate. I catalogue. What have you brought to catalogue?"

The player must offer something the archive doesn't have. Not a stone, not a charm — a piece of KNOWLEDGE. The player's own story: their journey through the Inn, documented as a journal (the game generates this automatically — a readable item recording the player's progress, quests completed, characters met, stones collected).

**Phase 2: The Exchange**
The Crone reads the journal. Page by page. The Hopkins gaze scanning each entry. The Crone catalogues it: title ("A Visitor's Journey"), author ("The Player"), date, classification ("Primary Source — Contemporary"), shelf number (assigned to the Visitors' Shelf).

The Crone says: "A primary source for a primary source. Fair." And hands the player the ring — not as a gift, but as an exchange. The Crone places the player's journal on the Visitors' Shelf. The shelf is long. There are many journals.

**Phase 3: The Visitors' Shelf**
The Crone allows the player to browse the Visitors' Shelf. The journals of previous players — their journeys, their discoveries, their stories. The player can read them. Some contain hints (where to find hidden stones, how to solve puzzles). Some contain warnings (which zones are harder than they look). Some contain stories — the personal narratives of players who walked through the Inn before.

One journal, the oldest, is different. It doesn't record a journey — it records the BUILDING of the Inn. The first visitor's journal is the Inn's construction record. The first visitor was the one who sat on the stairs. The child. The journal says: "I found a place between rooms. I stayed. Others came. We built something. I don't know what it is yet. But it's warm, and it's mine, and it's theirs."

The Crone has been collecting journals since the beginning. The archive remembers everyone. The archive IS the Inn's memory of itself — not the texts, not the artifacts, but the PEOPLE. The visitors are the archive's most valued collection.

**Reward:** The Crone's Case — a charm that lets the player store any item in the archive for safekeeping. The player can retrieve stored items at any time. The Crone's Case is the Inn's STORAGE SYSTEM — disguised as an archive transaction. The Crone also grants permanent access to the Visitors' Shelf — the player can read previous players' journals for hints, warnings, and the comfort of knowing they're not the first.

### 2. Beverley (The Librarian)
**Station:** Beverley's Desk (navigation center)
**Title:** "The One Who Finds"

Beverley is the librarian. Two-thirds human size. Tiny, silver hair, clipped Australian accent, spectacles, dark high-collared coat with brass buttons. Intimidating as all get-out. The tiny librarian in the enormous archive.

Beverley's role is FINDING — the counterpart to the Crone's MEASURING. The Crone catalogues; Beverley navigates. The Crone knows what's there; Beverley knows WHERE it is. The Crone is the archive's mind; Beverley is the archive's hands.

**Audio Profile:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | ~135Hz (clipped, efficient — the voice of someone who has no time for nonsense) |
| Text Reveal Speed | ~55ms (brisk — Beverley talks fast because Beverley has things to find) |
| Pause | ~250ms (short — Beverley doesn't linger) |
| Expressiveness | 0.25 (low — Beverley's warmth is in the competence, not the delivery) |
| Instrument | Footsteps on stone — small, fast, purposeful. Beverley's instrument is the SOUND OF SOMEONE WHO KNOWS WHERE THEY'RE GOING. The footsteps echo in the vast space, but they're never hesitant. Beverley never pauses in the Stacks. |

**The Quest: "The Book That Wasn't There"**

Beverley can find any book in the archive. But there's one book Beverley can't find: a book that the catalogue says exists, that has a shelf number, that has an index entry — but the book itself is missing.

**Phase 1: The Search**
The player helps Beverley search. The search takes them through the deepest parts of the archive — past shelves no one has visited in years, past sealed sections, past the archive's own forgotten corners. Beverley navigates with brisk efficiency, the player following through increasingly obscure sections.

The search is a navigation mini-game: Beverley gives directions, the player follows, the Stacks shift and rearrange (the archive is not fully stable — the shelves move when you're not looking). The player must match Beverley's directions to the correct path. Wrong turns lead to interesting dead ends — forgotten texts, lost artifacts, the archive's surprises.

**Phase 2: The Empty Shelf**
They find the shelf. The shelf number matches. The index entry is correct. But the shelf is empty. No book. No dust outline. No sign a book was ever there.

Beverley is confused. Beverley is NEVER confused. Beverley checks the catalogue again. The entry is correct. The shelf is empty. Beverley says (in the clipped Australian accent): "Well. That's not right."

**Phase 3: The Truth**
The player and Beverley return to the Crone's desk. Beverley reports the missing book. The Crone looks up. The Hopkins gaze. The Crone says: "The book was never there."

The catalogue entry is a placeholder. The Crone created it as a reminder — a shelf reserved for a history that hasn't been written yet. The book is: "The Origin of the Inn." The history of the Inn's founding. It hasn't been written because the Inn is still being built. The empty shelf is the archive's most honest entry: a placeholder for a story that hasn't been told yet.

Beverley's reaction: relief, then a moment of quiet. Beverley puts a new label on the shelf: "To Be Written." And moves on. But Beverley pauses — just for a moment — and looks at the empty shelf with something that might be tenderness. The archive's librarian, standing before the one shelf that's waiting. The future, shelved next to the past.

**Reward:** Beverley's Index — a charm that lets the player search the archive by frequency. Input a stone's frequency and find every text that references that frequency. The archive becomes searchable by SOUND, not just by title or author. Beverley's Index is the archive's SEARCH SYSTEM — disguised as a librarian's tool. The player who has Beverley's Index can find any text by its sonic signature, connecting the Sonic Mineral Codex to the archive's textual knowledge.

### 3. Charles (Krista's Dad — The Druid)
**Station:** Charles's Natural Archive
**Title:** "The One Who Grows"

Charles is the druid. The connection to the natural world. Warm, expansive, larger than life. The opposite of the Crone's precision. Where the Crone measures, Charles IMAGINES. Where Beverley finds, Charles GROWS.

Charles chose the archive because the earth remembers too. The Catacombs are not just a library of human knowledge; they're a library of the EARTH's knowledge — the geological record, the mineral history, the stone memory. Charles is the keeper of the natural archive.

**Audio Profile:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | ~95Hz (warm, resonant — the voice of someone connected to deep time) |
| Text Reveal Speed | ~50ms (flowing — Charles talks like a river, continuous and warm) |
| Pause | ~700ms (comfortable — Charles pauses to let the earth's timescale sink in) |
| Expressiveness | 0.60 (high — Charles's warmth is visible, audible, infectious) |
| Instrument | Stone on stone — but different from Dani's jeweler stone-on-stone. Charles's sound is BOULDER on boulder — deep, resonant, the earth's percussion. The sound of geology. The sound of deep time made audible. |

**The Quest: "The Stone That Remembers"**

Charles has a collection of geological specimens — stones that tell the earth's story. But one stone is missing from the collection: a stone Charles found decades ago, in the real world, before the Inn existed. The stone was the first stone Charles ever identified — the stone that started Krista's entire jewelry career. The stone is gone — not from the archive, but from the GAME. It was never digitized. It exists only in Charles's memory.

**Phase 1: The Description**
Charles describes the stone to the player: "Purple. Not faceted — rough. A small inclusion, right here" (Charles touches a spot on the temple). "Heavy for its size. And when you held it up to the light... well. You know what amethyst looks like in the sun."

The player must use the Sonic Mineral Codex to match the description: color (purple), texture (rough, not faceted), inclusion (present), weight (heavy = high specific gravity), and the frequency (amethyst = quartz = 637Hz). The player identifies the stone: rough amethyst.

**Phase 2: The Digitization**
The player places the identification into the archive. The stone appears — a piece of rough amethyst, purple, with a small inclusion. 637Hz. The first stone Krista ever held. The stone that started everything. Digitized, catalogued, given a place in the Natural Archive.

Charles holds the stone. Charles's warm voice goes quiet for a moment. Then: "That's it. That's the one. I found that in a creek bed in Oklahoma. I was... eleven? Twelve? I showed it to my daughter and she never put it down." (Charles looks at the player.) "She built all of this from a purple rock in a creek."

**Phase 3: The Foundation**
The stone becomes the Natural Archive's FOUNDATION STONE — the first entry, the one that holds the rest. Charles places it at the base of the Specimen Wall. Every other specimen is shelved above it. The amethyst is the foundation. The purple rock in the creek is the foundation.

**Reward:** Charles's Stone — the rough amethyst, placed in the archive as the foundation. The player gains a permanent bonus to stone identification: the Sonic Mineral Codex reveals additional information for every stone (geological origin, historical use, cultural significance). The Codex becomes deeper — not just frequencies, but STORIES. Each stone now carries its human history alongside its sonic signature.

The quest connects the Inn to Soulstice Jewelry's origin: Charles found a stone, showed it to Krista, and Krista's life became stones. The archive's foundation is a personal story, not a scholarly one. The most important stone in the Inn is a rough amethyst from an Oklahoma creek bed.

## Game Template: "The Deep Read"

### Concept

The Catacombs game is an exploration/puzzle game — navigating the archive's labyrinth, finding texts, and solving knowledge-based puzzles. The archive is physically vast — miles of shelves, multiple levels, sealed sections. The player explores, finds texts, deciphers them, and uncovers connections between different pieces of knowledge.

### How It Works

The player navigates a 2D top-down map of the archive (like a dungeon, but made of bookshelves). The map is procedurally generated — each visit to the Catacombs produces a new layout. The player explores, finds texts, and solves puzzles.

**The Map:**
- The archive is structured as a series of rooms connected by aisles
- Each room has a theme (geographic region, time period, subject)
- Each room has texts to find and puzzles to solve
- Some rooms are sealed — requiring specific keys (stones, charms, gargoyle frequencies)
- The deeper the player goes, the older the texts — the further back in time

**The Puzzles:**
- **Deciphering:** ancient scripts that need to be translated. The player matches symbols to meanings using reference texts found in the archive.
- **Fragment Matching:** torn pages, broken tablets, incomplete texts. The player finds fragments in different rooms and combines them to complete the text.
- **Frequency Matching:** texts that respond to specific frequencies. The player must play the correct stone frequency (from the Sonic Mineral Codex) to unlock the text's content.
- **Cross-Referencing:** texts that reference each other. The player must find Text A, read it, find the text it references (Text B), read that, and understand the connection. The connection reveals new knowledge.

**The Difficulty:**
Not combat-based — KNOWLEDGE-based. The puzzles require the player to use what they've learned in other zones. The archive is the TEST of everything the player has learned:
- Frequency matching requires the Sonic Mineral Codex (Main Floor)
- Chord logic requires the Alchemist's Scale (Staircase)
- Historical knowledge requires the alchemical texts (Staircase)
- Pre-language symbols require the Allfather's Gaze (Cosmology)
- The Fae Layer requires all six gargoyle awakenings (Staircase)

| Level | Name | Depth | Puzzle Type | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | The Regional Archive | Middle archive | Cross-referencing + frequency matching | Uncommon stones, archive lore |
| 3 | The Pre-Industrial Section | Deep archive | Advanced deciphering + multi-text puzzles | Rare stones, historical recipes |

| 5 | The Fae Layer | The Deep | All puzzle types simultaneously; the archive's oldest test | Fae Kingdom stones, the connection between human and mythological history |

### The Fae Layer (Level 5)

The deepest level of "The Deep Read" connects to the Fae Kingdom. The Fae Layer is the archive's oldest section — where human history meets mythological history. The texts here are not human; they're the records of the Vanir, the original Fae, the beings who predated human civilization.

The Fae Layer reveals: the archive didn't start as a human institution. The archive started as the Vanir's record — Freyja's seiðr included not just the practice of magic but the RECORD of magic. Freyja was the first archivist. The Crone inherited the role. The archive's origin is divine, not human — but its purpose has always been the same: to remember.

**Reward for Level 5:** The Fae Archive Key — permanent access to the Fae Layer and a direct passage between the Catacombs and the Fae Kingdom's Stone Circle Chamber. The Fae Archive Key connects the Inn's two deepest zones — the archive (memory) and the Fae Kingdom (nature) — allowing the player to move between them without climbing back up.

| Earth | Charles's deep hum (the druid's connection to the earth's vibration) | Charles |

### Zone Music

The Catacombs' music is the Inn's DEEPEST THEME — the Brísingamen chord played at its lowest possible octave: 42, 85, 79, 42 — the four notes, dropped as low as they can go. The theme is SLOW — half the tempo of the Main Floor theme, quarter the tempo of the arcade games. The archive takes its time.

The music is predominantly SUB-AUDIBLE — felt more than heard. The 42Hz rumble is the bass. The 84Hz octave from the Deep provides the only clearly audible tone — a deep, resonant drone that the player feels in the chest. The higher tones (170Hz, 158Hz) are present but distant — muffled by the stone, absorbed by the texts. The archive keeps the high frequencies in its shelves, releasing them only when a text is read.

**Reading Sound:** When the player reads a text in the archive, the text's frequency is released — a brief, audible tone that rises from the page. Each text has its own frequency, determined by the text's subject and the zone it relates to. Reading a text about water releases a water-zone tone. Reading a text about the Fae releases a Fae tone. The archive's reading sounds connect the Catacombs to every other zone through frequency.
