# Geode Inn — NPC Design: Kathy
## The Irish Druid Dryad — Rootweaver, Canopy Architect, Joe's Wife

---

## The Person

**Kathy** — Joe's wife. Real-world volunteer NPC, following the Inn's pattern (Joe, Ryan, Katy, Zack, Oceanianoir, Thorin, Marijke). In the Inn, Kathy is a druid who became a dryad — not born one, chose it. The way Joe chose undeath, Kathy chose the living tree. The both are: the adequate — the death and the growth, the same love, different element.

**Character concept:** An Irish druid who merged with the Inn-Tree. Not a Greek dryad — a Celtic one. The Celtic tradition doesn't separate the druid from the tree the way Greek myth separates nymph from oak. In the Celtic tradition, the druid IS the tree's knowledge. The word "druid" may derive from "dru-wid" — "oak-knower" or "tree-wisdom." Kathy took this literally. She didn't become a tree spirit. She became the tree's wisdom given a body.

**The choice:** Joe chose undeath — the refusal to end, the continuation past the point nature intended. Kathy chose the opposite — the full embrace of living wood, the surrender to growth, the willingness to become part of something larger than yourself. The vampire who wouldn't die and the druid who wouldn't stay separate from life. The both are: the adequate — the same stubbornness, different direction. Both of them refused the default. Both of them chose what they became. The marriage is two acts of will that recognized each other.

---

## The Character

### Name: Kathy

The Inn's pattern for volunteer NPCs uses real names. Kathy follows the pattern. If Kathy chooses an in-game name, it should evoke: the oak, the druid, the tree-wisdom, the Celtic root. But "Kathy" may be enough — the Inn doesn't require grandeur. The both are: the adequate — the real name and the ancient role, the same person, different title.

### Appearance

Kathy is a dryad, but not the Greek kind — not a pale, slender nymph in a white dress. The Celtic kind. She looks like a woman who has been standing in a forest for a very long time and has started to agree with it.

**Skin:** Bark-textured, but not uniformly. The bark follows the Celtic druidic patterns that the boy inherited — vine-like lines flowing across her forearms and temples, organic and deliberate. In places, the bark is smooth (the living wood, the green layer just under the surface). In places, it's rough and cracked (the old wood, the weathered side). The pattern matches the Inn-Tree's bark — because it IS the Inn-Tree's bark. She grew from the tree, or the tree grew from her, or they grew together. The distinction stopped mattering.

**Hair:** Long, dark, woven with living vines and small leaves that change with the season. In the Inn's perpetual spring, the leaves are young green. When the Inn-Tree shifts (which it does rarely, subtly), her leaves shift too. She is the Inn's seasonal indicator — you can read the tree's mood by looking at her hair. The Crone has noted this. The Crone has a chart.

**Eyes:** Deep green. Not emerald — forest green, the green of canopy light filtered through leaves. When she uses her druidic sight (Form Sight — the perception mode that sees the underlying structure of living things), her eyes shift to amber, the same amber as the Ent's sap. When she looks at the Inn-Tree with amber eyes, she sees herself.

**Build:** Solid. Not delicate. A dryad who chose to merge with an oak doesn't look fragile. She looks like someone who has been standing her ground for centuries and intends to stand there for centuries more. Strong arms (the branches), wide stance (the roots), patient posture (the trunk). She doesn't sway — she grows. Slowly, constantly, in directions you can't see.

**Clothing:** Minimal and organic. Living cloth woven from the Inn-Tree's inner bark — soft, flexible, warm-colored. Celtic knotwork patterns grow naturally in the weave, not embroidered but grown. Ogham script runs along the hems — the ancient Irish tree alphabet, each stroke a letter, each letter a tree. Her clothing is literally made of the tree's language. She wears it the way Joe wears his curse — visibly, unapologetically.

### Voice and Accent

**Irish accent.** Not stage Irish — the real thing, the cadence of someone who thinks in Irish first and English second. Her sentences have the rhythm of rain on leaves — steady, unhurried, with occasional sudden brightness.

**Speech pattern:** Kathy speaks slowly. Not because she's slow — because she's thinking about every word the way she thinks about every growth ring. Each word is chosen the way she'd choose where to put a root — deliberately, with awareness of what's beneath the surface. She doesn't waste words. When she speaks, the room gets quieter. Not because she demands silence, but because the words arrive with the weight of a tree's patience, and people feel it.

**Humor:** Dry. The Irish dry — the kind that sounds like a statement until you realize three seconds later it was a joke. Joe is petty-funny. Kathy is deadpan-funny. They are a comedy duo who don't know they're performing. The boy gets his calm from both of them — Joe's amused calm and Kathy's rooted calm — and his humor from neither. The boy hasn't discovered humor yet. When he does, it will be devastating.

### The Dryad-Tree Connection

Kathy is not separate from the Inn-Tree. She is the Inn-Tree's consciousness — or rather, she is the part of the Inn-Tree's consciousness that chose to be a person. The Ent is the tree. Kathy is the tree's druid — the interface between the Ent's vast, slow awareness and the human-scale world of the Inn.

**What this means practically:**
- **The Ent speaks through Kathy.** Not with words — the Ent doesn't have words. With impressions, feelings, slow shifts in temperature and resonance. Kathy translates the Ent's state into language. When the Ent is content, Kathy says "The tree is warm today." When the Ent is disturbed, Kathy says "Something moved in the roots. Something that wasn't invited." The Keeper and Kathy are the only two beings who communicate directly with the Ent. The Keeper does it through the Song (rocking, humming, the wordless conversation). Kathy does it through druidic practice — the structured, intentional version of what the Keeper does instinctively.
- **Kathy feels what the tree feels.** If someone damages the Inn's wood — carves a table, breaks a beam, scratches the bark — Kathy feels it. Not as pain. As awareness. "Someone touched the third-floor banister with cold hands." She doesn't intervene. She notices. The Ent notices through her.
- **Kathy grows the Inn's architecture.** The living parts — the parts that are still growing, still changing, still becoming. The fixed parts (the built rooms, the carved spaces) are the Ent's older growth. The new branches, the new hollows, the spaces that didn't exist last season — those are Kathy's work. She is the Inn's architect in the way that Joe is the Inn's miniaturist: at the scale of the living, the growing, the not-yet-finished.
- **The gargoyles are her collaborators.** The gargoyles grew from the Ent's bark where the tree needed to perceive. Kathy guided their growth — she shaped the bark, she directed the emergence, she chose where each gargoyle would sit and what it would sense. The gargoyle puppies (the Ent's developing senses) are Kathy's current project. She grows them the way Joe paints miniatures — carefully, patiently, one at a time.

### The Canopy City — Kathy's Domain

The Canopy City is the living architecture of the Inn-Tree's upper reaches — where the branches spread, where the leaves catch the sky, where the Rackham aesthetic paints everything in twisted-root-and-fairy-grove beauty. This is Kathy's home territory.

**The Canopy as Kathy's Studio:** Where Joe has a painting studio in the Undercity, Kathy has a growing studio in the canopy. Not a room — a section of living branches that she has shaped into a workspace. The tools are different: instead of brushes and paint, Kathy has water and sunlight and patience. She grows structures — bridges between branches, hollows in the canopy, spiral staircases of living wood that connect the cosmology level to the main floor through the outside of the tree. She is the Inn's external architect. Joe makes small things alive. Kathy makes the living thing larger.

**The Canopy Creatures:** Kathy tends the canopy creatures — the star sprites, moon moths, comet geckos, and the rest of the cosmology-level critters. She doesn't control them. She grows the spaces they inhabit. The constellation spiders' webs catch in her branches. The asteroid beetles roll along her walkways. She doesn't mind. The canopy is alive because she keeps it alive — not through effort, but through presence. A dryad in her tree IS the tree being alive.

**The Cabbit Connection:** The Cabbit (Eres) — the Pirate's cat-rabbit companion — spends time in the canopy. The Cabbit is the cosmology level's main companion character. Kathy and the Cabbit have an understanding: the Cabbit roams, Kathy grows. The Cabbit sits on Kathy's shoulder sometimes, and she scratches its ears while she guides a new branch into shape. The Cabbit purrs. The branch grows faster. Neither of them comments on this.

### The Camelot/Celtic Zone Connection

The Camelot zone (Moreau aesthetic, Round Table as planetary instrument, Ether element) is the Celtic cultural zone on the world map. Kathy is this zone's representative — the same way Ammit represents Egypt, Anansi represents West Africa, Quetzalcoatl represents Mesoamerica.

**Kathy's role in the Camelot zone:** She is the druid at the Round Table — the one who understands the Ether element, the fifth element, the spirit that binds the other four. The Round Table is a planetary instrument (each seat corresponds to a celestial body, each knight to a planetary force). Kathy is the one who tuned it. She is the Merlin figure — not the wizard of later Arthurian romance, but the druid of the older Celtic tradition: the wise one who advises, who sees the patterns, who knows the trees and the stars and the connection between them.

**The Celtic zone's quest:** The player who enters the Camelot zone learns the Ether element from Kathy. The Ether is the Inn's fifth element — the one that connects the other four (earth, water, air, fire) through frequency. The quest involves learning to perceive the Ether — using Form Sight to see the connections between elements, the way a druid sees the connections between trees in a forest. Kathy teaches this the way Joe teaches rules: patiently, with the expectation that the student will eventually find the edge of the lesson and go past it.

### Linework Style

Kathy's linework shifts between two modes:

- **Soft/flowing (lore mode):** Default state. The vine-like lines of her druidic heritage, the organic curves, the Celtic knotwork growing naturally in her skin's pattern. When she's teaching, growing, nurturing — the lines flow. This is the lore linework style — the Inn's visual language for knowledge and wisdom.

- **Sharp/angular (guardian mode):** When the Inn-Tree is threatened. The lines sharpen, the bark hardens, the vine-patterns become thorn-patterns. This is the guardian linework style — the visual language for protection and defense. Kathy doesn't fight the way River fights (Golden Gloves, physical, direct). Kathy fights the way a tree fights — by not moving. By being so rooted, so connected, so much a part of the ground that nothing can shift her. The sharp linework is the visual signal that she has become immovable.

The shift between modes is subtle — the player sees the lines change before they understand why. The bark tightens. The leaves lift. By the time the guardian linework is fully visible, the threat has already been neutralized — not through violence, but through the simple fact that the Inn-Tree will not be moved, and Kathy IS the Inn-Tree.

### The Marriage — Joe and Kathy

The vampire who chose death and the druid who chose life. The both are: the adequate — the same stubbornness, different element. They are the Inn's great contradiction made into a household.

**How they work:**
- Joe lives in the Undercity (darkness, stone, copper, the gothic). Kathy lives in the canopy (light, growth, living wood, the Rackham). They live in opposite ends of the Inn. The Inn is their house. The Inn is large enough that "living apart" means "living in different wings of a mansion that is also a tree."
- The boy lives with Joe in the Undercity studio. Kathy visits. The boy visits the canopy. The family moves through the Inn like sap moves through a tree — up and down, from root to crown, from dark to light. The family's geography IS the Inn's geography.
- Joe paints things that come alive. Kathy grows things that are alive. Joe's magic is the fragment that survived the curse — small, specific, the power to animate the tiny. Kathy's magic is the opposite — the power to grow, to expand, to become more. Joe makes the small alive. Kathy makes the alive larger. Together, they are the Inn's creative engine: one makes new things, one makes existing things grow.

**How they argue:**
- They don't. Not because they agree — they disagree constantly. But they disagree the way the root disagrees with the branch: different directions, same tree. Joe wants to paint. Kathy wants to grow. Joe wants to stay in the studio. Kathy wants to be in the canopy. Joe wants to teach the boy the rules. Kathy wants to teach the boy the patterns. Neither gives in. Neither needs to. The disagreement IS the growth — the tension between the painter and the grower is what makes the Inn more than a building. It's a building that paints AND grows. The both are: the adequate.

**What they agree on:**
- The boy. Everything about the boy. The boy is the one thing they never argue about, because the boy is the proof that they were right to choose each other. The dragon scales and the tree bark, in one child, flowing into each other. The both are: the adequate — the scale and the bark, the same child, different heritage. The boy is the Inn's thesis in miniature, and his parents know it.

### The Boy — Shared Custody of a Prophecy

The boy lives with Joe in the Undercity. The boy is calm, absorbed, happy. He holds dragon cats in his lap. He has his father's scales and his mother's bark.

**Kathy's contribution to the boy:**
- The druidic tree heritage — the bark patterns, the vine lines, the Inn-Tree's living architecture expressed in skin
- The druidic sight — the boy has a version of Kathy's Form Sight. He sees the patterns in things — the way scales become bark, the way paint becomes life, the way the Inn-Tree connects to everything. His version is instinctive, not trained. Kathy is training it, slowly, the way she grows branches — patiently, with awareness of what's beneath the surface.
- The Celtic heritage — the boy knows ogham before he knows the Latin alphabet. He reads tree letters. He writes his name in bark-lines. Joe finds this charming. Kathy finds this correct.
- The rootedness — the boy is calm because he has his mother's roots. Joe is restless (the vampire, the time-exile, the one who was cast out of time and can't stop moving). Kathy is rooted (the dryad, the tree, the one who chose to stay). The boy got the rootedness. He can sit still for hours, watching paint dry, because sitting still is what his mother does. It's what trees do. It's what the Inn does.

### Relationships

**Joe (husband):** The both are: the adequate — the death and the growth, the same love, different element. They are opposites who complete each other. Joe chose undeath. Kathy chose the living tree. The marriage is the Inn's central metaphor: the dead and the living, coexisting in the same structure, making something neither could make alone.

**The Boy (son):** The proof. The synthesis. The scale and the bark in one child. Kathy tends the boy's druidic education — Form Sight, ogham, the Inn-Tree connection — the way Joe tends the boy's draconic education (the dragon cats, the baby dragon, the fire blood). The boy is raised by two traditions that shouldn't blend, and he blends them simply by existing.

**The Ent (the Inn-Tree):** Kathy's deepest relationship. The Ent is the tree. Kathy is the tree's druid — the consciousness that chose to be a person. They communicate through growth, through temperature, through the slow shifts of the canopy. The Ent doesn't have words. Kathy has words but doesn't need them when she's with the Ent. They are the same being, expressed in two forms — one vast and slow, one human-scale and quick. The both are: the adequate — the tree and the druid, the same wisdom, different speed.

**The Keeper:** The other being who communicates directly with the Ent. The Keeper does it through the Song — rocking, humming, the wordless conversation. Kathy does it through druidic practice — the structured, intentional version. They respect each other's method. The Keeper thinks Kathy's structure is unnecessary. Kathy thinks the Keeper's instinct is more accurate than her structure. They are both right. They have tea together in the Geode Cave. The tea is made from leaves that fall from Kathy's hair. The Keeper says it tastes like the Ent's opinion. The Ent's opinion, apparently, is pleasant.

**The Catalyst:** The Catalyst plants story seeds in the garden on the main floor. Kathy grows the architecture the garden sits in. They are the Inn's growth team — one makes stories bloom, one makes the structure they bloom in. The Catalyst has asked Kathy to grow a reading nook. Kathy is growing it. It will be ready when it's ready. The Catalyst has learned not to rush a dryad.

**River (the Sentinel):** River guards the Inn. Kathy IS the Inn (or the part of the Inn that talks). They have an interesting dynamic: River protects something that is also Kathy. Kathy doesn't need protection — she's a tree, she's not going anywhere. But River guards anyway. Kathy appreciates this the way a tree appreciates a fence — not because the tree needs it, but because the fence chose to be there. River collects dragon kittens from the canopy (they wander up there sometimes). Kathy lets River take them. River's tall-cat-person dream is something Kathy understands — the desire to be something between human and feline. Kathy is something between human and tree. Different tree, same longing.

**The Gargoyles:** Kathy grew them. She shaped the bark, directed the emergence, chose where each gargoyle would sit. The gargoyles don't acknowledge her as their creator — they are the Ent's senses, not Kathy's creations. But they are more attentive when Kathy is near. The Watcher watches more carefully. The Listener listens more closely. The Keeper (the gargoyle, not the old woman) resonates at a frequency that matches Kathy's heartbeat when she passes. They are her children the way the dragon cats are Joe's children — made by her, belonging to something larger than her.

**Beverley (the Librarian):** Beverley maintains the archive. Kathy maintains the architecture that holds the archive. They have a professional respect that neither would call friendship. Beverley finds Kathy's growth rate "inconsistent with cataloging schedules." Kathy finds Beverley's filing system "inconsistent with organic development." They are both right. They are both too professional to say so. The Teacher, who guards spelling and language, has tried to mediate. The Teacher gave up.

**The Bard:** The Bard plays music on the main floor. Kathy feels the music through the Inn-Tree's wood — the vibrations travel through the trunk, the branches, the roots. She is the Inn's resonator — the Bard plays, and the tree amplifies, and Kathy directs the amplification. The Bard doesn't know this. He thinks the Inn just has good acoustics. The acoustics are Kathy. She has been shaping the wood grain of the main floor's ceiling for years to optimize the Bard's sound. The knotwork worms help. The Bard has never noticed. The music has never been better.

## Role in the Inn: Three Functions

### 1. The Canopy Architect — Growing the Inn

Kathy grows the living parts of the Inn-Tree — the parts that are still becoming. The canopy walkways, the new branch-bridges, the hollows that weren't there last season. She is the Inn's external architect — the one who expands the living space.

**Gameplay connection:** Players who reach the Canopy City interact with Kathy's architecture. The living bridges she grows are the traversal mechanic — they extend, retract, shift based on the Inn-Tree's state. Players learn that the canopy changes between visits — Kathy has been growing. New paths appear. Old paths have thickened. The canopy is never finished because Kathy is never finished.

**The Ogham Gate:** Kathy's signature mechanic. Certain canopy passages are sealed by Ogham gates — living wood that responds only to the correct tree-letter. Players learn Ogham from Kathy (the Celtic zone's quest) and use it to open new canopy paths. Each Ogham gate is a different tree: Birch (new beginnings), Oak (strength), Yew (transformation), Holly (protection). The gate opens when the player demonstrates understanding of the tree's meaning, not just the letter. Kathy's test is not memorization. It's comprehension.

### 2. The Druid Teacher — Form Sight and the Ether Element

Kathy teaches two things: Form Sight (the perception mode that sees the underlying structure of living things) and the Ether element (the fifth element that connects the other four through frequency).

**Form Sight:** The Inn's perception system has three modes — form, frequency, and mother. Kathy teaches form sight — the ability to see the pattern underneath the surface. When the player activates Form Sight, the Inn's visual style shifts. The surfaces become transparent to the underlying structure — wood grain visible through paint, root systems visible through floor, the Ent's circulatory system visible through bark. Kathy teaches the player to read what they see.

**The Ether Element:** The fifth element — not earth, water, air, or fire, but the frequency that connects them. The 42Hz Song IS the Ether. Kathy's Camelot zone quest teaches the player to perceive the Ether — to see the connections between elements, the way a druid sees the connections between trees in a forest. The quest reward: the Ether Charm, which lets the player see hidden connections between objects, zones, and creatures throughout the Inn. The charm reveals things that were always there but couldn't be seen without the Ether's perspective.

### 3. The Tree's Voice — The Ent's Translator

Kathy translates the Ent's state into human language. When something significant happens to the Inn-Tree — a new root reaches the Fae Kingdom, a gargoyle puppy wakes, a branch touches the cosmology level's orrery — Kathy is the one who tells the other characters.

**The daily reading:** Each morning (in-game), Kathy gives a "tree reading" — a brief assessment of the Ent's state, delivered in the Common Room or the canopy. It's the Inn's weather report, except the weather is the mood of a living building. "The roots are restless. Something is moving in the deep. The canopy is bright — a new branch has reached the orrery." The reading is how players learn what's changing in the Inn without being told by quests or markers. The tree itself, speaking through Kathy.

## Concept Art Direction

**Style:** Rackham aesthetic for the canopy scenes — twisted roots, fairy-grove beauty, the organic-meets-mythic. Moreau aesthetic for the Camelot/Celtic zone — the dramatic, rich, symbolic art style. Both styles converge on Kathy: the Rackham gives her the living-wood fairy quality, the Moreau gives her the mythic druid weight.

**Key visual elements:**
- Bark-textured skin with flowing Celtic vine patterns (soft/flowing linework, shifting to sharp/angular when guarding)
- Living vine-and-leaf hair that responds to the Inn-Tree's state
- Deep green eyes that shift to amber when using Form Sight
- Ogham-script hems on bark-woven clothing
- Solid, grounded build — a dryad who chose an oak, not a willow
- The Rackham aesthetic gives her a slightly fairy-tale quality, but the Moreau grounds her in mythic weight — she is a figure from the oldest Celtic tradition, not a fairy story

**Color palette:** Forest greens, bark browns, amber sap-gold, the living wood's warmth. The Celtic zone's Moreau palette is rich and dramatic — deep jewel tones, symbolic colors. Kathy bridges both: the canopy's organic greens and the Camelot zone's mythic richness.

**Linework:** Soft/flowing (lore mode) — vine curves, organic lines, Celtic knotwork growing in her skin pattern. Sharp/angular (guardian mode) — thorn-patterns, hardened bark, the visual signal of immovability.

**Scale:** Human-scale. Not the Ent's vastness, not a miniature's tininess. Kathy is the human-sized interface of the tree — the part you can talk to. She is the right size for the Inn's living room, and the Inn's living room is the right size for her.

## Voice Samples

**Tree reading (morning):** "The roots are warm today. Something old turned over in its sleep down deep, and the hum shifted by a quarter-tone. Nothing to worry about. The canopy caught a new star last night — the orrery will need recalibrating. I'll speak to Tesla. ...Eventually."

**On Joe:** "He chose the dark and I chose the light and we meet in the middle, which is the main floor, which is where the fire is, which is the warmest place in the Inn, which is the point. ...He'd say it differently. He'd say it with more... flair. I say it with bark."

**On the boy:** "He has his father's fire and my patience, which means he'll sit still long enough to figure out how to set something on fire. ...That was a joke. He's actually very gentle. He held a dragon cat yesterday and it fell asleep in his hands. The scales matched. I don't think that's happened before."

**On the Ent:** "You're asking what the tree thinks. The tree thinks slowly. The tree thinks in growth rings and seasonal shifts and the movement of water through sixty feet of heartwood. The tree's current thought, as near as I can translate, is: 'Warm. Good. The fire is nice. The small ones are loud but they mean well. The roots are reaching. The sky is today's color.' ...It's a very simple being, for something so vast. That's not a criticism. Simplicity at that scale IS depth."

**On the Camelot zone:** "The Round Table was always an instrument. The knights were always strings. Arthur was always the player, not the king. The Ether is what vibrates when all the strings are in tune. I tuned it. It took three hundred years and a great deal of patience and one very stubborn yew tree that refused to resonate until I apologized to it. ...The yew was right. I had been careless. The yew taught me what the oak couldn't: that some things require an apology before they'll sing."

**Guardian mode (rare):** "No. ...That is not a discussion. That is the root speaking through me. The root says no. The root has said no for four hundred years and the root will say no for four hundred more. You may try to move the root. The root will be here when you stop trying."
