# Geode Inn — The Mycorrhizal Network: "The Wood Wide Web"
## System: The Fae Kingdom's Trade and Communication System
## Access: Fae Kingdom → The Between (the shifting borderlands between Seelie and Unseelie)
## Prerequisite: Fae Kingdom access (Standing Stones game completed)

### Overview

The Mycorrhizal Network is the Fae Kingdom's underground trading system — a living network of fungal threads that connects every tree, every root, and every stone circle in the Inn. The network is the Fae Kingdom's nervous system: information, resources, and frequencies travel through the mycelium the way nerve signals travel through a body.

In the real world, mycorrhizal networks (the "Wood Wide Web") allow trees to share nutrients, send chemical warnings, and support each other through underground fungal connections. The Inn's version is the magical equivalent: Fae creatures can send messages, trade stones, and share frequencies through the network without ever leaving their zones.

The network is the Inn's DEEPEST trade system — deeper than the Artisan Market (surface trade), deeper than the Bead Composer (craft trade), deeper than the Caravan Master's routes (cultural trade). The mycorrhizal network is ROOT trade — the system that runs beneath everything, connecting zones at the root level.

## How It Works

### The Fungal Threads
The network is made of mycelium — thin, white, branching threads that grow through the soil beneath the Inn. In the game, the mycelium is VISIBLE in Form Sight: a glowing white network beneath the ground, pulsing at 42Hz, connecting every zone's root system.

The mycelium is ALIVE — it grows, extends, and responds to the player's activity. When the player visits a new zone, the mycelium extends a thread to that zone's root system. The more the player visits, the THICKER the thread — the stronger the connection.

### Sending Through the Network
The player can SEND through the network by placing an item on any mycelium node (visible in Form Sight as a glowing white point on the ground). The item is absorbed into the network and appears at a DIFFERENT node — one the player has visited before. The network acts as a postal system: items sent through the roots arrive at other zones.

What can be sent:
- **Stones** — the most common trade item. Stones sent through the network arrive at the destination with a slight frequency shift (the mycelium adds its own 42Hz hum to the stone's frequency, creating a harmonic)
- **Messages** — the player can write a short text message (in the tabletop voice) and send it through the network. The message appears as text on a leaf at the destination node. NPCs can read the messages and respond.
- **Frequencies** — the player can send a frequency (played on an instrument) through the network. The frequency travels the threads and plays at the destination — a musical message, a root-level serenade

### Receiving Through the Network
NPCs send items through the network too. The player who has a mycelium node near their favorite nook will occasionally find items left there by NPCs — a stone from a zone the player hasn't visited, a note from an NPC who remembered the player's favorite frequency, a charm from the Fae who appreciated the player's nook-sitting.

The receiving is not instant — items travel through the network at the speed of GROWTH (not instant, not slow — a few real hours). The waiting is part of the system: the mycorrhizal network doesn't rush. The network is a tree, and trees don't rush.

## NPC Interactions

### The Mycelium Tender
A new NPC — a Fae creature made of white threads, barely visible, the mycelium's caretaker. The Mycelium Tender is the network's gardener — maintaining the threads, extending the connections, ensuring the flow.

**Voice:** Whispered, from below, the sound of roots growing. The Mycelium Tender speaks from the ground, not the air. The voice comes UP through the soil.

**Dialogue:**
*"...The network connects. ...That's all it does. ...It connects. ...Stone to stone. Root to root. Zone to zone. ...The surface trades by walking. The depth trades by growing. ...The growing is slower. ...The growing is... [the whisper, the patience] ...more honest. ...You can't rush a tree. ...You can't rush a trade that goes through a tree. ...The waiting is the trust. ...You send. You wait. It arrives. ...The waiting is: the network saying 'I will carry this for you.' ...Carrying takes time. ...Carrying is: the adequate."*

### NPC Trades
NPCs use the network to trade with each other AND with the player:

- **Erik** (Norse Zone) sends mead through the network — a cask appears at the player's nook, marked "from the warm Viking." The mead is the Norse Zone's hospitality, delivered through the roots.
- **The Gardener** (Mediterranean) sends seeds — a seed packet appears, containing a plant that grows in the player's current zone. The Gardener's gift: a piece of the garden, sent through the earth.
- **The Examiner** (Desert Bone) sends fragments — papyrus fragments that the Examiner has identified but doesn't need. The fragments arrive annotated (the Examiner's handwriting, precise, clipped).
- **The Mycelium Tender** sends mushrooms — small, edible, glowing faintly at 42Hz. The mushrooms are the network's currency — eating one grants a temporary "Network Insight" buff (the player can see the mycelium without Form Sight for 10 minutes).

## Quest: "The Root Connection"

### Giver: The Mycelium Tender (at the Fae Kingdom's Between)
### Prerequisite: Fae Kingdom access + Form Sight + 5+ zones explored

**Quest Text:**
*"...The network has grown to you. ...You've been to five zones. ...Five threads. ...The threads are thin — you visited, but you didn't SIT. ...Sitting thickens the thread. ...The nook — the sitting — it's not just comfort. ...It's connection. ...Each nook you sit in grows a root. ...Each root connects to the network. ...The network is: the Inn's patience, made into a system. ...Go to each zone. Sit in a nook. ...Feel the thread thicken. ...When all five zones have thick threads, come back. ...I'll show you what the network looks like when it's strong."*

**Objective:**
- Visit 5 zones. In each zone, sit in a nook for 5 real minutes (the Nook Effect).
- After sitting, activate Form Sight and look at the ground — the mycelium thread to that zone has thickened (visually: from a thin white line to a thick, pulsing, 42Hz-carrying cable).
- Return to the Mycelium Tender with 5 thick threads.

**Reward:** The Root Key — a charm that grants permanent access to the mycorrhizal network's FAST LANE. Items sent through the network with the Root Key arrive in minutes instead of hours. The fast lane is the network's reward for the player who SITS — who takes the time to thicken the threads. The player who rushes gets the slow network. The player who sits gets the fast one.

## Quest: "The Dead Root"

### Giver: The Mycelium Tender
### Prerequisite: "The Root Connection" complete + Undercity access

**Quest Text:**
*"...There's a dead root. ...In the Undercity. ...The Bone Room. ...The network should connect through the preserved roots — the dead roots that still carry the signal. ...But the dead root is BROKEN. ...The signal stops. ...The Undercity is isolated — no network access. ...The Bone Keeper has been trying to fix it. ...The Bone Keeper doesn't know how. ...The dead root needs a LIVING stone — a stone with a strong frequency, placed at the break. ...The stone's frequency bridges the gap. ...The dead root becomes a living bridge."*

**Objective:**
- Go to the Undercity's Bone Room.
- Find the broken mycelium thread (visible in Form Sight — a white line that ends abruptly).
- Place a stone with a frequency above 500Hz on the break point.
- The stone's frequency bridges the dead root — the mycelium grows through the stone, connecting the living network to the dead root.
- The Undercity now has network access. The Bone Keeper can trade through the roots.

**Reward:** The Bone Keeper's gift — a fossil stone (a stone that contains a preserved frequency from the dead root's era). The fossil stone vibrates at 40Hz (the Rememberers' frequency — the dead root was from the Western Interior Seaway era, the same time as the Canadian Rockies' fossilized Fae). The fossil stone connects the Undercity's dead to the Appalachian Range's deepest layer (same frequency, same era). The dead root's repair creates a cross-zone connection that didn't exist before.

## Connections

- **The Fae Kingdom** — the network's hub. The Between is the network's center.
- **The Redwood Cathedral** — the redwoods' real-world shared root system is the network's most natural extension. The Cathedral IS the network, above ground.
- **The Nook Effect** — sitting in nooks thickens the threads. The nook system and the network are the SAME system: the player who sits is the player who connects.
- **Form Sight** — required to see the mycelium. The network is invisible without Form Sight. Perception and connection are linked: you can only connect to what you can see.
- **The Bone Room** — the dead root connects the living network to the preserved dead. The dead can trade through the network — fossils, memories, ancient frequencies.
- **The Hollow Earth** — the network's deepest node is the Resonance Engine (42Hz source). The network carries the Song from the Engine to every zone through the roots.
- **The Root Chamber** — the Norns' Well of Urd is a network node. The Well's water connects to the mycelium — the underground water system and the fungal network are the same system, expressed through different media (water and fungus).
- **The Wandering Program** — wandering creatures can send items back through the mycelium instead of carrying them. The network is the Wandering Program's return system — creatures send their findings through the roots.
- **The Bead Composer** — stones sent through the network arrive with a 42Hz harmonic added. The harmonic changes the stone's Bead Composer behavior — networked stones have richer sonic properties.
- **Cross-platform pairability** — the mycorrhizal network is the root-level connection system that unifies all zones. On mobile, the network is simplified (fewer visible threads). On Steam, the network is fully visualized. On web, the network is the navigation metaphor. The same system, adapted per platform.
