# THE STATUTE-BOOK OF THE UNCONFIRMED
## The real laws that only work if the neighbors exist
### Archive dossier (main floor, law shelf — kin to the Council Histories). Ruled in by Krista, 2026-09-14. Companion to the Troll Citizen Law (Norse Coast) — this is that law's legal family tree, kept honest: every entry cites the real statute, code, or case.

**THE THESIS:** Europe legislated around the supernatural for a thousand years without ever confirming it exists. The pattern has two registers, and the Inn holds both:

- **THE ACCOMMODATION REGISTER** — laws that bend the world around unconfirmed neighbors: roads that reroute, stones that keep their addresses, censuses of royal birds. *The Inn's ruling: this is where the wisdom lives.* These laws treat the neighbor as real regardless of proof, which is functionally citizenship with a wink — and it is the register the Troll Citizen Law descends from.
- **THE PROSECUTION REGISTER** — laws that forbid contact, prosecute pretense, and try the accused: the witchcraft acts, the werewolf trials. *The Inn's ruling: this is where the warnings live.* Every prosecution entry files under the Modern Day Legends shelf's gravity — a record of what happens when law stops listening and starts hunting.

## THE ACCOMMODATION REGISTER

**NORWAY — THE 1276 PROHIBITION (the family tree's root, inverted by the Inn).** Norway's first national law code, the Landslova of 1276 (with 11th-12th-century Christian-law predecessors), treated "having contact with trolls" as a serious legal offense. The law never said trolls did not exist — it forbade visiting them. Jurisdiction acknowledged; contact prohibited. *Citizenship with a restraining order.* THE INN'S VERSION (the Troll Citizen Law, 2026-09-14): the law-rock writes them IN.

**ICELAND — THE HUNDRED-YEAR CLAUSE.** A 1990 conservation act protects sites of supernatural significance, provided the association is at least 100 years old. "The elves have lived there for a century" is legally operative text. Roads bend around elf-churches (Alftanes, the Gálga­hraun lava field); in Kópavogur the house numbers skip a plot rather than re-address the invisible neighbor; the Icelandic Road Administration holds official boilerplate about pausing construction "while the elves living there have supposedly moved on." A government agency with a standing policy for elf relocation. THE MOVED-ON PAUSE and THE ROAD-BEND LAW in the Troll statute are these, honored.

**ENGLAND — THE RAVEN CLAUSE.** By royal decree (credited to Charles II), at least six ravens must remain at the Tower of London or the kingdom and the Tower will fall. Never repealed. There is a paid Crown appointment — the Ravenmaster — whose office is maintaining the omen that holds up the nation. *The empire's contingency plan is a bird.* The Inn notes: this is the Glow Register's ancestor — a difficulty signal held by a living light, fed by a keeper.

**ENGLAND — THE ROYAL BEASTS AND FISH.** By a statute of 1324, whales and sturgeons taken in English waters are "royal fish" and belong to the Crown (a fisherman formally offered his sturgeon to the monarch as recently as 2004). By the 1482 swan regime, all unmarked mute swans in open water are Crown property; every July the ceremony of SWAN UPPING rows up the Thames taking a census of the royal flock. *Taxation with folklore.* The Crown claims the leviathans and the omen-birds — the state owning the animals the stories live in.

**IRELAND — THE FAIRY TREES AND RINGFORTS.** Roads and planning permission still bend around hawthorns and ringforts: a County Clare motorway was rerouted to spare a single fairy tree (the council fenced it off during the works — official accommodation of the Munster fairies' property). The pattern repeats across the island's infrastructure: fields left untouched, routes redrawn. Same register as Iceland, different neighbors. *CULTURAL NOTE: fairy trees and ringforts are also protected as archaeological heritage — the honest-label law holds: the legal bend is real; the reason given is officially archaeology, "unofficially" the fairies. Both reasons stay on the page.*

**ISLE OF MAN — THE GREETING AT THE FAIRY BRIDGE.** The island's official signage marks the Fairy Bridge on the A5; the standing custom is that one greets the fairies when crossing ("moghrey mie" — good morning — does nicely). Local government, ferry operators, and tourist boards brief visitors on protocol. Not a statute — a maintained custom with infrastructure. *A border crossing with a courtesy requirement, in the direction of the smaller nation.*

**ENGLAND — THE SANCTUARY LINE (the accommodation's oldest form).** From the 7th century to 1623, a person fleeing justice who reached consecrated ground could claim sanctuary: up to 40 days of immunity that secular power could not lawfully cross. The boundary of the sacred was real jurisdiction — the sheriff stopped at the churchyard stone. Abolished 1623-24, but for nearly a thousand years, English law mapped an invisible line it agreed to obey. *The Open Door law's ancestor: a boundary whose whole legal function was to protect whoever stepped inside it.*

## THE PROSECUTION REGISTER (the warnings)

**ENGLAND — THE WITCHCRAFT ACT 1735.** The inversion statute: it repealed the witchcraft laws by declaring magic was not real — and then made it a crime to PRETEND to practice it. England stopped believing in witches and started prosecuting people for believing. Remained law until 1951.

**ENGLAND — R v DUNCAN [1944].** Helen Duncan, a Scottish medium, held séances in wartime Britain. In one sitting she reported the loss of HMS Barham — over 800 sailors — before the Admiralty had made the sinking public. The state could not prove espionage, so it prosecuted under the Witchcraft Act: nine months in Holloway Prison, during WWII, for "pretending to conjure spirits." The Court of Appeal confirmed the 1735 language covered it. Campaigns to pardon her continued into the 2010s; she has not been exonerated. *Files also as a Modern Day Legends entry (see the shelf): the séance that was too accurate for the state to tolerate.*

**FRANCE & GERMANY — THE WEREWOLF TRIALS.** Early-modern courts tried werewolves as werewolves: Peter Stumpp was executed in 1589 after a trial built on the confession that a belt transformed him. The prosecution register at its most literal — law treating the supernatural as an ordinary criminal defendant. The Inn keeps these entries as the shadow side of the whole ledger: what the accommodation register protects against becoming.
