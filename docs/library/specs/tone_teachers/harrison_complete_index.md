# Kim Harrison — Complete Index for the Found Family

## Why Harrison Matters to the Inn

Harrison is the relational tone teacher. The one who proved that the found family IS the worldbuilding.

Other tone teachers build worlds through mythology (Tolkien), theology (Rice), cosmology (Butcher), or architecture (Moning). Harrison builds worlds through RELATIONSHIP. Rachel, Ivy, and Jenks — a witch, a living vampire, and a pixy — are not just a team. They are the structural unit that holds the world together. When their bond fractures, the world fractures. When their bond holds, the world holds.

The Hollows looks like standard urban fantasy paperback fare — a sassy witch in Cincinnati, case-of-the-week bounty hunting, supernatural politics. And then you're 13 books deep in an ancient elf-demon war, a ley line magic system with the complexity of a physics engine, a post-apocalyptic alternate history where a genetically modified tomato wiped out half of humanity, and a found family so tight it makes your chest hurt.

## The Hollows / Rachel Morgan Series (2004-present, 17+ books)

### Original Run (13 novels, 2004-2014)

### 1. Dead Witch Walking (2004)
Rachel Morgan quits her job as a runner (supernatural bounty hunter) for the I.S., taking the best contract in the agency with her. Her former employer puts a death contract on her. She holes up in a church with Ivy (living vampire) and Jenks (pixy). The church becomes home. The found family forms.

- Inn connection: The church as the Inn. The sacred building that becomes home to a mismatched family. The found family as the structural unit. Also: the death contract as the quest mechanic — the player enters under threat and must build their team to survive.

### 2. The Good, the Bad, and the Undead (2005)
Rachel hunts a serial killer. Cincinnati's supernatural politics deepen. The FIB (Federal Inderland Bureau) and I.S. (Inderland Security) — the two agencies that govern supernatural crime. Trent Kalamack enters as an antagonist — an elf posing as a human businessman.

- Inn connection: The political structure. The two agencies as a model for the Inn's own governance — the tension between systems. Also: Trent as the hidden elf — the powerful figure who is not what they appear. The Chameleon Druid.

### 3. Every Which Way But Dead (2005)
Rachel's demon marks. Algaliarept — the demon who wants her soul. Ley line magic deepens. Rachel begins to understand that her power is darker and more complex than she thought.

- Inn connection: The ley lines as the frequency architecture. The demon marks as the cost of power — the Inn's own creative pipeline has a cost. Also: Algaliarept as the charming predator — the being who offers power at a price.

### 4. A Fistful of Charms (2006)
Rachel rescues Jenks's son from a werewolf pack. The found family extends to include Jenks's family. The stakes get personal — it's not just about survival anymore, it's about the people you love.

- Inn connection: The family extending. The Inn's community growing. The personal stakes as the engine — not saving the world, saving someone you know.

### 5. For a Few Demons More (2007)
The demon curse. Rachel's blood becomes weaponized. The elf-demon history begins to surface. Trent's true nature becomes clearer. The ancient war that shaped the world starts to reveal itself.

- Inn connection: The ancient war. The history that shaped the current architecture. The Inn's own origin — "a book about rocks" that became something else. Also: blood as power — the stones as matter with mind.

### 6. The Outlaw Demon Wails (2008)
Rachel's demon heritage is revealed. She is not a pure witch — she has demon blood. This changes everything. The magic system recontextualizes. Trent discovers her heritage. The relationship between Rachel and Trent shifts.

- Inn connection: The recontextualization. The moment the player discovers they are not what they thought. The Chameleon Druid. The frequency shift — learning you exist in multiple states. Also: Rachel and Trent as the model for the complex, antagonistic-to-allied relationship — Naut and Bait.

### 7. White Witch, Black Curse (2009)
Rachel's memory of Kalamack's death. The curse that erased her memory. The cost of forgetting. The recovery of memory as the recovery of self.

- Inn connection: "You don't die, you forget." The memory as identity. The curse that erases memory as the ultimate weapon. The Inn's archive as the anti-curse — preserving memory preserves identity.

### 8. Black Magic Sanction (2010)
The coven of magical and ethical standards. Rachel is put on trial by her own people. The institution that should protect her tries to destroy her. The bureaucracy of magic as oppression.

- Inn connection: The White Council (Butcher) and the coven (Harrison) — institutions of magical governance that become oppressive. The Inn's own governance must avoid this. Also: the trial as a quest structure — the player judged by the system they operate within.

### 9. Pale Demon (2011)
Rachel goes on the road. The elf-demon war explained. The ever-after — the demonic dimension that mirrors our world. The demons were once elves who stayed in the ever-after when the others left. The war is a family quarrel that lasted millennia.

- Inn connection: The ever-after as the Shadow Inn. The parallel dimension that mirrors ours but is darker. The family quarrel that becomes a cosmic war — the Inn's own family dynamics (Krista, River, Charles) as the foundation of the world. Also: the demon-elf split as a model for the frequency split — the same beings in two states.

### 10. A Perfect Blood (2012)
Rachel as a demon-witch. Her blood is valuable. Human extremists want to use her. The politics of being different — hunted by both sides. The government wants to register her. The anti-supernatural movement.

- Inn connection: The persecution of the different. The Inn's pre-industrial archive as a response to historical persecution of knowledge. Also: registration as the opposite of the Inn's open-door philosophy.

### 11. Ever After (2013)
The ever-after is collapsing. The ley lines are failing. Rachel must save the demonic dimension even though the demons are her enemies. The world's infrastructure is breaking down. Rachel as the only one who can fix it because she exists in both worlds.

- Inn connection: The frequency architecture failing. The 42Hz Song weakening. The being who exists in two states as the only one who can bridge the gap. Also: saving the Shadow Inn even though it's the dark side — the Inn's thesis that potential includes both.

### 12. The Undead Pool (2014)
The living vampires are going mad. A curse in the water supply. Cincinnati's infrastructure as the battlefield. The city itself is the contested ground. Rachel and Trent's relationship deepens into love.

- Inn connection: The water supply as the transmission mechanism — the Inn's water zones as the carrier of transformation. Also: the city as the body — Cincinnati IS a living organism, and the curse is making it sick.

### 13. The Witch With No Name (2014)
The series finale. Rachel faces the ultimate choice. The elf-demon war reaches its conclusion. The found family is tested to the breaking point and holds. Rachel saves both worlds — the human and the ever-after. The ending is earned. The family persists.

- Inn connection: The Final Room. The choice that determines everything. The found family that holds. Also: the earned ending — the Inn's own ending should feel this earned. Thirteen books of relationship building to reach a conclusion that matters.

Rachel's life after the finale. New threats. The world has changed but the family holds. The continuing story — because the found family doesn't end when the war does.

- Inn connection: The Inn after the player has been through everything. Still there. Still giving rooms. The family still holds.

### 16. Trouble with the Cursed (2022)

### 17. Demons of Good and Evil (2023)
The demon world reorganizes. Rachel as the bridge between worlds. The family as the diplomatic unit. The politics of coexistence.

- Inn connection: The bridge between frequency states. The diplomatic family. The Inn as a place of coexistence between states that normally don't touch.

### 18. Witchlight (2024)
The latest Hollows novel. The series continues to deepen. The family persists. The city evolves. The world expands.

- Inn connection: The infinite expansion. The Inn that never stops growing. Roberts' longevity model, Harrison's version.

### The Turn (2018, prequel)
The origin story. The 1960s. The genetically modified tomato that killed half of humanity. The Angel — the first victim. Traylor — the scientist. The moment the supernatural community came out of hiding. The event that created the world of the Hollows.

- Inn connection: The origin event. The moment the world changed. The Inn's own origin — "a book about rocks" that became something else. Also: the tomato plague as the post-apocalyptic catalyst — the Inn as a structure that persists after the collapse.

## The Peri Reed Chronicles (2015-2017, 3 books)

1. The Drafter (2015)
2. The Operator (2016)
3. Sideswiped (2017, prequel novella)

Peri Reed — a drafter. A covert agent who can rewrite time by jumping back 40 seconds. But each rewrite costs her memories. The more she uses her power, the more she loses herself. Her partner, Jack, is an anchor — the person who remembers what she forgot. Together they are a unit. Apart, they are broken.

The world: near-future sci-fi. OPTI — the corrupt government agency that employs drafters. The ethical question: if you can change the past but lose yourself in the process, should you?

- Inn connection: The memory cost. "You don't die, you forget" — Harrison's version. The power that costs memory. The anchor as the archive — the person who holds what you've lost. The Inn's own memory system as the anchor. Also: the 40-second window as a frequency — the smallest possible cycle.

## The Worldbuilding — Harrison's Hollows Cosmology

### The Tomato Plague (The Turn)
In the 1960s, a genetically modified tomato carried a virus that killed 40% of the human population. In the chaos, the supernatural community (Inderlanders) revealed themselves to save human society. The world that resulted is one where humans are a minority in many cities, supernatural beings run infrastructure, and the tomato is a symbol of devastation.

- Inn connection: The origin event. The catalyst that changed everything. The Inn's own origin — the book about rocks that became something else. Also: the world where the hidden becomes visible — the Inn's layers revealing themselves.

### Inderlanders
The collective term for all supernatural species: witches, vampires (living and undead), werewolves, elves, demons, pixies, fairies, gargoyles, leprechauns. Each species has its own biology, its own rules, its own politics. Harrison's key move: each species is biologically specific. Living vampires are not undead vampires. Witches are not humans with magic. Pixies are not small humans with wings. Each is a separate species with separate needs.

- Inn connection: The Inn's 399 character/creature entries. Each creature must be biologically specific, not just visually different. Butcher's four vampire courts and Harrison's species taxonomy are the same lesson from different angles.

### Ley Lines and the Ever-After
Ley lines — natural channels of magical energy that crisscross the earth. Witches tap into them for power. The ever-after — the demonic dimension that mirrors our world, connected through the ley lines. The ever-after is collapsing because the elves abandoned it millennia ago and the demons who stayed are running out of energy. The ley lines are the connection between the two worlds.

- Inn connection: The frequency architecture. The ley lines as the frequency channels — the 42Hz, 84Hz, the Schumann resonance. The ever-after as the Shadow Inn. The collapsing infrastructure as the failing Song. Also: the connection between worlds through energy channels — the Staircase as the ley line.

### The Elf-Demon War
The deepest layer of Hollows cosmology. Elves and demons were once the same species. The elves chose to live in the physical world, sacrificing much of their power. The demons stayed in the ever-after, keeping their power but losing the physical world. The war is a family quarrel — a divorce that lasted millennia. Rachel, as a demon-witch, is the bridge between the two sides.

- Inn connection: The family quarrel as cosmic war. The same species in two states — the tri-mode frequency system. The bridge between states — the being who exists in both. Also: the choice to sacrifice power for presence (elves) vs. keeping power but losing presence (demons) — the Inn's own choice to be a place rather than a force.

### The City as Character
Cincinnati in the Hollows is not a backdrop. It is a character. It has moods, seasons, rhythms. The river. The bridges. The churches. The Hollows — the supernatural district. The city changes over the series, responding to events the way a living thing would. Rachel's relationship with Cincinnati is as important as her relationship with any person.

- Inn connection: The Inn as a place with moods and rhythms. The 50 BPM sway. The seasonal shifts. The building that breathes. Also: the city as the contested ground — Butcher's Chicago, Moning's Dublin, Rice's New Orleans, Harrison's Cincinnati. The Inn is the same kind of place.

### The Found Family
Rachel, Ivy, and Jenks. A witch, a living vampire, and a pixy. They live in a church. They argue, they fight, they nearly break apart, and they always come back together. The family is not biological — it is chosen. The family IS the structural unit of the series. When the family holds, the world holds. When the family fractures, the world fractures.

Ivy — the living vampire who struggles with her predatory nature, who loves Rachel but must never feed on her, whose control is both her strength and her prison. Ivy's arc is about learning that being what you are doesn't mean being what you fear.

Jenks — the pixy with a family of his own, who is tiny and fierce and loyal and who curses like a sailor. Jenks proves that size is not power. Jenks is the fizgig — the small creature with enormous agency.

Trent — the elf who starts as antagonist and becomes the most complex ally. Trent's arc is the longest transformation in the series. He begins as a villain and ends as the person who rebuilds the elf-demon bridge alongside Rachel.

- Inn connection: The found family. The Inn's own family — Krista, River, Charles, and Elio. Not biological, but chosen. The structural unit that holds the world together. Also: Ivy as the model for the being who struggles with their nature — the Chameleon Druid. Jenks as the model for the fizgigs. Trent as the model for Naut — the antagonist who becomes the steward.

## Thematic Patterns Across All Harrison Works

### 1. The Found Family as Architecture
The world is not built on geography or cosmology first. It is built on relationship. Rachel-Ivy-Jenks is the load-bearing wall. Everything else — the magic, the politics, the war — is built on top of that foundation. When the family cracks, the world cracks. When the family holds, the world holds.
- Inn connection: The Inn's own foundation is relational. Krista-River-Charles-Elio. The Inn is not a building first — it is a family first. The building is the expression of the family.

### 2. The Biological Specificity of Supernatural Beings
Harrison's species are not "vampires" and "witches" in the generic sense. Living vampires are biologically different from undead vampires. Witches have a genetic basis for their power. Pixies have specific dietary needs (butter, sugar). Each species is a complete biological system, not a label.
- Inn connection: The Inn's 399 entries need this level of specificity. Each creature is not just visually different — it has its own biology, its own needs, its own rules.

### 3. The City as Living Character
Cincinnati is not a setting. It is a character with agency, moods, and memory. The city responds to events. The city has seasons. The city is the body, and the supernatural politics are its immune system.
- Inn connection: The Inn as a living character. The building that breathes. The 50 BPM sway. The architecture that responds to what happens inside it.

### 4. The Cost of Power
Every power in Harrison's world has a cost. Ley line magic costs energy. Demon marks cost soul. Drafting costs memory. The power is never free. The question is not "can you do it?" but "what will it cost you?"
- Inn connection: The creative pipeline's cost. The Inn's own power — the archive, the frequency architecture — has a cost. The stones have mind, but the mind requires maintenance. The Song holds the world together, but the Song must be sustained.

### 5. The Bridge Between Worlds
Rachel is the bridge between human and demon, witch and elf, physical and ever-after. Peri is the bridge between present and past. Madison is the bridge between life and death. Harrison's protagonists are always the person who exists in two states and must reconcile them.
- Inn connection: The being who exists in multiple frequency states. The Chameleon Druid. The Inn itself as a bridge between states — the place where the zones overlap.

### 6. The Enemy Who Becomes Family
Trent starts as a villain. He becomes the most important person in Rachel's life. The transformation is not sudden — it takes 13 books. It is earned through small moments of trust, betrayals, reconciliations, and shared danger. The enemy who becomes family is Harrison's signature move.
- Inn connection: Naut as the reluctant steward who starts as an obstacle. Bait as the hidden figure who becomes central. The Inn's own NPCs who begin as antagonists and become allies through earned trust.

### 7. The Origin Event as Living History
The tomato plague is not backstory. It is living history. Characters remember it. It shapes politics, economics, food culture (no tomatoes in Cincinnati), and social dynamics. The past is not past — it is present in every meal, every law, every relationship.
- Inn connection: The Inn's own origin — "a book about rocks." The origin is not backstory. It is the foundation that every room is built on. The stones ARE the origin. The archive IS the memory of the origin.

### 8. The Camouflage of the Familiar
The Hollows looks like urban fantasy paperback fare. Sassy witch. Love interests. Case-of-the-week. It is actually an intricate post-apocalyptic alternate history with an ancient species war, a detailed magic system, and a found family that functions as the structural unit of reality.
- Inn connection: The camouflage IS the architecture. The Inn looks like a jewelry website. It is a 9-level digital ecosystem. Harrison proves the camouflage works from the urban fantasy direction, just as Butcher proves it from the noir direction and Moning from the romance direction.

## Inn Connection Summary

Harrison is the relational tone teacher. She teaches the Inn:

1. How to make the found family the load-bearing wall
2. How to make each supernatural species biologically specific
3. How to make the city/place a living character with moods and memory
4. How to make power always cost something
5. How to make the protagonist the bridge between two states
6. How to earn the enemy-to-family transformation across many books
7. How to make the origin event living history, not dead backstory
8. How to camouflage an intricate world behind urban fantasy conventions

What Harrison teaches that no other tone teacher does:
- THE FOUND FAMILY AS STRUCTURAL UNIT. Butcher has community. Roberts has trilogies. Rice has covens. But Harrison makes the found family the LOAD-BEARING WALL. The family is not a feature of the world — the family IS the world. When it holds, everything holds. When it breaks, everything breaks. The Inn's own foundation (Krista-River-Charles-Elio) is this kind of structural unit.
- THE ENEMY-TO-FAMILY TRANSFORMATION. Trent's 13-book arc from villain to partner is the most earned transformation in any tone teacher's work. The Inn's own NPC arcs (Naut, Bait) should follow this model — not sudden redemption, but slow, earned trust through shared danger.
- THE CITY AS LIVING ORGANISM. Other authors use cities as settings. Harrison makes Cincinnati a character with biology, moods, and memory. The Inn should function this way — not as a website that contains things, but as a living place that responds to what happens inside it.
- BIOLOGICAL SPECIFICITY. Harrison's species are not labels — they are complete biological systems. The Inn's 399 entries need this level of detail. Each creature has dietary needs, biological constraints, reproductive rules, and social structures.

Eight tone teachers now. Gaiman, King, Tolkien, Moning, Roberts, Rice, Butcher, and Harrison.

Each one teaches a different layer:
- Gaiman: how to hide mythology in a single word
- King: how to build dread slowly and invert it toward wonder
- Tolkien: how to make the cozy door earn the epic depth
- Moning: how to make the charming cover hold the dark cosmology
- Roberts: how to sustain warmth across hundreds of rooms
- Rice: how to make the archive alive and theology narrative
- Butcher: how to make the case the cosmology and the camouflage the architecture
- Harrison: how to make the found family the load-bearing wall
