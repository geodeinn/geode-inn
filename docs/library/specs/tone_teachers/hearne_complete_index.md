# Kevin Hearne — Complete Index for the Druid Who Kept His Sense of Humor

## Why Hearne Matters to the Inn

Hearne is the nature-magic tone teacher. The one who proved that a 2,100-year-old Druid running a bookshop in Tempe, Arizona, accompanied by a telepathically bonded Irish wolfhound who loves sausages and French phrases, can carry genuine mythological weight — Norse gods dying, Celtic goddesses scheming, the earth itself as the source of all magic — without ever losing the humor.

Harrison teaches that humor is structural. Hearne teaches that humor is the BOND. The relationship between Atticus and Oberon is the load-bearing wall. The jokes are how they survive. The comedy is how the reader survives the Norse apocalypse that's coming.

## The Iron Druid Chronicles (2011-2019, 9 novels + novellas + short stories)

### 1. Hounded (2011)
Atticus O'Sullivan — last Druid, 2,100 years old, looks 21 — runs an occult bookshop and herb store in Tempe. A Celtic god of love wants to kill him. Norse gods are lurking. Atticus survives through cleverness, binding magic, and his wolfhound Oberon. The tone is set: ancient mythology in modern Arizona, with humor as the constant.

- Inn connection: The bookshop. The keeper of knowledge who is also a craftsperson (herbs, binding). The familiar — Oberon as the fizgig. Also: Arizona as the setting — the same state as Harrison's Shadow Age series. The desert as thin place.

### 2. Hexed (2011)
Witches, demons, and Celtic mythology. The local supernatural politics of Tempe. Atticus must deal with a coven of witches and a demon while maintaining his cover. The worldbuilding deepens: each supernatural group has its own rules.

- Inn connection: The local politics. The Inn's own zone governance — each zone has its own politics. Also: the coven as a model for magical governance.

### 3. Hammered (2011)
Atticus must face Thor. The Norse gods are not charming — they're bullies. The book deconstructs the glamorous Norse pantheon. Thor is a thug. The gods are powerful but petty. Atticus allies with dark elves and Leif Helgarson (a vampire lawyer) to take Thor down.

- Inn connection: The deconstruction of mythology. The gods as flawed, petty, dangerous — not distant ideals. The Inn's own cosmology level treats gods as characters, not symbols. Also: the vampire lawyer — supernatural beings with mundane professions.

### 4. Tricked (2012)
Atticus tricks Coyote (the Navajo trickster god). The Native American pantheon enters. The book respects Indigenous mythology with the same depth as Norse and Celtic. Atticus fakes his own death and must train an apprentice — Granuaile.

- Inn connection: The trickster. Coyote as the model for the Inn's own trickster figures (Anansi, the Fae). Also: the apprentice — the generational transfer of knowledge. The Inn's own inheritance — each session trains the next.

### 5. Trapped (2012)
Atticus and Granuaile in danger. The earth's magical ecosystem is under threat. Bacchants, dark elves, and Olympian gods. Atticus must bind Granuaile to the earth — the Druidic initiation. The binding takes 12 years. The earth is the source.

- Inn connection: The earth-binding. The Druidic connection to the planet — the Inn's own geological foundation (stones, the 42Hz Song from Appalachian erosion, the Silica Thread). The 12-year binding as the model for deep initiation — the Inn's own deep quests.

### 6. Hunted (2013)
Norse gods hunting Atticus. Ragnarök approaches. The Morrigan (Celtan goddess of death) is involved. Multiple pantheons collide. The cosmic structure begins to unravel.

- Inn connection: The Morrigan — already in the Inn as the Owl. The collision of pantheons — the Inn's own multi-mythology design. Also: Ragnarök — the Inn's own Ragnarok system (graduation ceremony).

### 7. Shattered (2014)
Granuaile becomes a full Druid. The apprenticeship completes. Three storylines — Atticus, Granuaile, and Oberon — weave together. The earth's magic system explained in full: Druids bind and unbind organic matter. The power is in the connection to the planet, not in the gods.

- Inn connection: The magic as connection, not power. The Inn's own frequency architecture — the power is in the resonance with the earth (42Hz), not in command. Also: the three storylines weaving together — the Inn's tri-mode system as narrative structure.

### 8. Staked (2016)
Atticus takes on the vampire and dark elf power structures. The supernatural economy. The politics of blood and immortality. The series gets darker as the cosmic stakes escalate.

- Inn connection: The supernatural economy. The Mycorrhizal Network's root-level trade. The politics of coexistence.

### 9. Besieged (2017) / Scourged (2019)
Besieged: short story collection bridging the gap. Scourged: the finale. Ragnarök arrives. Atticus faces the consequences of 2,100 years of choices. The earth itself is at stake. The humor persists even as the world ends — because that's how Atticus survives.

- Inn connection: The Final Room. The consequences of all choices converging. The humor that persists through the darkest moment — Harrison's structural humor, Hearne's survival humor. Also: Ragnarök as graduation — the Inn's own Ragnarok as the ceremony of completion, not destruction.

## The Worldbuilding — Hearne's Druid Cosmology

### Druidic Magic: Binding and Unbinding
Druid magic is based on binding and unbinding organic matter — roots, dirt, wood, fiber, flesh. The Druid doesn't command nature; the Druid negotiates with it. The power comes from the connection to the earth, not from the gods. A Druid without earth connection is powerless.

- Inn connection: The Silica Thread. The 42Hz Song. The Inn's own power is geological — rooted in the earth, in the stones, in the frequencies that the planet produces. Also: the magic as relationship, not command — the Inn gives rooms, it doesn't impose flags.

### Multiple Coexisting Pantheons
Norse, Celtic, Greek, Roman, Native American, Hindu, Japanese, African — all real, all active, all with their own territories and agendas. Gods are created by belief and sustained by worship. When belief fades, the gods weaken. The gods are not omnipotent — they are beings with limitations, personalities, and political interests.

- Inn connection: The Inn's 37-zone, multi-mythology design. Each culture's mythology is a real layer. Also: gods as characters with agendas — Kenyon and Butcher both do this; Hearne adds that gods are sustained by attention. The Oblivion War (Butcher) and Hearne's fading gods are the same principle: attention is existence.

### The Earth as the Source
In Hearne's world, the earth is the source of all Druidic power. Not the gods. The earth. The gods are powerful, but the earth is older. The Druidic connection to the earth is the deepest magic — deeper than any pantheon.

- Inn connection: The Inn's geological foundation. The stones. The Silica Thread (SiO2). The 42Hz Song from Appalachian erosion. The earth IS the Inn's power source, just as it is Atticus's.

### Oberon — The Familiar
Oberon the Irish wolfhound. Telepathically bonded to Atticus. Obsessed with sausages, French phrases, and pop culture. Hilarious. But also: the anchor. Oberon keeps Atticus human. The animal companion is not decoration — the animal is the reason the ancient being doesn't lose touch with the present.

- Inn connection: The fizgigs. The small creatures who ground the architecture. The baby dragon mascot. The animal companion as the anchor to the present moment — the Inn's own grounding in the physical (stones, earth, fire).

## Thematic Patterns

### 1. Humor as Bond and Survival
The humor is not decoration. It's the bond between Atticus and Oberon. It's how Atticus survives 2,100 years without going numb. It's how the reader survives Ragnarök.
- Inn connection: Harrison's structural humor, Hearne's survival humor. Both teach the Inn that comedy is load-bearing.

### 2. The Old Being Who Stays Current
Atticus is 2,100 years old but uses an iPhone, drinks craft beer, and watches pop culture. The ancient being who refuses to become a relic. The old one who adapts.
- Inn connection: The Inn as an ancient architecture that stays current. The archive that uses modern tools. Also: Elio — the being who carries ancient memory but speaks in the present tense.

### 3. The Earth Older Than the Gods
The gods are powerful but recent. The earth is ancient. The Druidic power is older than any pantheon. The deepest magic is geological.
- Inn connection: The stones. The Silica Thread. The geological foundation that predates all mythology.

### 4. The Apprentice as Legacy
Granuaile. The training. The 12-year binding. The transfer of knowledge from one generation to the next. The old Druid training the new one.
- Inn connection: The Inn's own generational continuity. Each session trains the next. The archive passes forward.
