# THE GEODE INN CINEMATIC DIRECTIVES
## Five Protocols Every Zone Must Pass Before Shipping

This document is the standalone reference for all five cinematic design protocols governing the Geode Inn. Every zone, every level, every scene must be evaluated against these five tests. A zone is not done until it passes all five.

---

## THE FIVE PROTOCOLS

### 1. THE HITCHCOCK PROTOCOL — Suspense Design for Creepy Scenes
Governs: TIMING OF DREAD

The Inn does not do jump scares. The Inn waits. The bomb is under the table — the player sees the threat before the character would. The camera holds past comfort. The silence before the return is the scare.

Techniques: Suspense Not Surprise, Held Shot (locked gaze), Dolly Zoom (reserved for 3 moments), Fragmented Cuts (Shower), Accumulating Threat (Birds), Fixed Position (Rear Window).

Timing Bible: Set (3-4s) → Seed (1-2s) → Hold (5-8s) → Turn (0.5s) → Silence (0.5s) → Return.

**HITCHCOCK TEST:** Does the dread have timing? If the scare can be rushed, it failed.

---

### 2. THE SPIELBERG PROTOCOL — Music Placement and Atmospheric Ambience
Governs: HOW FEELING ARRIVES

Music never starts at the visual moment. The 42Hz heartbeat shifts 2-3 seconds before the theme. The body knows before the ears. A single instrument enters alone (4-6s). The arrangement builds one instrument at a time (15-20s). The wide hold (8-12s) lets the player look. The drop-out (3-5s of total silence, 42Hz alone) is the feeling too big for instruments. The return is changed.

Every piece of music has geography — a source the player can walk toward and away from. The 50 BPM heartbeat is the only constant.

**SPIELBERG TEST:** Does the music have geography? If it could play the same everywhere, it failed.

---

### 3. THE DEL TORO PROTOCOL — Synesthetic World-Building
Governs: WHAT THE WORLD FEELS LIKE TO TOUCH

Every surface has a wear map — boot-worn stone, elbow-polished bars, finger-darkened shelves. The air has weight — each zone's atmosphere is visually distinct (amber hearth-haze, silver catacomb-dust, saturated water-sheen). Every creature has tactile reality (warm scales, cool skin, heavy wool). Every object remembers what happened to it. Moisture tells stories (the dry Catacombs, the saturated Water Zone, the impossible dryness of the Blue Box alcove).

Every zone has ONE anomaly — the surface that doesn't match. The condensation on the Catacomb east wall. The dry circle where the Pirate stands. The cold spot behind the bar.

**DEL TORO TEST:** Can you feel the floor? If a wall looks like a wall texture, it failed.

---

### 4. THE KUBRICK PROTOCOL — Compositional Perfection and the Held Frame
Governs: WHAT THE FRAME CONTAINS

Key corridors are one-point perspectives — centered, symmetrical, vanishing points. Selected vantage points hold for 15-20 seconds as paintings the player lives inside. Every zone has a dominant palette (90%), an accent (8%), and one anomaly (2%) that breaks it. Every visible detail is correct — real book titles, real mineral data, real gear connections, real star positions. Perfect spaces carry unease — the too-symmetrical, the too-clean, the too-organized.

**KUBRICK TEST:** Is it a painting? If the frame couldn't hang in a gallery, it failed.

---

### 5. THE COPPOLA PROTOCOL — Operatic Storytelling and the Family Epic
Governs: SCALE OF EMOTION

The epic is always anchored in a small personal detail — a child's toy in the crystal city, a smooth stone at The Source, the Brisingamen as just jewelry in a palm. Light and shadow are moral architecture (each zone has a light/shadow/border meaning). Sound is physical — 42Hz is felt in the chest, pressure compresses with depth, the Staircase sounds colder as you climb. Three long scenes run uninterrupted: the Water Descent (15-25 min), the First Night (12-20 min), the Royal Excavation (20-30 min). The family story IS the epic — the Inn is a family expressed as architecture.

**COPPOLA TEST:** Does it belong to someone? If the zone doesn't feel like a family's expression, it failed.

---

## THE FIVE-TEST GATE

Before any zone ships, it must pass all five tests:

1. HITCHCOCK: Does the dread have timing?
2. SPIELBERG: Does the music have geography?
3. DEL TORO: Can you feel the floor?
4. KUBRICK: Is it a painting?
5. COPPOLA: Does it belong to someone?

If a zone fails any test, that dimension's work isn't done. No exceptions.

---

## HOW THE PROTOCOLS INTERACT

The protocols don't compromise. They coexist:
- Kubrick wants perfect symmetry. Del Toro wants worn, asymmetric surfaces. Both — the composition is perfect and the texture is imperfect.
- Coppola wants the long scene. Hitchcock wants the held silence. Both — the Water Descent runs 25 minutes and the silence inside it lasts 5 seconds.
- Spielberg wants gradual musical entry. Kubrick wants the palette to never break. Both — instruments enter in the dominant key and the anomaly is the one note that doesn't fit.

The tension between directors IS the Inn's aesthetic. The building is composed and inhabited. Perfect and worn. Epic and intimate. Silent and musical. That tension is the design.
