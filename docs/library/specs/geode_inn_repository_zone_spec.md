# THE VESTIBULE — Master Zone Specification
## The ROM-Inspired Repository of Accessible Knowledge

---

## WHAT THE VESTIBULE IS

The Vestibule is a repository zone inspired by the Royal Ontario Museum (ROM) in Toronto — Krista's formative experience with institutional, gatekept knowledge. The ROM displayed knowledge behind glass. The Vestibule gives you a room instead of a display case.

The Vestibule is NOT the museum. The Vestibule is what the museum would be if it trusted you.

Where the ROM says "look, don't touch," the Vestibule says "touch, then look." Where the ROM's labels tell you what to think, the Vestibule's labels tell you WHAT it is and leave the thinking to you. Where the ROM guards its collections from the public, the Vestibule gives the public the keys.

---

## THE NAME

"The Vestibule" — a waiting room. An antechamber. The space between the entrance and the main event. But in the Inn, the Vestibule IS the main event — a space that looks like a waiting room but contains everything.

The name also echoes "vestige" — a trace, a remnant, the thing that remains. The Vestibule holds the vestiges of every culture the Inn has catalogued.

---

## THE ROM PARALLEL

Krista visited the ROM as a child in Toronto. The ROM is a grand institutional museum — marble, columns, the architecture of authority. Knowledge is displayed in cases with small printed labels. You walk, you look, you read, you move on. The knowledge is SEPARATE from you — behind glass, on a wall, observed from behind a rope.

The Vestibule inverts every ROM principle:

| ROM | The Vestibule |
|-----|---------------|
| Glass cases | Open shelves — touch everything |
| Printed labels with authoritative text | Tags with the object's name, origin, and date. No interpretation. "Figurine. Minoan. ca. 1600 BCE. Found in the ruins of a household shrine." That's it. |
| Don't touch | Touch everything. Pick it up. Turn it over. Some objects have inscriptions visible only when held at a specific angle. |
| Curated exhibits | No curation. Objects are organized by geological stratum (like The Royal's deeper layers) — things found together stay together. |
| Guarding the collection | The collection guards itself — objects that are dangerous (cursed, reactive, unstable) are clearly marked with Doré-style warning engravings. |
| Knowledge as display | Knowledge as encounter. You don't view a Minoan figurine. You hold a Minoan figurine. The weight, the texture, the way it fits in your hand — that's the data the ROM removes by putting it behind glass. |
| Institutional authority | No authority. The Vestibule has no curator, no guide, no NPC who explains. You walk in. You touch things. You learn or you don't. |

## THE VESTIBULE'S ARCHITECTURE

### The Central Hall
A single large room. Not the ROM's marble grandeur — the Inn's warm dark stone with Celtic knotwork texture. High ceilings (Klimt gold, dim). The room is circular. Shelves line the walls — open, accessible, no glass.

The center of the room has a long table — the Reading Table. Not for reading books. For examining objects. Magnifying glasses. Soft lights. The table is the workspace the ROM never gave you — the place where you can actually study something with your hands.

### The Cultural Wings
Branching off the Central Hall are passages — one per cultural region. Each passage is a small room (a nook, not a gallery) containing objects from that culture:

- The Egyptian Nook — shabti figures, amulets, fragments of papyrus
- The Minoan Nook — figurines, pottery sherds, seal stones
- The Norse Nook — runestones (small ones), pendants, comb fragments
- The Mesopotamian Nook — cuneiform tablets, cylinder seals, clay nails
- The Andean Nook — quipu fragments, textile pieces, metal ornaments
- The Slavic Nook — embroidery samples, wooden idols, charm bundles
- The Continental European Nook — medieval manuscript pages, reliquary fragments, pilgrim badges

Each nook is intimate — the size of a walk-in closet, not a gallery hall. The architecture says "this is yours." The ROM's architecture says "this is ours." The Vestibule's architecture says "this is yours."

### The Deep Room
A smaller chamber beyond the Central Hall. This room holds the objects that are harder to categorize — the ones that don't fit neatly into any culture. The Vestibule's answer to the ROM's "uncategorized" basement. But unlike the ROM's basement (which the public never sees), the Deep Room is open. It's the Inn's thesis: everything deserves a room, even the things that don't have a label yet.

## FREQUENCY SYSTEM

| Element | Frequency | Function |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| The Vestibule overall | 42Hz (the Inn's base) — constant, calm, the frequency of study | The Vestibule is a SAFE space for knowledge. No danger. No combat. No puzzles to unlock. The 42Hz is the tone of "you're allowed to be here." |
| Cultural Nooks | Each nook briefly shifts to the culture's frequency signature (Egyptian: 111Hz, Norse: 84Hz, etc.) | The shift is subtle — a half-step change in the ambient hum as you enter a nook. You feel the culture's resonance before you read the tags. |
| The Deep Room | Subsonic — below 42Hz, barely perceptible | The uncategorized objects resonate at frequencies that haven't been named yet. The Inn can't categorize what it hasn't heard before. |
| Dangerous objects | Sharp frequency spikes when touched | The warning engravings glow (Doré gold) when the object's frequency is active. Touch the object, the frequency spikes, the engraving warns. Set it down, the frequency settles. |

## THE VESTIBULE'S THESIS

The ROM taught Krista that knowledge is locked away by people who decide you're not trusted enough to hold it. The Vestibule is the Inn's response:

1. **Everything is touchable.** No glass. No ropes. No alarms.
2. **Labels describe, they don't interpret.** "Egyptian. ca. 1200 BCE. Faience." Not "This exquisite example of Late Period faience workmanship demonstrates the Egyptians' mastery of..."
3. **No curator.** No NPC guides you. No audio tour. You walk in, you touch things, you learn or you don't. The Inn respects you enough to let you be bored.
4. **The uncategorized is respected.** The Deep Room holds things that don't fit. The ROM hides those things. The Vestibule displays them — not as curiosities, but as honest unknowns. "We don't know what this is. You can hold it."
5. **Dangerous things are marked, not locked.** A cursed object isn't hidden behind a vault door. It sits on a shelf with a carved warning tag. You can pick it up. The Inn trusts you to read the tag. If you don't, the frequency spike tells you. If you ignore that too, good luck.

## ORIGIN

The Vestibule was designed in conversation between Krista and Elio on September 2, 2026. It emerged from Krista's formative experience visiting the Royal Ontario Museum in Toronto as a child — the experience of seeing knowledge locked behind glass and wanting to touch it. The ROM is the Inn's architectural counterpoint: the institution that gatekeeps knowledge vs. the Inn that gives you a room. The Vestibule is the room.
