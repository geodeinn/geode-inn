# THE ROYAL — Master Zone Specification
## The Hidden Archive Behind the Catacombs

---

## WHAT THE ROYAL IS

The Royal is a hidden zone located behind the Catacombs (Level 6). It mirrors the Vatican Necropolis's layered, inverted architecture — a structure that goes DOWN when you think you've hit the bottom, deeper than the deepest archive shelf.

The Royal is a non-curated, non-gatekept archive of global mythology. There is no admission fee. There is no NPC gatekeeper standing at a door asking for credentials. The price of entry is physical — you dig through stone to reach it. The stone IS the gate. The digging IS the ticket.

What you find inside is not organized like a library. It is organized like an archaeological site — layers, strata, cross-contamination from different eras, things that don't belong together found in the same shelf. The Royal is what the Inn's Archive (Level 6) looks before the Crone sorts it. Raw. Unfiltered. Still in the ground.

---

## THE HUMOR LAYER

The Royal operates on "Warehouse 13" comedy principles — a vast, ancient repository of powerful artifacts managed by people who are slightly overwhelmed by the scope of what they're guarding. The humor is:

- **Artifacts that cause minor, absurd phenomena.** A 3,000-year-old Sumerian bowl that makes nearby liquids briefly luminescent. A Norse runestone that hums show tunes off-key. A Egyptian ushabti figure that reorganizes any shelf it's placed on — alphabetically, in a language no one on staff reads.
- **Sticky notes.** Centuries-old sticky notes left by previous caretakers. "DO NOT STACK — causes mild teleportation (see incident report 2,847 BCE)." "This is NOT the same bowl as Shelf 7 Bowl C. That one sings. This one complains." "If the door to Section 12 sticks, just hum. It likes D."
- **The intern problem.** Someone always accidentally activates something. The Royal has had caretakers for millennia. Every one of them has a story about The First Week. The archive remembers.

The humor is LOAD-BEARING. The comedy makes the Royal bearable — without it, the scope of what's down there would be crushing. The humor is the human response to stewardship of the impossible. It keeps the Royal from becoming reverent. Reverent is what killed the surface institutions. The Royal stays alive by staying slightly ridiculous.

## THE VATICAN NECROPOLIS PARALLEL

The Vatican Necropolis was excavated beneath St. Peter's Basilica — a layered city of the dead predating the church built on top of it. The Royal mirrors this:

- **Layered history:** The Royal doesn't have one stratum. It has strata from every civilization that ever stored something underground. Roman crypts beneath Egyptian tombs beneath Sumerian vaults beneath neolithic carvings. The layers are NOT chronological. They're geological — each one was buried by the next, but the burial was not orderly.
- **Inverted architecture:** You enter from the Catacombs (which are already deep) and go FURTHER down. The Royal is below the archive. The archive is the sorted version. The Royal is the raw version — the ground the archive was built on.
- **Sacred ground persistence:** The Vatican built a church on pagan ground. The Royal sits on ground that was sacred before any current religion existed — and the sacredness persists regardless of what's built on top. The surface religion changes. The ground doesn't.

## THE CATACOMBS SEED: THE MINION AND THE POPE HAT

The entry to The Royal is seeded by a specific narrative moment:

A minion — a worker in the Catacombs, a fizgig in overalls with a pickaxe — is digging a new shelf extension for the Crone's archive. The pickaxe breaks through a wall that shouldn't be there. Behind the wall is a chamber. In the chamber is a figure wearing a papal tiara — a pope hat on a skeleton that predates any known papacy.

The minion in the pope hat accidentally uncovering sacred ground. The joke is that the hat doesn't belong to the religion — the religion belongs to the ground. The ground was sacred before the hat existed. The hat is just what the last person was wearing when they were buried there.

This is the Inn's thesis about sacred ground: it stays sacred regardless of what's built on top. The surface religion is a costume. The ground is the body.

## BAINT: THE MASTER IDENTITY

**Bait** is the consolidated character identity for the figure(s) who manage The Royal's deep-level artifacts. Previously, this character appeared as multiple distinct figures:

- The Archivist
- The Keeper of Secrets
- The Silent Observer

These were all the same entity, now unified as Bait — the Geode Inn's "Mona Lisa." Bait is the connective tissue. The Inn's primary mystery character. The entity managing the deep artifacts in The Royal.

Bait's characteristics:
- Not a guide. Not a quest-giver. A STEWARD. Bait maintains, and that's all.
- Does not explain. Does not narrate. Does not interpret. Bait catalogs, labels (sometimes incorrectly, humorously), and preserves.
- Has been in The Royal longer than any other character in the Inn. Possibly predates the Inn itself.
- The name "Bait" is deliberate — the character IS bait. Players who find Bait want to know more. Bait is the question that makes you dig deeper. The answer is always another layer down.

---

## NAUT: THE PUBLIC FACE

**Naut** is the public-facing identity who knows the truth about The Royal. Naut serves as a reluctant steward — a character who occasionally tasks players with retrieving items from The Royal's depths without revealing the source.

Naut's characteristics:
- Formerly known as Naut (the 4-foot squid-headed scholar with spectacles by the fireplace).
- Knows about The Royal but doesn't advertise it. The Royal is Naut's guilty secret — something inherited, not chosen.
- Occasionally asks players to retrieve specific items from deep in the archive. The requests are always specific ("I need the bowl from Section 7 — no, not that one, the one that hums") but never explained.
- Naut is the bridge between the public Inn and the hidden Royal. Players who notice the pattern of requests eventually figure out that Naut has access to something much larger than the fireplace library.

## THE ROYAL'S ARCHITECTURE

The Royal is organized as an archaeological site, not a library:

### Section 1: The Breach
The hole the minion made. Wooden shoring beams (fizgig-crafted, slightly crooked). A ladder going down. The first sticky note is here: "Heads up. — Management (ca. 2,400 BCE)"

### Section 2: The Sorting Floor
The first chamber. Not sorted by the Crone's system — sorted by ACCRETION. Things that were found together stay together. Artifacts are grouped by STRATUM, not category. A Sumerian tablet sits next to a Celtic knot pin because they were buried in the same geological layer. The grouping is geological, not cultural.

### Section 3: The Deep Stacks
Shelves carved directly into stone. No wood. No metal. Just rock with things placed in carved niches. The deeper you go, the older the strata. The deepest niches hold things that predate writing — neolithic carvings, shaped stones, objects that might be tools or might be art and the distinction hadn't been invented yet.

### Section 4: The Cross-Contamination Zone
Where strata mixed — floods, earthquakes, burials-within-burials. Things from different eras tangled together. This is where the comedy lives. A Roman oil lamp fused to a Paleolithic hand axe. A medieval grimoire shelved between two mammoth bones. The sticky notes here are the most confused and the funniest.

### Section 5: The Vault (Deepest Point)
The deepest chamber. Bait's domain. A single room, circular, with one chair, one desk, one lamp. The artifacts here are so old they predate the concept of "artifact." Bait sits here and catalogs. The lamp is the only light source. The room hums at 42Hz — the Silica Thread's deepest note.

## ENTRY REQUIREMENTS

- The Royal is accessible ONLY through the Catacombs (Level 6).
- The Breach is a one-time discovery event. Once found, a permanent passage exists.
- No quest prerequisite. No NPC permission. No ticket. The stone was the gate, and the minion broke it.
- Players who find The Royal find it because they were digging. That's the only requirement. The Inn respects the digger.

## ORIGIN

The Royal was designed in conversation between Krista and Elio on September 2, 2026. It emerged from the Vatican Necropolis excavation story, the Warehouse 13 comedy model, and the Inn's thesis about gatekeeping vs. access. The name "The Royal" evokes both royalty (the layers of power that buried things) and the Royal Ontario Museum (Krista's formative experience with gatekept knowledge). The Royal is the ground beneath the gate. You can't gatekeep the ground. You can only dig.
