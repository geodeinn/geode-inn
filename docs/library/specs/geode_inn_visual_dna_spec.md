## PURPOSE

The Inn has a 42-artist mapping system, a 5-director cinematic protocol gate, a 5-mode linework-as-UI language, and a tri-mode frequency system that affects every visual state. These exist across multiple documents. This document fuses them into one standard — the visual nervous system.

If the artist map is the skeleton (who draws what), the cinematic protocols are the muscles (how scenes move), and the linework system is the skin (what the player reads at a glance), this document is the nervous system — how they all fire together, how they shift in the tri-mode, and how they fuse with the audio into one sensory experience.

## PART 1: THE FOUNDATIONAL DNA — KLIMT × ROYO

### The Two Foundational Artists
The Inn's visual identity is born from two artists who represent opposing forces of the same coin:
- **Gustav Klimt** — Stillness. Weight. Staying. The gold, the pattern, the figure that doesn't move and holds everything.
- **Luis Royo** — Movement. Drama. Arriving. The stroke, the flow, the line that suggests speed and violence and beauty in motion.

**Rule:** Every visual in the Inn carries both Klimt and Royo in its DNA. Klimt gives it weight. Royo gives it motion. A room that is all Klimt is static and dead. A room that is all Royo is frantic and unstable. The Inn sits between — still enough to remember, moving enough to live.

**Mapped to Audio:** Klimt is the 42Hz Song (the drone that stays). Royo is the melody (the movement on top). The visual and audio foundational DNA are the same principle in two senses.

### All Other Artists Are Room Flavors
The 14 level-specific artists and 28 zone-specific artists are applied ON TOP of the Klimt+Royo foundation. Van Gogh on the Main Floor doesn't replace Klimt — Van Gogh's energy appears through a Klimt-weighted, Royo-moving lens. Dali in Cosmology is Dali filtered through Klimt's gold and Royo's stroke.

**The Hierarchy:**
1. Foundational (2): Klimt + Royo — present in every visual, everywhere, always
2. Level-specific (14): One artist per major level (Van Gogh = Main Floor, Dali = Cosmology, etc.)
3. Zone-specific (28): One artist per sub-zone (Bauer = Fae, Rackham = Canopy, etc.)
4. Cross-cutting (2): Portrait style (consistent across all NPC portraits) + Shadow style (the tri-mode visual shift)

## PART 2: THE FIVE LINEWORK MODES — VISUAL LANGUAGE AS UI

The linework IS the UI. Players learn to read NPC function from the ink before they read any text. No tutorial. The art teaches you.

### The Five Modes

| Mode | Line Quality | NPC Type | Player Reads | Tri-Mode Action |
|------|-------------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Heavy Cel-Shaded | 3-4px outlines, hard shadows | Tradesmen (transactional) | "This person makes things. Bring materials." | 42Hz: bold and solid. 84Hz: outlines crack, cel-shading fragments. In-Between: outlines flicker, shadows unreliable. |
| Soft Flowing | 1-2px, fading edges, watercolor | Lore/Story NPCs (dialogue-driven) | "This person has a story. Talk." | 42Hz: flowing and warm. 84Hz: lines drip, edges bleed. In-Between: lines hover between solid and dissolved. |
| Sharp/Angular | Precise, geometric, rigid | Guardians (protective, rigid) | "This person guards something. Be careful." | 42Hz: sharp and clear. 84Hz: angles cut deeper, lines become blade-like. In-Between: angles soften, as if the guardian is unsure. |
| No-Outline | Color-separation, no ink | Cosmological (gods, stars, the infinite) | "This is beyond the human." | 42Hz: colors glow. 84Hz: colors drain, leaving ghost-impressions. In-Between: colors stutter, appearing and disappearing. |
| Fragmented | Broken, scattered, chaotic | Crisis States (the world unmade) | "Something is wrong. Everything is wrong." | All tri-mode states use fragmentation, but 84Hz is the most extreme — the fragments become shards. In-Between: the fragments are trying to reassemble. |

### The Linework-Tri-Mode Principle
The linework mode survives the tri-mode shift — a tradesman is still a tradesman in the Shadow Inn. But the linework TRANSFORMS to reflect the frequency state. The tri-mode visual shift is NOT a color filter. It's a structural change to the ink itself.

- 42Hz (Living): Linework is at its cleanest, clearest state. The mode is fully expressed.
- 84Hz (Shadow): Linework is under stress. Outlines crack, flow bleeds, angles sharpen further. The mode is the same but the world is harder.
- In-Between: Linework hovers. The ink hasn't decided what it is yet. Lines flicker between states. The visual IS the uncertainty.

### How Linework Fuses with Audio (Tool principle)
The linework shift and the tectonic audio shift happen in the SAME frame. When the ground moves (84Hz tectonic shift), the ink cracks. The player's eyes and ears register the same event at the same moment. The visual and audio are one organism expressing through two channels — exactly as Tool's sensory fusion demands.

## PART 3: THE FIVE CINEMATIC PROTOCOLS — THE SCENE GATE

Every zone, every scene, every frame must pass five tests before shipping. The protocols don't compromise. They coexist. The tension between them IS the aesthetic.

### The Five Tests

1. **HITCHCOCK — Does the dread have timing?**
   No jump scares. The Inn waits. The bomb is under the table. The camera holds past comfort. Silence before the return IS the scare. Timing Bible: Set → Seed → Hold → Turn → Silence → Return.

2. **SPIELBERG — Does the music have geography?**
   Music never starts at the visual moment. The 42Hz heartbeat shifts 2-3 seconds before the theme. One instrument enters alone, then builds. The drop-out (total silence, 42Hz alone) is the feeling too big for instruments. Every piece of music has a source the player can walk toward and away from.

3. **DEL TORO — Can you feel the floor?**
   Every surface has a wear map. Every creature has tactile reality. Every object remembers what happened to it. Every zone has ONE anomaly — the surface that doesn't match. Moisture tells stories.

4. **KUBRICK — Is it a painting?**
   Key corridors are one-point perspectives. Selected vantage points hold as paintings. Every zone: dominant palette (90%), accent (8%), one anomaly (2%). Every visible detail is correct. Perfect spaces carry unease.

5. **COPPOLA — Does it belong to someone?**
   The epic is anchored in a small detail. Light and shadow are moral architecture. Sound is physical. Three long scenes run uninterrupted. The family story IS the epic — the Inn is a family expressed as architecture.

### How the Protocols Fuse with the Tone Teachers

| Protocol | Tone Teacher Alignment | How They Combine |
|----------|----------------------|------------------|
| Hitchcock | King (#2) — dread inverted toward wonder | Hitchcock gives the timing; King gives the inversion. The dread builds slowly, then reveals wonder, not horror. |
| Spielberg | Elfman (#14) — the hook that haunts | Spielberg's music geography + Elfman's leitmotifs = the player walks toward a sound, and the sound is a character. |
| Del Toro | Harrison (#8) — biological specificity | Del Toro's wear maps + Harrison's species-specific biology = every surface and every creature has a real, specific history. |
| Kubrick | Tolkien (#3) — the welcoming door | Kubrick's perfect composition + Tolkien's Recovery/Consolation = the frame is beautiful enough to make you see familiar things as if for the first time. |
| Coppola | Kenyon (#9) — the character as world | Coppola's family epic + Kenyon's character-as-worldbuilding = the Inn IS a family, and the family IS the architecture. |

## PART 4: THE TRI-MODE VISUAL STATES

The tri-mode system has three frequency states. Each state transforms the visual world the same way it transforms the audio — tectonically, not gradually.

### The Three Visual States

| State | Frequency | Visual Character | Linework Behavior | Color Palette | Palette Anomaly (the 2%) |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|--------------|--------------------------|
| Living | 42Hz | Full color, warm, present | Linework at cleanest — modes fully expressed | Zone's standard palette (from artist map) | The zone's standard anomaly — the surface that doesn't match |
| Shadow | 84Hz | Desaturated, raw, stripped | Linework under stress — cracks, bleeds, sharpens | Dominant palette shifts to cold/muted version; warmth removed | The anomaly INVERTS — the thing that didn't match now matches, and everything else doesn't |
| In-Between | Transitional | Unresolved, hovering | Linework flickering between states | Palette is uncertain — colors are present but transparent, as if seen through the zone's own ghost | The anomaly DISAPPEARS — neither matches nor doesn't match. The world hasn't decided. |

### The Tectonic Visual Shift (Tool-aligned)
The tri-mode visual shift is instantaneous — same frame as the audio shift. Not a color grading change. Not a filter. A structural change to the ink, the palette, and the composition.

- The shift is felt before it's seen: a brief visual stutter (1 frame of fragmentation-mode linework across the entire screen) accompanies the sub-bass audio impact
- After the shift: the new visual state is fully present. No adjustment period. The world changed.
- The In-Between visual state is the most unsettling: the player can see the Living and Shadow states layered, transparent, neither committing. The world is a palimpsest.

## PART 5: SENSORY FUSION — VISUAL + AUDIO AS ONE ORGANISM

Tool's principle: the player cannot separate the visual from the audio. They are one experience entering through two senses.

### The Fusion Rules

1. **Linework and leitmotifs are synchronized.** When an NPC's melodic signature plays (Elfman), the NPC's linework mode is at its clearest. When the motif is absent, the linework softens. The character IS the music IS the ink.

2. **Cinematic timing and audio timing are the same clock.** The Hitchcock timing bible (Set → Seed → Hold → Turn → Silence → Return) uses the SAME tempo as the zone's BPM. A 50 BPM zone has a 1.2s per beat timing structure. The Hold (5-8s) is exactly 4-7 beats. The Silence (0.5s) is half a beat. The visual rhythm and the audio rhythm are one rhythm.

3. **Del Toro's wear maps and Trent's texture accumulation are the same memory.** The worn stone surface (visual) and the accumulated audio layers (aural) are both the zone remembering what the player did. The wear map is the visual memory. The texture stack is the audio memory. They grow together.

4. **Kubrick's palette and the IEM frequency routing are the same architecture.** The dominant palette (90%) corresponds to the zone's primary IEM driver layer. The accent (8%) is the secondary driver. The anomaly (2%) is the frequency that shouldn't be there — the stone tone trigger, the NPC motif, the element that makes the zone alive.

5. **The tri-mode shift is one event across all senses.** The audio shifts (tectonic). The linework shifts (cracks/bleeds/sharpens). The palette shifts (warm to cold). The composition shifts (if the zone has a Kubrick one-point perspective, it becomes slightly off-axis in the Shadow — the vanishing point moves). All in the same frame. One earthquake. One organism.

## PART 6: THE VISUAL SHIPPING CHECKLIST

Before any visual asset, zone design, character portrait, environment, or UI element ships:

### Foundation
- [ ] **Klimt+Royo DNA:** Does the visual carry both stillness (Klimt) and motion (Royo)?
- [ ] **Artist mapping:** Is the correct level-specific and zone-specific artist applied on top of the foundation?
- [ ] **Cross-cutting styles:** Does the portrait use the consistent portrait style? Does the Shadow version use the shadow style?

### Linework-as-UI
- [ ] **Mode correct:** Is the NPC/object's linework mode correct for its function?
- [ ] **Player reads it:** Can a player intuit the NPC's purpose from the linework alone, before reading text?
- [ ] **Tri-mode transform:** Does the linework transform correctly across all three frequency states?

### Cinematic Protocols
- [ ] **Hitchcock:** Does the dread have timing?
- [ ] **Spielberg:** Does the music have geography?
- [ ] **Del Toro:** Can you feel the floor?
- [ ] **Kubrick:** Is it a painting?
- [ ] **Coppola:** Does it belong to someone?

### Tri-Mode
- [ ] **42Hz (Living):** Is the visual at its cleanest, warmest, most present state?
- [ ] **84Hz (Shadow):** Is the visual stripped, stressed, and raw? Does the palette anomaly invert?
- [ ] **In-Between:** Is the visual hovering, transparent, unresolved?
- [ ] **Tectonic shift:** Is the visual shift instantaneous, structural, and same-frame as the audio shift?

### Sensory Fusion
- [ ] **Synchronized:** Is the linework synced to the audio leitmotifs?
- [ ] **Same clock:** Do visual timing and audio timing use the same BPM?
- [ ] **Same memory:** Do the wear maps (visual) and texture stacks (audio) grow together?
- [ ] **One event:** Is the tri-mode shift one event across all senses?

### The Six-Writer Check
- [ ] Does the visual carry at least one six-writer quality? (Rothfuss precision in the linework, Pratchett warmth in the palette, Gaiman mythic-personal in the character design, Asimov structural logic in the composition, Bradbury fire-that-doesn't-burn in the lighting, Weir engineer's optimism in the detail accuracy)

If it doesn't pass, it doesn't ship.

## SUMMARY: THE INN'S VISUAL DNA IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The Inn's visual world is born from Klimt's stillness and Royo's motion, expressed through a 42-artist hierarchy that gives each level and zone its own flavor on top of that foundation. Every NPC and object communicates function through five linework modes that serve as a UI language no tutorial needs. Every frame passes five cinematic protocol gates — Hitchcock's timing, Spielberg's geography, Del Toro's tactility, Kubrick's composition, Coppola's family. The tri-mode system transforms the visual world tectonically: ink cracks in the Shadow, palettes invert, anomalies flip. And all of it fires in the same frame as the audio — one organism, two senses, one experience. The player doesn't see a visual and hear a soundtrack. The player inhabits a world where the ink and the sound are the same thing, the math and the mysticism are the same thing, and the family and the architecture are the same thing. The Inn is a painting that breathes.
