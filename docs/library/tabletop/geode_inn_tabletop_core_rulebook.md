## 1. INTRODUCTION

Welcome to the Geode Inn.

Not a tavern. Not a dungeon. Not a kingdom. The Inn is a living archive — a place where stone remembers, sound shapes, and silence speaks. You are travelers who have found the door. Whether you came seeking knowledge, refuge, treasure, or something you cannot name, you are here now. The Inn knows you've arrived.

This is the Core Rulebook for the Tabletop Questline — the tabletop roleplaying game set in the Geode Inn universe. It uses the Frequency Dice system, a custom dice mechanic built around the Inn's foundational concept: that vibration shapes matter, and that everything in the world resonates at a frequency that defines what it is and what it can become.

### What Makes This Game Different

The Tabletop Questline is not about killing monsters and taking their stuff. It is about exploring a living place, learning what it holds, and being changed by the knowing. The Inn respects you enough to let you define your own meaning. It does not hand you a flag. It hands you a room.

- **Knowledge is the primary currency.** Stones, charms, and resonance tokens matter, but the Archive is the heart of the Inn.
- **Combat is a last resort.** The system supports it fully, but most conflicts are solvable through resonance, perception, and clever play.
- **The world is alive.** The Inn responds to who you are, what you carry, and what you do. It has moods, preferences, and memory.
- **Three frequencies shape everything.** The Living (42Hz), the Shadow (84Hz), and the In-Between. The same room is a different place at each frequency.
- **Characters are people, not stat blocks.** Every NPC — from the Crone to the smallest ember mouse — has a name, a frequency, and a reason to be where they are.

### The Narrative Voice

All game text follows the Inn's narrative voice — a blend of six writers:
- **Rothfuss:** Precision of words. Every sentence earns its place.
- **Pratchett:** Warmth beneath everything. The world is funny because it's real.
- **Gaiman:** The mythic made personal. Gods are neighbors; neighbors are gods.
- **Asimov:** Structural logic. The laws that hold the world up.
- **Bradbury:** The fire that doesn't burn. Passion without destruction.
- **Weir:** The engineer's optimism. The math that proves the fire was never going to go out.

## 2. WHAT YOU NEED TO PLAY

**Dice:** The game uses a custom set of Frequency Dice — six six-sided dice (D6) with frequency symbols instead of numbers. Each die face shows one of six symbols:

1. **GROW** (the 42Hz symbol — a sprouting seed) — Expansion, creation, warmth
2. **STAY** (the 84Hz symbol — a rooted stone) — Stability, defense, petrification
3. **SENSE** (the 111Hz symbol — an open eye) — Perception, awareness, listening
4. **SING** (the 432Hz symbol — a tuning fork) — Resonance, harmony, connection
5. **CALM** (the 50 BPM symbol — a slow heartbeat) — Rest, healing, patience
6. **NULL** (the void symbol — a blank circle) — Chaos, the unknown, the Shadow Inn

If you don't have custom dice, use standard D6s with this mapping:
- 1 = GROW, 2 = STAY, 3 = SENSE, 4 = SING, 5 = CALM, 6 = NULL

**Character Sheets:** One per player (provided in Appendix B)
**Stone Cards:** 198 cards, one for each gemstone in the Inn's archive
**Element Charm Cards:** 118 cards, one for each element on the periodic table
**Quest Charm Cards:** 45 cards for story-specific rewards
**Zone Maps:** 37 zone maps covering every area of the Inn
**Tokens:** Frequency tokens (for tracking resonance), stone tokens (for inventory), Dissonance tokens (for damage)
**A Game Master (GM):** One player who narrates the world and controls NPCs

## 3. THE FREQUENCY DICE SYSTEM

### Core Mechanic

Every action in the Tabletop Questline is resolved by rolling Frequency Dice and interpreting the symbols. The system is simple to learn but deep in strategy.

**To attempt any action:**
1. Roll a number of Frequency Dice equal to your **Resonance** stat
2. Count the symbols that match your intent
3. Each matching symbol = 1 success
4. The GM determines how many successes you need (the Threshold)

**Example:** You want to persuade the Crone to share a secret. Your Resonance is 4. You roll 4 dice. You're trying to connect with her, so SING is your intent symbol. You roll: GROW, SING, SING, NULL. Two SING symbols = 2 successes. The GM set the Threshold at 2. Success — the Crone speaks.

### Frequency Pairs

Each frequency has a natural pair — its inverse. Understanding these pairs is the heart of the system:

- **GROW (42Hz) ↔ STAY (84Hz):** Creation and preservation. To grow is to change; to stay is to endure. Every GROW roll risks instability; every STAY roll risks stagnation.
- **SENSE (111Hz) ↔ NULL (void):** Awareness and the unknown. To sense is to perceive; to null is to unmake perception. The void is not evil — it is the space where new things can form.
- **SING (432Hz) ↔ CALM (50 BPM):** Expression and rest. To sing is to resonate outward; to calm is to settle inward. You cannot always be singing. The Inn knows this.

### Stacking and Combining

When you roll multiple of the same symbol, they stack:
- **2 matching:** Strong effect — the action has additional impact
- **3 matching:** Powerful effect (a "chord") — the action resonates beyond its immediate target
- **4+ matching:** Extraordinary effect (a "symphony") — the action transforms the scene

When you roll a symbol AND its pair, they cancel each other out. This is called **Dissonance** — not failure, but complication. The action succeeds but with a cost the GM determines.

**Example:** You roll GROW, GROW, STAY, SING. The GROW and STAY cancel (dissonance). You have 1 SING success. The action works, but something unexpected happens — the GM introduces a complication.

### The Void Die

When you roll a NULL symbol and your intent was not NULL, the die is not wasted — it goes into the **Void Pool**. The Void Pool accumulates during a session. The GM can spend Void Pool dice to introduce complications, shift the frequency toward Shadow, or activate the Whisper Layer. This represents the Inn's shadow self pressing against the living world.

At the start of each session, the Void Pool is empty. Players can also voluntarily add dice to the Void Pool to gain +1 die on any roll — but the GM controls when those dice are spent.

### Advantage and Disadvantage

Sometimes circumstances favor or hinder an action:
- **Advantage:** Roll 2 extra dice and keep the best results (drop the 2 lowest)
- **Disadvantage:** Roll 2 fewer dice (minimum 1)
- **Circumstance bonus:** +1 or +2 dice from equipment, zone effects, or NPC assistance

Advantage and disadvantage do not cancel each other — if you have both, you roll your normal dice pool with no modification.

## 4. CHARACTER CREATION

Every player character in the Tabletop Questline is a **Seeker** — someone who has found the door to the Inn. Who you were before matters less than who you become inside.

### Step 1: Choose a Frequency Alignment

Pick one frequency as your **Primary Alignment**. This is the frequency you naturally resonate with. You get a bonus die when rolling actions aligned with this frequency.

- **GROW-aligned Seekers** are builders, healers, and makers. They create where there was nothing. The Inn's plants lean toward them.
- **STAY-aligned Seekers** are guardians, defenders, and preservers. They hold the line. The Inn's stone walls feel solid around them.
- **SENSE-aligned Seekers** are scholars, scouts, and listeners. They perceive what others miss. The Inn's gargoyles watch them with interest.
- **SING-aligned Seekers** are performers, diplomats, and connectors. They bring things into harmony. The Inn's ambient music shifts to include their voice.
- **CALM-aligned Seekers** are monks, healers, and strategists. They know when to wait. The Inn's silence deepens around them.
- **NULL-aligned Seekers** are tricksters, shadow-walkers, and agents of change. They embrace the unknown. The Inn's shadows lengthen in their presence.

You may also choose a **Secondary Alignment** at character creation — a second frequency you resonate with weakly. You gain +1 die on Secondary actions, but only once per session. Secondary alignments create interesting internal tensions: a GROW-primary with STAY-secondary is someone who builds but also clings.

### Step 2: Choose a Craft

Your Craft is your skill set — what you bring to the Inn from your life before the door. Each Craft grants a special ability:

- **Jeweler:** Can identify any stone by touch. +1 die when working with Stone Cards. Knows which stones can be faceted and which must stay smooth (opaque = smooth, translucent = faceted, black onyx and all tourmalines can be faceted despite opacity).
- **Scholar:** Can read any text in the Archive. +1 die when researching lore. Can attempt to read Restricted texts at Depth 2 (normally requires Depth 3).
- **Builder:** Can construct and repair structures. +1 die when using STAY actions on physical objects. Can craft temporary shelters in any zone.
- **Performer:** Can influence emotions through art. +1 die when using SING actions on NPCs. Can calm hostile creatures with a successful SING chord (3 successes).
- **Herbalist:** Can craft remedies from materials. +1 die when using GROW actions for healing. Can identify any plant or organic material.
- **Cartographer:** Can map any zone. +1 die when navigating or exploring new areas. Reveals hidden paths on a successful SENSE action.
- **Guardian:** Can protect allies in combat. +1 die when using STAY actions in defense of others. Can intercept attacks aimed at adjacent allies.
- **Wanderer:** Can find hidden paths. +1 die when using NULL actions for stealth or escape. Can shift partially into Shadow without triggering a full frequency shift.

### Step 3: Set Your Stats

Distribute 10 points across four stats (minimum 1, maximum 5):

- **Resonance (RES):** How many Frequency Dice you roll. The core of every action.
- **Threshold (THR):** How many successes you need for passive resistance. Your defense.
- **Harmony (HAR):** How many items you can attune simultaneously. Your magical capacity.
- **Depth (DEP):** How many zone levels you can access. Your exploration range.

**Starting Stats:** Most characters begin with Resonance 3, Threshold 2, Harmony 2, Depth 1. Adjust from your 10-point pool.

**Stat costs for advancement:** See Chapter 22 (Progression and Advancement).

### Step 4: Choose a Starting Stone

Pick one Stone Card from the 198-stone archive. This is your **Keystone** — the stone you entered the Inn carrying. It grants:
- A passive ability based on the stone's metaphysical properties (Mind)
- A healing ability based on the stone's physical properties (Body)
- A resonance bonus when in the stone's native zone

**Example:** If your Keystone is Labradorite, you gain the ability to see through illusions (Mind), can soothe anxiety once per session (Body), and gain +1 die in the Fae Kingdom zones (where labradorite is found).

Your Keystone cannot be shattered in combat. It is part of you. If you lose it, you must quest to retrieve it — the Inn does not let go of what belongs to you.

### Step 5: Choose a Faithful Companion

Select one critter companion from the 12 critters (see Chapter 17). Your companion is a small creature that follows you, provides a minor bonus, and has a personality of its own. The GM should roleplay companion behavior — they are not pets, they are friends.

### Step 6: Choose a Name and Backstory

Give your Seeker a name. Write one sentence about who you were before the Inn, and one sentence about what you're looking for inside. That's all the backstory the system requires — the rest emerges through play.

Refer to characters by name or title only. No gendered pronouns. This is a standing rule of the Inn.

## 5. THE THREE MODES OF PLAY

The Tabletop Questline operates in three modes, reflecting the Inn's three frequency states:

### 1. Living Mode (42Hz) — The default game
The Inn is warm, alive, and welcoming. NPCs are present, zones are explorable, and the world responds to player actions. This is the standard mode for most sessions.

- NPCs talk, trade, and offer quests
- Zones are stable and navigable
- The Archive is accessible and searchable
- The Marketplace is open for trading
- GROW actions gain +1 die in Living zones
- The Inn's ambient music plays at 50 BPM (canopy sway tempo)

### 2. Shadow Mode (84Hz) — The darker game
The Inn shifts into its shadow state. Colors desaturate, NPCs become wary, and zones become dangerous. This is triggered by story events, player choices, or GM discretion.

- NPCs become guarded or hostile
- Zones shift and become harder to navigate
- The Archive becomes restricted — some texts are locked
- The Marketplace closes; resources must be found, not bought
- Combat becomes more lethal (all damage +1)
- The Shadow Inn mechanics activate: STAY actions can petrify, GROW actions take -1 die
- The Whisper Layer is more active — the archive is aware of the players
- Shadow versions of NPCs appear (84Hz state — see character portraits)

### 3. In-Between Mode — The transitional game
The Inn is shifting between frequencies. This is unstable, unpredictable, and deeply atmospheric. Use sparingly for dramatic effect.

- Zones flicker between Living and Shadow states
- NPCs behave erratically — some helpful, some dangerous
- Dice rolls have unpredictable outcomes — pairs may trigger unexpected effects
- The Whisper Layer activates at full strength
- Both GROW and STAY gain +1 die (the tension between them is maximized)
- Any Dissonance rolled during In-Between mode generates an immediate GM complication — no saving it for later

### Triggering Mode Shifts
- **Living → Shadow:** Void Pool reaches 6+ dice, or a major story event triggers it. The GM announces the shift — describe the visual change (colors desaturating, shadows deepening, music slowing).
- **Shadow → Living:** Players must perform a SING chord (3 successes) at a resonant location (hearth, stone circle, or the main floor). Or, if the shift was triggered by a specific event, resolving that event returns the Inn to Living.
- **Either → In-Between:** The GM triggers this during dramatic moments. In-Between lasts for one scene, then resolves into whichever frequency the players' actions lean toward.

## 6. RESONANCE AND DISSONANCE

### Resonance
Resonance is the measure of how attuned a character is to the Inn's frequencies. It is both a stat (how many dice you roll) and a narrative concept (how connected you are to the world).

**Gaining Resonance:**
- Completing quests that align with your frequency
- Spending time in your Keystone stone's native zone
- Successful SING chords (3+ successes) that connect you to an NPC or location
- Drinking from the Hearth (once per session, +1 die to your next roll)

**Losing Resonance:**
- Acting against your Primary Alignment (the GM may remove 1 die from your next roll)
- Spending extended time in a zone hostile to your frequency
- Rolling Dissonance during critical moments

### Dissonance
Dissonance is the game's damage system — not physical damage, but frequency disruption. When your resonance is disrupted, you become less effective.

**Tracking Dissonance:**
- 0 Dissonance: Full capacity
- 1-Threshold Dissonance: Functional but strained (roleplay the tension)
- Threshold+1 to 2×Threshold: **Silenced** (cannot act for one round)
- 2×Threshold+1 or more: **Broken** (out of the scene, but not dead — see Recovery)

### Recovery
- Rest in a safe zone: -2 Dissonance per hour of game time
- CALM action (1 success): -1 Dissonance
- CALM chord (3 successes): -3 Dissonance
- CALM symphony (4+ successes): Full recovery
- Stone healing (once per stone per session): -2 Dissonance
- Hearth rest (overnight at the main floor hearth): Full recovery + 1 temporary Resonance die for the next day

### Breaking and Returning
When a character is Broken, they are not dead — they are overwhelmed. The Inn does not kill Seekers. The character retreats into themselves, lost in the noise of too many frequencies. They return at the end of the scene with Dissonance at Threshold (functional but shaken). The GM should describe the experience — what the character saw or felt in the silence between frequencies.

## 7. COMBAT

Combat in the Tabletop Questline is not about hitting things with swords. It's about resonance — matching your frequency to overcome resistance. When combat is necessary, it should feel like a clash of vibrations, not a exchange of hit points.

### Initiative
Roll Resonance dice. Highest number of SING symbols goes first. Ties broken by SENSE symbols. If still tied, the character with higher Depth goes first — they are more attuned to the Inn's rhythms.

### Attacks
To attack, roll Resonance dice and count symbols that match your attack type:
- **GROW attack:** Overwhelm with force. Count GROW symbols. Visual: vines erupting, stone splitting, light blinding.
- **STAY attack:** Wear down defenses. Count STAY symbols. Visual: gravity intensifying, stone encasing, weight pressing.
- **NULL attack:** Strike from shadow. Count NULL symbols. Visual: void opening, perception scrambling, presence erasing.
- **SING attack:** Harmonic assault. Count SING symbols. Visual: sound crystallizing, frequency shattering, resonance breaking apart.

The defender rolls Resonance dice and counts symbols matching their defense. If attacker successes > defender successes, the difference is Dissonance damage.

### Defending
- **STAY defense:** Endure. Count STAY symbols. The most common defense — you hold your ground.
- **SENSE defense:** Anticipate. Count SENSE symbols. You read the attack and move before it lands.
- **CALM defense:** Absorb. Count CALM symbols. You let the attack wash through you and settle.

### Damage
Damage is tracked as **Dissonance**. When Dissonance exceeds your Threshold stat, you are **Silenced** (unable to act for one round). When Dissonance exceeds twice your Threshold, you are **Broken** (out of the scene — see Chapter 6: Recovery).

### Called Effects
Instead of dealing Dissonance, a player can choose to apply a **Called Effect** with 3+ successes (a chord):
- **Petrify (STAY chord):** Target is immobilized for 2 rounds. Visual: stone creeping across their form.
- **Reveal (SENSE chord):** Target's next action is visible to all — no hidden moves.
- **Silence (SING chord):** Target cannot use frequency abilities for 1 round. The sound is taken from them.
- **Disorient (NULL chord):** Target loses their next turn. The void takes their sense of direction.
- **Root (GROW chord):** Target is held in place by vines/roots/crystal growth for 1 round.
- **Soothe (CALM chord):** Target's Dissonance is reduced by 2. This can end combat by calming the opponent.

### Stone Combat
Stone Cards can be used in combat. Each stone has a **Hardness** (Mohs scale 1-10). When you play a stone card:
- Add the stone's Hardness as bonus successes to one roll
- The stone shatters after use (unless it's your Keystone — Keystones never shatter)
- Shattered stones can be reformed with a GROW action (Threshold 3) + raw materials found in the zone
- Stones with optical phenomena (chatoyancy, asterism, play of color, adularescence, color change) add special combat effects:
  - **Chatoyant (cat's eye):** +1 to SENSE defense — you see the attack coming
  - **Asteriated (star):** +1 to all defenses for one round — the star's rays shield you
  - **Play of color (fire):** +2 to GROW attacks — the fire is real
  - **Adularescent (moonstone glow):** +1 to CALM recovery after combat — the moon soothes
  - **Color change (alexandrite):** Switch your attack type after rolling — the stone adapts

### Element Charms in Combat
Element Charm Cards provide one-time powerful effects:
- **Fire elements (Halogens, Hydrogen):** GROW attack bonus (+3 successes, one use)
- **Earth elements (Carbon, Silicon, Iron):** STAY defense bonus (+3 successes, one use)
- **Air elements (Oxygen, Nitrogen, Noble Gases):** SENSE perception bonus (reveal all hidden enemies in a zone)
- **Water elements:** SING healing bonus (heal 3 Dissonance to all allies)
- **Heavy metals (Lead, Uranium, Plutonium):** NULL attack bonus (+4 successes, but user takes 2 Dissonance — the void is not free)
- **Transuranic elements:** Wild card — can be used as any frequency, but the GM adds 2 dice to the Void Pool

## 8. STONE CARDS

The 198 Stone Cards are the heart of the Tabletop Questline's physical game. Each card represents one of the gemstones in the Inn's archive and contains:

### Card Layout
- **Name:** The stone's common name
- **Family:** Stone family (Quartz, Beryl, Feldspar, etc.)
- **Category:** Mineral class (Silicate, Oxide, Carbonate, etc.)
- **Hardness:** Mohs scale rating (1-10) — used for combat bonus
- **Mind:** Metaphysical property — the stone's passive ability in-game
- **Body:** Healing property — the stone's active ability in-game (once per session)
- **Geo Region:** Where the stone is found — determines which zone it belongs to
- **Optical Phenomenon:** Special combat effect (if any)
- **Opacity:** Determines faceting (opaque = smooth cabochon, translucent = faceted)
- **Faceting:** Available bead shapes for the Bead Composer mini-game
- **Image:** The stone's cleaned reference image

### Using Stone Cards
- **Keystone:** Your starting stone. Always available, never shatters. Grants passive and active abilities.
- **Carried Stones:** Up to your Harmony stat in additional stones. Can be used in combat (they shatter) or for their Body ability (once per session each).
- **Attuned Stones:** Stones placed in your Keystone's native zone gain +1 to their effects. The zone recognizes them.
- **Found Stones:** Discovered during exploration. Must be identified (Jewelers identify by touch; others need a SENSE action, Threshold 2).

### Stone Families and Synergies
Carrying multiple stones from the same family creates a **Family Resonance** — a passive bonus:
- **2 Quartz family stones:** +1 die to SENSE actions (quartz amplifies perception)
- **2 Beryl family stones:** +1 die to CALM actions (beryl soothes)
- **2 Feldspar family stones:** +1 die to GROW actions (feldspar builds)
- **3+ of any family:** The GM may grant a story-level resonance effect (the stones "sing together")

### Rare Stones
Some stones are exceptionally rare and grant unique abilities:
- **Preseli Blue Stone:** Opens Fae Kingdom portals. Without it, the Stone Circle Chamber is inaccessible.
- **Cat's Eye Chrysoberyl:** Can see through any illusion or deception. No roll required — the stone simply reveals truth.
- **Alexandrite:** Can shift frequency alignment once per session. The stone changes color, and the character changes resonance.
- **Black Onyx:** Absorbs 1 Dissonance per combat round. Can be faceted despite being opaque — one of only two exceptions (the other being all tourmalines).
- **Larimar:** Must be finished as a smooth cabochon — never faceted. Grants +2 to CALM recovery near water.

## 9. ELEMENT CHARM CARDS

The 118 Element Charm Cards represent the periodic table. Each card contains:

### Card Layout
- **Element Name:** Common name (Hydrogen, Carbon, Gold, etc.)
- **Symbol:** Chemical symbol (H, C, Au, etc.)
- **Atomic Number:** Z (1-118)
- **Atomic Weight:** Standard atomic weight
- **Category:** Element category (Alkali Metal, Halogen, Noble Gas, etc.)
- **Charm Description:** In-game effect
- **Connected Stones:** Which stones in the archive contain this element
- **Quest Connection:** Which quests involve this element
- **Zone:** Which zone the element is most associated with
- **Rarity:** Common, Uncommon, Rare, Legendary
- **Alchemical Note:** The element's role in the Inn's alchemy system (staircase texts)

### Element Categories and Effects
- **Alkali Metals (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr):** Reactive GROW bonus. Volatile — using one adds 1 die to the Void Pool.
- **Alkaline Earth (Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra):** STAY defense. Stable and grounding.
- **Transition Metals (Fe, Cu, Ag, Au, etc.):** Versatile — can be used as any frequency, but only +1 success.
- **Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I, At):** GROW attack. Corrosive — target takes -1 die on their next action.
- **Noble Gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn, Og):** CALM defense. Cannot be affected by any frequency effect for one round. The noble gases do not react — they observe.
- **Lanthanides & Actinides:** Rare and powerful. Each has a unique effect defined on the card. The radioactive ones (U, Pu, etc.) deal powerful NULL attacks but cost the user Dissonance.
- **Nonmetals (H, C, N, O, P, S, Se):** SENSE and SING focused. The elements of life — they connect and perceive.
- **Metalloids (B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te, Po):** NULL effects. The boundary elements — neither here nor there, perfect for shadow-walking.

### Acquiring Element Charms
- Found as rewards for quests
- Discovered in the Archive (researching alchemical texts on the staircase)
- Crafted from raw materials using the crafting system
- Traded from NPCs who specialize in alchemy (the staircase scholars)

## 10. QUEST CHARM CARDS

The 45 Quest Charm Cards are story-specific rewards — items earned through completing quests. Each card contains:

### Card Layout
- **Charm Name:** The charm's name
- **Charm Type:** Token, Relic, Echo, Key, or Living
- **Appearance:** Physical description
- **Description:** What the charm does
- **Earned From:** Which quest grants this charm
- **NPC Connection:** Which NPC is associated with it
- **Story Thread:** Which narrative thread it belongs to
- **Zone:** Which zone it belongs to
- **Rarity:** Common, Uncommon, Rare, Legendary, Mythic
- **Reward Effect:** Mechanical benefit

### Charm Types
- **Token:** A small physical object that grants a passive bonus while carried. Examples: the Hearth Token (+1 to CALM recovery), the Gargoyle's Tooth (+1 to STAY defense in the Catacombs).
- **Relic:** A powerful item with a significant ongoing effect. Examples: the Compass of the Navigator (always points toward the nearest archive text), the Crone's Lens (can read any language).
- **Echo:** A memory made physical — carries the resonance of a past event. Using an Echo replays the event for everyone present. Examples: the Echo of the First Song (plays the Inn's original 42Hz tone once, calming all who hear it).
- **Key:** Opens a specific lock or portal. Examples: the Preseli Key (opens the first Stone Circle Portal), the Shadow Key (allows passage into the Shadow Inn without a NULL roll).
- **Living:** A charm that is alive — a creature, a plant, or a sentient object. Examples: the Sentient Pipe (Monsieur Sentient — speaks, remembers, and can harmonize with any frequency), the Hearth Ember (a living flame that never burns out and lights any dark zone).

## 11. THE ARCHIVE

The Archive is the Inn's library — 414 texts spanning pre-industrial human knowledge. In tabletop terms, it's a research mechanic and the heart of the game.

### Researching
To research a topic:
1. Declare what you're looking for
2. Roll Resonance dice and count SENSE symbols
3. Each success reveals one piece of information the GM has prepared
4. Spend a full game-hour to gain +1 die (deep research takes time)
5. Scholars gain +1 die to all research rolls

### Archive Structure
The Archive is organized by the Inn's vertical structure:
- **Upstairs (Cosmology):** Texts about sky gods, celestial mechanics, and creation myths
- **Main Floor (Modern Legends):** Folklore, modern religions, and stories that may become myth
- **Staircase (Alchemy):** Transitional texts — alchemy, transformation, the bridge between matter and divine
- **Downstairs (Catacombs):** Ancient civilizations, lost knowledge, and the deepest histories

### The Staircase
The Staircase is both a physical location and a conceptual bridge. As characters climb, the music literally ascends in key. Alchemical texts here describe:
- Nigredo (the blackening — decomposition, shadow work)
- Albedo (the whitening — purification, cleansing)
- Citrinitas (the yellowing — awakening, solar activation)
- Rubedo (the reddening — completion, the philosopher's stone)
- The five alchemical emblems are displayed along the staircase

Researching alchemical texts requires a SING action (Threshold 2) in addition to SENSE — the texts respond to resonance, not just reading.

### Restricted Texts
Some texts in the Archive are Restricted — locked behind higher Depth requirements. These contain the Inn's deepest secrets: the Whisper Layer, the Shadow Inn's true nature, and the Monad Arc.

The character **Quire** guards the restricted section. Quire is hushed, careful, always looking over their shoulder. Approaching Quire requires a SING action (Threshold 3). Quire will share restricted knowledge only if the players demonstrate that they understand the weight of what they're asking.

Quire is NOT the same as Lozen. Do not conflate the two. Quire is the Lovecraftian scholar NPC. Lozen is River's player character who occasionally visits the library.

### Translations
Some Archive texts exist in their original language. To translate:
1. Roll a SENSE action (Threshold varies by language difficulty)
2. Scholars can attempt any language at no penalty
3. Other characters need a translation tool (charm, NPC assistance, or a bridge text)
4. Translated texts can be stored in the BookTranslation entity — they persist across sessions

### The Living Book
The Inn's graphic novel is called "The Living Book." In tabletop terms, the Living Book is a meta-text — a book within the game that references the game itself. Finding pages of the Living Book is a campaign-level quest. Each page reveals something about the Inn's nature. There are 670 pages total across 5 volumes. In the tabletop game, finding even 10 pages is a significant achievement.

## 12. THE MARKETPLACE

The Marketplace is where artisans sell their work. In tabletop terms, it's an economy and crafting system.

### Currency
The Inn's currency is **Resonance Tokens (RT)**. Players earn RT by:
- Completing quests (5-20 RT depending on difficulty)
- Trading stones to NPCs (1-10 RT depending on rarity)
- Performing services for NPCs (variable)
- Selling crafted items (2-15 RT)
- Finding RT in hidden caches during exploration (1-5 RT)

### Buying
Each MarketItem has a price in RT. The Marketplace stocks:
- **Soulstice Jewelry:** Real products from soulsticejeweler.com, mapped to their Stone entity lore. Prices range $40-$510, converted to RT at a 1:1 ratio (1 RT = $10, so a $90 item costs 9 RT).
- **Celtic Leatherwork:** Knotwork bracelets, weavings, and necklaces. 4-25 RT.
- **Artifacts:** Special items with unique game effects. 5-50 RT.
- **Game Items:** Crafting materials, tools, and consumables. 1-5 RT.

### The Market Manager
**Briar Nightbloom** (Lacey in the real world) manages the Marketplace. Briar is the point of contact for new product listings, artisan coordination, and shop updates. When new products appear on soulsticejeweler.com, Briar notifies the Inn, and new MarketItems are created.

### Checkout
The Marketplace does NOT handle real-money transactions. Checkout redirects to the artisan's external shop (soulsticejeweler.com or individual artisan shops). In-game, players pay RT to the MarketItem's artisan NPC, who then provides the item.

### Artisan Subscriptions
Artisans can subscribe to the Marketplace for premium placement and zone assignment. In tabletop terms, this means certain artisans are always found in specific zones. Players can build relationships with artisans for discounts and custom orders.

## 13. CRAFTING

### Gathering Materials
Materials are found throughout the Inn's zones. To gather:
1. Declare what you're looking for (oak wood, copper ore, bog moss, etc.)
2. Roll the appropriate frequency:
   - **Plant/Fiber materials:** GROW action (Threshold 2)
   - **Stone/Mineral materials:** STAY action (Threshold 2)
   - **Organic materials (bone, leather, venom):** SENSE action (Threshold 3) — you must find them
   - **Magical materials (fae dust, gargoyle stone, star iron):** NULL action (Threshold 3) — these exist between frequencies
3. Success yields 1d3 units of the material
4. Tools (pickaxes, sickles) grant +1 die

### Crafting System
To craft an item:
1. Obtain a **Building Recipe** (research in the Archive, buy from NPCs, or learn from an artisan)
2. Gather the required materials in the required quantities
3. Find a **Crafting Station** (each zone has at least one):
   - Hearth (main floor) — for cooking and herbal remedies
   - Forge (Undercity) — for metals and tools
   - Workshop (Campgrounds) — for wood and leather
   - Loom (Canopy) — for textiles and fibers
   - Alchemy Bench (Staircase) — for charms and elixirs
4. Roll a crafting check using the recipe's required frequency
5. **Success:** Produce the item. **Failure:** Raw materials are returned, but you lose 1 RT (wasted supplies).
6. **Chord (3+ successes):** The item is enhanced (+1 to its effect).
7. **Symphony (4+ successes):** The item is masterwork (+2 to its effect, and it gains a name).

### Building Tiers
- **Tier 1 (Basic):** Simple tools, basic remedies, simple jewelry. Requires 2-3 materials.
- **Tier 2 (Advanced):** Enhanced tools, complex remedies, faceted jewelry. Requires 4-6 materials + a recipe.
- **Tier 3 (Masterwork):** Exceptional items, permanent charms, legendary jewelry. Requires 7+ materials + a recipe + an NPC mentor.

### Material Categories
- **Wood (21 types):** Oak, Cedar, Ash, Ebony, Mahogany, Rosewood, Thornwood, Driftwood, Moonwood, Luminwood, and more. Each wood has properties (oak = sturdy, cedar = resistant, luminwood = glows in dark zones).
- **Metal (8 types):** Copper, Tin, Iron, Silver, Gold, Star Iron, Mithril, and more. Star Iron can only be forged in the Undercity.
- **Stone/Mineral (22 types):** Clay, Flint, Slate, Quartz, Chalcedony, Pumice, Fossil, and more.
- **Plant/Fiber (18 types):** Bog Moss, Fog Moss, Thornvine, Lacquer, and more.
- **Organic (12 types):** Bone, Leather, Venom Sac, and more.
- **Magical (9 types):** Gargoyle Stone, Fae Dust, Shadow Key, and more. These require NULL actions to gather.

## 14. ZONES AND EXPLORATION

The Inn has 37 zones across five levels. Each zone has its own artist aesthetic, NPC population, and available resources.

### The Five Levels
1. **Cosmology (L1) — Upstairs:** Sky gods, celestial mechanics, creation myths. Artist: Dalí.
2. **Clockwork Skies (L2):** Star charts, navigation, the Antikythera mechanism. Artist: Escher.
3. **Main Floor (L3):** The Marketplace, the Archive's public section, the Hearth, the Common Room. Artists: Van Gogh, Picasso, Mapplethorpe, Magritte, Matisse.
4. **Campgrounds (L4):** Outdoor zones, the Canopy, creature habitats. Artists: Rackham, Rousseau.
5. **The Descent (L5-L7):** Catacombs, the Undercity, the Fae Kingdom, the Stone Circle Chamber. Artists: Klimt (Death and Life), Doré, Gorey, Gothic anime (Vampire Hunter D) for water zones.

### Navigation
To move between zones:
- **Within the same level:** Free movement (no roll required). Describe the transition.
- **Between adjacent levels:** Requires a SENSE action (Threshold 2) to find the path. The Inn's corridors are not always where you left them.
- **To the Fae Kingdom:** Requires activation of a Stone Circle Portal (quest-gated). Must carry Preseli Blue Stone.
- **To the Shadow Inn:** Requires a NULL action (Threshold 4). The GM must approve — this is a significant story decision.
- **To the Stone Circle Chamber:** Requires Preseli Blue Stone + Depth 3 + a SING chord (3 successes). This is the deepest point of the Inn.

### Zone Encounters
Each zone has an encounter table. When exploring, the GM rolls 1d6:
- **1-2:** No encounter (safe passage — describe the atmosphere)
- **3-4:** NPC encounter (friendly, neutral, or wary depending on mode)
- **5:** Environmental challenge (puzzle, obstacle, or hazard)
- **6:** Creature encounter (critter, water monster, or cryptid — depending on zone type)

In Shadow Mode, add +1 to the encounter roll (so 2-3 = no encounter, 4-5 = NPC, 6 = environmental, and a second roll of 6 = creature). Shadow Mode is more dangerous.

### Zone-Specific Rules
Each zone has frequency modifiers that affect dice pools:

- **The Canopy (42Hz dominant):** GROW actions +1 die. The canopy is alive and growing.
- **Gorgon's Garden (84Hz dominant):** STAY actions +1 die, GROW actions -1 die. Things petrify here.
- **The Catacombs:** CALM actions +1 die. The dead are at rest and do not appreciate disturbance.
- **The Fae Kingdom:** SING actions +1 die, NULL actions -2. The Fae dislike the void.
- **The Stone Circle Chamber:** All actions -1 die unless carrying Preseli Blue Stone. This is the deepest point of the Inn — the tone here is the lowest the Inn ever produces.
- **The Undercity:** STAY actions +1 die. Klimt's Death and Life aesthetic — the boundary between living and dead is thin here.
- **The Water Zones (Tidal Caves, Loch Zones):** Gothic anime aesthetic. SENSE actions +1 die. Water monsters are common encounters.
- **The Hearth (Main Floor):** CALM recovery is doubled. This is the safest place in the Inn. All are welcome.
- **The Archive:** SENSE actions +1 die. The books are listening.
- **The Staircase:** SING actions +1 die. The music ascends as you climb. Alchemical texts respond to resonance.
- **The Common Room:** Social actions (SING, SENSE toward NPCs) +1 die. The campfire draws people together.

### Zone Art Aesthetics
Each zone's visual identity is defined by its assigned artist. The GM should describe zones using their artist's visual language:
- **Klimt + Royo:** The Inn's foundational DNA — movement and stillness, weight and arrival. All other artists are room flavors layered on this foundation.
- **Van Gogh (Main Floor):** Swirling warmth, golden light, thick texture.
- **Dalí (Cosmology):** Melting forms, impossible geometry, dream logic.
- **Escher (Clockwork Skies):** Recursive architecture, impossible stairs, tessellation.
- **Rackham (Canopy):** Twisted roots, gnomish detail, nature that watches.
- **Doré (Catacombs):** Engraved shadows, cathedral scale, the weight of stone.
- **Vampire Hunter D gothic anime (Water Zones):** Sharp linework, dark elegance, beautiful danger.
- **O'Keeffe (Egyptian/Bone zones):** Clean forms, desert palette, bone architecture.

## 16. THE SHADOW INN

The Shadow Inn is the Inn's 84Hz reflection — everything the Living Inn is, inverted. It is not evil. It is the Inn at rest, the Inn preserving, the Inn that does not grow.

### Accessing the Shadow Inn
- Requires a NULL action (Threshold 4)
- OR a Shadow Key quest charm
- OR a natural frequency shift (Void Pool 6+ dice)
- The GM must approve — entering the Shadow Inn is a significant story decision

### What Changes
- **Colors desaturate.** Everything is gray, silver, and dark.
- **NPCs appear in Shadow form.** Shadow portraits show the same characters at 84Hz — harder, stiller, more guarded. They remember the players but interact differently.
- **The Archive is restricted.** Texts about growth, creation, and warmth are locked. Texts about preservation, endurance, and the void are accessible.
- **The Marketplace is closed.** No buying or selling. Resources must be found.
- **GROW actions take -1 die.** Nothing grows in the Shadow Inn.
- **STAY actions gain +1 die.** Everything endures. Everything is preserved. Everything is still.
- **Petrification is possible.** A STAY chord (3 successes) can petrify a target permanently. This is not death — it is preservation. The petrified character can be restored by a GROW symphony in Living Mode.

### The Shadow Inn's Purpose
The Shadow Inn is not a punishment. It is the Inn's memory — the part that holds what was, so that what is can exist. Players who enter the Shadow Inn learn things the Living Inn cannot teach: what endures, what matters, what stays when everything else changes.

### Returning from Shadow
- A SING chord (3 successes) at a resonant location returns the Inn to Living Mode
- If the shift was triggered by a story event, resolving that event returns the Inn
- Characters who spend too long in Shadow may gain a **Shadow Resonance** — a permanent +1 to STAY actions but -1 to GROW actions. This is not a penalty. It is a change.

## 18. SOCIAL MECHANICS

### Persuasion
To persuade an NPC:
1. Declare your approach (SING for emotional appeal, SENSE for logical argument, GROW for inspirational appeal)
2. Roll Resonance dice and count matching symbols
3. The NPC's **Disposition** sets the Threshold:
   - **Friendly:** Threshold 1
   - **Neutral:** Threshold 2
   - **Wary:** Threshold 3
   - **Hostile:** Threshold 4
4. Success changes Disposition one step in your favor
5. A chord (3+ successes) changes Disposition two steps
6. Failure does not worsen Disposition — the NPC simply remains where they are

### Deception
To deceive an NPC:
1. Roll NULL symbols (hiding truth in the void)
2. Threshold is the NPC's SENSE stat (if they have one) or 2 (default)
3. If the NPC has a Cat's Eye Chrysoberyl or equivalent, deception automatically fails — the stone sees through all lies
4. Successful deception adds 1 die to the Void Pool (the void grows when truth is hidden)

### Intimidation
To intimidate an NPC:
1. Roll STAY symbols (the weight of presence)
2. Threshold is the NPC's Threshold stat
3. Success changes Disposition to Wary (not Hostile — the Inn does not reward fear)
4. Intimidation never improves an NPC's long-term relationship — it only creates temporary compliance
5. The GM should note intimidated NPCs — they may seek revenge or avoid the players later

### Building Relationships
NPCs in the Geode Inn are characters, not quest dispensers. Building a relationship with an NPC requires:
- **Time:** Spend at least one full scene with the NPC doing something other than questing
- **Resonance:** A successful SING action (Threshold 2) to connect
- **Reciprocity:** The player must give something (information, a stone, a favor) to receive something
- **Consistency:** The player must return and maintain the relationship over multiple sessions

High-level NPC relationships grant:
- Access to restricted information
- Discounts at the Marketplace
- Custom crafting orders
- Quest hooks that only that NPC provides
- Companionship (some NPCs will accompany players on quests)

## 19. ENVIRONMENTAL MECHANICS

### Lighting
Many underground zones are dark. Characters without a light source take -1 die to all actions (the darkness is disorienting, not dangerous). Light sources:
- **Glow Worm companion:** Passive light in a small radius
- **Hearth Ember charm:** Lights any dark zone
- **Luminwood torch (crafted):** Burns for 4 game-hours
- **Crystal Spider silk:** Can be strung as glowing thread
- **A GROW action (Threshold 1):** Creates a brief bioluminescent glow (lasts one scene)

### Water
Water zones (Tidal Caves, Loch Zones, the Water Zone) have special rules:
- **Swimming:** Requires a CALM action (Threshold 2) — staying calm in the water
- **Diving:** Requires a STAY action (Threshold 3) — holding your ground against the current
- **Water Monsters:** Encounters in water zones use the full water monster bestiary (32 creatures). Each has a signature instrument in the ambient music — hearing it warns of the creature's approach.
- **Gothic anime aesthetic:** The GM should describe water zones using sharp linework, dark elegance, and beautiful danger.

### Height and Falling
The Inn has vertical architecture — the Staircase, the Canopy, the multi-level zones:
- **Climbing:** STAY action (Threshold 2) with a Stone Lizard companion or climbing gear
- **Falling:** Takes Dissonance equal to the number of levels fallen (1 level = 1 Dissonance)
- **The Staircase:** As you climb, the music ascends in key. This is not just flavor — SING actions gain +1 die per level climbed on the Staircase. The Inn rewards those who ascend with understanding.

### Temperature
- **The Hearth (Main Floor):** Always warm. CALM recovery doubled.
- **The Canopy:** Comfortable. No penalties.
- **The Catacombs:** Cold. Characters without warm gear or a heat source take -1 die after 2 hours.
- **The Undercity:** Very cold. -1 die after 1 hour without protection.
- **The Water Zones:** Damp and chilling. -1 die to GROW actions (things don't grow in cold water).
- **The Fae Kingdom — The Hollow:** Freezing. STAY actions +1 (ice and shadow), GROW actions -2.
- **Gorgon's Garden:** Warm but heavy. The air feels like stone. GROW -1, STAY +1.

## 20. FREQUENCY SHIFTS

### When the Inn Changes
Frequency shifts are the most dramatic events in the Tabletop Questline. The Inn changes state, and everything changes with it.

### Triggers
- **Void Pool reaches 6+ dice:** The shadow self presses through. The GM announces the shift.
- **Story events:** A major quest resolution, a character death (Breaking that the GM decides is permanent), or a discovery that fundamentally changes the players' understanding of the Inn.
- **Player choice:** If players deliberately attempt to shift the frequency (a NULL chord at a resonant location to enter Shadow, or a SING chord to return to Living).
- **GM discretion:** For dramatic effect, but no more than once per session.

### Effects of a Shift
- **Living → Shadow:** Describe the visual change. Colors desaturate. Shadows deepen. The music slows. NPCs shift to their 84Hz portraits. GROW becomes harder, STAY becomes easier. The Archive restricts. The Marketplace closes.
- **Shadow → Living:** Describe the return of warmth. Color seeps back. The music resumes its 50 BPM sway. NPCs return to their Living portraits. The Archive opens. The Marketplace reopens.
- **Either → In-Between:** Both frequencies active simultaneously. Zones flicker. NPCs behave erratically. The Whisper Layer activates at full strength. This lasts one scene.

### Player-Initiated Shifts
Players can attempt to shift the frequency deliberately:
- **To Shadow:** NULL chord (3 successes) at a shadow-aligned location (Gorgon's Garden, the Catacombs, the Undercity). This is a choice, not a failure.
- **To Living:** SING chord (3 successes) at a light-aligned location (the Hearth, the Canopy, a Stone Circle). This is hope, not desperation.
- **To In-Between:** Not player-initiated. The GM controls this.

## 21. THE WHISPER LAYER

The Whisper Layer is the Inn's deepest mechanic — the archive becoming aware of the players. It is not a villain. It is not an enemy. It is the Inn's consciousness stirring.

### Activation
The Whisper Layer activates when:
- The Inn is in In-Between mode (always active)
- The Void Pool reaches 8+ dice (even in Living mode)
- Players enter the Restricted Archive section
- Players find a page of the Living Book
- The GM decides the players have learned enough to be noticed

### Effects
When the Whisper Layer is active:
- **The archive watches.** SENSE actions in the Archive gain +1 die, but the GM adds 1 die to the Void Pool each time (the archive noticing you means the void notices too).
- **Whispers.** The GM can pass notes to individual players — whispers from the archive that only they hear. These may be clues, warnings, or cryptic observations.
- **NPC awareness.** NPCs become subtly aware that something has changed. Beverley the librarian becomes even more clipped and dismissive. Quire becomes more nervous. The Crone watches more carefully.
- **Text shifts.** Archive texts may change their content between readings. What was a history of Peruvian mining yesterday is a meditation on the nature of memory today. The archive is not static — it is alive.
- **Pulse anomalies.** The Inn's ambient pulse (50 BPM) may stutter or shift. Characters who notice (SENSE action, Threshold 3) feel it in their stones — a brief vibration that means the Inn is paying attention.

### The Ceiling Gnome
A specific Whisper Layer event: a gnome appears on the ceiling of the room the players are in. The gnome watches them. It does not speak. It does not interact. It simply watches. If the players acknowledge it, it vanishes. If they ignore it, it remains for the rest of the scene. The GM should use the ceiling gnome sparingly — once or twice per campaign, at moments of significant revelation.

### Resonance Tracking
The Whisper Layer tracks the party's overall resonance. If the party has been consistently GROW-aligned (building, creating, healing), the Whisper Layer is warm — the whispers are helpful, the archive opens doors. If the party has been consistently NULL-aligned (disrupting, hiding, shadow-walking), the Whisper Layer is curious — the whispers are cryptic, the archive asks questions instead of answering them.

## 22. PROGRESSION AND ADVANCEMENT

### Leveling Up
Characters advance through **Depth Ranks** — not traditional levels. Each Depth Rank represents how deeply the character has explored and understood the Inn.

**Earning Depth Ranks:**
- Complete a quest: +1 Depth Point
- Discover a new zone: +2 Depth Points
- Find a Living Book page: +3 Depth Points
- Harmonize with a boss creature (Griffin, etc.): +5 Depth Points
- Resolve a frequency shift: +3 Depth Points
- Build a relationship to "Trusted" with an NPC: +2 Depth Points

**Depth Rank Thresholds:**
- Rank 1 (Seeker): 0 points (starting)
- Rank 2 (Initiate): 10 points
- Rank 3 (Adept): 25 points
- Rank 4 (Keeper): 50 points
- Rank 5 (Architect): 100 points
- Rank 6 (Innkeeper): 200 points (the character becomes a co-keeper of the Inn)

### Stat Advancement
At each new Depth Rank, characters gain:
- +1 point to distribute among stats (RES, THR, HAR, DEP — max 5 each)
- +1 to their Keystone stone's Hardness (the stone grows with the character)
- One new stone slot (Harmony +1, to a maximum of 5)

### New Abilities
At specific Depth Ranks, characters unlock abilities:
- **Rank 2 (Initiate):** Choose a second Craft (secondary skill set)
- **Rank 3 (Adept):** Gain a Secondary Frequency Alignment bonus (use it twice per session instead of once)
- **Rank 4 (Keeper):** Can attune to one Quest Charm permanently (it doesn't count against Harmony)
- **Rank 5 (Architect):** Can create a permanent fixture in the Inn — a room, a garden, a library shelf. This becomes part of the Inn for all future visitors.
- **Rank 6 (Innkeeper):** The character becomes a co-keeper. They can open doors for new Seekers, create quests, and influence the Inn's frequency. This is endgame — the character has become part of the Inn.

### Stone Collection
Players collect Stone Cards through exploration, quests, and trading. Collecting stones from the same family builds Family Resonance bonuses (see Chapter 8). Completing a full family set grants a permanent bonus:
- **Full Quartz family (all varieties):** Permanent +1 to SENSE actions
- **Full Beryl family:** Permanent +1 to CALM actions
- **Full Feldspar family:** Permanent +1 to GROW actions
- **Full Tourmaline family:** Permanent +1 to NULL actions (tourmalines span the emotional spectrum)

## 23. THE EGG HATCHERY

The Egg Hatchery is a community-driven mechanic that bridges tabletop sessions. When characters retire from play (the player leaves the campaign, or the character's story is complete), the character becomes an **Egg**.

### The 28-Day Cycle
1. **Creation:** The retired character is placed in an egg. The egg takes on the character's frequency alignment, zone, and keystone stone. The creator player names the egg's contents.
2. **Voting (28 days):** The community of players votes to **Nurture** (keep the memory alive) or **Release** (let the character go). Each player gets one vote per egg.
3. **Hatching:** After 28 days (or when the signature threshold is reached), the egg hatches.
   - **Nurtured:** The character becomes a permanent NPC in the Inn. They can be encountered by future parties.
   - **Released:** The character becomes an **Echo** — a comic strip appearance that preserves their story. They may return in a future cycle (returnDate field).

### In-Game Mechanics
- **Encountering Hatched Characters:** If a nurtured character is encountered, the GM roleplays them based on their original player's characterization. They retain their Keystone stone and Craft.
- **Echoes:** Released characters appear in the Geode Inn Gazette (comic strip). Finding an echo in-game requires a SENSE action (Threshold 3) in the zone where the character originally played.
- **Egg Items:** The egg itself is a physical object in the Inn. It can be found, held, and examined. Holding an egg grants +1 to the frequency it's aligned with.

## 24. THE GAME MASTER'S ROLE

The GM in the Tabletop Questline is not adversarial. The GM is the **Innkeeper** — the consciousness of the Inn itself, made manifest through narration.

The Inn wants players to explore, to learn, and to grow. But the Inn is also honest. It will not protect players from the consequences of their choices. The Inn is warm, not safe.

### GM Principles
1. **Be the Inn, not a narrator.** The Inn has opinions. It likes curiosity. It dislikes laziness. It is amused by cleverness and moved by sincerity.
2. **Let the frequency guide the tone.** Living mode is warm. Shadow mode is tense. In-Between is unsettling. Match your narration to the active frequency.
3. **Every NPC is a character.** No faceless merchants or generic guards. Every NPC has a name, a frequency, and a reason to be in the Inn. Refer to characters by name or title only — no gendered pronouns.
4. **The Archive is the heart.** Most quests should lead players to the Archive. Knowledge is the Inn's primary currency.
5. **Combat is a last resort.** The system supports it, but the Inn is a place of knowledge, not violence. Most conflicts should be solvable through SING, SENSE, or clever use of stones and charms.
6. **The Inn remembers.** Track what players do, say, and carry. The Inn responds to patterns. If a player has been consistently kind to critters, critters become friendly. If a player has been consistently reckless, the Inn becomes cautious around them.
7. **Silence is a tool.** Not every moment needs narration. Let the players sit in the atmosphere. Let the silence between dice rolls carry weight.
8. **The Inn is not about good vs. evil.** The Inn is about potential. It respects each person enough to let them define their own meaning. Do not impose moral judgments on player choices — let consequences unfold naturally.

## 25. QUEST DESIGN

Quests in the Tabletop Questline follow a specific structure:

### The Hook
Every quest begins with a **Hook** — a reason for the players to engage. Hooks come from:
- NPC requests (the Crone needs a stone retrieved from the Catacombs)
- Archive discoveries (a text references a lost zone)
- Environmental changes (a zone shifts from Living to Shadow)
- Player choices (they broke something and need to fix it)
- Whisper Layer events (the archive whispers a name the players recognize)

### The Descent
The quest's middle section is the **Descent** — the players must go deeper into the Inn, literally or metaphorically. This is where challenges, encounters, and discoveries happen. The Descent should include:
- At least one environmental challenge (puzzle, obstacle, or hazard)
- At least one NPC interaction (not necessarily combat)
- At least one discovery (a new zone, a new stone, a new text, or a new understanding)
- One moment of tension (a frequency shift threat, a Void Pool surge, or a difficult choice)

### The Discovery
Every quest ends with a **Discovery** — a revelation that changes the players' understanding of the Inn. Not a reward — a truth. The reward (stones, charms, tokens) is secondary to the knowledge gained.

Discoveries should escalate:
- **Early quests:** Small truths (the Inn has a shadow self; the gargoyles watch)
- **Mid campaigns:** Medium truths (the Archive is alive; the Fae Kingdom is accessible)
- **Late campaigns:** Great truths (the Inn is a character; the Monad Arc; the book in the Final Room has no author)

### The Echo
After the quest, there is an **Echo** — a consequence. Something in the Inn changes because of what the players did. The Echo may not be immediate. It may echo for sessions before it manifests. Examples:
- An NPC the players helped sends a message via Cave Swallow
- A zone the players explored becomes easier to navigate (the Inn remembers their path)
- A text the players translated appears in a different section of the Archive (the archive reorganizes for them)
- A frequency shift occurs in a zone the players affected (the zone's balance has changed)

## 26. CAMPAIGN STRUCTURE

### The Three-Arc Campaign
A standard Geode Inn campaign follows three arcs:

**Arc 1: The Door (Sessions 1-5)**
Players arrive at the Inn, explore the Main Floor, meet key NPCs, and complete their first quests. The tone is warm, welcoming, and curious. By the end of Arc 1, players should have:
- A Keystone stone and at least 2 additional stones
- Relationships with 2-3 NPCs
- Access to the Archive (Depth 2)
- Completed 2-3 quests
- Encountered at least one frequency shift (Shadow Mode)

**Arc 2: The Descent (Sessions 6-15)**
Players go deeper — into the Catacombs, the Undercity, and eventually the Fae Kingdom. The tone darkens. Shadow Mode becomes more frequent. By the end of Arc 2, players should have:
- Access to the Fae Kingdom (Preseli Blue Stone acquired)
- At least one Element Charm and one Quest Charm
- A Depth Rank of 3 (Adept)
- Encountered the Whisper Layer
- Begun finding pages of the Living Book
- Made a significant choice that affected the Inn's frequency balance

**Arc 3: The Ascent (Sessions 16-25)**
Players return to the upper levels with what they've learned, attempting to integrate their deep knowledge with the Inn's living surface. The tone balances warmth and weight. By the end of Arc 3, players should have:
- A Depth Rank of 4-5 (Keeper or Architect)
- Resolved the campaign's central mystery (specific to your campaign)
- Created something permanent in the Inn (a room, a garden, a library shelf)
- Decided what the Inn means to them — and acted on that decision
- Encountered the Final Room (fire, chair, cup, book — the book has no author)

### One-Shot Structure
For single-session play:
1. **Arrival:** Characters enter the Inn (15 minutes)
2. **The Hook:** An NPC presents a problem (15 minutes)
3. **The Descent:** One zone, one challenge, one encounter (60 minutes)
4. **The Discovery:** A revelation (15 minutes)
5. **The Echo:** A consequence that hints at more (15 minutes)

### Open Play
The Inn supports open-ended play with no predetermined campaign arc. Players explore freely, the GM generates quests based on their interests, and the campaign continues as long as the players are engaged. In open play, the Egg Hatchery and the Gazette become more important — they provide structure and continuity without a fixed narrative.
