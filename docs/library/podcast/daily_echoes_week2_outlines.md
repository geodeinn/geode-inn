# Daily Echoes — Week 2 Episode Outlines

## Episode 6: "The Cartographer's Mistake"
**Duration:** 15 minutes | **Zone:** The World Map (z36) | **Artist:** O'Keeffe/Monet blend

The Cartographer has been mapping the Inn since before the Archive existed. Every corridor, every zone, every hidden door — drawn by hand in ink that smells of iron. But the Cartographer has a secret: one zone on the map has no corresponding room. It's been there since the first draft. A zone that was drawn but never built. Or was it built and then hidden? Or did it build itself in response to being mapped?

The episode explores the idea that a map creates the territory it describes — that the act of recording something gives it permission to exist. The Cartographer's mistake (the extra zone) becomes the most important feature of the map. It's the space where anything could happen. It's the blank page.

**Audio:** Wind across parchment. Quill scratching. The faint hum of 42Hz beneath the map's surface, as if the ink itself carries frequency. The unmapped zone has no sound — a hole in the audio field that the listener's ear is drawn to.

**Inn Mode DSP:** Room Profile 3 (Staircase) — the ascending pitch, because a map is always trying to go higher, to see more.

## Episode 7: "Waystone and the Long Recovery"
**Duration:** 15 minutes | **Zone:** Camelot (unnumbered, downstairs extension) | **Artist:** Wyeth

Waystone sits in the Camelot zone, which is not a recreation of Camelot but the place where knights go when they can no longer ride. The zone is a long hall with cots and firelight and the sound of breathing — not dying breathing, but healing breathing. The kind of breathing that happens when someone has finally stopped trying to be strong.

The episode is about recovery as a heroic act. Waystone doesn't fight. Waystone doesn't quest. Waystone sits with people while they heal, and that sitting is the hardest thing anyone in the Inn does. The name comes from the Waystone Inn in Rothfuss — the place where the story is told. Every recovery is a story being told slowly, one breath at a time.

**Audio:** 50 BPM breathing rhythm. Fire crackle at low volume. No music — just breath and fire. When someone heals enough to speak, a single note of 42Hz returns to the soundscape. The zone's audio is defined by what returns, not what's there.

**Inn Mode DSP:** Room Profile 1 (Main Floor) — the warmth of the fire, the sense of belonging even in brokenness.

## Episode 8: "The Siren and the Autoimmune War"
**Duration:** 15 minutes | **Zone:** The Siren's Coast (z33) | **Artist:** Waterhouse

Andra — The Siren — lives on the coast where the water meets the stone. Andra's body attacks itself. The immune system, designed to protect, has turned. The Siren's song is not luring sailors to their doom — it's the sound of a body at war with itself, the frequency of self-destruction mistaken for self-preservation.

The episode reframes autoimmune disease as a civil war inside a country that doesn't know it's fighting itself. The Siren doesn't want to sing the destructive song. The Siren's body sings it without permission. The most terrifying part: the defense system is doing exactly what it was designed to do. It just identified the wrong threat.

**Audio:** Two voices — one melodic (the true self), one dissonant (the immune response). They overlap and clash. The 42Hz Song tries to establish itself but keeps being interrupted by sharp 84Hz bursts. The listener experiences what autoimmune feels like from the inside: the constant interruption of peace by the body's own alarms.

**Inn Mode DSP:** Room Profile 7 (Shadow Inn) — the reversed reverb, the sense that something is coming from the wrong direction.

## Episode 9: "The Pirate and the Caregiver's Compass"
**Duration:** 15 minutes | **Zone:** The Campgrounds (z10) | **Artist:** Bierstadt/Carr

Zee — The Pirate — navigates not by stars but by the needs of others. The Pirate's compass doesn't point north; it points toward whoever needs help most. This episode is about caregiving as navigation — the way a caregiver's entire orientation shifts to center on the person they're caring for, and how that shift, while heroic, can also cause the caregiver to lose their own coordinates.

Zee sits by the campfire and talks about the night the compass broke — the moment when the person being cared for was gone and the compass kept spinning, pointing at everything and nothing. The Pirate who could navigate any storm couldn't navigate an empty room.

**Audio:** Campfire sounds. Wind in trees. The compass clicking — a metronomic tick at 50 BPM that becomes erratic when the compass breaks, then slows to silence. When Zee finds a new direction (helping another traveler), the tick returns, steady and sure.

**Inn Mode DSP:** Room Profile 5 (The Loch) — the water reflection, the sense of looking at yourself through someone else's surface.

## Episode 10: "The Weaver and the Thread That Remembers"
**Duration:** 15 minutes | **Zone:** The Workshop (z11) | **Artist:** Wyeth

The Weaver works in the Workshop alongside the Smiths, but while they forge and hammer, the Weaver threads. DNA is a thread. Memory is a thread. Every story in the Inn is a thread that connects to every other story. The Weaver's loom doesn't make cloth — it makes connections visible.

This episode is about the thread that connects Krista's brain surgeries to the Inn's existence. The scar tissue on the left frontal lobe is a thread. The 25 years of medication is a thread. The chaos that replaced the fog is a thread. The Weaver pulls one thread and the whole tapestry shifts — because every thread is connected to every other thread, and pulling one is the same as pulling all of them.

The Weaver's secret: the thread that remembers is not the thread that was woven. The thread that remembers is the space between threads — the gap where the needle passed through. Memory lives in the holes, not the thread.

**Audio:** Loom clacking rhythm (50 BPM). Thread pulling — a sustained high frequency that shifts pitch as different threads are touched. The 42Hz Song is present as a bass drone — the foundation thread. When the Weaver pulls the scar tissue thread, the drone wavers, then resolves into a new pattern — the pattern that became the Inn.

**Inn Mode DSP:** Room Profile 2 (Archive) — the long dark cathedral reverb, because the Weaver's work is about deep time and long memory.

## Episode 11: "The Tuesday Incident" (Part 2)
**Duration:** 45 minutes (Friday Deep Dive) | **Zone:** All zones (the gargoyles are connected) | **Artist:** All (the incident touched everything)

The Tuesday Incident continues. In Episode 5, we established that the six gargoyles tuned to something below the Inn. In this deep dive, we follow what happened next.

On Wednesday, the gargoyles were silent. Not their normal silence — the active silence of something listening. By Thursday, the 111Hz frequency they'd been humming had shifted to 111.3Hz — a fraction of a tone higher. By Friday, the Archive's books began to migrate. Not physically. Their positions on the shelves shifted by millimeters, as if the books themselves were leaning toward whatever the gargoyles had heard.

The Inn has a heartbeat, and the heartbeat skipped.

This episode explores the idea that the Inn is not a static structure but a living system with its own nervous system — the gargoyles being the nerve endings. When they detected something below the foundation, the entire Inn responded. The question: what is below the Inn? And should the Inn's residents be worried, or curious, or both?

The episode ends with a new sound appearing in the Whisper Layer: a single note at 7.83Hz — the Schumann resonance. The Earth itself, answering the gargoyles' call.

**Audio:** Full soundscape tour. We move through all 7 DSP room profiles as the incident ripples through the Inn. The 111Hz gargoyle frequency is the throughline — it appears in every room, subtly modified by each room's profile. The 7.83Hz Schumann note appears only at the very end, felt not heard, in the bones.

**Inn Mode DSP:** All 7 profiles, sequentially. The episode is a guided tour of the Inn's acoustic architecture, using the Tuesday Incident as the narrative thread that connects every room.
