## COLD OPEN (0:00 - 2:00)

*[Audio: A sistrum — the ancient Egyptian rattle — shaking once, twice. The sound is bright and strange, like a tambourine that decided to be something else. Then: purring. Not a cat's purr — the vibration of a room that happens to have a cat in it.]*

**NARRATOR:**
A cat's purr vibrates between 25 and 50 Hertz.

This is not folklore. This is veterinary science. Cats purr at frequencies that promote bone growth, reduce inflammation, and accelerate healing. The research is real. You can look it up. Cats, without knowing it, without meaning to, without any of the intention that humans require before they'll believe something matters, are healing machines. They vibrate at the frequency of repair.

The Inn knows this. The Inn has always known this. The Inn built a game around it.

*[Audio: The sistrum shakes again. The purring drops to 42Hz — the Inn's foundational tone. The two sounds merge.]*

Welcome to Bastet's Herding.

## STORY (2:00 - 12:00)

*[Audio: Egyptian zone room profile — warm, dry, with a reverb that suggests sandstone walls. O'Keeffe palette: clean forms, desert air. A single hand drum at 50 BPM.]*

**NARRATOR:**
Bastet's Herding is one of the Inn's strangest games, which is saying something for a building that contains a reverse-Jaws simulator and a Doki Doki Literature Club episode set in a spacecraft.

The premise: cats. A basket. A sistrum. That's it.

The cats are archetypes — every cat personality you've ever met, distilled into a game mechanic. The Aloof Cat, who walks in the opposite direction of the sistrum's pulse. The Curious Cat, who approaches but won't enter. The Lazy Cat, who is already near the basket but refuses to move without three pulses in succession. The Feral Cat, who must be guided in a spiral — approach, retreat, approach, retreat, each pass closer. The Queen, who will only enter the basket if no other cat is watching.

The sistrum is the player's only tool. Shaking it produces a pulse — a wave of vibration that cats respond to based on their archetype. The pulse has a frequency. The player controls the frequency. Too high and the cats scatter. Too low and they fall asleep. The trick is finding each cat's resonant frequency — the specific vibration that makes that particular cat want to move toward the basket.

*[Audio: Sistrum pulses at different frequencies. A cat meows. The pulse adjusts. The meow becomes a purr.]*

This is, when you think about it, exactly what the Inn does. The Inn doesn't push. The Inn doesn't force. The Inn resonates. The Inn finds the frequency that makes you want to move in a particular direction, and then it sustains that frequency until you do. Not through coercion — through invitation. The Inn doesn't herd cats. The Inn is a cat herder.

The reward for completing Bastet's Herding is Cat's Eye Chrysoberyl — the stone that sees through all illusions. This is not a random reward. The Inn gives Cat's Eye to cat herders because cat herding teaches you the one thing you need to see through illusions: patience. The willingness to approach, retreat, approach again, each pass closer. The willingness to let the cat come to you.

Illusions, like cats, cannot be forced. They can only be seen through — and seeing through requires the same skill as herding: finding the resonant frequency. What vibration, applied gently and consistently, makes the illusion thin enough to see through? What frequency makes the mask slip?

*[Audio: 42Hz tone. A purr — definitely 42Hz now, not 25. The cat has tuned itself to the Inn.]*

Bastet presides over the game, which means Bastet watches. Bastet is a chibi NPC — small, golden, with the head of a cat and the body of a figure that radiates the specific warmth of a sun that has been worshipped for six thousand years and is not tired of it. Bastet does not speak. Bastet purrs. The purr is the game's score — as the player guides more cats into the basket, Bastet's purr grows louder, deeper, more resonant. At full resonance — all cats collected — Bastet's purr drops to 42Hz, and the room vibrates, and the Cat's Eye Chrysoberyl materializes in the basket, and Bastet blinks slowly, which is cat for "I see you. You may stay."

The sistrum itself has a history. The sistrum is one of the oldest instruments in the world — a percussion instrument sacred to Hathor and Bastet, used in temple rituals to shake away evil spirits. The evil spirits, as it turns out, were frequency disruptions — sounds that disturbed the temple's resonant harmony. The sistrum didn't drive spirits away. It restored the baseline. It shook the room back into tune.

This is what the Inn's games do. Every game, at its core, is a tuning exercise. Standing Stones (Tetris) tunes spatial reasoning. Tomb Tunnel (Dig Dug) tunes excavation patience. Monster of the Deep (reverse Jaws) tunes empathy — you are the monster, and the game asks you to understand what it's like to be the thing everyone fears. Bastet's Herding tunes attentiveness. The frequency of attention.

*[Audio: The sistrum resolves into a warm chord. The purr fades. A single chime — Cat's Eye Chrysoberyl, hardness 8.5, frequency mapped from Mohs.]*

There's a postscript to the game that most players miss. If you play Bastet's Herding in Shadow Mode — if the Inn has shifted to 84Hz — the cats don't come to the basket. They come to you. One by one. They sit around you. They don't enter the basket. They just sit. The sistrum doesn't work. No frequency you try moves them toward the basket. They move toward you instead.

Bastet's purr in Shadow Mode is lower — 84Hz — and it doesn't sound like contentment. It sounds like comfort. The cats are comforting you. Because in Shadow Mode, the Inn is cold and still, and the cats have decided that if you can't herd them, they will sit with you until the frequency shifts back.

You don't get the Cat's Eye in Shadow Mode. You get something else. You get the understanding that some games can't be won when the frequency is wrong, and that's not failure — that's the Inn telling you to rest.

*[Audio: 84Hz purr. Slow. Deep. Then: the 50 BPM heartbeat, warming the tone back toward 42Hz.]*

## ECHO (12:00 - 15:00)

*[Audio: 42Hz returns. The sistrum, gentle now. A distant purr.]*

**NARRATOR:**
Cats vibrate at the frequency of healing. The Inn built a game around that fact. But the game isn't really about cats, and it isn't really about baskets.

It's about finding the frequency that makes things come to you.

You've experienced this. You tried to force something — a relationship, a project, an idea — and it scattered like a cat hearing a wrong note. You stopped forcing. You started listening. You found the frequency. And the thing came to you on its own, because it wanted to, because you'd made it comfortable enough to choose you.

The Inn's lesson is simple and annoying: you cannot force what you want. You can only resonate at the frequency that invites it. And you can only find that frequency by paying attention to what's already vibrating.

Here's your echo: what are you trying to force right now? What would happen if you stopped shaking the sistrum and started listening for the purr?

Bastet would blink slowly at you. That's cat for: "You're learning."

*[Audio: A slow blink sound — a soft, warm chime. 50 BPM heartbeat. Fade.]*
