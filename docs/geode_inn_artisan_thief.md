# Geode Inn — The Artisan Thief
## Character Profile — The Selkie Who Chose the Bench

## Origin Note

The Artisan Thief is Krista's player character and one of the five starting classes based on her real family. The class was originally defined as: nimble fingers, low charisma — the jeweler who makes beautiful things and can't sell them. Now the character deepens: she is a selkie whose human form resembles a feminine depiction of the Sandman (Netflix series).

## The Selkie

### What She Is

A selkie. A seal who sheds her skin to walk on land in human form. In Celtic and Norse folklore, selkies are gentle, beautiful, and fundamentally wild. They come ashore to dance, to love, to breathe — but the sea is always calling. Selkie stories are always about the tension between two worlds: the world of the land (where things are made, where love is felt, where beauty is crafted) and the world of the sea (where the selkie is free, weightless, formless, home).

The Artisan Thief is a selkie who *chose* the land. Not trapped — not the selkie whose skin was stolen, not the selkie who longs for the sea and weeps on the shore. She chose. She came ashore, she shed her skin, she picked up tools, and she made something. And the making was so good she stayed.

But the sea doesn't stop calling because you stop listening. The sea is patient. The sea is older than the land. And the selkie who chose the bench still hears it — in the sound of water running over stones, in the clink of tools that sounds like waves on a rocky shore, in the deep hum of the Song of Making that sounds, when you're very quiet, like the ocean heard from very far away.

### The Skin

The selkie's skin is her most precious possession. In folklore, if the skin is stolen, the selkie is trapped on land. If she finds it, she returns to the sea and never comes back.

The Artisan Thief's skin is her *jeweler's apron.* The leather she wears at the bench. The thing she puts on when she works and takes off when she's done. The apron is the skin — the thing that lets her transform from human-shaped jeweler into something older and wilder. When she ties the apron, the seal slides off and the maker slides on. When she unties it, the maker slides off and the seal slides on.

Nobody touches the apron. Nobody. The Crone once reached for it — to straighten it, motherly instinct — and the selkie's eyes went black and cold and ancient for one second, and the Crone, who is afraid of nothing, took a step back. The Crone never mentioned it. The selkie never apologized. They understand each other. The Crone's glasses are her skin. The apron is the selkie's. Some things are not shared.

### The Dual World

The selkie belongs to two worlds:
- **The land** — the market, the bench, the tools, the stones, the making. The world where things are solid and shaped and sold. The world where the Artisan Thief makes her living and can't sell it.
- **The sea** — the underwater traversal layer, the catacombs, Charon's river, the water zones. The world where the selkie is free and weightless and wild. The world where the Artian Thief doesn't need charisma because the sea doesn't require performance. The sea requires *being.*

This connects directly to the Inn's architecture: the main floor (land, market, making) and the catacombs/water layers (sea, freedom, wildness). The Artisan Thief is the only character who moves between both worlds as naturally as breathing. She walks the market in human form, hands working, stones shaping. She dives into the catacombs and the underwater zones and the seal slides on and the water remembers her.

The other characters see this. The Host sees a woman who is sometimes restless and sometimes calm and the calm is not peace — it's the calm of something that has chosen to be still. The Catalyst sees a woman who grows things from stone the way the Catalyst grows things from seeds and the parallel is not lost on either of them. The Crone sees a woman who is precise with her hands and wild in her eyes and the combination is the Crone's favorite kind of person — dangerous and careful at the same time.

## The Human Form — Feminine Sandman

### Visual Design

The Netflix Sandman (Tom Sturridge as Morpheus): pale, angular, sharp cheekbones, dark hair, dark eyes, gothic elegance, androgynous beauty, the face of something ancient looking out through a young mask. The stillness of a being who has been shaping dreams for millennia.

The feminine version — the Artisan Thief's human form:
- **Face:** Angular, sharp-boned, pale. High cheekbones that catch light like facets on a cut stone. A jaw that could have been carved from marble. Dark eyes — not warm, not cold, *deep.* The eyes of someone who has seen the bottom of the ocean and the inside of a gemstone and found them similar.
- **Hair:** Dark, long, often tied back when working. Loose when not. The hair when loose has a weight to it — not flowing, not airy. *Heavy.* Like something that has been in salt water and dried. The hair of a swimmer.
- **Body:** Lean, precise, the hands doing most of the talking. The body is still — the Sandman stillness, the selkie stillness, the stillness of a being that doesn't waste movement. When she works, the hands move and nothing else does. The hands are fast, precise, and unsettling in their competence.
- **Presence:** Low charisma. Not because she's unattractive — she's striking, the way a cliff face is striking. Low charisma because she doesn't *perform.* The Sandman doesn't charm. The Sandman *is.* The selkie doesn't sell. The selkie *makes.* The gap between making and selling is where the Artisan Thief lives, and the gap is wide, and she does not cross it.
- **Aesthetic:** Dark, gothic, practical. Working clothes, not costume. The apron. The tools. The stone dust on her fingers. She wears silver — her own work — but never gold. Gold is for the sun. Silver is for the moon and the sea. The selkie wears the metal of her element.

### The Sandman Quality

The Sandman's defining quality is *weight.* Dream/Morpheus carries the weight of every dream ever dreamed. He is ancient, precise, and unflinching. He does not perform. He does not charm. He simply *is* — and the being is enough.

The feminine Sandman — the Artisan Thief — carries the same weight, but hers is the weight of *matter.* She carries the weight of stones. She knows what every stone weighs, what every stone means, what every stone can do. She shapes the weightless into the weighted — dreams into metal, meaning into metal, the Song of Making into a pendant that hums at 42Hz when you hold it to your ear.

The Sandman connection to the Inn:
- Dream's ruby is one of the most powerful objects in the Sandman universe — a jewel that shapes reality. The Artisan Thief is a jeweler. She understands that stones are not decoration. Stones are *power.* The right stone in the right setting can change the world. The wrong stone in the wrong hands can kill. (Malachite in the morning tea.)
- Dream's helm is made from the spine of a dead god. The Artisan Thief works with materials that have their own ancient life — stones formed over millions of years, metal pulled from the earth. She respects the materials because the materials are older than she is. Older than the sea.
- Dream is the lord of the space between waking and sleep. The selkie is the lord of the space between land and sea. Both are beings of *liminality* — the threshold, the in-between, the place where two worlds touch.

### The Androgynous Edge

The Sandman's beauty is androgynous — not male, not female, something between and beyond. The feminine version keeps the edge: she is beautiful in a way that doesn't resolve. Not pretty. Not handsome. *Striking.* The kind of face that makes people stare and then look away because the staring feels like trespassing. The kind of beauty that is uncomfortable because it doesn't ask for attention and doesn't reward it.

This matters for the character: the Artisan Thief doesn't use her appearance. She doesn't charm. She doesn't flirt. She doesn't perform femininity or abandon it. She simply *exists* in her skin, and the existence is enough to unsettle people, and the unsettling is not her problem.

## The Class — Artisan Thief

### Stats (from the Festival questline family design)

- **High Dexterity:** The hands. The nimble fingers. The jeweler's precision. She can pick a lock as easily as she sets a stone.
- **High Intelligence:** She knows her stones. She knows her metals. She knows which combinations are beautiful and which are lethal. She knows malachite is copper carbonate and toxic when ground and ingested. She knows this the way some people know recipes.
- **Low Charisma:** She can't sell her work. She can't pitch. She can't perform. The selkie doesn't charm — the selkie *is.* And being is not the same as selling.
- **Medium Strength:** Not a fighter. Not weak. The strength of someone who works with her hands for a living — the specific, practiced strength of a maker.
- **Low Constitution:** The selkie's vulnerability. She chose the land, and the land is hard. The air is dry. The sea is far. Her endurance is not what it was in the water.

### The Dual Skill Set — Beauty and Danger

The Artisan Thief is the only class that makes *beautiful things* and *dangerous things* with the same hands, the same tools, the same bench.

**Beauty:**
- Stone setting, metalwork, design. The jewelry that Soulstice sells. The pieces in the marketplace.
- Each piece is a game object with in-game effects — the Aurora Pendant reveals Fae paths, the Bluestone Key opens portals. The Artisan Thief makes things that work.
- She makes the truth-teller's ring. Someday. When she's ready.

**Danger:**
- Ground malachite in the morning tea. Copper carbonate. Toxic when ingested. The Artisan Thief knows this because she knows her stones the way a chef knows her knives.
- The "theft" in Artisan Thief isn't about stealing — it's about *redistribution.* The selkie's wild sense of justice. The selkie who comes ashore and sees humans hoarding and the seal inside her says: that's not how the sea works. In the sea, everything flows. In the sea, what you hoard sinks you.
- She doesn't fight. She *adjusts.* The way a current adjusts the position of things on the ocean floor. Quietly. Precisely. Without asking permission.

### The Special Abilities

**Appraise:** The Artisan Thief can read any item in the marketplace. Other players see the game description. The Artisan Thief sees the game description AND the real description AND the stone composition AND the maker's technique AND the flaws. She sees what the item *is,* not just what it says it is. The Crone taught her to read this way. The Crone is the only person the selkie respects without reservation.

**Identify:** The Artisan Thief can identify stones by touch. Blindfolded. In the dark. In the water. The weight, the temperature, the texture — every stone has a fingerprint and she reads it. This is the selkie's sea-sense applied to land-things. The sea reads through current and pressure and temperature. The Artisan Thief reads through her hands.

**Forge:** The Artisan Thief can combine stones and metals to create new items. The crafting system. She takes raw materials and makes them into game objects with effects. The Bluestone Key was forged. The Aurora Pendant was forged. Everything in the marketplace that is Soulstice was forged by her hands.

**Dive:** The Artisan Thief can access the underwater traversal layer. The selkie's skin — the apron — transforms, and she enters the water zones that other classes can't reach without special equipment. In the water, her stats shift: Dexterity increases, Constitution increases, Charisma doesn't matter because the sea doesn't care about charisma. In the water, she is home.

## The Character Relationships

### The Crone (Krista's Mom)
The Artisan Thief respects the Crone without reservation. The Crone taught her to appraise. The Crone taught her precision. The Crone's one-word praise — "Better" — is the only external validation the selkie trusts. The Crone sees the selkie's wildness and does not flinch. The Crone reached for the apron once and saw the black eyes and stepped back and never mentioned it. The Crone understands that some things are not shared. The Crone is land. The selkie is sea. They love each other across the element.

### The Bard (Krista's Dad)
The Bard is the selkie's father. He is loud, warm, romantic — everything the selkie is not. He sells things. He charms people. He does the thing the selkie cannot do. The selkie watches him perform and feels the gap between making and selling and the gap is the space where her father lives and she does not. She loves him across the gap. She never crosses it.

### The Catalyst (The Bartender)
The Catalyst and the Artisan Thief are mirrors. The Catalyst grows things from seeds. The Artisan Thief shapes things from stone. Both are makers. Both are wild underneath the performance (the Catalyst performs chaos; the selkie performs stillness). Both have a secret garden/garden of their own. They don't talk much. They work in the same silence. Occasionally, the Catalyst leaves a seedpod on the selkie's bench. Occasionally, the selkie leaves a finished pendant in the Catalyst's bar. Neither acknowledges the exchange. Both understand it.

### The Host (Patrick Stewart)
The Host sees the selkie and sees the sea. He is the anchor. She is the tide. He holds the space and she moves through it. He never asks her to stay. He never asks her to go. He is the shore. The shore doesn't hold the sea. The shore is *there* — constant, present, unchanged — and the sea comes and goes and the being-there is the shore's whole function. The Host is the shore.

### Charon
The selkie and Charon have an understanding. She is a creature of water. He is a creature of the water. She dives the catacombs and crosses his river without paying, because she is already of the river. Charon doesn't charge the river. The river doesn't charge itself. When they pass, there is a nod. The nod is the recognition of two beings who belong to the same element and have chosen different relationships with it. Charon chose to carry. The selkie chose to make. The water is the same.

## The Deeper Lore

### Why She Chose the Land

The selkie chose the land because of a stone.

In the sea, there are no stones that *hold light.* There are stones — the ocean floor is stone — but they are wet and dark and heavy and the light that reaches them is blue and dim and borrowed. The selkie was diving one day and found, in the shallows, a stone that had fallen from a cliff. A sunstone. A piece of labradorite. And when the light hit it, the stone *held* the light. Caught it. Kept it. Made it permanent in a way the sea had never shown her.

The selkie held the stone and felt something the sea had never given her: *permanence.* The sea is movement. The sea is change. The sea is the thing that never stops. The stone was the thing that never moves. The stone was still. The stone was *there.*

She came ashore to find more stones. She stayed because the making was the most beautiful thing she had ever done. The making was the sea's opposite — where the sea shapes through erosion, through the slow wearing away, the maker shapes through addition, through the slow building up. The selkie who had spent millennia being shaped by the sea chose to become the thing that shapes.

She never regretted it. She never stopped hearing the sea.

### The Sandman Connection — Dream and Matter

The Sandman (Dream of the Endless) is the lord of dreams — the place where things are imagined before they are made. The Artisan Thief is the lord of *making* — the place where imagined things become solid.

Dream and the Artisan Thief are two halves of the same function: Dream imagines, the Artisan Thief makes. Dream is the concept. The Artisan Thief is the execution. Dream is the sketch. The Artisan Thief is the finished piece.

The selkie's connection to the Sandman is not a casting reference — it's a *functional* connection. The Inn exists because someone dreamed it. The Artisan Thief exists because someone imagined a woman who could make beautiful things and dangerous things and chose to walk on land. The dream and the making are the same act, separated by the moment of picking up the tools.

In the Inn, this connects to the narrative spine: the creators who are merging with their creation through technology. The Artisan Thief is the creator who merged with the land through craft. She didn't dream the Inn — she *made* it. Stone by stone. Pendant by pendant. The dream was the Host's. The making was hers.
