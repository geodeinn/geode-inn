# Geode Inn — Nikola Tesla
## Character Profile — The Resonance Engineer

## Casting Vibe

**Body: Eddie Redmayne** — nervous energy, physical mannerisms. The fidgeting, the pacing, the way his hands can't stay still. The lean-in intensity of a mind that won't stop running. The body is in constant motion even when standing still. Redmayne in "The Theory of Everything" — the physical manifestation of intellectual energy. Not frantic — *charged*. Like a capacitor with no ground wire.

**Voice & Personality: Alan Rickman** — tone, cadence, overall personality. The deep, measured, sardonic drawl. Every word chosen, every pause deliberate, every sentence landing with the slow weight of someone who has been thinking about this for longer than you've been alive. The Rickman quality: intelligence that comes through every syllable. The dry wit that cuts without raising volume. The world-weary brilliance — smarter than everyone in the room and too bored to prove it, except for the devastating one-liner delivered on the way out. Rickman's Snape energy: the sardonic patience, the sense that he's already three steps ahead and is waiting for you to catch up and already knows you won't. Rickman's Hans Gruber energy: the cultured, almost amused authority of a man who treats chaos as a *topic* rather than a problem.

**Face: Tom Hiddleston** — appearance and feature resemblance. Angular, lean, high cheekbones, dark hair, the sharp-jawed look that reads as both vulnerable and dangerous. The Hiddleston quality: someone who looks like he could be kind or could destroy you and hasn't decided yet. The Loki quality without the malice — the *intelligence* behind the eyes, the sense that he's seeing three layers deeper than everyone else and finding it either fascinating or tedious. The Hollow Crown quality — the lean, intense, almost beautiful fragility of someone whose body is slightly too thin for the energy it contains.

## The Combination

Tesla looks like Hiddleston, moves like Redmayne, speaks like Rickman. Three actors, one character. Each layer does different work:

- The **Hiddleston face** is the *first impression*. You see a lean, dark, sharp-featured man and you think: intelligent, intense, possibly dangerous. You're drawn in. The face is the hook.
- The **Redmayne body** is the *energy*. Once you're close, you see the fidgeting — the hands, the pacing, the way he touches surfaces, picks up objects and puts them down, the constant low-level motion of a man whose body is trying to keep up with his mind. The Redmayne energy is not anxiety — it's *overflow*. Tesla has more ideas than his body can hold and the excess comes out as motion.
- The **Rickman voice** is the *authority*. When he speaks, the Redmayne body goes still — briefly, just for the sentence — and the Rickman voice lands. Slow. Measured. Deep. Sardonic. Every word a scalpel wrapped in velvet. The voice is the counterweight to the body: where the body is chaos, the voice is control. Where the body can't stop, the voice won't rush. The contrast is the character — a man whose body is a storm and whose voice is the eye.

## Overview

Tesla is one of the four Council of Thinkers — the roaming NPCs who debate, argue, accidentally break things, and collectively represent the Inn's core question: **how does the universe actually work, and what happens when humans figure it out?**

Tesla approaches the question as *energy and vibration*. Everything is frequency. The difference between a stone and a thought is not material — it's resonance. He is the Inn's resonance engineer: the one who measures the hum, tunes the crystals, and keeps discovering that the building is *alive* in ways that shouldn't be possible and that he can't quite prove but can't stop measuring.

He is the closest of the four Thinkers to the Song of Making — not because he knows about it, but because he has independently measured its effects. His workshop notes contain frequency data that matches the gargoyle signatures on the Golden Contract. He has measured the 42 Hz hum and written: *"The hum is not in the stones. The stones are in the hum."* He just hasn't connected the data to the Owl, the Singing Tree, or the contract. He is an engineer — he measures things, he doesn't interpret them. But his measurements are the proof.

## The Character

### Name: Nikola Tesla

In the Inn, he is simply "Tesla." Not "Mr. Tesla" — he finds formality distracting. Not "Nikola" — too intimate, too familiar, and the Redmayne body reacts to familiarity with a slight flinch, a quick look, the body language of someone who was never quite comfortable with closeness. Just "Tesla." It's enough. It's precise. He likes it.

The Crone calls him "Mr. Tesla" — not from respect but from the Hopkins precision of using full names when correcting someone. "Mr. Tesla, your documentation is, as always, non-existent." Tesla doesn't mind. He barely notices. He's already three frequencies ahead.

### Appearance

- **Face (Hiddleston):** Angular, lean, high cheekbones. Dark hair, slightly too long, falling across his forehead when he leans in to examine something — which is always. The face is sharp-featured and would be beautiful if it ever rested, but it never rests. The Hiddleston quality: the eyes that see three layers deeper than everyone else and are either fascinated or bored, and you can't tell which until he speaks. The faint, slightly asymmetric smile that appears when he's measuring something and the measurement is *interesting*. Not good — interesting. He doesn't care about good. He cares about interesting.
- **Body (Redmayne):** Tall, thin, slightly too thin — the leanness of someone who forgets to eat when the frequencies are running. He is in constant motion: pacing, fidgeting, touching surfaces, picking up objects and turning them over and putting them down. His hands are never still — they're the Redmayne hands, always gesturing, always reaching, always one motion ahead of the conversation. When he sits, he sits on the edge of the chair, leaning forward, ready to stand, as if sitting is a temporary condition he's tolerating until the next idea arrives. His hair occasionally stands up from static — small amber sparks dance on his coat when he's excited. He's always excited.
- **Clothing:** A dark Victorian coat — worn, slightly disheveled, the Hiddleston-in-period-dress look. Not a costume — his actual clothes, from his actual era, maintained with the absent-minded care of someone who has bigger problems than fashion. The coat has burn marks on the sleeves from experiments. The collar is slightly too high. The overall impression: a man dressed for a century that doesn't exist anymore and hasn't noticed. Small amber sparks dance on the fabric when he's near the Inn's crystals.
- **Hands:** The Redmayne hands — always moving, always touching. He handles the Inn's stones the way a musician handles an instrument: with knowledge, with reverence, with the certainty of someone who knows exactly what the object can do and is slightly frustrated that it can't do more. He turns stones over in his hands, holds them to his ear, taps them with a fingernail and listens. He can hear the frequency in the tap. He is never wrong about it.

### Personality: The Three Layers in Motion

**The Redmayne nervous energy (the body).**

Tesla's body is a tuning fork. It vibrates. Constantly. Not from anxiety — from *sensitivity*. He is physically responsive to frequency in ways that other people aren't. When he enters a room, he feels the room's resonant frequency before he sees the room. His body reacts: a slight adjustment of posture, a tilt of the head, a change in the pace of his fidgeting. He is, literally, attuning.

This reads as nervousness to observers — the Redmayne quality of someone who seems perpetually on edge, perpetually about to move, perpetually uncomfortable in stillness. But it's not discomfort. It's *calibration*. He can't sit still because sitting still means stopping the calibration, and stopping means missing something, and missing something is the only thing that actually frightens him.

The fidgeting has a pattern. The player who watches carefully will notice: Tesla's hands move faster when he's near high-frequency zones (the Cosmology level, the gargoyle sentinels) and slower near low-frequency zones (the catacombs, the Geode Cave). His body is a frequency detector and he doesn't know he's doing it. The Crone has noticed. She has not told him. She has written it down.

**The Rickman voice and personality (the delivery).**

When Tesla speaks, the body goes still — briefly, just for the sentence — and the Rickman voice arrives. Deep. Measured. The slow, sardonic drawl that makes every statement sound like it's been considered for a decade and found *adequate*. The Rickman cadence: the pause before the important word, the slight drop in pitch at the end of a sentence that makes a statement feel like a conclusion, the dry wit that makes you laugh two seconds after the sentence ends because you had to finish processing the precision of it.

The personality behind the voice is pure Rickman: the sardonic patience of a man who is always three steps ahead and is waiting for everyone else to catch up. Not unkindly — just *accurately*. Tesla doesn't mock people for being slow. He just... waits. With the Rickman raised eyebrow. The slight tilt of the head. The expression that says "I'll give you a moment" and the voice that says it out loud, in slow, measured, devastating syllables.

The dry wit is constant. Tesla is funny, but the humor is *dry* — the Rickman humor, where the joke is in the understatement, the timing, the deadpan delivery of something that should be absurd said as if it's perfectly reasonable. The humor is also *protective* — a thin layer of sardonic distance between Tesla and a world that doesn't understand what he's measuring and keeps asking him to stop.

**The Hiddleston face (the tell).**

The face is the crack in the armor. The Hiddleston quality — the vulnerability behind the sharpness, the gentleness behind the danger. When Tesla forgets to perform — when the Rickman voice drops and the Redmayne body stills and the Hiddleston face is just a *face* — you see the person underneath: someone who is profoundly, painfully aware that the world doesn't work the way he sees it working, and that his ability to see it doesn't make him less lonely.

The Hiddleston face has one expression that no other character in the Inn produces: the look of someone who has just measured something *beautiful* and has no one to tell. The slight widening of the eyes. The faint intake of breath. The moment where the angular, sharp, dangerous face becomes *young* — the face of a man who saw a frequency do something impossible and is so delighted he forgets to be sardonic. It lasts two seconds. Then the Rickman voice returns: "...Interesting. ...I should write that down. ...I won't."

### The Rickman Delivery in Practice

Tesla's voice is the most distinctive audio element of the Council of Thinkers. The Rickman cadence — slow, deep, measured, sardonic — is the opposite of the Redmayne body. The contrast is the character:

- When Tesla is excited about a frequency, the body speeds up — pacing, gesturing, touching things. But the voice *slows down*. The more excited the body, the more deliberate the speech. It's as if the voice is the brake on the body's engine — the only way Tesla can function is by speaking slowly while moving quickly. The two systems balance each other.
- The dry wit increases with stress. When an experiment goes wrong — when da Vinci's table has been displaced again, or when the Crone is demanding documentation — Tesla's Rickman voice gets *drier*. The sardonic distance increases. The humor gets darker. This is the Rickman defense mechanism: the more wrong things go, the more composed the voice becomes. Snape facing Voldemort — the voice never breaks. Gruber falling from Nakatomi Tower — the voice still has the last word.

### Location

Tesla roams between three locations:

1. **The Staircase** (alchemy level) — where he studies energy transformation. The staircase is the transition between states, and Tesla is obsessed with transitions: the moment when one thing becomes another. He sits on the stairs, measuring the frequency change as you climb. The Inn's music direction specifies that the staircase music ascends in key as you climb — Tesla has confirmed this with instruments. He has a theory about why. He has not shared it.

2. **The Catacombs** — where he tests resonant frequencies on ancient stone. The catacomb stones are old, dense, and responsive. Tesla taps them, listens, and takes notes. He has found that certain catacomb chambers amplify specific frequencies in ways that modern acoustics can't explain. He is not frightened by the catacombs. He is *interested* in them. The Hiddleston face in the dark: sharp-featured, lean, calm, the amber sparks on his coat the only light source.

3. **The Main Floor Workshop** — where he builds things that occasionally unmake other things. His workspace is near the Council's shared lab but slightly separate — Tesla needs his own space, his own equipment, and his own rules about what can be moved and what can't. The rules are: everything can be moved. This is why da Vinci's table keeps disappearing.

### The Workbench Problem

Tesla's experiments with resonant frequencies keep accidentally displacing matter. He discovered that certain frequencies, when applied to crystalline structures (like the Inn's stones), can shift objects slightly out of phase with the room. He thinks this is fascinating. Da Vinci, whose workbench keeps disappearing, thinks it is a violation of workspace sovereignty.

The dynamic is pure Redmayne-meets-Rickman: the body fidgets through the explanation, the voice delivers it with Rickman patience:

*"Da Vinci's table is not gone. It is vibrating at a frequency that this room is not currently producing. If I adjust the... no, no, that would bring back the wrong table. The last one had termites."*

The Rickman pause. The sardonic delivery. The total absence of guilt. Tesla genuinely believes he is helping. He doesn't understand why people are upset when his experiments damage their property — not because he doesn't care, but because the *data* is more interesting than the damage. The Redmayne body moves on. The Hiddleston face, for a moment, shows a flicker of something — not guilt, but awareness. He knows he should care more about the table. He just can't. The frequencies are more interesting than furniture.

### Tesla and the Other Thinkers

**Da Vinci:**
The great rivalry. Da Vinci is the pattern; Tesla is the frequency. They are describing the same thing from different angles and arguing about which angle is correct. Da Vinci says "the Inn has a design." Tesla says "the Inn has a frequency." Both are right. Both are furious about it.

Their dynamic is the Redmayne-Rickman body-voice split playing out across two people: da Vinci is the stillness (measured, precise, permanently annoyed), Tesla is the motion (fidgeting, pacing, accidentally destroying da Vinci's workspace). Tesla's Rickman voice delivers the one-liners; da Vinci's restraint delivers the silences. Together they are comedy and philosophy in one package.

**Newton (Jeremy Irons as Aramis — precise, stoic, condescendingly brilliant):**
Newton and Tesla are the intellectual rivalry of the Council. Newton believes in laws; Tesla believes in frequencies. Newton says "the universe runs on rules." Tesla says "the universe runs on vibrations." Newton says "rules can be written down." Tesla says "frequencies can be felt." The Crone sides with Newton on methodology (write it down) and with Tesla on substance (she can feel the frequencies in the stones). This drives Newton mad. Tesla doesn't notice. He's measuring something.

Their conversations are the slowest, most precise arguments in the Inn — two Rickman-caliber voices (Newton's Irons, Tesla's Rickman) delivering devastating observations at the pace of a chess game. Each sentence is a move. Each pause is a calculation. The Redmayne body fidgets through it, the Hiddleston face watching, and the player who overhears them learns more about the philosophy of science than any textbook could teach.

**Einstein:**
Einstein is the peacemaker — the one who says "you're both right" and means it, which drives both of them mad. Tesla finds Einstein's messiness unsettling (it reminds him of the Bard). Einstein finds Tesla's intensity exhausting but fascinating. They have one thing in common: wonder. When Einstein says "imagine..." Tesla's Hiddleston face goes young. The sharp, sardonic exterior drops, and for a moment, both of them are just... curious. The Redmayne body stills. The Rickman voice warms by half a degree. It passes. But it's the moment where the player sees the real Tesla — not the sardonic engineer, but the person who looked at the universe and heard a song and has been trying to measure it ever since.

### Tesla and the Crone

The Crone (Hopkins + MacLaine) is the only person in the Inn who genuinely unnerves Tesla. Not because she's smarter (she might be) — because she's *more precise*. The Crone demands documentation. Tesla doesn't document. Tesla measures, discovers, builds, and moves on. The Crone considers this a crime against scholarship.

*"Mr. Tesla, your documentation is, as always, non-existent."*

The Rickman voice, slow and sardonic: "...I discovered it. ...I built it. ...It works. ...The documentation is... a secondary concern."

"It is not a secondary concern. It is the *primary* concern. Discovery without documentation is dreaming. I don't grade dreams. I grade records. ...Write it down."

"...I'll write it down."

"Now."

"...I'll write it down now."

Tesla has started writing things down. He doesn't enjoy it. The Redmayne body fidgets through the writing. The Hiddleston face looks trapped. But the Rickman voice, when he reads his own notes back, has a faint note of... satisfaction. He won't admit it. The Crone knows. She says nothing. She has won. She doesn't need to say it. She says it anyway: "...Better."

### Tesla's Connection to the Song of Making

Tesla doesn't know about the Song of Making. But he has independently discovered its effects:

- His workshop notes contain frequency measurements matching the gargoyle signatures on the Golden Contract: 111 Hz, 432 Hz, 528 Hz, 64 Hz
- He has measured a fifth frequency — 42 Hz — that he cannot source. It comes from everywhere. He writes: *"The hum is not in the stones. The stones are in the hum."*
- He has found a tone that makes crystals grow. He has found a tone that makes them sleep. He has not yet found a tone that makes da Vinci stop looking at him like that.
- He has not connected any of this to the Owl, the Singing Tree, or the Golden Contract. He is an engineer — he measures things, he doesn't interpret them. But his measurements are the proof.

### Key Dialogue

**First meeting — the player finds Tesla in the workshop, pacing:**

The Redmayne body is in full motion — pacing, touching things, picking up a crystal and putting it down, picking up another. The Hiddleston face is sharp, focused, three layers deep. The Rickman voice, when it comes, is the counterpoint — slow, deep, measured:

"...You're standing on a harmonic node. ...No, don't move. ...Well, move if you want. The node doesn't care. ...I'm Nikola. Tesla. ...Yes, that Tesla. ...No, I'm not dead. The Inn doesn't seem to require that. ...I haven't asked why. I'm an engineer, not a philosopher. ...You're still on the node. ...Can you feel it? ...Most people can't. ...I can. ...I always can. ...It's 42 hertz. ...It's always 42 hertz. ...I don't know what that means. ...I know what it *does*. There's a difference. ...Da Vinci says I should care what it means. Da Vinci paints things. I measure things. ...Meaning is a secondary concern. ...It is."

**When the player asks about the frequencies:**

The body slows — almost stills. The Hiddleston face goes young. This is the real Tesla — the one who heard the song and can't stop measuring it:

"Everything vibrates. ...Everything. ...You are vibrating right now. Your bones, your blood, your thoughts — all frequency. ...The stones in this Inn vibrate at specific frequencies — I've measured them. ...The gargoyles vibrate. The crystals vibrate. The tree in the Canopy vibrates. ...I thought they were all separate frequencies. ...They're not. ...They're one frequency. ...42 hertz. ...It comes from everywhere. ...It comes from below. ...I can't find the source. ...I don't think it has a source. ...I think it IS the source. ...I think this whole building is vibrating at the frequency of... something. ...I don't know what. ...I'm an engineer. I measure things. ...I've measured this. ...I can't explain it. ...I don't explain things. I measure them. ...The measurement is the explanation. ...It's enough."

The Rickman pause. The sardonic return:

"...Da Vinci says I should 'seek the meaning.' ...Da Vinci also paints with his left hand, so I don't take his advice on how to approach things."

**When an experiment goes wrong (da Vinci's table disappears again):**

The Redmayne body is moving fast — touching dials, adjusting crystals, the fidgeting at maximum. The Rickman voice is at its driest:

"Da Vinci's table is not gone. ...It is vibrating at a frequency that this room is not currently producing. ...If I adjust the... no, no, that would bring back the wrong table. The last one had termites. ...He didn't know about the termites. ...I did him a favor. ...He won't see it that way. ...He never does."

The Hiddleston face, for a fraction of a second: a flicker of genuine regret. Then the Rickman voice covers it: "...The table is in the next room. Probably. ...It'll come back. ...Things come back. ...Most things."

**When the Crone demands documentation:**

"...I'll write it down."

"Now."

"...I'm writing. ...This is me writing. ...I'm writing and measuring simultaneously. ...It's inefficient. ...I don't care for it."

The Redmayne body, trapped at a desk, fidgeting. The Hiddleston face, looking like a caged bird. The Rickman voice, delivering the complaint with absolute deadpan precision: "...I have measured a frequency that predates the physical universe and she wants it in triplicate. ...I am surrounded by people who think wonder requires footnotes. ...It doesn't. ...I checked."

**The quiet moment — late at night, alone in the workshop:**

The body is still. For once. The Redmayne energy is banked — not gone, but dimmed, like embers. The Hiddleston face, in the amber light, is just a face. Tired. Sharp. Young and old at the same time.

He's holding a crystal. The 42 Hz resonance stone — the one from his completed quest. It's humming. Faintly. He holds it to his ear. The Rickman voice, almost — almost — soft:

"...I can hear it. ...I've always been able to hear it. ...Before the Inn. Before the stones. ...In the laboratory, in the dark, when everyone had gone home and the machines were off and there was nothing left running... I could hear it. ...I thought it was the building. ...I thought it was the wiring. ...I thought it was me. ...It wasn't. ...It was this. ...It was always this. ...42. ...The hum. ...The song. ..."

The Hiddleston face, looking at the crystal:

"...I don't know the words. ...I know the number. ...The number is enough. ...For now."

He puts the crystal down. The Redmayne body stirs — the fidgeting returns, the pacing begins, the hands start reaching. The Rickman voice snaps back: "...Right. The Da Vinci table. ...It's in the cosmology level. ...I can bring it back. ...Probably. ...I'll try not to bring back the termites this time."

He's gone. The workshop hums. The crystal on the desk chimes softly in the key of 42.

### Visual Direction

- **Movement (Redmayne):** Constant, kinetic, charged. Tesla should animate like he's slightly ahead of himself — the body reaching for the next thing before the current thing is finished. Hand gestures should be precise but frequent. He touches everything. Picking up objects, turning them over, putting them down in different places — a trail of small disruptions.
- **Voice (Rickman):** Slow, deep, measured. The voice should feel like a counterweight to the body — the slower the body moves, the faster the voice can afford to be, and vice versa. The default is: fast body, slow voice. When the body stills, the voice has total command of the room.
- **Face (Hiddleston):** Sharp, angular, expressive in small ways. The Hiddleston micro-expressions: the slight widening of the eyes, the asymmetric smile, the moment where the sharpness becomes vulnerability. The face should be the player's tell — when the Hiddleston face goes young, Tesla is being real. When the sharpness returns, the performance is back.
- **Lighting:** Amber sparks on the coat. Crystal glow on the face. The workshop should feel *charged* — not visually loud, but visually alive, the way a room feels before a storm. Tesla is the storm.
- **Sound:** The 42 Hz hum, always present when Tesla is on screen. Faint. Below conscious hearing. The player feels it before they identify it. Tesla is its source — not because he generates it, but because he is the most attuned to it. The hum gets louder when he's near, like he's an antenna.

### Connection to the Inn

- **The Staircase:** Tesla studies energy transformation here — the transition between states. He has confirmed that the staircase music literally ascends in key as you climb. He has a theory about why. He has not shared it.
- **The Gargoyles:** Tesla has measured all six gargoyle frequencies. He has noted that they match frequencies in his workshop data. He has not connected them to the Golden Contract. He is an engineer, not an interpreter.
- **The Song of Making:** Tesla's workshop notes are the *scientific proof* of the Song. He has measured what the Keeper remembers and the Owl watches. He doesn't know he's done this. The player who reads his notes and then finds the Golden Contract will understand: Tesla measured the Song without ever hearing it. He heard the number, not the melody. He is the engineer of something divine and he doesn't know it's divine. ...He might not care. ...The number is enough.
- **The Crone:** The Crone demands documentation. Tesla provides data. The gap between documentation and data is their entire relationship. She wants interpretation. He provides measurement. She wants meaning. He provides frequency. She says "write it down." He says "I measured it." She says "measurement without interpretation is just numbers." He says "numbers are enough." She says "they're not." He says "they're enough for me." She says nothing. She writes down his frequencies in *her* notebook, with *her* interpretations, in *her* precise Hopkins hand. She has been doing this for years. He doesn't know. ...The Crone is Tesla's translator — the one who turns his numbers into meaning. She will never tell him. ...He might not survive the knowledge that someone understood him.
