# THE GEODE INN — RESOURCE GATHERING & BUILDING SYSTEM
## The Forest as Playable Layer

---

## DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

The forest surrounding each cultural zone is a playable resource layer where players gather raw materials from the local geology and ecology, then build progressively — from a simple yurt to a castle to whatever they can imagine. Player-built structures naturally aggregate into towns that become trade hubs on the Silk Road.

The building system mirrors the tree's vertical architecture: the forest is the canopy's raw material, the buildings are the trunk's expression, and the stone foundations are the roots. The gathering is: the reaching. The building is: the becoming. The town is: the belonging.

---

## THE SEVEN BASE MATERIALS

Every building in the Inn is made from combinations of seven base materials. Each material is gathered from the zone's natural environment — the geology and ecology determine what's available.

---

### 1. WOOD
*The present tense. The material that is alive.*

**Source:** Trees in the forest surrounding each zone. Different zones offer different wood:
- **Norse zone:** Pine, spruce, birch — cold-climate softwoods. Fast-growing, easy to work, warm-colored.
- **Peruvian zone:** Mahogany, cedar, balsa — tropical hardwoods. Dense, durable, deep-grained.
- **Celtic zone:** Oak, ash, yew — the sacred trees. Slow-growing, hard, storied.
- **Slavic zone:** Linden, birch, fir — the forest of Kells. Pale, flexible, illuminated.
- **Mediterranean zone:** Olive, cypress, fig — dry-climate woods. Twisted grain, aromatic, ancient.
- **Canopy City:** All woods available (the Silk Road brings everything).

**Gathering method:** Fell trees (SIT near a tree first — the Inn's permission mechanic. The tree may drop a branch instead of falling. The tree decides.). Split logs. Carve planks. Strip bark for fiber.

**Building uses:**
- Structural frames (post-and-beam, wattle)
- Walls (planks, wattle-and-daub)
- Roofs (shingles, beams)
- Tools (handles, mallets, looms)
- Furniture (tables, chairs, shelves — Erik builds tables)

**Alchemical stage:** Present in all stages, but primary in Nigredo (raw shelters) and Albedo (refined houses). Wood is: the first thing you build with. The first thing is: the adequate.

**Inn connection:** Wood is the present tense — the material that is alive. The Cartographer maps forests. The Crone measures wood's adequacy. Hoggle knows which wood for which purpose. The Gardener watches you take from the forest — the communion vs. will mechanic applies.

- **Mediterranean zone:** Marble, tuff, limestone — the carved stone. Rock-hewn church material.

**Gathering method:** Quarry (Subtractive architecture — carve from geological features). Mine (dig from deposits). Collect (loose stone from riverbeds, talus slopes, cave floors). Break (split larger stones with tools — the Artisan Thief knows stone).

**Building uses:**
- Foundations (every permanent structure starts with stone)
- Walls (dry-stack, mortared, carved)

- Roofs (slate tiles, stone slabs)

**Alchemical stage:** Present in all stages, but primary in Citrinitas (silver/set stone — refined stonework) and Rubedo (gold/forged — monumental architecture). Stone is: the future memory. The building in stone is: the message to someone who hasn't arrived yet.

### 3. CLAY
*The first transformation. The material that becomes.*

**Source:** Riverbanks, lakebeds, wetland edges in each zone. Clay deposits form where water meets earth. Different zones offer different clay:
- **Norse zone:** Grey clay — cold, dense, good for insulation.
- **Peruvian zone:** Red clay — iron-rich, the color of the terraces.
- **Egyptian zone:** Nile black clay — the fertile clay. The clay that grew civilization.
- **Celtic zone:** White clay — kaolin. The porcelain clay. Delicate.
- **Mediterranean zone:** Terracotta clay — the fired-earth clay. Roof tiles, pots, the warmth of the south.
- **Canopy City:** All clays available via trade.

**Gathering method:** Dig from deposits (near water). Knead (work the clay to remove air and align particles — the same tactile geometry as bead-work). Wet (add water to adjust plasticity).

**Building uses:**
- Walls (wattle-and-daub — clay over woven branches)
- Floors (rammed earth, clay tiles)
- Roofs (fired clay tiles — terracotta)
- Storage (pots, jars, cisterns)
- Ovens (clay ovens — the mead hall's bread oven)
- Decoration (relief carvings, tile mosaics)

**Alchemical stage:** Primary in Albedo (copper/wire-wrapped — the first transformation). Clay is: the first material the player transforms. Raw clay becomes fired clay. Wet becomes hard. The transformation is: the alchemical principle in material form. The clay teaches: you can change things.

**Inn connection:** Clay is the first transformation — the material that teaches alchemy. The Catalyst (Helena Bonham Carter) would love clay — the biological answer to geological patience. ### 4. SAND
*The gap between. The material that fills.*

**Source:** Riverbanks, beaches, deserts, eroded stone deposits in each zone. Different zones offer different sand:
- **Norse zone:** Black sand — volcanic, sharp, heavy.
- **Peruvian zone:** White sand — quartz, fine, the desert sand.
- **Egyptian zone:** Golden sand — the Sahara. The sand that buries and preserves.
- **Celtic zone:** Coastal sand — shell fragments, salt-tinged.
- **Mediterranean zone:** White sand — the beach sand. The warm sand.
- **Coast Hub:** The richest sand deposits — the ocean brings everything.

**Gathering method:** Scoop from deposits. Sieve (separate grain sizes). Wash (remove salt and impurities — important for mortar quality).

**Building uses:**
- Mortar (sand + clay + water = the binding agent for stone walls)
- Concrete (sand + clay + crushed stone — the Roman method)
- Glass (sand + fire = glass — the Citrinitas transformation. The Artisan Thief can make glass at high alchemical stages)
- Filtration (sand filters water — the coast hub's water purification)
- Templates (sand casting — the Artisan Thief's casting method for metal charms)

**Alchemical stage:** Present in all stages, but transformative in Citrinitas (glass-making) and Rubedo (concrete and advanced construction). Sand is: the gap between — the material that fills the space between stones, between ideas, between what you have and what you need.

**Inn connection:** Sand is the gap between — the material that makes things fit. The In-Between is: the sand of the Inn. The murmur is: the sand between the notes. The Duck likes shiny sand. The shiny is: the adequate.

**Gathering method:** Mine (dig from veins). Pan (sift from river sediment — gold, the Artisan Thief's method). Smelt (separate metal from ore using fire — the forge). Cast (pour molten metal into molds — the Artisan Thief's silver casting).

**Building uses:**

### 6. HEMP
*The quiet revolution. The material that was forgotten.*

**Source:** Cultivated in the forest surrounding each zone. Hemp grows almost anywhere — one of the most adaptable plants on earth. Different zones produce slightly different fiber qualities, but hemp is: hemp. The adequacy is: universal.

**Gathering method:** Plant (from seeds — the Gardener provides seeds to players who ask). Grow (hemp grows fast — the Catalyst's domain). Harvest (cut the stalks). Ret (soak in water to separate fiber from woody core). Dry (hang the fiber). Spin (twist fibers into rope and thread). Weave (make cloth, nets, mats).

**Building uses:**
- Rope (binding, lifting, structural tension — the most essential construction material after wood and stone)
- Fabric (wall coverings, curtains, insulation — the wattle-and-daub's inner layer)
- Thatch substrate (the base layer under thatching — hemp fiber holds the thatch together)
- Cordage (tying, lashing, hanging — every tool needs cordage)
- Mats (floor coverings, wall coverings — the Canopy City's elf aesthetics)
- Nets (fishing at the Coast Hub, the water monster mini-games)
- Paper (the archive's books — hemp paper is: the most durable paper. The Examiner prefers hemp-paper records.)
- Oil (hemp seed oil — lamp fuel, waterproofing, wood treatment)

**Alchemical stage:** Present in all stages — hemp is: the universal connector. The material that holds other materials together. Nigredo (raw fiber for binding shelters), Albedo (woven fabric for houses), Citrinitas (refined cordage for fine construction), Rubedo (hemp paper for the archive, hemp oil for preservation). The hemp is: the thread that runs through everything.

**Inn connection:** Hemp is the quiet revolution — the material that was demonized and is simply... adequate. The Inn doesn't argue. The Inn uses it. The using is: the statement. The statement is: not a statement. The Gardener grows it. The Catalyst accelerates it. The Artisan Thief spins it. The Crone measures hemp rope: "It held. Adequate." **Alchemical stage:** Present in all stages — thatching is: the skill that doesn't change, only the materials do. ## DERIVED MATERIALS (Composite Craft)

The seven base materials combine through crafting to produce derived materials — composites that are more than the sum of their parts. These are not new materials. These are: what happens when the player learns what the materials can do together.

---

### STUCCO
*The skin. The finish that makes the wall a face.*

**Made from:** Sand + Lime (from limestone, a stone) + Water. Optionally: Hemp fiber for reinforcement.

**Crafting process:**
1. Crush limestone (stone) and fire it in a kiln to produce quicklime
2. Slake the quicklime with water to produce lime putty
3. Mix lime putty with sand (2:1 sand to lime ratio)
4. Add hemp fiber for tensile strength (optional but recommended)
5. Apply in layers over a wall surface — scratch coat, brown coat, finish coat

**Building uses:**
- Exterior wall finish (weatherproof skin over stone, adobe, or wattle-and-daub)
- Interior wall finish (smooth, paintable surface)
- Relief decoration (carved or molded while wet — the Mediterranean zone's signature)
- Sculptural elements (the Mediterranean zone's stucco reliefs — rock-hewn church interiors)
- Acoustic shaping (stucco can be sculpted to direct sound — the Music Room's interior)

**Alchemical stage:** Primary in Albedo (copper — the refined exterior, the first finish) and Citrinitas (silver — the decorative stucco, the carved relief). Stucco is: the skin of the building. The building without skin is: structure. The building with skin is: architecture. The architecture is: the adequate.

**Zone specialties:**
- **Mediterranean zone:** Stucco is THE material — the white-washed walls, the carved reliefs, the rock-hewn church interiors. The Mediterranean zone is: stucco's homeland.
- **Egyptian zone:** Stucco over mudbrick — the temple finish. The finish that makes mud look like stone. The making-mud-look-like-stone is: the first illusion. The illusion is: the adequate.
- **Peruvian zone:** Stucco over adobe — the terrace finish. The finish that catches the light. The light is: the dream-language.

**Inn connection:** Stucco is: the skin. The Crone measures stucco's adequacy by running a hand across the surface. The smoothness is: the measurement. The Examiner reads stucco reliefs the way the Examiner reads bones — the surface tells the story of what's underneath. The Artisan Thief knows stucco (mold-making translates directly — the same wet-to-hard transformation). The Host's Inn is: stucco-finished. The welcoming is: the smooth surface that says: come in.

## THE GATHERING LOOP

The player's gathering experience follows a loop that mirrors the Inn's philosophy:

### 1. OBSERVE (the forest)
The player walks into the forest and observes what's available. The zone's geology and ecology determine the materials. The OBSERVE action reveals what's gatherable in the immediate area.

### 2. LISTEN (to the tree/stone/deposit)
Before gathering, the player can LISTEN to the material source. A tree's frequency tells the player its wood quality. A stone deposit's resonance tells the player its hardness. Clay's tone tells the player its plasticity. The listening is: the asking.

### 3. GATHER (the material)
The player gathers. The gathering takes time — the Inn doesn't rush. The gathering speed is: 50 BPM (the heartbeat tempo). The player works at the Inn's pace. The pace is: the adequate.

**The Gardener's permission:** Living materials (wood, hemp, thatch) require the Gardener's communion — the player asks the plant. The plant may offer a branch instead of falling. The plant may grow faster if the player sits near it first. The asking is: the adequate.

**The Crone's measurement:** After gathering, the Crone (if present) measures the gathered material's quality. "Adequate" means: good quality. "Inadequate" means: try again. The player learns to gather better through the Crone's feedback.

### 4. CRAFT (the material)
The player processes the raw material at a workbench. Wood becomes planks. Stone becomes blocks. Clay becomes bricks. Ore becomes ingots. Hemp becomes rope. Thatch becomes bundles. The crafting is: the transformation. The transformation is: the alchemy.

### 5. BUILD (the structure)
The player builds. The building system uses the five-tier alchemical progression (see below). The building takes time — each stage requires more materials, more skill, and more patience. The building is: the becoming.

### 6. NAME (the structure)
When complete, the player names the structure. The naming is: the saving mechanism. The named structure appears on the Cartographer's map. The naming is: the claiming. The claiming is: the adequate.

### 7. SIT (in the structure)
The player sits in their finished building. The SIT mechanic triggers. The 42Hz hum becomes audible. The building is: the home. The home is: the adequate. The adequate is: enough.

## THE FIVE BUILDING TIERS

Each tier corresponds to an alchemical stage and requires increasing material sophistication:

### TIER 1: SHELTER (Nigredo — Iron, Raw Stone)
*The first roof. The first home.*

**Materials required:**
- Wood (frame — 10 logs)
- Hemp (cordage — 20 bundles)
- Thatch (roof — 15 bundles)
- Clay (daub — optional, for wattle-and-daub walls)

**Structure types:**
- Yurt (wood frame + hemp walls + thatch roof — the nomadic shelter)
- Lean-to (wood frame + thatch lean-to roof against a stone outcrop)
- Wigwam (bent wood frame + bark/thatch covering)
- Tent (hemp fabric over wood poles)

**Time to build:** 1 session (2-3 hours of gameplay)

**NPC interactions:**
- **Hoggle** delivers stone for a simple foundation. "Here. It won't fall down. Probably." (It won't fall down.)
- **Dobby** carries the wood. Happy to help. The carrying is: the devotion.
- **The Gardener** provides hemp seeds if asked. The asking is: the communion.

**The Crone's verdict:** "It stands. Adequate."

**After completion:** The shelter appears on the Cartographer's map as a small dot. The dot is: the first mark the player makes on the Inn's world.

## TOWN FORMATION

When enough player-built structures aggregate in one area, a town forms. The town is: the community that grew from the gathering.

### Town Thresholds

| Structures | Town Level | What Happens |
|------------|-----------|--------------|
| 1 | Homestead | Single structure on the map. The Cartographer notes it. |
| 3-5 | Hamlet | Structures visible as a cluster. NPCs begin to visit. Hoggle delivers stone. Dobby carries materials. A path forms between structures. |
| 6-10 | Village | The village appears on the Silk Road map as a trade stop. NPC vendors set up stalls. Erik brings a communal fire. The mead begins. |

- **Communal Fire** (unlocked at Hamlet): Erik's gift. A shared hearth. The fire is: the gathering point.

### Town Governance

- The town's welcome policy (open to all, invitation-only, or the frog-transformation system for troublemakers)

**The Host's only rule:** "Room's ready." (The room is: always ready. The always-ready is: the adequate.)
