## The Layers — How Each Artist Contributes

### 1. O'Keeffe → The Crystal Structures (Form)
The crystal city's buildings are O'Keeffe forms — stripped, essential, simplified. The crystal towers are not ornate (that would be Klimt). They are FORMS — pure shapes, the essential geometry of crystal. The player who learned Form Sight in the Desert Bone recognizes the same simplification here: the crystal city reduced to its bones. O'Keeffe's thesis — the form is enough — reaches its deepest expression in the Hollow Earth. The crystals ARE the form. The form IS the crystal.

### 2. Klimt → The Crystal Luminosity (Decoration)
The crystal surfaces GLOW — and the glow is Klimt's gold. Not literal gold leaf, but the golden luminosity that Klimt gave to his surfaces. The crystals emit light, and the light is warm, and the warmth is the Inn's warmth — the same amber-gold that fills the main floor, the same golden glow that Klimt used in 'Death and Life.' The crystal city is Klimt's ultimate decorative surface: the entire city is ornamented with its own light. The gold IS the crystal. The crystal IS the gold. The Bone Room's collision (form + decoration) is the Hollow Earth's foundation (crystal + light).

### 3. Dalí → The Impossible Geometry (Surrealism)
The crystal city's architecture is IMPOSSIBLE — non-Euclidean, dreamlike, the way Dalí painted melting clocks and floating forms. The crystal towers lean at angles that shouldn't stand. The crystal bridges span distances that shouldn't cross. The underground ocean reflects a sky that isn't there. The Hollow Earth's geometry is Dalí's: the impossible made visible, the surreal made solid. The player who learned the Cosmology level's Dalí lens (the sky's logic) recognizes the same logic here, inverted: the EARTH's logic, which is just as impossible, just as dreamlike, just as real.

### 4. Doré → The Underground Ocean (Drama)
The underground ocean is Doré's deepest engraving — the subterranean dark, the single light source (the crystal city's glow on the water), the dramatic contrast between the luminous city and the black water. The ocean is the zone's Doré layer: the vast underground space, the tiny figures (the Deep Ones surfacing), the darkness that is not empty but FULL. Doré's Cave City was a carving in progress. The Hollow Earth's ocean is the FINISHED engraving — the plate complete, every line cut, the image fully emerged from the dark.

### 5. Roerich → The Resonance Engine (Sacred Altar)
The Resonance Engine — the crystal spire that generates the 42Hz frequency — is Roerich's sacred altar. The Engine is the zone's Roerich element: the monument that is also a prayer, the structure that is also a presence, the altar where the earth meets the divine. The Engine glows with Roerich's luminous violet — the same light as the Slavic zone's aurora, the same sacred geography. The Engine is the Hollow Earth's equivalent of Roerich's Himalayan mountains: the highest point in the deep, the place where the physical and the spiritual converge. The player who learned to read the aurora (Roerich) in the Slavic zone recognizes the Engine's glow as the same language — the light that is also a text, the frequency that is also a word.

### 6. Rackham → The Root Structures (Living Architecture)
The Singing Tree's roots grow THROUGH the crystal city — and the roots are Rackham's living trees. The roots are alive, gnarled, expressive, the same Rackham energy that built the Canopy City's platforms. But here, the trees grow DOWN, not up. The roots twist through the crystal architecture, the living wood intertwining with the crystalline stone, Rackham's organic line wrapping around O'Keeffe's geometric form. The root-city is the Hollow Earth's Rackham layer: the tree as architecture, inverted. The Canopy was the tree's branches made into a village. The Hollow Earth is the tree's ROOTS made into a city. Same Rackham energy. Opposite direction.

### 7. Bauer → The Deep Ones (Ancient Creatures)
The Deep Ones — the beings who swim in the underground ocean — are Bauer's ancient creatures. They are not beautiful (that would be the Seelie Lee's light). They are not cute. They are OLD — older than the Inn, older than the Song, older than the crystal city. They swim in the dark water with eyes that glow, the way Bauer's trolls moved through the winter forest. The Deep Ones are the Hollow Earth's Bauer layer: the ancient, the patient, the indifferent. They were here before the city. They will be here after. The player who met Bauer's Unseelie Court recognizes the Deep Ones as the same kind of being — not evil, not hostile, just PRECEDING. Older than the story. Older than the game.

### 8. Monet → The Ocean Surface (Reflection)
The underground ocean's surface — where the water meets the crystal city's light — is Monet's reflection. The water reflects the crystal towers, the glowing spires, the root bridges, but the reflection SHIMMERS — the way Monet's Mediterranean shimmered. The reflection is not a mirror (that's too precise). The reflection is Monet's impression — the light broken into fragments, the colors scattered, the image dissolving into pure color at the edges. The underground ocean's surface is the Hollow Earth's Monet layer: the water that doesn't reflect but IMPRESSES. The player who learned the Mediterranean's Monet lens recognizes the same water here — but inverted. The Mediterranean reflected the sky. The underground ocean reflects the city. Both are Monet. Both are light on water. Different ceilings.

### 9. Carr → The Crystal Forests (Cathedral Nature)
The crystal formations that grow in clusters around the city — the crystal "forests" — are Carr's cathedral nature. Emily Carr painted trees as cathedrals. In the Hollow Earth, the crystals ARE cathedrals — towering, organic, luminous. The crystal forests are the Hollow Earth's Carr layer: the natural formation that is also a sacred space. The player who visited the Redwood Cathedral recognizes the same energy here: the awe of standing inside something that grew. The redwoods grew up. The crystals grew down. Both are Carr's cathedrals.

### 10. Wyeth → The Echoes (Weathered Memory)
The Echoes — the memory-ghosts of the creators, frozen mid-step when the ley lines stuttered — are Wyeth's weathered figures. The Echoes are not dramatic (not Doré). They are not surreal (not Dalí). They are WEATHERED — the way Wyeth painted the figure in the field, distant, still, patient. The Echoes are the Hollow Earth's Wyeth layer: the surface that shows its age, the figure that has been here so long it has become part of the texture. The Echoes are the zone's deepest patience — they have been waiting for millions of years, the way the Appalachians have been eroding for 300 million. Both are Wyeth: time made visible on a surface.

### 11. The Reader's Mirror → The Archive District
The Archive District — where the 12 crystal records store the creators' final messages — is the Inn's OWN lens. Not an artist's lens — the Inn's lens. The archive is the Inn's thesis made physical: this place is a story, and you are inside one. The Archive District is the collision's CORE — the point where all the artists' energies converge into a single statement. The 12 records are the Inn's version of the Reader's book — the text that acknowledges its own textuality. The player who reads all 12 records hears the creators' last words, and the last record says: "Others will hear the Song. Others will come." The collision's deepest layer is not an artist. It is the Inn itself, speaking in its own voice.
