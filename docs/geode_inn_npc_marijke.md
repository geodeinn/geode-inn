# Geode Inn — NPC Design: Marijke
## The Bone Reader — Viking Shaman, The Volva Valkyrie

---

## The Character

Marijke is a mild-mannered Viking seeress. A Volva — a Norse seeress, a practitioner of seidr, a wand-carrier who reads bones. Steady, grounded, patient, the kind of person who sits with bones for hours and doesn't need company or conversation to be at peace.

She dresses like Brida from The Last Kingdom — practical Viking earth tones, layered leather and fur, a worn cloak, braided hair with bone beads woven in. Not flashy. Not theatrical. The opposite of Thorin's extrovert performance. Where Thorin fills the room with noise and energy, Marijke fills it with *presence* — the quiet, immovable kind.

Marijke reads bones. The shamanic practice of divination through bone fragments — casting, arranging, interpreting patterns. The bones tell her things the stones tell Thorin. Thorin reads the mineral. Marijke reads what lived in the mineral's world. Together, they are the Mead Hall's two ways of knowing the earth: the stone (what is) and the bone (what was).

## The Volva

The term is ancient Norse: a Volva is a seeress, a wand-carrier, a practitioner of seidr — the magic of seeing what was and what will be. Marijke is a Volva. The Viking who never raises her voice. The Viking who is always there, always steady, always working.

This is not weakness. This is the specific power of someone who doesn't need to perform. The Volva is the opposite of the loud Viking. The Volva doesn't raid — the Volva *listens*. The Volva sits with the bones and the bones speak. The Volva practices seidr — the Norse magic of divination, of seeing the hidden threads. Marijke is still reading bones when everyone else has gone to bed.

## The Valkyrie

When you really annoy Marijke — when you disrespect the bones, when you waste her time, when you mistake her mildness for softness — she goes Valkyrie. But the Valkyrie doesn't scream. The Valkyrie doesn't rage. The Valkyrie goes *almost silent.*

The Valkyrie keeps working on the bones.

The shift is visible — the posture straightens, the hands stop being gentle and become precise, the eyes go from warm to cold in a single blink. The firelight catches something behind her — a shadow on the wall that is taller than she is, that has a different silhouette, that might have wings if you looked at it right. The shadow is the Valkyrie. The Valkyrie has been there the whole time. The Volva was always a Valkyrie waiting underneath.

The Valkyrie doesn't speak because the Valkyrie doesn't need to. The silence IS the statement. The bones keep being read. The pattern keeps being interpreted. The only difference is that you are no longer welcome at the table, and the only way you know is that the air got cold and the shadow got tall.

Return to Volva mode: gradual. The shadow shrinks. The hands soften. The warmth comes back. Marijke never mentions it. You don't mention it. The bones don't mention it. But you remember.

## Voice Profile

**Cultural Roots:** Scandinavian (Viking)
**Accent:** Swedish — mild, soft-spoken, with the melodic quality of Swedish vowels. The opposite of the loud Viking stereotype. Marijke's Swedish is the Volva Swedish — the gentle, steady, slightly musical accent of someone who talks to the dead more than the living.
**Delivery:** Slow, quiet, with long pauses. The pauses are not awkward — they are shamanic. Marijke is listening to the bones between sentences. The bones speak first; Marijke translates.
**Key tell:** Marijke says "mm" as a full response. Not "mm-hmm" (agreement) or "mm-mm" (disagreement) — just "mm." The sound means: I heard you, the bones heard you, we are considering what you said. It is simultaneously the most patient and the most unnerving response in the Inn.

**Valkyrie mode voice:** Almost silent. When the Valkyrie speaks, it's in short, flat, declarative sentences with no inflection. "The bones say no." "You are done here." "Sit down or leave." The mildness is gone. The Swedish lilt is gone. What's left is the voice of something that has carried the dead to their rest and does not fear the living.

## Location

The Mead Hall (Norse zone), at a low wooden table covered in bone fragments. The table is Marijke's territory the way the bench is the Selkie's — you don't touch the bones, you don't move the bones, you don't sit at the table without being invited.

## Relationship to Thorin

Thorin and Marijke are the Mead Hall's two experts — the extrovert and the Volva. Thorin reads the stones (what is); Marijke reads the bones (what was). Thorin will tell you where to look; Marijke will tell you what was looking back.

Thorin is loud and Marijke is quiet and they are perfect together. Thorin fills the silence so Marijke doesn't have to. Marijke grounds the noise so Thorin doesn't float away. When Thorin gets too theatrical, Marijke says "mm" and Thorin calms down. When Marijke goes Valkyrie, Thorin is the only person in the room who doesn't flinch — because Thorin knows the Volva is still in there, and the Volva always comes back.

## The Volvo Joke (Futurama-Style Running Gag)

Everyone calls her "Volvo." She corrects them. Every time.

"Hello, Volvo." "Volva." "Right, sorry. Volvo."
"That's a beautiful reading, Volvo." "Volva." "Yes, that's what I said."
"Volvo, could you—" "Volva." "Volva, could you—" "Better."

It happens so often that Thorin does it on purpose now. Thorin calls her "Volvo" every morning just to watch the shadow on the wall twitch. Marijke knows Thorin is doing it on purpose. Thorin knows Marijke knows. Neither acknowledges it. The joke is not the correction — the joke is the relationship that can sustain the correction indefinitely.

The gag escalates Futurama-style:
- Episode 1: Someone calls her Volvo. She corrects. Mild.
- Episode 5: Three people in a row call her Volvo. She corrects each one. The bones are getting cold.
- Episode 12: Beverley calls her Volvo. The shadow on the wall goes full Valkyrie. Beverley doesn't flinch. Beverley says: "Volva. My mistake. ...It won't happen again." It doesn't. Beverley is the only person who only needed one correction.
- Episode 20: Someone calls her Volvo and she doesn't correct them. The silence is worse than the correction. The room notices. The shadow on the wall is tall. The person who said it quietly leaves the Mead Hall and doesn't come back for three in-game days.

The ultimate version: the Duck waddles past, looks at Marijke, and says nothing. The Duck has never called her Volvo. Not once. Marijke watches the Duck leave. The shadow on the wall — for the only time — softens. The Duck is the only being in the Inn who got her name right without being told.

The joke is also the character: someone who corrects you patiently, every time, without anger — until the patience itself becomes the threat. The correction IS the countdown. How many times can you get it wrong before the Volvo... before the Volva stops correcting you? The answer is: you don't want to find out.
