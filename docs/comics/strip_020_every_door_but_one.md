# Strip #020 — "Every Door But One"
**Date:** 2026-09-29
**Zone:** The Catacombs → The Slavic Zone (Boreal Chime Wind Carillon)
**Characters:** The Catacombs Doorkeeper (unnamed — the masked figure)
**Art Style:** Klimt gold-on-dark (Catacombs) transitioning to Royo warm amber (Slavic zone carillon) — Gazette house style
**Player Adventures:** None — NPC feature (The Catacombs Doorkeeper; the Boreal Chime Wind Carillon)

---

## Panel 1 — The Setup
The Catacombs. Gold-on-dark, geometric, Klimt. The Doorkeeper in plague mask and black leather stands at the mouth of a long corridor of ornate doors. The skeleton key glints in hand.

**Caption:** The Catacombs Doorkeeper kept the doors below. Every lock answered to the skeleton key — every door in the building's bones. The key had been cut for obedience, and the dead had learned to wait their turn.

## Panel 2 — The Moment
The Doorkeeper walks the corridor, key turning in lock after lock. Doors open. Through one, a faint amber glow and the shape of birch chimes.

**Caption:** The key turned. That was the trade. The dead had doors, and the living had a keeper, and between them stood the figure in the beak mask — a 1619 invention by Charles de Lorme, repurposed for a building that needed someone who wouldn't flinch.

## Panel 3 — The Twist
The last door — the one to the surface. The mask sits on a bench beside the carillon's birch posts. The skeleton key hangs from a belt loop. The key hovers near the mask's straps but can't turn.

**Caption:** But the key had one door it never touched. The mask came off by the calendar, not by the lock. Imbolc. Ostara. Beltane. Midsummer. Lammas. Mabon. Samhain. Seven turns of the wheel, seven times the beak was set down among birch and amber. The one mystery in the building that was a schedule.

## Panel 4 — The Button
The Slavic zone. The Boreal Chime Wind Carillon — hollow birch, amber beads. The Doorkeeper sits among the clan, mask off, face toward the chimes. The skeleton key rests on the ground, untouched.

**Caption:** The keeper of doors, on the thinnest night, did not keep them. Samhain was the door festival — the veil at its finest, the dead at their nearest — and the Doorkeeper sat with the clan and let birch sing what keys couldn't. The skeleton key could open anything in the Catacombs. The clan could open the one thing it couldn't. Some doors aren't locks. Some doors are seasons.

## Notes

The Catacombs Doorkeeper's first Gazette feature. Fresh canon from September 28-29: the Boreal Chime Wind Carillon (tuned hollow birch strung with raw amber beads, Slavic zone), the mask-off ritual (the Doorkeeper sits among the clan during the Wheel's turns and listens, not performs), and the clan's Druidic wheel ruling (Imbolc, Ostara, Beltane, Midsummer, Lammas, Mabon, Samhain — seven turns ruled, Yule left as the open gate).

The strip's spine: the skeleton key that opens every door in the Catacombs cannot open the mask. The mask comes off by the calendar, not by the lock. The one character in the building whose mystery is a schedule, not a quest.

Voice notes: Rothfuss precision in the key's engineering ("cut for obedience," "the dead had learned to wait their turn"); Pratchett warmth in the gentle absurdity (the all-powerful key defeated by a strap); Gaiman mythic-made-personal in the final panel (the keeper who, on the thinnest night, stops keeping and starts listening).

The button connects to the Doorkeeper's core paradox: keeping doors is the trade, but the mask-off is the truth. The clan opens what the key can't. Samhain is the load-bearing turn — the door festival, the thinnest veil — the one night the keeper of doors doesn't keep them.
