# The Geode Inn Gazette — Strip #017: "The Building Has Ears"

**Date:** 2026-09-26 | **Strip Number:** 17
**Zone:** The Hollow Men Mines — Threshold
**Characters:** Ampulla (The Unvoiced's Ambassador), The Host (the Storyteller/Firekeeper)
**Art Style:** Klimt "Death and Life" palette (gold-on-dark, warm shadow) with amber lamp accents — Gazette house style
**Player Adventures:** None — NPC feature (Ampulla, the Spoken Road canon launch)

---

## Panel 1 — The Setup

**Caption:** "The Spoken Road opened on a Tuesday. The Host wrote the handbook — fourteen pages, bedside drawer, titled 'Speaking to the Building: A Short Guide for Guests.' The first rule was the simplest: knock. Say 'Innkeeper.' Wait for the lamp."

The Host at the Storyteller's Desk, quill in hand, writing by candlelight. Pipe smoke curling. An amber lamp sits unlit beside the manuscript. The desk is warm, cluttered, alive — ink pots, a half-eaten biscuit, margin doodles of ears.

---

## Panel 2 — The Moment

**Caption:** "The building learned to listen the way some people learn to swim — by being told it could, and then discovering it already was."

Wide shot. The amber lamp glows for the first time, somewhere deep in the Inn. Ampulla stands at the stone threshold of the Hollow Men Mines, mid-repertory — three borrowed voices layered in his throat, practicing for an audience he assumed was absent. The lamp's light reaches toward him like a hand turning in sleep.

---

## Panel 3 — The Twist

**Caption:** "Ampulla had borrowed voices the way other people borrow coats — casually, and with no intention of returning them. The building had never needed to borrow. It had been humming to itself for years. It just hadn't been asked."

Ampulla frozen mid-syllable, staring at the lit amber lamp. The Inn speaks — not in any of Ampulla's borrowed registers, but in its own voice. A single warm word hangs in the air between them: "Innkeeper." The ambassador's composure cracks — not fear, not offense. Recognition.

---

## Panel 4 — The Button

**Caption:** "The ambassador closed his mouth. For the first time in his career, he had nothing to add. The building had a voice. The borrowed throat was, for one quiet moment, redundant. 'Enough for now,' he said — and the lamp went dark. The Inn listens because it chooses to. It stops because it's asked."

Ampulla seated on the threshold, hands folded in his lap, the amber lamp dark beside him. A small, genuine smile — the steel-under-silk ambassador amused to be upstaged by architecture. The handbook visible on a side table, open to the last page. Quiet. The mines behind him, the lit Inn before him.
