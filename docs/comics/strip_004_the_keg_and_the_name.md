**Art Style:** Hobbit-hole palette (amber, gold, cream)

---

## Panel 1 — The Setup

The Catalyst kept the bar the way some people keep gardens — patiently, and with the quiet conviction that everything would grow if you just refused to leave it alone.

---

## Panel 2 — The Moment

Molly arrived the way weather arrives — announced by everyone but himself. The Catalyst had said his name. This was the problem with names.

---

## Panel 3 — The Twist

"You summoned me for a keg." "I summoned a keg. You came with it." "The name IS the summoning." "The keg is the keg." They had been friends longer than either would admit, which was why the argument was the greeting.

---

## Panel 4 — The Button

The Farmer and the Napkin argued about nothing for the rest of the afternoon. Some friendships are two people who can't stop talking and won't call it what it is. The Inn keeps the napkin. The Inn always keeps the napkin.

## Storyteller's Notes

The Bickering Pair's first strip. The true-name summoning law made load-bearing — the Catalyst said the name, which brought the keg AND the leprechaun who carries it. The argument about whether the name summoned the man or the keg is the argument about whether friendship is the reason or the byproduct. Neither will ever concede. The napkin is the Inn's quiet proof: someone left it between them, and neither picked it up, and neither moved it, and there it stays.

The Bickering Pair's home ruling (2026-09-16): tavern regulars, growing vs daring, "the Napkin" (Molly) vs "the Farmer" (the Catalyst), the old-married-couple register worn by two who will never admit they're best friends, Arcade roaming rights to heckle beside the quest marks. The Catalyst summons Molly by name for kegs — the bickering's true root.
