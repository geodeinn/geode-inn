La Ciguapa: Perhaps the most famous Dominican mythical creature, the Ciguapa is a nocturnal woman with long, dark hair and backward-facing feet, making her footprints impossible to track. She is said to live in the deep mountain forests, often luring men into the woods before making them disappear.

El Galipote: A shape-shifting man who can transform into animals (such as a black dog, or Lugarú) or inanimate objects like trees and rocks. Galipotes are known for their immense strength, cruelty, and immunity to firearms.

El Bacá: A devilish, shape-shifting animal familiar (often a black dog or bull with glowing red eyes) created through black magic. It grants wealth, land, or protection to its owner, but requires the soul of a loved one or material sacrifices in return.

Las Brujas (Witches): European-derived tales of women who make pacts with the devil, allowing them to fly, cast curses, and transform into large birds.

Las 21 Divisiones: This is the primary folk religion of the Dominican Republic. Rooted deeply in West African Vodou traditions, it classifies spirits into distinct "divisions" (like Rada, Petro, and Gede). It also incorporates Taíno ancestral spirits and is practiced alongside Catholicism.

Los Indios de las Aguas: Harmless, generous spirits that live in rivers, lakes, and underwater caves. They are believed to be the ancient Taíno inhabitants and are known for their extensive knowledge of herbal medicine.

Taíno Resistance: The island's original folklore reveres historical figures like Enriquillo, a Taíno cacique (chief) who fought Spanish colonial oppression, and Anacaona, a revered Taíno queen and poet.

Carnival: Dominican Carnival (celebrated heavily in February and March) brings folklore to life. It features iconic characters like the Diablo Cojuelo (limping devil) who playfully whips spectators, and the Taimáscaros of Puerto Plata, which blend Taíno and African masks to celebrate the island's dual heritage.
