Jong Batu: A rock outcrop in the Brunei River shaped like a sinking ship. It is the core of the Nakhoda Manis (Captain Sweet) legend, which tells the tale of an unfilial son whose ship was cursed and turned to stone by his mother. [1, 2]

Pulau Pilong-Pilongan & Lumut Lunting: Two islands/sandbanks in the Brunei Bay associated with ancient folklore and the early beginnings of the Sultanate.

The Golden Mountain and the Weeping Rice: A classic myth about a magical, treasure-filled mountain that sought to bless a generous society, but sailed away forever upon hearing the cries of a neglected, weeping community.

Awang Alak Betatar: The legendary founder of Brunei. According to tradition, he was originally a local prince who became the first Sultan of Brunei (Sultan Muhammad Shah) after winning a historic boat race and converting to Islam.

Tujuh Telaga Mandian Puteri: A Tutong legend about two heavenly princesses who flew down from Mount Mulu to bathe in seven hidden sand-wells during every full moon.

Nakhoda Ragam: The tale of a mighty Sultan and warrior whose unyielding spirit lives on in local legends tied to the mysterious three-tomb site in Brunei Darussalam.

Nature Spirits and Guardian Trees: Ancient, large spiral trees often feature in urban legends as being guarded by, or containing, vengeful or protective spirits of the land.

Hantu Penanggalan & Hantu Raya: Like much of Bornean ghost lore, Brunei folklore features supernatural spirits. The Penanggalan is a vampiric woman whose head detaches at night, and the Hantu Raya is a giant guardian spirit that can be bound by a master.
