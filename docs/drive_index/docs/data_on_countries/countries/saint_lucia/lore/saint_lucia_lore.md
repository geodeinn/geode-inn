Papa Bwa (Papa Bois): The elderly protector of the forest depicted with features like a goat or cloven hooves; he helps lost travelers and misleads harmful hunters. [1, 2]

Ladjablès: A seductive woman in 19th-century Creole dress who lures men into the deep woods at crossroads, concealing a cow's or horse's foot beneath her skirt.

Mama Glo (Mama Dleau): A half-woman, half-snake mother of the rivers who acts as a guardian of fresh water and aquatic life.

Compere Lapin: The clever rabbit trickster of Caribbean oral tradition who outsmarts larger animals through wit.

Mary Ansent & Papa Djab: Figures symbolizing the hardships of the past and cultural duality, tied tightly to local Creole history.

Night Rules: Whistling or calling someone's full name in the dark bush is avoided so unseen spirits or malicious entities do not answer back.

Threshold Safety: Placing bowls of salt water or protective herbs like basil and rosemary by the doorway blocks negative energy from visitors.

Nature Omens: Loud frog choruses or crabs moving inland signal incoming heavy rain, while old giant trees are treated with deep respect as ancient spirit markers.
