El Cadejo: A supernatural hound that stalks night travelers. A white version protects the innocent and virtuous, while a terrifying, red-eyed black Cadejo punishes drunkards and the ill-intentioned. [1, 2, 3, 4]

La Segua: Originating as a beautiful woman who flags down passing men, she transforms into a monster with a rotting horse skull once men pick her up. She targets wandering, unfaithful, or intoxicated men, frightening them to death.

La Llorona: A widespread Latin American legend heavily featured in Costa Rica, telling the tragic story of a weeping woman wandering rivers and lakes for her drowned children.

La Tulevieja: A restless spirit of a sinful, unwed mother who transformed into a wretched old woman wearing a conical hat. She wanders forests and riverbanks, kidnapping unbaptized children.

La Mona (The Witch Monkey): The legendary ghost of a wicked witch who curses her family and turns into an ape-like creature that terrorizes remote towns from the treetops.

El Duende: A mischievous goblin who lives in the woods and plays tricks on travelers, often kidnapping children or tangling horses' manes in the night.

Sibú: The primary creator deity in Bribri and Talamancan indigenous mythology, responsible for creating the Earth, humanity, and protecting the sacred forests.

Volcano Legends: Many of Costa Rica's famous volcanoes feature legends of their origins. For instance, Rincón de la Vieja is named after an old woman who lived near the volcano. The Turrialba Volcano tells the story of Cira, a girl from a local tribe who fell in love with a warrior from a rival group, which eventually brought the mountain to life.

Maria La Negra: Part of the famed "Witches of Escazú" lore, this mountain town spirit is associated with black magic, hidden treasures, and ancient sorcery.
