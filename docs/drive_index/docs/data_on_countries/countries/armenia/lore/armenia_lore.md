Aramazd: The supreme father god of the ancient Armenian pantheon, creator of heaven and earth.

Anahit: The golden mother goddess of fertility, healing, and wisdom.

Vahagn: The god of fire, thunder, and war, famous as a dragon-slayer.

Hayk: The legendary archer and patriarch who defeated the Babylonian king Bel, establishing the Armenian nation.

Vishap: Massive serpent-like dragons living in mountains, lakes, or clouds that control storms and water sources.

Aralez: Dog-like celestial spirits that descend from sacred peaks to revive fallen heroes by licking their wounds.

Grogh: The celestial scribe who records human lives and imprints destiny (chakatagir) onto a newborn's forehead.

Mardagayl: Mythical werewolves representing cursed humans or dark spirits.

Armenian oral traditions feature classic quest motifs, magical objects, and witty animals, often compiled by modern folklorists like Leon Surmelian (Apples of Immortality). Tales frequently follow clever youths outsmarting wicked kings, interacting with fairies (pari), or journeying to find the daughter of the King of the East with brothers possessing superhuman gifts.
