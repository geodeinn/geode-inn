Music & Dance Styles: Rooted in the countryside with rhythms like the vidalita, milonga, pericón (national folk dance), and chamarrita. [1, 2, 3]

Festivals (Criollo Fests): Large gatherings featuring jineteadas (bareback horse riding), folk singing, and campfires, best seen at the Fiesta de la Patria Gaucha in Tacuarembó.

Daily Customs: Drinking yerba mate via a metal straw (bombilla) from a hollowed gourd, and baking tortas fritas (fried cakes) on rainy days.

Candombe: A powerful UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage rhythm played on three sizes of barrel drums (tamboriles).

Carnaval: The longest carnival in the world (spanning 40 days from mid-January to late February), highlighted by the massive Llamadas parade and satirical musical theater troupes called murga.

Guaraní Myths: Tales of the creator god Tupá and the origin of yerba mate, where the moon descends to earth and gifts the plant to a mortal.

Luz Mala: A ghost light or will-o'-the-wisp seen over the pampas, believed by rural folklore to be a wandering soul.
