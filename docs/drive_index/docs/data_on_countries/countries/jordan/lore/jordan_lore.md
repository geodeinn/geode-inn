Jinn and Trial Tales: Folktales often involve trials between humans and jinn, or dialogues with the dead. They explain natural phenomena or cosmologic creations. [1]

The Bedouin Tradition: Bedouin folklore is deeply rooted in the desert, focusing on bravery, hospitality, and the protection of travelers.

The Dabke: This famous Levantine line dance is a staple of Jordanian celebrations, featuring loud ground-pounding leg movements accompanied by traditional instruments like the Oud and Rebab.

The Lost City of Petra: The Nabataean city was lost to the West for centuries. Local folklore claims the Siq (the narrow gorge) and the Khazneh (The Treasury) were carved by the wand of Moses to bring forth water for the Israelites.

The Cave of the Seven Sleepers: Located near Amman, this site tells the story of righteous youths who sought refuge in a cave and slept for centuries to escape religious persecution.

The Dead Sea: Legends suggest the mineral-rich mud and warm waters of the lowest point on Earth were the beauty secrets of Cleopatra and a favored retreat of King Herod.
