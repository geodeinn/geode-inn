Lạc Long Quân and Âu Cơ: The Dragon Lord and Fairy Queen who hatched 100 children, becoming the mythical ancestors (con rồng cháu tiên) of the Vietnamese people.

The Four Immortals (Tứ Bất Tử): Comprising the child hero Thánh Gióng, the mountain god Tản Viên Sơn Thánh, the marsh prince Chử Đồng Tử, and the mother goddess Liễu Hạnh.

Ông Trỗi: The supreme sky deity associated with creation, weather, and the heavens.

Lang Liêu: A poor prince who invented bánh chưng (square sticky rice cake) and bánh giầy (round cake) to symbolize the earth and sky.

Mai An Tiêm: Exiled to a deserted island, he discovered watermelons and proved his self-reliance.

Ông Táo: The Kitchen Gods who report household activities to the Jade Emperor before the Lunar New Year.

Sơn Tinh and Thủy Tinh: The eternal battle between the Mountain God and Water God, representing seasonal monsoons and flood prevention.

An Dương Vương: The king who built the spiral citadel of Cổ Loa with the help of a golden turtle (Kim Quy) and the magic crossbow.

Lê Lợi and Hoàn Kiếm Lake: The Emperor who received a magical sword (Thuận Thiên) to defeat foreign invaders and later returned it to a giant golden turtle in Hanoi.

Ma da: Malevolent water ghosts of people who drowned, trying to pull others under to free themselves.

Trùng tang: The feared phenomenon of repeating deaths within a family lineage due to restless spirits.
