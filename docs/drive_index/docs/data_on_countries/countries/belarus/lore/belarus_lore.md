Mavka / Navka: Beautiful or sorrowful female forest spirits representing young souls or those who died unwed, often said to lure wanderers.

Tsmok: A traditional benevolent dragon or water serpent tied to local lakes, such as the Lepel Tsmok.

Domovik: A local home or hearth spirit acting as a guardian or household trickster depending on the region.

Blukach and Dzyad-Baradzed: Wandering road spirits and wizened old grandfather figures of the woods.

Kupala Night (Ivan Kupala): A midsummer celebration featuring bonfires, leaping over flames, flower wreath-weaving, and searching for the legendary fern flower.

Kalyady: Winter Christmastide caroling, games, and masked traditional plays like Tereshka’s Marriage.

Dazhynki: An important autumn harvest festival honoring agricultural workers with bread-making, songs, and communal feasts.

Maslenitsa: The playful pancake-eating week marking the end of winter.

Sun and Moon: Myths framing celestial bodies as a heavenly family, where moonspots are said to come from bites by the Devil.

The Stork Origin: Etiological stories explaining storks and black-backed birds as transformed humans who failed to obey divine commands regarding earthly pests.
