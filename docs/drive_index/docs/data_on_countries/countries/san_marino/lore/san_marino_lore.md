The Stonemason's Flight: Marinus, a Christian stonemason from the island of Rab (modern-day Croatia), fled to the hills of Italy in 257 AD to escape the anti-Christian persecutions of Roman Emperor Diocletian. [1, 2, 3]

The Gift of the Mountain: After settling and preaching on Mount Titano, his fame as a holy man and healer prompted a wealthy Roman landowner named Felicissima (or Felicita) to gift the entire mountain to him after he supposedly cured her sick son.

The Eaten Donkey: According to folklore, when Marinus returned to his hermitage one day, he discovered that a ferocious bear had attacked and eaten his sole companion and helper, a donkey.

The Tamed Helper: Instead of attacking the beast, Marinus gently reasoned with the bear, explaining the pain it had caused. The repentant bear complied, submitted to the saint, and took over the donkey's workload as his loyal helper. This scene is widely memorialized in local art and bas-reliefs across the republic.

The Foundational Motto: On September 3, 301 AD—the traditional date of the republic's founding—Marinus passed away.

Leaving the Legacy: Just before his death, he allegedly uttered the phrase that remains the nation’s sacred motto: "Relinquo vos liberos ab utroque homine" (“I leave you free from both men”—meaning free from the political rule of both the Roman Emperor and the Pope).
