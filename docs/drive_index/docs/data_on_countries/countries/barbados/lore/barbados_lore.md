Anansi the Spider: A famous trickster figure originating from West African traditions who uses cleverness and wit to survive.

The Heart Man: A chilling urban legend of a man dressed in black driving a black hearse who targeted disobedient children.

The Baku (or Baccoo): A small, dwarf-like creature living in a bottle that can grant wishes or cause chaos depending on how it is treated.

The Steel Donkey & Lagahoo: Mythical nocturnal entities said to roam rural or wooded roads late at night.

Duppies: Restless spirits or ghosts of the dead that can return to haunt the living.

Protective Customs: Traditional ways to keep duppies away include sprinkling rum on the ground, walking backward into a house, and hanging specific herbs by doors and windows.

Crop Over: A historic harvest celebration marking the end of the sugar cane season, evolving into a major national festival.

The Barbados Landship: A unique 19th-century organization and theatrical group performing dances that mimic British Navy maneuvers to African rhythms.

Folk Characters in Play: Traditional street characters like the Shaggy Bear, Mudda Sally, and Stilt-men appear during local celebrations.
