Rangi and Papa: The primordial sky father and earth mother whose tight embrace kept the world in darkness until their children forced them apart to let light into the world. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]

Māui: The resourceful demigod credited with fishing up the North Island (Te Ika-a-Māui) using a magic jawbone hook, with the South Island serving as his canoe (Te Waka o Aoraki). He also snared the sun to slow its journey across the sky.

The Milky Way: Formed when Māui placed the giant shark Māngōroa high up into the night sky.

Taniwha: Water spirits or dragon-like creatures residing in deep pools, rivers, or the sea; they can act as fierce maneaters or protective tribal guardians.

Patupaiarehe: Mysterious, pale-skinned forest fairies said to live in misty mountain ranges, playing flutes and avoiding the direct sunlight.

Hokioi: A massive, nocturnal mythical bird of prey whose haunting cry foretold impending death or disaster.
