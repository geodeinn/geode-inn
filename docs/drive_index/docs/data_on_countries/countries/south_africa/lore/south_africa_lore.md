Grootslang: A colossal elephant-headed, serpent-bodied monster said to dwell in a deep cave (the "Wonder Hole") in the Richtersveld, fiercely guarding a hoard of diamonds.

Inkanyamba: A massive, carnivorous river serpent believed by the Zulu people to live in the deep pools near waterfalls, such as Howick Falls, capable of stirring up fierce storms.

Antjie Somers: A popular Cape folklore bogeyman—often described as a man who dresses in women's clothing—used to frighten disobedient children into behaving.

Tokoloshe: A mischievous and wicked dwarf-like spirit in Zulu mythology that can become invisible and cause trouble or illness unless bed legs are raised off the ground.

The Hare: The ultimate clever hero of southern African animal tales, frequently outwitting larger, more powerful adversaries like the lion or elephant.

The Jackal: Portrayed as cunning, slick, and self-serving, often matching wits or competing directly with the hare and the lion.

The Tortoise: Uses slow and steady cleverness to navigate out of tight spots created by his own or others' mischief.

The Praying Mantis (Cagn): Regarded by the San people as a vital divine creator figure, culture hero, and messenger from the spiritual realm.

Stellar Lore: Indigenous groups across the region held distinct beliefs about the heavens; for instance, the Karanga viewed stars as the eyes of the dead, while other groups thought them to be holes in a solid rock sky.
