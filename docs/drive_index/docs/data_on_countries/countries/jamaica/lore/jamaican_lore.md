Anansi is a cunning spider who originated from the Ashanti people of West Africa.  He uses his wit to outsmart physically superior animals (like Brother Tiger or Brother Snake) to survive and teach moral lessons.  In one famous tale, Anansi tried to hoard all the world's wisdom in a pot, but dropped it, shattering the pot and scattering wisdom across the earth for everyone to share.

In Jamaica, ghosts and restless spirits are referred to as duppies.

The Rolling Calf: The ghost of a wicked person—often a dishonest butcher. It appears at night as a fearsome, fire-breathing creature with clattering chains, haunting road intersections.

Ol' Higue (or Soucouyant): A shape-shifting witch who sheds her human skin at night, becomes a ball of fire, and sneaks into homes to suck the breath or blood of infants.

The Blackheart Man: A shadowy, elusive boogeyman figure often used to keep children out of harm's way.

River Mumma: A beautiful, half-human, half-fish river spirit. She guards fresh waterways, often leaving a golden comb on a rock to lure unsuspecting victims underwater.

Three Finger Jack: Jack Mansong, an escaped enslaved man who led a rebellion in the St. Thomas mountains in the 1700s. He used Obeah (Caribbean folk magic) to make himself invulnerable, becoming a legendary folk hero of resistance.

The White Witch of Rose Hall: The legend of Annie Palmer, an 1800s plantation owner accused of practicing voodoo, torturing enslaved people, and murdering her three husbands. Her spirit is said to still haunt the Rose Hall Great House in Montego Bay.

Nine Nights: An extended, celebratory wake lasting nine days. It features music, dancing, and food, originally performed to ensure the deceased person's spirit is put to rest and doesn't return as a duppy.

Jonkunnu (John Canoe): A traditional masquerade parade held around Christmas, featuring dancers wearing elaborate headpieces and masks.
