The Old Higue: A wicked witch who sheds her human skin at night, transforms into a ball of fire, and sneaks into homes to suck the blood of sleeping babies. Villagers often protect themselves by scattering uncooked rice at their doors, which the spirit is compelled to count all night. [1, 2]

The Baccoo: A short, mischievous spirit without kneecaps that lives on milk and bananas. Known for bringing both immense wealth and devastating chaos to its owner, it can be trapped inside a corked glass bottle and thrown into a river.

The Moon Gazer: A towering, shadowy figure (sometimes described as 12 feet tall) that stands on quiet roads or cliffs during full moons, crushing anyone who walks into its path.

The Bush Dai Dai (Bush Yai Yai): A squat, hideous forest spirit that protects the jungle and its mineral wealth. It can lure travelers into the bush by transforming into a beautiful woman, only to turn into a wild beast and devour them.

The Massacura Man: Often associated with the dense waterways, this water spirit is steeped in history and was said to be named after the armed trackers once used by plantation owners to hunt down runaway slaves.

Water Mamas: River-dwelling water spirits (known as Tuenkaron in Makushi) that represent the Indigenous people's ancient history of European contact, alluring travelers with promises of capricious wealth.

The Silk Cotton Tree: Considered the most feared haunt in Guyanese folklore. These massive trees are said to be the home of wandering spirits, jumbies, and slaves whose blood was spilled at their roots.

Amerindian Legends: The nine Indigenous tribes of Guyana possess vibrant oral histories.
