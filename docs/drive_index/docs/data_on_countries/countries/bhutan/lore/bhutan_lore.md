  The Divine Madman (Drukpa Kunley): One of Bhutan’s most famous saints used unorthodox methods—like humor, sarcasm, and "crazy wisdom"—to spread Buddhism in the 15th century. He is most famous for subduing a terrifying demoness at the Dochula Pass and trapping her with his "thunderbolt of flaming wisdom" (phallus), which is why phalluses are still painted on village walls as protective symbols. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]

The Four Mythical Animals: These are central to Bhutanese iconography and represent sacred Buddhist qualities. They include the Garuda (wisdom/power), the Dragon (vast vision), the Snow Lion (joy/humility), and the Tiger (confidence). [1]

The Yeti (Migoi): In Bhutanese lore, the Yeti is not always a terrifying monster. Legends, such as the tale of the shepherdess Pemma, portray the Yeti as a mountain guardian and a gentle protector that guides lost travelers to safety.

Mythological Creatures & Spirits: The Takin, Bhutan's national animal, was said to be magically created by Drukpa Kunley using the bones of a cow and a goat. Locals also revere Yeshey Gonpo (Mahakala, a wrathful protector) and Aum Jomo, a wise, fierce mountain spirit governing weather and animals.

Classic Tales & Heroic Quests: Folktales often deal with overcoming dark forces. For example, the tale of Bumu Sinci details a clever young girl who outwits a flesh-eating demon (Simple) to free his captives, or the legend of Nyala Duem, a fearsome demoness subdued by local heroes.
