The Miengu (Singular: Jengu): Celebrated water spirits in the traditional beliefs of the coastal Sawa people. Often described as beautiful mermaid-like figures with long hair and gap-toothed smiles, they are believed to act as intermediaries between the spiritual and human worlds. [1, 2]

Efasa Moto: The formidable mountain god of the Bakweri people near Mount Cameroon. He is believed to be half-human, half-stone, and his anger causes natural disasters.

The Ebigane: A fearsome monster from the Fang legends (Mvett) that can shape-shift between human and animal forms—often described as a terrifying cross between a bat, a buffalo, and a vampire.

Creation Myths: Stories vary by region, such as the Bakundu creation myth, which explores the origin of both good and evil races on Earth, and the Great Spirit Gba-Só worshiped by the Gbaya people in the East.

The Tortoise: In the coastal regions (Duala/Batanga), the tortoise is revered in folklore as a clever genius. Conversely, savannah tribes like the Bafia view the tortoise as an ominous bringer of trouble.

Trickster and Orphan Tales: Common moral allegories, such as the Sense-Pass-King trope or orphan heroines, center on outsmarting the proud and punishing jealousy, pride, and gluttony.
