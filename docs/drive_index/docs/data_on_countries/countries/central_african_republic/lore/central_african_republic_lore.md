In Mandjia and Banda folklore, Tere is a major supernatural, trickster figure. Represented as an ambiguous character—part human, part deity—Tere often outwits larger jungle animals (like the leopard or elephant) using cunning and deceit. However, just as frequently, his own greed and foolishness backfire, teaching important lessons about humility, respect, and community values.

Because much of the country lies within the Congo Basin rainforest, the animal kingdom features prominently in CAR folklore. Fables typically use animals to personify human traits:

The Turtle/Tortoise: Often depicted as the wisest and most resilient character, utilizing intelligence to overcome brute strength.

The Leopard: Symbolizes danger, arrogance, and physical power.

The Spider: A classic trickster figure in Central and West Africa, known for outsmarting larger, more dangerous predators.

Many local legends are tied directly to the natural landscape. A famous example is the legend of the Lake of Sorcerers (Lake Mamelles), located near the town of Bocaranga. According to local myth, a woman appeared alone on the shores of a newly formed black lake, laughing and crying simultaneously. The lake’s unfathomable depth and unvarying water levels across the dry and rainy seasons contribute to its supernatural reputation.

Storytelling serves as an educational tool, preserving social harmony, passing down ancestral wisdom, and reinforcing the community’s connection to the land. These tales are traditionally shared at family gatherings, agricultural festivals, and communal ceremonies, accompanied by dance, music, and traditional puppet theater.
