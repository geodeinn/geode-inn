Ninki Nanka: This is Gambia's most famous and feared legendary creature. Often described as a massive, reptilian monster with the body of a crocodile, a giraffe-like neck, and three horns, it is said to live in swampy forests and deep river caves. Sightings are historically viewed as omens of death, and folklore dictates the only way to kill it is by showing the beast its own reflection.

The Samay: Among the Diola people, the Samay is a revered mythological figure representing a two-horned deer. Functioning as a master of ceremonies and an enforcer of community order, the Samay is believed to know everything that happens in the village and wields a stick to keep the peace during festivals.

Hare and Hyena: These are the most common protagonists. The Hyena represents greed, deceit, and foolishness, while the smaller Hare uses his sharp wits and ingenuity to constantly outsmart the Hyena and teach him a lesson.

Cat and Rats: Another popular fable features a cat that goes on a pilgrimage to Mecca and returns home acting self-righteous. The eldest rat challenges the cat's sudden transformation, reminding him of his predatory, deceptive old ways to teach a lesson against hypocrisy.

Massaneh Ceesay and Bakary Niuminko: This is a famous legend about two men who fought for the hand of a beautiful Banjulian girl named Fatou. To win her affection, both men consulted local marabouts and utilized powerful mysticism.

Legends of the Silk Cotton Tree: The towering Kapok (or Silk Cotton) tree is central to Gambian superstition. Traditionally, it is believed to be the home of spirits, acting as a gateway to the unseen world, while ecologically anchoring the river ecosystem and protecting biodiversity.
