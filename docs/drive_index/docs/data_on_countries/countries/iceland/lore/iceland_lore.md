Unlike the short, pointy-eared elves in pop culture, the huldufólk are human-sized, beautiful beings who live in rocks, cliffs, and hills.

Habitat: They are invisible to the human eye unless they choose to reveal themselves, often on New Year's Eve or the summer solstice.

Road Construction: Many Icelandic roads are diverted around massive boulders or hills to avoid disturbing the hidden people. You can even find tiny wooden houses, called álfhóll, built for elves to inhabit.

In Icelandic legends, trolls are colossal, often slow-witted giants who reside in the harsh mountains.

The Rules of Daylight: Trolls are petrified (turned into stone) if they are exposed to the sun's rays.

Specific Locations: This legend is used to explain many of Iceland's strange rock formations. For example, the striking Reynisdrangar sea stacks off the coast of Vík are said to be the silhouettes of two trolls who were caught by the morning sun while attempting to haul a ship to shore.

The Troll Gateway: The dark Dimmuborgir lava field in northern Iceland is frequently described as the gateway to Hell and the home of the most famous troll parents, Grýla and Leppalúði.

Grýla and the Cat: Grýla is a terrifying, child-devouring ogress who prowls the winter countryside for misbehaving children. She is accompanied by the Jólakötturinn (Yule Cat), a giant feline that eats anyone who doesn't receive new clothes for Christmas.

The 13 Yule Lads: Grýla’s 13 sons visit human settlements one by one in the 13 nights leading up to Christmas. While they were originally malevolent pranksters, today they leave small gifts in children's shoes (or rotting potatoes for those who misbehave).

The Land Wights (Landvættir): These are the four mythical guardians that protect the four quarters of Iceland: a dragon in the east, a griffin in the north, a bull in the west, and a mountain giant in the south. They were even said to have frightened away a sorcerer attempting to invade Iceland in the form of a whale.

Lake Monsters: The Lagarfljót Worm in east Iceland is an aquatic monster similar to the Loch Ness Monster, while maritime tales abound of terrifying sea creatures known as skrímsli.
