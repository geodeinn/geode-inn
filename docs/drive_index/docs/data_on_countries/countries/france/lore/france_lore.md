The Tarasque (Provence): A terrifying hybrid monster with a lion's head, six legs, a bull's body, and a turtle's shell that terrorized the Rhône river before being tamed by Saint Martha.

The Beast of Gévaudan (Lozère): A real-life historical mystery in the 1760s where a giant, wolf-like creature terrorized the countryside, killing dozens of people.

Mélusine (Poitou): A beautiful water spirit who transforms into a half-serpent every Saturday. She married a lord on the condition he never spy on her, leading to tragedy when he broke his promise.

The Questing Beast (Arthurian): A creature with the head of a serpent, the body of a leopard, the buttocks of a lion, and the feet of a hare.

The Cheval Gauvin (Jura Mountains): A malevolent, demonic water horse that tries to lure riders to their doom.

Ankou (Brittany): The personification of death. Often depicted as a tall, gaunt man wearing a wide hat and long cloak, he drives a squeaking, skeletal cart to collect the souls of the deceased.

Dames Blanches (White Ladies): Female spirits and spectral fairies found across Normandy and the Pyrenees. They act as guardians of bridges and natural phenomena but will curse travelers who ignore or anger them.

Much of French lore overlaps with Celtic mythology, heavily localized in the Forest of Brocéliande (modern-day Paimpont).

Merlin & Viviane: It is here that Merlin was famously trapped in an invisible prison by the Lady of the Lake.

The Fountain of Barenton: A legendary enchanted spring where pouring water onto a specific rock is said to summon violent storms.

Charles Perrault: In the 17th century, he compiled foundational stories like Cinderella, Little Red Riding Hood, Sleeping Beauty, and Puss in Boots.

The Song of Roland (La Chanson de Roland): France's oldest surviving major work of literature. It tracks the semi-mythical, heroic last stand of Charlemagne’s knight, Roland, in the Pyrenees and the magic of his unbreakable sword, Durandal.
