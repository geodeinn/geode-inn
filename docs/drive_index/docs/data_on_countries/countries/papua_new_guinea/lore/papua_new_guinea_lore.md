Masalais (Nature Spirits): Local guardians or volatile entities residing in specific rocks, bends in rivers, or holy groves. For instance, looking directly at certain sacred forests is believed to invite a masalai-induced trance that causes a traveler to lose their sanity for seven days. [1, 2]

Nokoti / Nokondi: A famous bush spirit from the Eastern Highlands (specifically the Fore people). Though known for mischievous or criminal pranks against settlements, he is a cultural icon whose likeness is featured on the Eastern Highlands Province flag.

Mythical Creatures: Traditional lore includes entities like Erumía (a gigantic, stinging jellyfish from Papuan coastal waters) and Utumos (ghosts of decapitated men).

The Sky Rope (The Legend of Laka): An Enga Province tradition stating the sky used to sit much closer to earth, connected by a robust vine called Tany. A hunter named Laka climbed past the mist into the spirit realm, but when he stayed past sunset, the spirits severed the vine—transforming Laka into a shooting star and permanently raising the sky to its current height.

Water and Brotherhood Origins: Many coastal and river tribes (such as those along the Sepik River) share ancient motifs where a seismic strike from a sacred wooden drum or a primordial snake spirit split the earth, gushed forth the ocean, and divided brothers into distinct ancestral clans.
