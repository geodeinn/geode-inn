Birth of Aphrodite: The island's most famous myth. The goddess of love and beauty is said to have risen from the sea foam near Petra tou Romiou (Aphrodite's Rock) off the coast of Paphos. According to tradition, swimming around the rock on a full moon grants eternal youth and beauty.

Digenis Akritas: The quintessential Cypriot hero. This fearless, half-Arab and half-Byzantine warrior is famed for his supernatural strength. Legend says he created the Pentadaktylos (Five Finger) mountain range in North Cyprus by leaving his handprint as he leapt across the sea to defend the island.

King Pygmalion & Galatea: The ancient king of Cyprus who, disillusioned by mortal women, sculpted a statue so perfect he fell in love with it. He prayed to Aphrodite to bring her to life, and his wish was granted.

The Kallikantzaroi: Mischievous, goblin-like creatures believed to live underground for most of the year. They emerge during the 12 days of Christmas to wreak havoc on households, only to retreat underground on the Epiphany.

Ayia Napa Sea Serpent: A local monster that dwells in the waters of Cape Greco. Described as half-crocodile and half-serpent, it is said to have been spotted since Roman times and is known to locals as a gentle protector of the sea.

Kataklysmos: The Festival of the Flood, celebrating the story of Noah's Ark as well as the birth of Aphrodite. Celebrated 50 days after Orthodox Easter in coastal cities like Larnaca, it features water-fights, folk dancing, and sea-sports.

Dragons of the Heartland: Stemming from a blend of myth and St. George, village fountains throughout Cyprus feature tales of dragons and mythical beasts.

Cypriot folklore is brought to life through traditional instruments like the violin and laouto (lute). Folk dances such as the Karsilama (a face-to-face dance between couples) and Sirto (a circular wrist-hold dance) are staples of village weddings and communal gatherings.
