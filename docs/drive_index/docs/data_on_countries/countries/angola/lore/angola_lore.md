Kianda: The sacred water goddess or mermaid of rivers and lakes, known to offer wealth to the pure of heart or punish the greedy.

Kishi: A dangerous, two-faced spirit that looks like a handsome man from the front and a ravenous hyena from the back, used in cautionary tales to lure unwary villagers.

Ma-Kishi: Mythical entities or masked ancestral figures featured in traditional stories of vigilance, wit, and survival.

Musoso: The native term for fictitious or artistic folktales, which often explain social customs, laws, and origins (aetiologic tales).

Kimanaueze: A recurring mythological figure functioning as the king or father of fictitious heroines in traditional narratives.

Animal Fables: Frequently feature trickster figures like the rabbit (Kabulu) or tortoise who outsmart larger predators like leopards and pythons.
