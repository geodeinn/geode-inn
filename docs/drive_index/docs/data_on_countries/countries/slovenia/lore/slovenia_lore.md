Kurent (Korant): The most famous traditional mascot of Slovenian folklore, a chaotic and joyful pagan spirit clad in sheepskins and cowbells meant to chase away winter. [1, 2]

Martin Krpan: A folksy, ultra-strong fictional giant-slayer from an inner Carniolan village who traded contraband English salt and saved the Viennese court from a brute.

King Matjaž: A mythical, sleeping good king of Carantania who waits inside a mountain with his army until his beard grows around a stone table nine times.

Kekec: A brave, clever fictional shepherd boy from the Slovenian mountains who outwits wicked peddlers and giants in regional children's stories.

Zlatorog (Goldenhorn): A magical white alpine ibex/chamois living near Mount Triglav whose golden horns unlock a massive mountain treasure.

Ajdi: A race of benevolent or fearsome pre-Christian giants said to have built the early settlements and tossed massive boulders across the alpine valleys.

Žalik Žene / Bele Žene ("White Women"): Benevolent mountain fairies or nymphs who helped local shepherds, spun thread, and tended to flocks.

Perkmandlc: A helpful or mischievous mining gnome living in deep shafts around towns like Trbovlje and Velenje, warning miners of danger.

Ljubljana Dragon: The fierce protector of the capital city, whose statues now decorate the famous Dragon Bridge.
