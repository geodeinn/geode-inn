The dense Congolese rainforest and its vast river systems act as the backdrop for most mythological entities, viewed as conscious, spiritual realms.

Biloko: In the mythology of the Mongo people, these are lingering, vengeful spirits of ancestors who roam the deep forests as vicious, dwarf-like entities. They are often characterized by insatiable appetites and serve as a warning against wandering into the jungle unprepared.

Minkisi (Singular: Nkisi): Deeply rooted in Bakongo mythology, these are both supernatural spirits and the charms or objects that house their power. They act as intermediaries to heal the sick, maintain balance, and solve disputes.

Nzambi Mpungu: The supreme creator god in many Bantu and Bakongo traditions. He is often distant but occasionally intervenes in worldly affairs.

Bokele: A famous hero figure who, in regional mythology, stole the sun from the sky to bring light and warmth to his village.

Lukasa Boards: Unique cultural artifacts belonging to the Luba people; these are intricate, beaded wooden boards that act as historical records and tools for oral storytelling.

The Tortoise: Frequently depicted as the cleverest animal, using its intelligence and patience to outwit larger predators like leopards and eagles, representing the triumph of brains over brawn.

The Spider: Another celebrated trickster figure who frequently embarks on perilous journeys—such as retrieving heavenly fire—to prove his worth or secure a bride.

Mokele-mbembe: Perhaps the most famous legend. Indigenous folklore describes this as a massive, amphibious, plant-eating water monster that resides in the swamps of the Congo River basin, often described in terms reminiscent of a sauropod dinosaur.

J'ba Fofi: Enormous, spider-like creatures said to build camouflaged webs on the forest floor.
