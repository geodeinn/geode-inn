The Three Sisters (Drei Schwestern): This tale explains the origin of the striking rock formation high above the Rhine Valley. Three young sisters skipped church on Lady’s Day (August 15) to go berry picking. When they refused to share their berries with a beautiful woman, she turned them into the three giant rocks that stand as the Drei Schwestern mountain chain today.

The Lochgass Horse: Legend tells of a horse thief in Vaduz who stole a beautiful white horse on Christmas Eve. The animal, which was the Devil in disguise, rode the man wildly across the valley before throwing him and breaking his neck.

The Golden Boos: This popular tale features a stout woman carrying a chest on her back. When she stops at an inn, a small man climbs out of the chest and robs the place blind.

The Guschg Herdsmen's Doll: Herdsmen spending the summer in mountain cabins crafted a life-size rag doll to entertain themselves. In the autumn, the doll came to life and demanded one herdsman stay with her. She subsequently skinned him and hung his skin on the cottage roof to dry, sitting next to it and laughing.

The Wildmannli: Known as the "Wild Men," these are fur-covered, bear-like creatures from the mountains who wore no clothes. They were generally helpful, often caring for cattle, calling local midwives when needed, and turning coal into shiny coins for good people.

Hans Jöri: During the intense witch hunts of the 17th century, the region generated tales of witchcraft. A famous legend details a fiddler who unknowingly played at a witches' party. When he refused to toast to the Devil and instead toasted to himself, the witches vanished, and he awoke at a scaffold holding a bleeding ox's hoof.
