At the heart of Bakongo cosmology is Nzambi Mpungu, the supreme creator and ruler of the earth and the heavens.

Nzazi: The god of thunder who commands a pack of lightning dogs.

Mbumba: The rainbow serpent who controls the waters and often conflicts with Nzazi for control of the world.

Simbis: Revered nature spirits who guard the rivers, forests, and local ecosystems.

Mokele-mbembe: A legendary, semi-aquatic monster—often described as dinosaur-like—said to inhabit the deep river systems like Lake Télé.

Biloko: Cannibalistic trolls or dwarves from local mythology known to lure travelers deep into the jungle.

Indombe: A colossal, mythological copper snake several miles long, said to live in the canopy of the forest.

The Tortoise frequently appears as the ultimate trickster hero, using cleverness and wit to outsmart much larger, stronger animals like the Elephant and Hippopotamus.

Tales often explore themes of jealousy, humility, twin brothers, and magical elements like the Magic Tree that imprisons and frees villagers along the river.

Nkisi: Central to Congolese spirituality are Nkisi—sacred objects, often carved wooden figures, that contain spiritual forces or medicines to heal the sick, offer protection, and govern social laws.

Preservation: Modern projects like the Odzala-Kokoua National Park-based initiatives have actively gathered traditional myths and paired them with modern visual storytelling to keep these oral narratives alive today.
