Khodumodumo (or Khanyapa): A massive, gluttonous monster that swallowed every human and animal in its path. In the epic tale of Ditaolane, only one woman survived by hiding in ashes, and she gave birth to a miraculous boy who eventually cut the monster open to free the swallowed villagers. [1, 2]

Monyohe: The Great Water Snake. Monyohe is deeply tied to the landscape of Lesotho, symbolizing fertility, procreation, and rainfall. In many tales, a chief’s daughter must marry the snake to restore access to water during severe droughts.

Nanabolele: A bioluminescent, reptilian creature that glows in the dark like stars and lives in deep waters. Its hide is considered incredibly valuable and deadly.

'Mutla (The Hare): The ultimate trickster. 'Mutla constantly outsmarts larger predators like the wolf or jackal through quick thinking and deception.

The Talking Bird: A motif found in tales like Masilo and Marcelon. When a greedy brother murders the other for a miraculous cow, a talking bird trails him back to the village and exposes his crimes to the villagers.

Storytelling (Ts'omo): Folktales were traditionally told in the evening around the fire. They are highly interactive, often featuring songs and choruses that the audience—typically children—must sing to help the story progress.

The Origin of the Mokorotlo: Lesotho's iconic, conical straw hat is said to have been designed in the shape of Qiloane Mountain, a prominent peak near the historic fortress of Thaba Bosiu.
