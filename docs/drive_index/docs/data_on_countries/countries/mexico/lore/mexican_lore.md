La Llorona: The weeping ghost of a woman named María who searches near rivers for the children she drowned.

El Charro Negro: A phantom horseman dressed in black who offers wealth and misfortune to midnight travelers.

El Sombrerón: A shadowy figure wearing a huge hat who wanders through rural towns.

Nahuales: Human sorcerers or shamans possessing the power to shapeshift into animals like jaguars or coyotes.

Aluxes & Chaneques: Mischievous, elf-like nature spirits that guard the forests, fields, and sacred corn crops.

Ahuízotl: A mythical dog-like water creature from Aztec lore with a monkey-like hand on its tail.

Día de los Muertos (Day of the Dead): A November festival honoring departed loved ones with vibrant marigold paths, music, and food-laden altars rather than fear of death.

Corridos: Narrative folk ballads that historically recorded the real-life struggles, heroes, and rebellions of ordinary people.
