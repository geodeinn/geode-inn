The San Creation Story: The supreme creator god, Kang (or Cagn), made the world and all animals. In the beginning, humans and animals could speak to one another. However, after humans disobeyed Kang by lighting a fire above ground, the harmony was broken, and humans permanently lost the ability to communicate with wildlife.

The Sacred Hills: The San people believe the Tsodilo Hills (known as the "Female Hill") in the Kalahari are the resting place for the spirits of the dead, with powerful gods said to live in hidden caves from which they rule the world.

Kadimba (The Hare): The ultimate trickster. Despite his small size, Kadimba consistently outsmarts larger predators like the Elephant and the Lion by using wit, speed, and clever riddles.

Phiri (The Hyena): Often depicted as greedy, foolish, and easily duped by the smarter animals in the bush.

The Tladi (Lightning Bird): A powerful mythical bird deeply feared in Batswana culture, believed to physically manifest as lightning and bring fire from the heavens.

Totems: The Batswana tribes are organized by totems (morafe) based on specific animals. For example, the Batawana tribe’s totem is the lion, and the Basubiya revere the hippopotamus.

Birds and the Afterlife: The hamerkop (mmamasiloanoka) and the owl (morubisi) are frequently mentioned in myths regarding death, distress, and the spiritual realm.
