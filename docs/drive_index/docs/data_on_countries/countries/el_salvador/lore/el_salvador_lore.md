La Siguanaba: A terrifying specter who targets unfaithful men and womanizers. She appears at night near bodies of water as a beautiful, alluring woman bathing with a golden gourd. When a man approaches, she turns to face them—revealing a horse-like skull or horrifying face—and drives them mad.

El Cipitío: The son of La Siguanaba, condemned to remain a 10-year-old child forever as part of a divine curse. He has a massive pot-belly, wears a huge palm hat, and has backward-pointing feet. He is not malicious, but acts as a playful trickster, eating ashes and throwing flowers at young women.

El Cadejo: A pair of supernatural dogs that roam the nights. The white Cadejo is a benevolent spirit that protects travelers and single men, while the black Cadejo is a hostile, glowing-eyed demon that threatens them.

La Carreta Chillona (The Squeaky Wagon): An ominous, bone-rattling wooden cart driven by a condemned spirit. It roams empty midnight streets, often accompanied by the ghost of El Padre Sin Cabeza (The Headless Priest), and serves as an omen of doom.

La Lechuza (The Owl Witch): An owl-witch who transforms by chanting or removing her legs. The sudden screech or presence of an owl near a house is said to bring bad luck or indicate that a witch is looking for a victim.

El Tabudo: Known as the "Man with Big Knees," this legend tells of a wealthy fisherman who was taken by the sea and reappeared as a half-human, half-fish entity. He tricks unsuspecting visitors into following him to the middle of lakes before transforming them into sirens or fish.

The Cuzcachapa Lagoon Siren: A tragic, mythical maiden who is said to emerge from the waters of the lagoon on full-moon nights, mesmerizing fishermen and luring them to the depths of the water.
