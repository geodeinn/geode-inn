El Lobizón: A werewolf-like creature. Legend says the seventh consecutive son turns into a beast on full moons. To stop this curse, the sitting president of Argentina traditionally becomes the child's godfather. [1, 2]

Luz Mala: A glowing light seen floating over rural roads at night. Country folk believe it represents the souls of the dead and avoid it.

El Pombero: A short, hairy goblin from Guaraní tradition in the northern forests. He protects nature and plays tricks on people who disrespect the woods.

Pachamama: An earth goddess worshipped by indigenous Andean communities. People give her offerings of food, wine, and leaves to show thanks and ask for good crops

Gauchito Gil: A legendary outlaw from Corrientes Province who was hung upside down from a tree in 1878. Shrines wrapped in red flags dedicated to him dot roadsides across the country for miracles and healing.

Difunta Correa: A woman who died of thirst in the desert while trying to save her baby. Her shrine is a major pilgrimage site where travelers leave bottles of water.

Beyond Tango: While tango belongs to Buenos Aires, regional folk music thrives in community halls called peñas.

Chacarera and Zamba: Fast and slow partnered folk dances originating from the northwest.

Chamamé: A lively, accordion-based accordion style from the northeast mixing Guaraní and European sounds.
