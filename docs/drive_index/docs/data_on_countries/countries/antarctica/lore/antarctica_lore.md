Terra Australis Incognita: Ancient Greek philosophers theorized a massive unknown southern land must exist to "balance" the weight of the landmasses in the Northern Hemisphere. [1]

Mythological Cartography: Early mapmakers drew imaginative boundaries at the bottom of the world, populating them with dragons, uncharted hazards, and abyssal sea monsters.

Underworld Topography: Sealers and researchers later mirrored these classical fables by naming harsh or volcanic regions after mythological sites, such as Neptune's Bellows or Lucifer Crags.

Haunted Huts: Historic outposts like Scott's Hut at Cape Evans are tied to local lore of lingering dread, shadowy apparitions, and the restless souls of perished polar parties.

The Third Man Syndrome: Stranded or imperiled explorers frequently report experiencing a comforting, watchful, and unexplained "presence" or companion helping them through survival crises.

Mount Erebus Tragedies: Staff at modern stations like McMurdo carry urban legends regarding residual hauntings tied to the 1979 tragic Mount Erebus disaster.

The Ningen: A contemporary Japanese internet cryptid describing a colossal, pale, whale-like humanoid supposedly sighted by research vessels in southern waters.

Lovecraftian Horrors: H.P. Lovecraft’s novella At the Mountains of Madness cemented Antarctica in modern pop culture as the hidden site of ancient, alien "Elder Things" and forbidden plateaus.

Conspiracy Lore: Popular internet culture frequently recycles fringe ideas about hidden Nazi redoubts, subterranean alien bases, or lost civilizations locked beneath miles of thick ice.
