The Kikuyu (Agĩkũyũ): According to folk history, the creator deity Ngai placed the first man, Gikuyu, and woman, Mumbi, on earth at Mukure wa Nyagathanga near Murang'a. They were given a vast expanse of land under Mount Kenya (Kirinyaga) and blessed with nine daughters.

The Maasai: The Maasai believe their pastoralist heritage was gifted by Enkai, the supreme creator. As their legend goes, the creator lowered the Maasai, along with the Dorobo and Kikuyu, from heaven using a leather thong.

The Luhya (Bukusu): The Bukusu creation and settlement myths focus heavily on Wele Khakaba (the provider) and emphasize collaboration, familial ties, and the spiritual relationship between their people and the land.

Lwanda Magere (The Luo): A legendary warrior whose flesh was made of stone, making him invincible against enemy arrows and spears. Folklore details his incredible strength in defending his clan, as well as his tragic downfall.

Mekatilili Wa Menza (The Giriama): Known as a wonder woman and a fierce 20th-century freedom fighter, her legends recount her rallying her people to resist British colonial rule.

Syokimau (The Kamba): A 19th-century prophetess who foresaw the building of the "iron snake" (the Kenya-Uganda railway) and people with foreign skin before colonization.

Mugo wa Kibiru (The Kikuyu): A seer who predicted the coming of white settlers and the fall of a massive fig tree in Thika that symbolized the eventual end of colonial rule.

The Nandi Bear: A creature from Western Kenya said to stalk the deep forests, often described as a massive, fierce, and aggressive animal.

The Crowing Crested Cobra: A venomous coastal legend. This large snake was said to have a red crest, and the males were rumored to crow like a rooster.
