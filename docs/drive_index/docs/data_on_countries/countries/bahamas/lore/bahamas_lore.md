Lusca: A giant half-shark, half-octopus creature said to live in the blue holes and deep underwater caves around Andros Island. Its breathing is believed to cause powerful tidal currents and whirlpools.

Chickcharnies: Small, mischievous, three-toed sprites with red eyes and feathery bodies that hang upside down from pine trees on Andros Island. Legend says they bring good luck if treated well or turn a person's head backward if angered.

Pretty Molly Bay: A haunting spirit from Exuma; tales describe her either as a drowned slave roaming the beach or a young woman transformed into a mermaid. [

B'Bookie and B'Rabbie: Central figures of Bahamian storytelling (comparable to Brer Rabbit and Brer Fox), these trickster tales use clever animals to outsmart larger foes or the Devil (B'Debbil).

Jack Tales: Folktales featuring a hero named Jack who uses wit and foresight to defeat ogres or supernatural adversaries in comically tense situations.

Witches: Commonly believed to transform into household animals like cats, rats, or short fat snakes adorned with ribbons.

Obeh: A system of traditional spiritual practices and healing, where practitioners could "fix" troubles or cast serious protective or binding curses.
