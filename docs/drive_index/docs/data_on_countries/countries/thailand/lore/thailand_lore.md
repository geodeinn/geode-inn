Phi Ta Khon: A famous masked ghost festival held in Loei Province to celebrate merit-making and local spirits.

Pret: Very tall, skinny hungry ghosts derived from Buddhist teachings with tiny needle-like mouths.

Mae Nak: The legendary, tragic ghost of a loyal wife who died during childbirth, whose shrine still stands in Bangkok.

Spirit Houses (San Phraphum): Small shrines placed outside homes and buildings to shelter local guardian spirits.

Naga (Phaya Nak): Semi-divine, dragon-like serpents believed to live in rivers like the Mekong, acting as water guardians.

Garuda (Krut): A powerful half-human, half-bird creature that serves as the national and royal emblem of Thailand.

Kinnaree: Graceful mythical beings from the mystical Himmapan Forest with the upper body of a human and the lower body of a bird.

Ramakien: The national Thai epic adapted from the Hindu Ramayana, detailing the battles and triumphs of Phra Ram.

Loi Krathong: A beautiful festival of lights on the full moon of the 12th lunar month to honor water spirits.
