The 37 Nats: A formalized pantheon of deified historical figures, heroes, and tragic spirits (Nat Sein) who command respect and localized shrines (Nat Sin) to avoid misfortune.

Ma Phae Wah: A terrifying graveyard ghost of midnight lore who wanders villages carrying a miniature coffin to mark households destined for tragedy.

Bo Bo Gyi: Ubiquitous guardian patron spirits found at the thresholds of towns, sacred trees, and ancient pagodas.

Thagyamin: The king of devas adapted from Hindu-Buddhist Sakka, whose annual descent to Earth marks the Thingyan Water Festival. He records good deeds in a golden book and sins in a dog-leather hide.

Creation Myths: Stories detailing the emergence of the cosmos from primordial waters where luminous beings gradually transformed into humans after consuming the Earth's rich soil.

Pyinsarupa: A symbolic creature composed of parts from five distinct animals (elephant, carp, horse, bull, and lion).

Manussiha (Manotethiha): Lion-bodied, human-headed sphinx-like guardian statues traditionally placed at the corners of Buddhist pagodas.

Kinnara & Kinnari: Mythical half-bird, half-human figures famous in regional dances and hill-state artistic traditions symbolizing eternal love.
