King Ravana: The ten-headed ruler of Lanka from the Ramayana epic, depicted in local tradition as a brilliant, devoted, and scientifically advanced scholar rather than just a villain. [1]

Prince Vijaya and Sinhabahu: The foundational myth of the Sinhala lineage, telling of a prince born to a lion-human union whose son Vijaya arrived to establish early Sri Lankan civilization.

Queen Vihara Maha Devi: A courageous queen who offered herself to the sea to appease the gods and later mothered national heroes.

King Kasyapa and Sigiriya: The dramatic legend of a patricidal king who built his palace atop a massive rock fortress.

Yakkas and Demons: Ancient spirits believed to inhabit forests and water bodies, thought to cause illnesses when angry and driven away through traditional exorcism and devil-dancing rituals (Sanni Yakuma).

Mahasona: The "Great Graveyard Spirit," a fierce nocturnal entity said to have the body of a man and the head of a bear.

Makara and Kinnara: Composite mythical creatures featuring parts of crocodiles, elephants, fish, and birds, frequently carved into temple architecture.

Gamarala: A stock village chieftain character in lighthearted moral tales, often portrayed as comical or simple-minded.

Andare: The royal court jester of southern Sri Lanka, famous for quick-witted, humorous retorts that poked fun at royal authority while sharing underlying truths.

Hidden Royal Treasures: Consensus on Reddit discussions reveals widespread folk beliefs regarding ancient buried treasures guarded by unseen forces and marked by rock carvings of animals like turtles or peacocks.
