The most significant historical legend in Ivorian folklore belongs to the Baoulé people, who are part of the broader Akan group. According to legend, during the 18th century, Queen Pokou led her people away from the expanding Ashanti Empire. Reaching the Komoe River, her followers faced certain capture by their enemies with no way across. The water spirits demanded the ultimate sacrifice: the Queen's only son. She cast him into the river, prompting a line of hippopotamuses to surface, allowing her people to safely walk across their backs. Distraught, the Queen uttered "bauli" ("the child is dead"), which gave the Baoulé people their name.

Masks are considered living entities and spiritual vessels in Ivory Coast, holding immense power and deeply tied to the land's folklore.

The Zaouli: Originating from the Guro people, this is widely considered one of the most technically demanding mask dances in the world. The breathtaking, rapid-fire footwork was created in honor of a legendary village woman or mother.

The Boloye (Panther Dance): A sacred, acrobatic ritual performed by the Senufo people in northern Ivory Coast. It is performed by initiated members of the secret Poro society to protect the village and honor the animal spirits of the bush.

The Zankallala and the Dodo: The Zankallala (a clever trickster bird) frequently appears in folklore outwitting the dodo—a fearsome, lumbering monster known as the "Swallower of Men". The story follows the bird getting repeatedly swallowed and magically bursting out of the monster's stomach, back, and finally its head to defeat it.

Nature Spirits: Folk tales often focus on the bush and its hidden spirits, which can bring either incredible blessings or terrible curses if the harmony of nature is disrupted.

Like much of West Africa, trickster tales feature clever animals standing in for human archetypes. The Tortoise represents wisdom, patience, and intellect, frequently using its brain to humiliate stronger, larger animals like the Elephant or Leopard. Conversely, the Dog often represents loyalty and fate, with various myths explaining how he acts as a bridge between the spirit world and humanity.
