The Creator God (Imana): In Burundian mythology, Imana is the supreme being and creator of the land, the rivers, and the people. He is often depicted in stories as walking among humans in the rolling hills of the central plateau. [1]

The Coming of Death: One of the most famous myths details how Death entered the human world. According to the tale, Imana was hunting Death to rid the world of it. Death disguised itself and hid inside a woman. Because she hid Death, Imana declared that Death would forever destroy her and all her descendants.

Ntare Rushatsi: The legendary founder of the Kingdom of Burundi, Ntare Rushatsi, is framed as a civilizing hero. According to legend, he possessed extraordinary strength, managed to kill a wild beast threatening the people, and established harmony across the land, setting the blueprint for the royal bloodline.

Ngabo the Brave Warrior: This legend honors selflessness. During a time of unrest, Ngabo stepped up as an elite warrior to defend his people, demonstrating that true courage comes from sacrifice and loyalty to the community rather than brute force.

Sacred Instruments: The drum is the absolute heart of Burundian folklore. The Ingoma are not mere instruments but symbols of power, truth, and community unity. Legend states that the god of music first struck the hide of the drum, cementing its sacred status.

The Sanctuary of Gishara: The tradition of the royal drums is famously tied to King Mwezi Gisabu, who sought refuge in the Gishara compound during the 19th century. According to lore, locals gifted him bulls for a feast, and their skins were used to create the first raw drums. The drumming continues to be celebrated to honor the bravery of the ancestors.
