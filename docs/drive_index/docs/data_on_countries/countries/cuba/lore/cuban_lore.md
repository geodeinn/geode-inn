Elegguá: The mischievous trickster and gatekeeper of all paths who dances between the living and spirit worlds.

Yemayá: The powerful goddess of the sea and mother of all.

Changó: The fiercely vibrant Orisha of thunder, lightning, and justice.

Resource: Explore Afro-Cuban deities at the Casa de Africa museum in Old Havana.

El Güije: A small, shadowy, mischievous goblin-like creature that lives in rivers and ponds.

La Madre de Aguas: A giant, mythical snake that guards the island's water sources and rivers.

El Cagüeiro: A famous shape-shifting myth from eastern Cuba capable of dangerous transformation.

La Luz de Yara: A popular legend of a glowing light seen at night near the town of Yara, originally believed to be the burning flames of the Taíno chief Hatuey.

Opia: Gentle spirits of children who passed away too young, thought to live between worlds and symbolized by fireflies.

La Llorona / La Gritona: The Cuban variation of the weeping woman haunting rural fields and mountain roads.

Martina Kukaracha: A famous Cuban/Spanish folktale of a beautiful Havana cockroach who holds court with various suitors before ultimately marrying a mouse.
