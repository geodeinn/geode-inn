Pachamama: The supreme Mother Earth goddess, revered across the Andean region. Bolivians perform daily rituals to her, such as "challa," which involves pouring a few drops of alcohol or burying symbolic objects before drinking or building.

El Tío: The legendary guardian spirit and "devil" of the mines. Miners in places like Potosí leave offerings of cigarettes, coca leaves, and alcohol to El Tío in exchange for protection and rich veins of ore.

Tunupa / The Mountains: In the southern altiplano, Tunupa is a mythic heroine who shaped the Andean landscape. Mountains like Illimani and Illampu are often personified as jealous warriors fighting over her, whose tears formed the landscape and the multi-colored kantuta flower.

Carnaval de Oruro: An epic UNESCO-recognized festival where thousands of dancers perform indigenous and colonial dances.

La Diablada (Dance of the Devils): Originating in Oruro, this dance features elaborate, terrifying devil masks and represents the triumph of good over evil.

Caporales: An energetic, modern folk dance based on the Afro-Bolivian Saya. Dancers wear jingling bells and bright velvet costumes, reenacting the movements of historic colonial ranch overseers.

Phujllay: A satirical, celebratory dance from Tarabuco that celebrates Indigenous courage and mocks Spanish Conquistadors by having dancers wear large, helmet-like hats.

Salar de Uyuni: The vast salt flats are said to be the result of a massive flood sent by the creator god Viracocha, or are alternatively tied to the tears of the goddess Tunupa.

Lake Titicaca: Legend dictates the sun and moon rose from this lake. It was also the birthplace of Manco Capac and Mama Ocllo, the founders of the Incan Empire.

El Toborochi: A Guarani legend tells the tale of Araverá, a woman who hid inside the amphora-shaped trunk of a toborochi tree. The tree blooms into a beautiful flower as her spirit comes out to attract hummingbirds.

Alasitas Fair: Held annually in La Paz, this festival honors Ekeko, the god of abundance. People buy miniature items (like tiny cars, money, or houses) hoping Ekeko will bless them with the real things in the coming year.
