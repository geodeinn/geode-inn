The Mvet: An epic musical performance and mythos of the Fang people. Accompanied by a harp-like instrument, "raconteurs" sing of mythical superheroes, gods, and legendary battles against forces of the universe. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]

The Bwiti Religion: An indigenous spiritual practice centered around ancestor veneration, polyrhythmic music, and the sacred Iboga root. Ceremonies often last all night long, featuring fire, dance, and spiritual journeys.

Ebigane: A terrifying, shapeshifting monster from Fang folklore. It is depicted as a mix between a bat, a buffalo, and a vampire, capable of diving from the sky and carrying off large prey like pythons.

Muniama: A legendary, malevolent sorcerer from village lore who returns in the form of a supernatural black cat to haunt those who banished him.

Animal Tricksters: Similar to many African traditions, Gabonese fables use animals to teach moral lessons. The Tortoise, in particular, frequently outsmarts larger, stronger predators like the Leopard through wit and cleverness.

Nature Spirits: Babongo and other forest peoples view the jungle as a nourishing entity, and tales often feature spirits of the river, trees, and the moon.
