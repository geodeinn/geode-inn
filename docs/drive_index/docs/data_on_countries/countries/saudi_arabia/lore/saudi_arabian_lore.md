Jinn and Ifrit: Supernatural spirits created from smokeless fire, capable of shape-shifting into humans or animals, acting as helpful or malevolent forces. [1, 2]

Ghouls: Pre-Islamic demon-like humanoids and shape-shifters said to haunt graveyards, ruins, and lonely deserts to prey on travelers. [1, 2]

Umm Al-Saaf and Al-Leil / Humar Al-Qaylah: Traditional cautionary bogeymen and witch-like figures used in regional folklore to deter children from wandering out at night.

Zarqa' Al-Yamama: A legendary pre-Islamic woman from the Yamama region famed for her extraordinary eyesight, able to spot enemy armies from days away.

The Saudi Ardah: The national sword dance originating from the Najd region

Al-Khutwah: A rhythmic step-and-clap tribal group dance native to the southern Aseer region.

The Northern Dahha: A performance featuring deep, roaring vocal sounds resembling lions, traditionally done to hype warriors before a battle.

Oral Poetry: Bedouin poetry detailing tribal history, heroism, farming, and romance passed down through generations.
