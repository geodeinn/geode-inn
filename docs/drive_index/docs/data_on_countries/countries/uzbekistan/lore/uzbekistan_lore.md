Alpamysh (Alpomish): The preeminent Turkic heroic epic celebrating unity, tribal resilience, and the hero’s trials.

Gorogly and Rustamkhan: Major romantic and martial epic cycles performed across regions.

Bakhshis: Traditional oral poet-singers who perform dastans using instruments like the dombyra, dutar, or kobyz via archaic vocal styles and guttural techniques.

Nature and Ancestral Spirits: Pre-Islamic remnants including water and wind personifications like Suvchi (water spirits), Choymomo, and seasonal or weather spirits like Ayamajuz.

Magical Beings: Figures inherited from ancient Central Asian mythologies such as devs (giants), peris (fairies), and dragons.

Hodja Nasreddin: A legendary satirical folkloric wise fool and trickster popular across regional Islamic oral traditions.

Samarkand and Bukhara Legends: Sacred geography stories attached to sites like the Shah-i-Zinda necropolis, the tomb of Kussama ibn Abbas, and the Chashma Ayub (Job’s Spring) in Bukhara.

Historical-Cultural Lore: Local folk histories romanticizing ancient figures, warrior women of the desert edges, and early hero-defenders like Shiroq and Tomyris.
