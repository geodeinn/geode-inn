The Anthropomorphic Coconut Tree: According to legend, the first settlers of the Maldives died in massive numbers. To help the survivors, a great sorcerer (fanḍita) cast a spell so that coconut trees sprouted from the skulls of the buried dead, giving the tree an anthropomorphic origin.

The Tuna Fish: Legend dictates that the valuable tuna was brought to Maldivian waters by a mythical seafarer called Bodu Niyami Kalēfanu, who traveled to the Dagas (a mythical tree at the edge of the world) to secure the fish.

The Rannamaari: The most famous Maldivian legend describes a sea monster that terrorized the ancient capital. Every month, islanders were forced to leave a young virgin at the temple, who would be sexually abused and killed by the monster. The monster was finally defeated when a Maghrebi traveler named Al-Barbari (Ibn Battuta in some variations) recited the Qur'an. This event is credited with the Maldives' conversion to Islam.

Handi & Ferēta: Dangerous, shape-shifting female spirits (handi) who would lure humans or mimic the voices of loved ones. The Furu Ferēta were malevolent reef monsters originating from the ocean.

Foolhudhigu Handi: A terrifying monster bound by its own umbilical cord that was famous for terrorizing humans.

Makana's Treachery: A wide variety of animal fables centered on the local fauna, such as the Mākana (the grey heron), who is often depicted in humorous or treacherous encounters with crabs, cats, and humans.

The Girl in the Shark's Belly: A classic fairytale-style myth recounting the adventures of ordinary islanders braving the open sea, navigating castaway situations, and dealing with cannibalistic tribes on isolated atolls.

The Moon in the Jar: Humorous stories focused on comical, ignorant kings and the clever responses they received from everyday subjects or quick-witted wise men.
