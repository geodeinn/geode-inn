The North (Atacama & Andes)

Alicanto: A magical, nocturnal bird that feeds on gold and silver ore. Its wings glow brilliantly in the dark. Miners follow it to strike it rich, but if they get greedy, the bird turns off its light and abandons them in the desert.

La Tirana: A legendary festival honoring an Inca princess who fell in love with a Spanish priest. It remains one of Chile's biggest religious celebrations.

El Chonchón: A severed, flying human head. Its ears grow into massive wings that allow it to soar through the night sky, bringing bad luck or death to those who hear its screech.

Pedro Urdemales: The ultimate Chilean folk trickster. Originating from Spanish tales but localized in Chile, he is a clever folk hero who outwits wealthy landowners, priests, and even the devil.

La Pincoya: A breathtakingly beautiful ocean goddess who protects the sea and rescues lost fishermen. Her dance on the beach indicates the ocean's bounty; if she faces the sea, fishing will be abundant, but if she faces the shore, a food shortage is coming.

El Trauco: A short, deformed dwarf living in the deep southern forests. He carries a stone axe and possesses a hypnotic, magnetic gaze that no woman can resist, making him a fixture in local origin stories.

El Caleuche: A mythical, glowing ghost ship crewed by drowned fishermen and sorcerers. It travels invisibly through the fog, hosting vibrant parties at night, and is believed to transport the souls of the ocean bound.

La Cueca: Chile's national dance. It is a flirtatious, courtship dance between a man and a woman who wave white handkerchiefs, representing the interactions between a rooster and a hen.

Trova & Canto Nuevo: Musical styles deeply rooted in rural farm life and urban social movements, famously using instruments like the guitarra, guitarrón chileno, and pandero.
