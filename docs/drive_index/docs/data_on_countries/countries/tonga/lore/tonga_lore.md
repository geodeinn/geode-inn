The Three Divine Siblings: The cosmos is divided among Tangaloa (sky god), Māui (earth/underworld demigod), and Hikuleʻo (ruler of Pulotu, the sacred spirit underworld).

Fishing Up the Islands: Māui or Tangaloa used a magical fishhook to pull the rocky islands up from the deep ocean floor.

ʻAhoʻeitu: The semi-divine son of the sky god Tangaloa ʻEitumatupuʻa and a mortal woman, Vaʻepopua, who became the very first Tuʻi Tonga (king of Tonga).

The Rat and the Octopus (Feke): A rat trapped at sea is saved by an octopus, but tricks the creature and escapes without paying. This explains why octopuses snatch at floating objects and bear spotted patterns on their heads.

The Origin of Kava: The sacrifice of a sacred child (Fevanga's daughter) at Eueiki island sprouts the first kava and sugarcane plants from her grave, forming the basis of the cultural kava ceremony.

Night Marchers (Fehikitakiʻa): Spectral groups of ancient warriors said to roam sacred paths and historical sites after dark.

Whistling at Night: A long-standing taboo warning people not to whistle after sunset because it invites wandering malevolent spirits (tevolo).
