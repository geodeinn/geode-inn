The Martyrdom: A young Christian woman from Corsica tortured and killed by Romans in the 3rd century.

The Journey: Her body was placed in a boat by priests guided by a white dove, washing ashore in Monaco where she was buried.

The Tradition: Commemorated every January 26 with a national holiday featuring the burning of a wooden fishing boat.

The Disguise: On January 8, 1297, François Grimaldi ("Malizia") dressed as a Franciscan monk to gain entry and seize the fortress on the Rock.

The Symbolism: Immortalized on Monaco's coat of arms, which is flanked by two sword-wielding monks.

The Myth: An angered woman (often said to be a wronged maiden or a witch) placed a curse on the family declaring that "never will a Grimaldi find happiness in marriage".

The Lore: Popularly tied by modern tabloids and storytellers to the various historical tragedies and marital breakups within the royal line.

Hercules Monoikos: Greek and Roman myths claim Hercules passed through the area, leaving a single temple that inspired the name Monoikos (single house).

The Olive Branch Rite: Families on Christmas Eve stir a burning Yule log with an olive branch dipped in old wine to invoke blessings and good health.
