Indigenous Legends (First Nations)

Glooscap: The Maliseet and Mi'kmaq people tell tales of the powerful creator, Glooscap. Legend dictates he created the Saint John River by killing a giant frog monster that was hogging all the water.

The Little People: Stories of Ke-Whis-Wask, tiny, secretive beings that live in the woods and interact with humans.

The Burning Ship: A famous phantom ship ablaze with fire is said to be seen drifting off the coast of the Acadian Peninsula, often foretelling harsh weather.

Aboiteaux and the Land: The earliest Acadian settlers didn't use modern machinery; instead, their folklore often highlights their ingenuity in reclaiming tidal salt marshes using a system of earthen dikes called aboiteaux.

The Dungarvon Whooper: Perhaps the most famous lumber camp ghost story in northern New Brunswick. It centers around the murder of an Irish cook in the 1800s, whose ghostly, shrieking wails were said to echo through the Dungarvon woods.

The Wizard of the Miramichi: Tales surround Joseph Cunard—a prominent 19th-century railway and timber baron—whose immense power and sudden financial ruin led locals to believe he possessed mystical, otherworldly control over the river.

Singing Spectres and Hellish Hounds: Folklore archives in the region, famously collected in Carole Spray’s Will o' the Wisp, detail tales of phantom headless women, glowing Will-o'-the-wisps, and phantom boats.
