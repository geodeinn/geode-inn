  Sedna (Mother of the Sea): The most significant figure in Arctic lore. Sedna was a human woman who was cast into the sea by her father; as she clung to the side of a kayak, her fingers were chopped off, transforming into the whales, seals, and walruses that sustain the Inuit. She lives at the bottom of the ocean and controls the migration of marine mammals.

Qallupilluit (or Qalupalik): Scaly, humanoid creatures that wear clothing made of moss and seaweed. They are said to live along the icy shorelines of Baffin Bay and steal children who wander too close to the water or wander off unattended.

Ijiraat: Shapeshifting spirits that can change into any Arctic animal. They are notoriously tricky and are often blamed for disorienting hunters and causing people to become lost on the tundra. They can never disguise their glowing red eyes.

Mahaha: A malicious demon that terrorizes the frozen wilderness. He is incredibly thin, wears no clothing, and tickles his victims to death while laughing maniacally.

Amarok: The great spirit of the wolf. While he can be a fearsome threat to solitary hunters in the dark, he is also known to help maintain the health of caribou herds by culling the weak.
