The Indigenous oral history of the Yukon—encompassing the Gwich’in, Hän, Kaska Dena, Tagish, Tlingit, and Tutchone peoples—serves both as cultural mythology and historical record of how people and the land evolved.

The Crow (Raven): Adopted as the Yukon's official bird, the Raven is the territory’s paramount creator and trickster. According to tradition, Crow created the rivers and mountains,and made the first men and women from cottonwood bark before establishing the dual-clan (Crow and Wolf) social system.

Smart Beaver: A legendary figure who traveled down the Yukon River, killing giant cannibalistic monsters and shrinking giant animals to the sizes we know today, making the land safe for humans.

Bushmen & Keelut: The "bushman" is a pervasive shadow-figure or woodland giant. Closely related to the Sasquatch, this mysterious figure is whispered about along the Takhini River and Labarge. In some Northern traditions, the Keelut (a hairless, killer land-spirit) is said to bring bad luck to anyone it tracks.

The Palraijuq: A terrifying creature from Inuit mythology. Described as a massive, six-legged crocodilian, the Palraijuq was said to lurk in lakes and swamps, waiting beneath the ice to snatch unsuspecting travelers.
