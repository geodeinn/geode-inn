The Giant Beavers: In Dene folklore, Hachoghe (the Giant) fought and defeated three colossal beavers to reshape the land, while other legends feature Yamǫ́rıa traveling the territory taming the giant animals.

The Great Flood: Passed down by the Inuvialuit, this narrative explains how the world was once submerged, resulting in the deep waterways and unique pingos of the Arctic coast.

Zhamba Déja: A clever hero from the Dehcho region who outsmarted an evil family, using wit and bravery to survive the harsh early times.

Spirits of the Sky: The breathtaking Aurora Borealis is deeply respected by the Dene and Cree. Rather than just a natural phenomenon, the lights are believed to be the spirits of ancestors dancing in the sky, watching over their living loved ones.

The Windigo: A terrifying cannibalistic monster that embodies winter starvation and insatiable greed. It is prominent across Northern Canadian folklore, preying on those who lose their moral way in the woods.

The Waheela: A massive, fearsome, wolf-like predator said to roam the remote stretches of the Northwest Territories and Alaska.
