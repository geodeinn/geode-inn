Sedna (or Nuliajuk): The most prominent figure in Inuit mythology. She is the sea goddess who controls all marine mammals (seals, whales, walruses). Her story tells of a girl cast into the ocean whose severed fingers became the sea’s creatures.

The Sun and the Moon: The creation of day and night is often attributed to the story of a brother and sister. Fleeing a conflict, the sister became the sun (Sittamat) and the brother became the moon (Tatqeq).

The Raven (Tulugak): Revered as a creator figure. In some stories, the raven acts as a trickster who brings light to the dark world.

Kiviuq: The most famous epic hero in circumpolar folklore. He is an immortal, kind-hearted wanderer who faces endless magical obstacles—such as giant sea monsters and cannibalistic spirits—only to survive and always find his way back home.

Tikta'Liktak: A classic survival story about a lost hunter stranded on an ice floe who uses his wits, respect for animals, and perseverance to survive and return to his community.

Qallupilluit: Scaly, humanoid sea creatures wearing large parkas (amautiit). They wait beneath the sea ice to snatch disobedient children who wander too close to the water.

Mahaha: A menacing, smiling spirit with no clothes who hunts in blizzards. He tickles his victims to death, making it culturally dangerous to get separated during a whiteout.

Ijiraat: Shapeshifting monsters that can take the form of any Arctic animal but can never disguise their glowing, red eyes.

Tuniit (or Sivullirmiut): The ancient race that populated the Arctic long before the Inuit. Folklore portrays them as mystical giants who possessed superhuman speed and strength.
