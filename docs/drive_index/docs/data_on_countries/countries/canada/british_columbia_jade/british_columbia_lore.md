Ogopogo (N'ha-a-itk): A famous serpent-like lake monster residing in Okanagan Lake. Indigenous peoples have known of this "Sacred Creature of the Water" for centuries before the name was coined in 1912. [1, 2]

Cadborosaurus (Caddy): A marine reptile said to inhabit the coastal waters of Victoria and the Pacific Northwest, with a long neck, horse-like head, and flippers.

Sisiutl: In Kwakwakaʼwakw mythology, a terrifying three-headed sea serpent whose glance turns adversaries to stone.

Sasquatch (Bigfoot): The famous hairy biped that is said to wander the dense, mountainous wilderness of southwestern British Columbia.

Dzunukwa / Tsonokwa: Known as the "Wild Woman of the Woods," this giant in Kwakwaka'wakw culture is a cannibalistic giant who steals children but can be outwitted.

Bakwas: The king of the ghosts; a small, emaciated green spirit with a curving nose that haunts the forests and tempts the living toward the world of the dead.

The Lost Gold Mine of Pitt Lake: Legends claim that a massive, unmapped vein of gold exists in the remote, mountainous wilderness around Pitt Lake, based on directions given by an Indigenous man to a relative on his deathbed.

Jackalope: A legendary horned-rabbit cross-bred creature that became a staple taxidermy trophy found in rural B.C. pubs.

Forbidden Plateau: A mountainous area on Vancouver Island with a dark Comox First Nation history. Legend states that during times of war, women and children were sent there for safekeeping, though they inexplicably disappeared, leaving behind only their belongings.
