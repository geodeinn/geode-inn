The St. John’s Water Dog: Ancestors of the modern Lab were called St. John's Dogs. They were bred by fishermen to dive into the freezing Atlantic, retrieve escaping fish, and pull heavy lines to shore.

The "Soft Mouth" Mythos: Legend holds that these dogs could retrieve live fish or even waterfowl without breaking the skin, leading to the famous trait of a "soft mouth".

English Aristocrats: In the 1800s, British nobles visiting Canada were mesmerized by the dogs' waterborne majesty and brought them to England to refine as gun dogs. The Earl of Malmesbury is credited with officially coining the name "Labrador".

The "Silver" Debate: Modern folklore sometimes suggests Silver Labradors are a rare ancient genetic line; however, canine historians and the AKC classify them as a dilute color variant often linked to crossbreeding in the 20th century.

The Phantom Trapper: A popular local legend tells of a deceased trapper who led a wicked life and was cursed to drive a team of fourteen pure white huskies through the snow for eternity. However, in folklore, he redeems himself by guiding lost travelers and trappers to safety through heavy blizzards.

The Caribou King (Atekwabe'o): Deep in Innu and Montagnais legends lies the story of the Caribou Man—a mysterious, supernatural guardian of the caribou herds who protects them and is sometimes seen conversing with human hunters, who in turn refrain from hunting his herd.

The Traverspine Gorilla: A famous 20th-century mystery centers around giant, ape-like footprints found deep in the Labrador wilderness by experienced local trappers. While some blamed a wildman or unclassified creature, others chalked it up to strange bear tracks magnified by freeze-and-thaw cycles.

The Origin of Fog: A prominent Labrador Inuit myth explains the sudden, rolling ocean fogs. In the tale, a man outwits a deadly bear by tricking it into drinking a river until it bursts, creating a thick fog that blankets the land.
