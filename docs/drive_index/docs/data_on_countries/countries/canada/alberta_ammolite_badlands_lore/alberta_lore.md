The Fort Kent Wendigo: The boreal forests of Alberta are historically tied to the Wendigo myth—a flesh-eating, winter demon. Famous local accounts, such as the 19th-century Swift Runner case near Fort Kent, form a dark pillar of the province's supernatural lore.

The Badlands Hoodoos: Indigenous traditions (such as those of the Siksika/Blackfoot) view the sandstone Hoodoos of the Drumheller Valley as the ancient, petrified remains of giants or spirits.

The Water Serpent of Medicine Hat: According to regional Blackfoot and Cree oral traditions, a giant predatory water snake once lived in the South Saskatchewan River at Medicine Hat, which helped inspire the city's unique name.

The Phantom Train of Medicine Hat: A famous prairie ghost story claims that on certain dark, foggy nights, a spectral train can be heard rumbling along the local tracks, foreshadowing tragedy or echoing a pioneer train's disappearance.

The Skeleton Spirits: Legends from the Rocky Mountain Cree and Blackfoot tell of Packos (a skeleton figure that punishes careless hunters) and skeletal ghosts that roam the foothills, with tales extending to the areas around present-day Lethbridge.
