Much of the foundational folklore originates from First Nations stories, which explain the creation of the land and are deeply connected to the natural landscape.

Nanaboozhoo (Nanabozho): A central trickster spirit and cultural hero in Anishinaabe tradition who wanders the forests, shapes the natural world, and plays tricks on villages.

Memegwesewak: Hair-covered, supernatural water dwarfs or "little people" who dwell inside the cliffs and caves along the Canadian Shield, often spotted near lakes and rivers.

The Wendigo: A malevolent, cannibalistic winter spirit from Northern Ontario lore. It is said to stalk the freezing woods, driven by an insatiable hunger.

Ontario's vast network of massive freshwater lakes has birthed multiple stories of mysterious, serpentine creatures.

Igopogo: A legendary sea monster said to swim the deep, chilly waters of Lake Simcoe.

Mussie: The resident cryptid of Muskrat Lake, with multiple sightings stretching back over a century.

Lake Ontario's Sea Serpent: Various historical accounts from the 1800s and 1900s describe a giant, snaky figure shrieking and swimming through the lake's waves.

As European immigrants (such as the Cornish and Irish) settled in Southern Ontario, they merged their own fairy and pixie tales with local Canadian settings.

Haunted Don Jail (Toronto): One of Toronto's most famous ghost stories, the historic jail—the site of numerous hangings—is rumored to be plagued by lingering spirits.

Underground Alien Bases (Toronto): Cabbagetown urban legends claim that an area along Parliament Street sits on top of a magnetic, underground alien tunnel system.

The Ghost of Old Finch Road (Toronto): A classic local apparition tale about the spirit of a murdered girl haunting a bridge near Morningside Road.
