Preah Thaong and Neang Neak: The foundational creation myth of Cambodia. An exiled Indian prince (Preah Thaong) landed on an island and fell in love with the daughter of the Naga (serpent) king. The Naga king drank the surrounding water to expose the land, which became the Khmer Kingdom, and presented the land as her dowry.

Phra Mae Thorani: The Earth Goddess, often depicted squeezing a torrent of water from her long hair to drown the armies of the demon Mara, protecting Buddha as he reaches enlightenment.

The Naga: Powerful, multi-headed serpent beings capable of shifting between human and serpent forms. They are revered as the original guardians of the land.

Reahu: A monstrous, eclipse-causing spirit who devours the sun and moon.

The Ahp or Krasue: A terrifying malevolent spirit appearing at night as a floating female head with dangling entrails and glowing organs.

The Ghost Banana Tree: A famous localized ghost story warning people never to build houses too close to banana trees, as restless spirits are said to use the large leaves to enter homes.

Judge Rabbit (Ah Trow): One of the most beloved figures in Khmer folklore. Despite his tiny size, the rabbit uses his superior wit and intellect to outsmart much larger, more dangerous animals (like crocodiles and tigers) and even ghosts and corrupted kings.

Tales of Alev: A famous series of comedic, satirical stories featuring a clever, mischievous orphan who gets the better of greedy figures, pirates, and his own elders.
