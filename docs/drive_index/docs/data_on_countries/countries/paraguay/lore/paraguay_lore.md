According to Guaraní creation myth, the union of the spirit Tau and the mortal Keraná produced seven legendary, cursed brothers who shape Paraguayan folklore:

Teju Jagua: A lizard with a dog's head, known as the lord of caverns and fruits.

Mbói Tu'í: A giant snake with a parrot's head, protector of amphibians and moisture.

Moñái: A horned snake and thief of field animals, tied to open fields and air.

Jasy Jatere: A golden-haired, wand-carrying elf who wanders at noon to charm and lead children away.

Kurupi: A fast, wild dwarf representing fertility and sexuality who guards the deep woods.

Ao Ao: A ferocious, sheep- or boar-like creature that hunts humans in packs, shouting its own name.

Luisón (Luison): The seventh son—a terrifying man-beast or werewolf-like entity that roams cemeteries at night eating the dead.

El Pombero: A famous nocturnal goblin of the forest. He whistles in the dark, protects wildlife, can bring good or bad luck to farmers, and punishes those who disrespect nature.
