Colapesce: A young fisherman from Messina named Nicola who loved the sea so much he tested his limits underwater, eventually staying below to hold up one of Sicily's three structural pillars to keep the island from sinking.

The Moor’s Heads (Teste di Moro): Colorful ceramic vases shaped like human faces originating from a grim folktale about a young Sicilian noblewoman who decapitated her foreign lover and used his head as a basil planter.

Mount Etna Giants: Greek lore says the monster Typhon or the giant Enceladus is trapped beneath Mount Etna, causing eruptions and rumblings whenever they turn or breathe fire.

Aci and Galatea: A coastal myth explaining the origins of towns near Etna, where a jealous cyclops named Polyphemus killed the shepherd Aci, who was then transformed into a flowing river by the gods.

Opera dei Pupi (Sicilian Puppet Theater): Traditional marionette theater dramatizing medieval knight battles, the crusades, and the epic poems of Charlemagne.

The Trinacria: The official symbol of Sicily featuring a three-legged Medusa head, representing the island's three geographic capes and ancient protective magic.

Truvature: Folk beliefs regarding hidden, spell-guarded treasures scattered across the rural Sicilian landscape and caves.
