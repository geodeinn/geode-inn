Tengri: The supreme god and sky deity, representing the heavens.

Umai: The goddess of fertility, virginity, and protector of mothers and newborn children.

Ak Ana: The primordial creator goddess associated with water.

Creation Myth: Legends say that when the creator distributed landscapes to the nations, the Kazakhs were granted nothing but the endless, flat steppe. But with this vastness, they were given ultimate freedom and a deep, spiritual bond with nature.

The Kazakh people highly revere their batyrs (warriors/superheroes). Wandering bards (aqyns) would sing these epics, accompanied by traditional instruments like the dombra or kobyz.

Kobylandy Batyr: A legendary hero likened to Hercules, known for defending his people.

Tulpar: No batyr is complete without a Tulpar—a magical winged horse capable of crossing mountains and deserts in a single gallop. Horses are the cornerstone of Kazakh culture; they are viewed as faithful companions and mystical beings.

Samruk: A giant, sacred, golden bird of happiness and prosperity, representing the sun.

Idahar: A terrifying, chaotic mythical serpent.

Albasti: A malicious spirit that brings terror and chaos.

Shurale: A mischievous spirit that lives deep in the forests and mountains.

Storytellers (zhyrau and aqyn) passed down short fables designed to teach life lessons and morality.

Aldar Kose: A famous trickster character who outwits greedy landowners and rich beys to help the poor.

The Brave Swallow: In one famous fable, a clever swallow outsmarts the evil serpent Idahar and a mosquito to save humanity, getting a forked tail as a result.

The Legend of Aisha-Bibi: The most famous love story, centered around a mausoleum in Taraz. It tells the tragic tale of 16-year-old Aisha who traveled across the steppe to defy her father and marry her lover, Karakhan.

The Legend of the Dombra: A musical fable explaining the creation of the famous two-stringed instrument. Two giant brothers lived in the Altai Mountains, one arrogant and narcissistic, and the younger, who spent all his time joyfully playing the dombra.
