The Ramayana: Follows the journey of Prince Rama as he rescues his wife, Sita, from the demon king Ravana with the help of an army of monkeys. This epic is central to the lore behind events like Diwali.

The Mahabharata: The longest epic poem ever written. It tells the story of a great dynastic war between the Pandava and Kaurava brothers, and includes the Bhagavad Gita.

Creation & Cosmology: Traditional flood myths (like King Manu and the Matsya avatar of Vishnu) and regional tales explain the origins of rivers like the Ganges and mountain ranges.

The Panchatantra: Composed in Sanskrit around 300 BC, these are interlocking "frame stories" featuring animals that embody human virtues and vices to teach princes statecraft.

The Jataka Tales: Over 500 stories detailing the previous lives of the Buddha, which emphasize virtues like self-sacrifice, honesty, and compassion.

The Hitopadesha: A later spin-off of the Panchatantra designed to instruct the youth in worldly wisdom.

North & Central India: Tales feature a mix of pure and impure godlings, nature spirits, and the folklore of tribal groups like the Gonds, which explain local phenomena like why humans no longer have tails.

South India: Highly rich in hero-stones, local village protector deities (Ayyanar), and epics like the Silappatikaram.

Northeast India: Tribal folktales often focus on deep connections to the forest, such as the Lotha Naga legend of "The Girl Who Married a Tiger".
