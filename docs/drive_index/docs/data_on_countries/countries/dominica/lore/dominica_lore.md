he umbrella term for most supernatural entities in Dominica is Jumbie (or Zombi). Unlike the harmless ghosts of some traditions, these beings are forces of the rainforest that you must actively outsmart:

La Diablesse (Lajables): Known as the Devil Woman, she appears as a beautiful, elegantly dressed woman wearing a large hat and layered skirts. She walks with a charismatic grace to captivate unsuspecting men, only to reveal a cloven (hoof-like) foot. She famously leads men deep into the rainforest to their doom.

Soukouyant (Succouyant): A shape-shifting, vampire-like hag who looks like an ordinary elderly woman by day. At night, she sheds her human skin, transforms into a ball of fire, and flies through the sky to suck the blood of sleeping villagers.

Lougarou (Lagahoo): The male counterpart to the Soukouyant. These are villagers who have traded their souls for superhuman powers. They can shapeshift into animals (often dogs, cats, or large birds) or travel invisibly to cause mischief and cast spells.

The Bolom: Created through Obeah, these are spirits forced to serve humans or perform menial tasks like theft.

The Silk Cotton Tree (Ceiba): The most sacred and feared tree in Dominica. It is viewed as a skyscraper for Jumbies. Cutting one down is a massive taboo, as it is believed the displaced spirits will take revenge on the whole community.

Bakwa, the Snake King: Part of the traditional Kalinago creation myths. Bakwa is a giant snake spirit who guards the Kalinago people.

Ole Mas Masquerade: You can still experience the ghosts of the past during Dominica's annual Carnival celebrations. Revelers dress up as traditional folklore characters to celebrate emancipation and freedom from colonial masters.

Creole Day: Legends and stories are still shared by elders, keeping the island's oral traditions alive during independence celebrations.
