Soungoula: A clever animal trickster borrowed from African-Malagasy roots, often matched against Konper Torti (Brother Tortoise).

Tizan: A common folk hero character representing resourcefulness.

The Swallowing Monster: A classic mythological creature featured in regional oral tales.

Loulou and Kousoupa: Figures representing fearsome or sly creatures adapted into island storytelling.

The Vanishing Woman: A mysterious recurring archetype in Creole fables.

Mermaids and Sirens: Early sailors and locals reported seeing mermaids along the shores, which scientists attribute to sightings of the gentle, marine Dugong (sea cow).

Coco de Mer: The massive, uniquely shaped double-coconut native to Praslin inspired myths of a giant underwater tree. General Charles Gordon even theorized the local Vallée de Mai was the original Garden of Eden.

Pirate Treasures: Tales of the pirate Olivier Levasseur ("La Buse") who hid massive stolen fortunes across isolated coves like Anse Forbans.

Bonom di bwa: Traditional local healers, seers, or "men of the woods" often consulted for guidance or protection.

Gris-gris: Protective or harmful magical charms tied to traditional spiritual beliefs. [

Daily Omens: Popular local rules include not starting projects on Fridays, avoiding eating straight from the cooking pot to prevent rain on one's wedding day, and dropping a fork or spoon to signal an incoming visitor's gender.

Moutya: A UNESCO-listed traditional Creole dance and rhythmic drum performance historically used to preserve community memory and express freedom after long workdays.
