Vodník (Water Goblin): A famous water sprite often dressed in green with a tailcoat. He dwells underwater and collects the souls of drowned people in porcelain teapots, but is equally known for frequenting local pubs. [1]

The Twelve Months: A classic fairy tale where a young girl is sent into a freezing forest to find impossible items (like strawberries). She encounters the 12 personified months gathered around a magical fire who grant her requests.

Ježibaba: The Czech equivalent to Baba Yaga; a forest-dwelling hag who lives in a chicken-legged hut.

Silly Devils: Czech folklore features many devils, but they are often portrayed as foolish, easily tricked by clever peasants.

Forefather Čech: A legendary figure who led his Slavic tribe up Mount Říp, gazed out at the "land of milk and honey," and founded the first Czech settlement.

Prague Castle & The Golem: The capital's streets teem with legends, including stories of the alchemists under Emperor Rudolf II and the Golem—a clay creature brought to life by Rabbi Loew to protect the Jewish quarter.

The Dancing Skeleton / Karel Jaromír Erben: Author Karel Jaromír Erben’s 1853 collection A Bouquet (Kytice) details macabre and haunting Slavic ballads about ghosts and shape-shifting monsters, famously inspiring Antonín Dvořák's compositions.

Masopust (Carnival): Pre-Lenten celebrations featuring vibrant parades, masks, and traditional foods like koblihy (doughnuts).

Erecting the Maypole: Unmarried men from a village cut down a tall tree, strip its branches, and decorate it with ribbons to symbolize the arrival of spring. It is a point of honor to guard the pole, as men from rival villages will often try to chop it down.

Burning of the Witches (Čarodějnice): Celebrated on April 30th, communities light massive bonfires to welcome spring and ward off evil spirits.
