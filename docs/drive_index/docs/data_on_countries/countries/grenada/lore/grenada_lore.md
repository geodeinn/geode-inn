La Diablesse: Translating to "female devil," this beguiling woman dresses in a long flowing gown and a wide-brimmed hat. She attends village dances to seduce men and lure them into the deep forest, her long dress cleverly hiding her cloven, cow-like foot.

Soucouyant: A resident witch who sheds her human skin at night and turns into a ball of fire to fly through the sky. She enters homes to suck the blood of sleeping victims.

Ligaroo: Derived from the French loup-garou (werewolf), this shapeshifter is known to prowl the island at night to hunt and suck the blood of villagers.

Papa Bois: The ancient, hairy guardian of the forest. He has the feet of a deer or goat and acts as a protector of animals, blowing a cow's horn to warn wildlife of approaching hunters.

Dwenn: The spirit of a child who passes away before reaching baptism, often linked to playful or eerie occurrences.

The "Jumbie" Realm: "Jumbie" is a broad term used for any Caribbean spirit or ghost. To confuse evil spirits, children were traditionally given physical, event-based, or trickster nicknames rather than being called by their actual birth names.

Protecting the Home: To keep Soucouyants out, locals spread salt on window sills, leave a heap of uncounted rice outside the door (so the witch stays up all night counting every grain), or lean a broom upside down against the doorway.

Nighttime Taboos: Elders traditionally teach that whistling at night, sweeping the house after dark, or walking out without covering your head invites bad spirits.

Jab Jab: Originating during the era of slavery, this is a central and defiant figure in Grenadian Carnival (held in August). Jab Jabs cover their bodies in black grease or blue paint, wear horned helmets, and carry chains as they march through the streets, mimicking and subverting their former colonizers.

Folklore remains a living culture across Grenada and its sister island, Carriacou, where vibrant storytelling and traditions like the River Sallee Saraka (a traditional feast and drumming ceremony) keep the island's roots alive.
