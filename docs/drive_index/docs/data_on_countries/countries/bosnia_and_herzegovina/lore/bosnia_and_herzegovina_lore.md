Vila: Radiant mountain fairies who live in dense forests, sing, and help—or test—heroes. [1, 2]

Zmaj & Aždaha: The Zmaj is a noble, weather-controlling dragon, while the Aždaha is a destructive, multi-headed monster representing chaos.

Mora & Drekavac: The Mora is a spirit that sits on sleeping chests to cause nightmares, while the Drekavac is the shrieking, tragic ghost of an unbaptized child.

Tur: According to Central Bosnian folklore, the Tur is a colossal mythical bull holding the world on its horns. Earthquakes happen whenever he moves.

Sevdalinka: A legendary genre of folk music deeply influenced by the Ottoman era. It is heavily emotive, often telling tragic, aching stories of separated lovers.

Kolo: The traditional, fast-paced line dance performed at weddings and festivals. In some regions, the Silent Kolo is performed without any instruments; dancers use the rhythmic stomping of their feet and the clinking of silver coins on their traditional aprons to keep the beat.

Traditional clothing—like the famous Zmijanje embroidery—is woven with heavy silk and features intricate geometric designs in red, black, and gold. Women’s outfits are heavily adorned with silver coins, beads, and woven aprons.
