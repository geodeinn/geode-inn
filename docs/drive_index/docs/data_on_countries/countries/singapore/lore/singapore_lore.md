Sang Nila Utama and Singapura: A 13th-century prince from Palembang who threw his crown into the sea during a storm to save his ship. Landing on Temasek, he spotted a majestic beast he believed was a lion (singa), naming the island Singapura ("Lion City").

Redhill (Bukit Merah): The tragic tale of a village plagued by leaping swordfish. A clever boy named Nadim suggested building barricades of banana tree trunks, which trapped the fish by their snouts. Fearing his growing brilliance, the jealous Sultan ordered the boy killed on a hill, staining the soil red.

Kusu Island: Legend says a magical giant turtle turned itself into an island to rescue shipwrecked Malay and Chinese sailors, who later built shrines and a temple there.

Pulau Ubin and Pulau Sekudu: An elephant, a pig, and a frog challenged each other to swim to Johor. Those who failed were turned to stone; the frog became Frog Island (Pulau Sekudu).

Badang the Strongman: An ordinary slave who gained superhuman strength after capturing a supernatural forest spirit, becoming a legendary hero of the ancient Singapura kingdom.

The Pontianak: Southeast Asia's most famous vampiric female ghost, believed to be the spirit of a woman who died during childbirth, haunting banana trees with a chilling wail and the scent of frangipani.

The Midnight Radio Spirit: A common National Service urban legend in military barracks where an unseen entity persistently tunes transistor radios to late-night Chinese broadcasts, regardless of how often it is turned off or unplugged.

The Punggol Tiger Spirit: Folklore surrounding northeast Singapore suggesting a mystical white tiger spirit guards the land where old pig farms once stood
