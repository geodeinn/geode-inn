The Mythological Cycle: Details the arrival of ancient races and the Tuatha Dé Danann—a pantheon of skilled gods and goddesses who governed the land before retreating into the subterranean otherworld to become the Faerie Folk.

The Ulster Cycle: The Irish equivalent to Greek epics, focusing on the heroic feats of Cú Chulainn (the Hound of Ulster) and Queen Medb's legendary Cattle Raid of Cooley (Táin Bó Cúailnge).

The Fenian (Ossianic) Cycle: Chronicles the adventures of Finn McCool (Fionn mac Cumhaill) and his band of warriors, the Fianna.

The Historical Cycle: Blends myth and pseudo-history to chronicle the ancient High Kings of Ireland, focusing heavily on political lineage and the seat of power at the Hill of Tara.

The Children of Lir: A jealous stepmother turns four royal children into swans, condemning them to wander the lakes of Ireland for 900 years.

Diarmuid and Grainne: A tragic romance in which a legendary warrior and a princess break a royal engagement to run away, leading to a relentless, continent-wide pursuit.

The Salmon of Knowledge: Finn McCool tastes an enchanted fish that grants him universal wisdom, kickstarting his legendary career.

The Aos Sí (or Sidhe): The fairy folk believed to be the descendants of the Tuatha Dé Danann, inhabiting ancient mounds and hills.

The Banshee (Bean Sídhe): A female spirit whose mournful wail or keening is an omen that death is approaching a specific family.

The Púka: A shape-shifting trickster spirit that can bring either great good fortune or immense mischief to those who cross its path.

The Dullahan: A terrifying, headless horseman who rides through the night carrying his own head, serving as an omen of death.

The Leprechaun: Rooted in old myths about a fairy cobbler, he is known for hoarding pots of gold at the end of the rainbow.

Changeling: A faerie child secretly left in a human cradle to replace a human baby that has been stolen by the faeries.
