Nareau the Spider: Prominent in Kiribati tradition, a spider deity who spun the universe, shaped reality, and organized the cosmos out of a dark void.

Sky Separation: Universal motifs detailing how primordial entities or eels (like Riiki) pushed the low-hanging sky away from the earth to let light and life flourish.

Tree of Life: Legends like the Kai-n-Tiku-Aba from which ancestral human clans trace their descent across the Pacific islands.

Olofat (Letao): A famous Caroline Islands trickster god who brought fire to humankind, shaped marine life features (like shark teeth or stingray tails), and walked the line between helping and teasing humans.

Aluluei: The celestial seafaring god whose many eyes became the night-sky navigation stars used by traditional voyagers.

Bue: A culture hero from the Gilbert Islands who taught islanders canoe building, house architecture, and magical wind-raising.

Isokelekel: A legendary semi-mythical warrior from Pohnpei who overthrew the oppressive Saudeleur dynasty in the 1500s/1600s and founded the modern system of traditional chiefdoms.

Origin of Kava (Sakau): Pohnpeian sacred accounts explaining how the revered ceremonial plant first grew and became central to island diplomacy and ritual.

Spirits of the Dead: Traditional beliefs dictating that a soul lingers near the deceased for four days before family rituals guide it safely to its final spiritual resting place.
