Puteri Gunung Ledang (Johor): The legendary fairy princess of Mount Ophir who demanded impossible tasks (like a bridge of gold or a bowl of an infant's blood) from the Sultan of Malacca to win her hand.

Mahsuri (Langkawi): A tragic 19th-century tale of a falsely accused woman whose execution released white blood, causing her to curse the island with seven generations of drought and misfortune.

Hang Tuah (Malacca): The ultimate Malay folk hero and warrior, whose unyielding loyalty to the Sultan serves as the pinnacle of bravery and silat martial arts.

Batu Belah Batu Bertangkup (Selangor): A cautionary tale of a neglected, grieving mother who was swallowed whole by a magical, devouring rock after her children ate her favorite fish.

Sang Kancil: The clever mouse-deer who outwits larger predators (like crocodiles and tigers) in fables.

Pontianak: The most infamous ghost in Malay lore; she is a vengeful spirit of a woman who died during childbirth, recognizable by her long black hair and the scent of jasmine.

Penanggalan: A terrifying vampire-like spirit consisting of a disembodied female head with its internal organs dangling below, flying through the night to hunt for blood.

Toyol: A ghostly, green-skinned infant spirit conjured by a shaman using black magic to perform mischief or steal money for its master.

Orang Bunian: Mythical "invisible people" who dwell deep within dense Malaysian forests, often said to lure lost travelers into their hidden, magical realm.

Bujang Senang (Sarawak): The terrifying legend of a massive, man-eating crocodile that roamed the Batang Lupar river before its capture.

Mount Kinabalu (Sabah): A Kadazan-Dusun legend tells of an angry dragon holding a giant pearl at the peak, while another folklore recounts a Chinese prince who scaled the mountain to find the creature.
