Japanese myth begins with the primal deities Izanagi and Izanami, who birthed the Japanese islands and many gods. When Izanami died, Izanagi traveled to Yomi (the underworld) to retrieve her. After failing, his purification rituals birthed the most important deities:

Amaterasu (Sun Goddess): Born from Izanagi's left eye, she is the ruler of the heavens and the mythological ancestor of the Japanese Imperial family.

Kitsune: Age-old, shape-shifting fox spirits that serve as messengers to the rice god, Inari. They grow in power and intelligence, gaining up to nine tails over hundreds of years.

Tanuki: Mischievous, shapeshifting raccoon dogs known for their large bellies and playful trickery.

Kappa: Amphibious, child-sized water sprites that inhabit rivers and ponds. They are known for their love of cucumbers and the dish of water on their heads.

Namazu: A colossal catfish living in the mud beneath the islands, whose movements and thrashing are said to cause devastating earthquakes.

Momotarō (The Peach Boy): Found floating down a river inside a giant peach, he grows into a brave warrior who travels to Onigashima (Demon Island) with a dog, monkey, and pheasant to defeat ogres.

Urashima Tarō: A fisherman who rescues a turtle and is taken to the undersea palace of Ryūgū-jō. He returns to the surface to find hundreds of years have passed.

Kaguya-hime (The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter): A tiny, radiant girl is found inside a glowing stalk of bamboo. She grows up to be a beautiful moon princess who must eventually return to the heavens.

Yūrei: Traditional ghosts of people who died with unresolved anger, jealousy, or sadness. They are typically depicted with long, unkempt black hair, pale faces, and floating without feet.

Modern Legends: Urban legends like Kuchisake-onna (The Slit-Mouthed Woman) or Aka Manto (Red Cloak) are modern iterations of terrifying folk spirits that haunt schools and public bathrooms.
