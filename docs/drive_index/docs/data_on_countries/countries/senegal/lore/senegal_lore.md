Yumboes: Two-foot-tall, pearly-white or silver-haired fairy-like beings in Wolof and Lebou folklore who live near the Paps hills and dance in the moonlight.

Konderong: Small, bearded woodland spirits or dwarfs with backward-facing feet who guard wild animals.

Pangool: Revered Serer ancestral spirits tied to specific natural landmarks like baobab trees and rivers.

Diola Figures: Traditional Casamance masquerade figures like the Kumpo, Samay (a deer-like master of ceremonies), and Niasse.

Griots: Traditional hereditary bards, musicians, and praise-singers who preserve genealogies, laws, and history using instruments like the 21-string kora.

Leuk the Hare and Bouki the Hyena: Famous trickster fables popularized in literature by storytellers like Birago Diop (Tales of Amadou Koumba), featuring the clever hare outsmarting the foolish hyena.

Marabouts and Brotherhoods: Spiritual Islamic leaders in Senegal who act as mystic mediators, heavily integrated with local beliefs in protective amulets and unseen forces.

Gris-Gris: Personal amulets or talismans worn for protection against misfortune, illness, or malevolent spirits.

Sarax: Traditional offerings or ritual meals (such as lakh) left near rivers or beaches to appease water and nature genies.
