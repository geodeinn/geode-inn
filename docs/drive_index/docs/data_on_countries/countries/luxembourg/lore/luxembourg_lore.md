The foundational legend of Luxembourg is the tale of Melusina, a water spirit. In 963, Count Siegfried of the Ardennes met a beautiful maiden named Melusina. She agreed to marry him under one condition: he must never spy on her while she bathed. Years later, Siegfried broke his promise and found that, when bathing, her legs turned into a serpent's or fish's tail. Melusina vanished into the rock walls of the city. It is said she still dwells beneath the city, returning every seven years as a serpent to hold a golden key. If a brave soul takes the key or kisses her, she will be freed.

The Wild Hunt of the Ardennes: Deep in the Éislek (the northern highlands), a cursed knight is doomed to ride through stormy skies at night as punishment for his disrespect toward nature.

The White Lady of Vianden: High above the Our River, the spectral White Lady wanders the towering battlements of Vianden Castle. She is a tragic noblewoman bound to the castle due to an unresolved, tragic injustice.

Wild Women of Lasauvage: In the mining villages of southwestern Luxembourg, lore tells of mysterious, untamed women living in rock crevasses who interact with forest dwellers.

Witches and Wizards: Many tales involve magical locals, like Klopptreinchen, a mischievous witch from Manternach who loved spooking horses, or D., the legendary Wizard of Vianden who could turn invisible.

Éimaischen: Occurring on Easter Monday in the village of Nospelt and the Fishmarket in Luxembourg City, this traditional festival features the trading of small, baked-clay bird whistles (called Péckvillercher).

Buergbrennen: Celebrated on the first Sunday of Lent, massive wooden crosses are burned on hilltops to banish winter and welcome spring.
