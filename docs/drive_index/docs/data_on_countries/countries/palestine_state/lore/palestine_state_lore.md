The Hikaye Tradition

Definition: Fictitious oral tales traditionally told by women over age 70.

Setting: Spontaneously narrated during winter evenings at home among small groups of women and children.

Purpose: Offers a subtle critique of Middle Eastern Arab society and family structures from a female perspective.

Tunjur, Tunjur: A well-known cumulative folk story about a tiny clay pot that comes to life and goes on a journey.

The Daughter of the Rose and the Jasmine: A distinct regional variant of the Cinderella narrative.

Collections: Major academic and cultural preservation works like [Speak, Bird, Speak Again] (by Ibrahim Muhawi and Sharif Kanaana) document dozens of authentic rural tales divided into cycles of family, courtship, and quests.

Deep Roots: Customs and agricultural calendars merge ancient Near Eastern and Canaanite seasonal rhythms with later monotheistic traditions.

Symbolism: The olive tree acts as an enduring motif representing peace, heritage, and connection to the land across millennia.
