Te Pusi mo te Ali (The Eel and the Flounder): A widely shared creation myth stating that an eel (te Pusi) and a flounder (te Ali) created the islands. The flat body of the flounder formed the low atolls, and the eel became the model for the coconut palm. Because of this, moray eels are sacred (tapu) and forbidden to eat on several islands.

The Circling Spirits: An oral tradition from Nui describes traveling spirits from beyond the horizon who gathered in a circle to rest, then dove to the sea bed to pile up stones, mud, and sand, creating the atolls.

Founding Ancestors: Individual islands maintain distinct oral histories regarding their first peoples. Nanumea traces its roots to Tongan legends, while Niutao, Funafuti, and Vaitupu attribute their founding figures to voyagers from Samoa.

Foilape: A prominent pan-insular mythological figure revered across several islands as a powerful warrior king and god of martial prowess.

Traditional folk practices include invocation rituals, specialized floral garland weaving, and specific dances performed on the beach toward passing sunset clouds to summon rain during severe droughts.
