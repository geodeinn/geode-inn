Dhegdheer: A famous, terrifying ogress or cannibalistic woman whose name means "Long Ears," used as a cautionary tale to teach children obedience and good behavior. [1, 2, 3]

Queen Araweelo (Arawelo): A legendary and controversial ancient queen who flipped traditional gender roles and ruled with absolute, sometimes ruthless power; her complex legacy is still remembered with starkly divided traditions by men and women.

Cigaal Shidaad (Igal Chidal): A legendary folklore character known across Somalia for his exaggerated cowardice, featured in humorous tales of nervous misadventures.

Wiil-Waal: A wise historical/folklore governor associated with the city of Hargeisa, known for solving complex riddles and tests of wit to maintain justice.

Waaq (Eebbe): The ancient sky and creator god worshipped by Cushitic populations before the arrival of Islam, linked closely to rain, fertility, nature, and sacred trees.

Cosmic Bull and Cow: An old mythical belief that the universe balances on the horns of a great bull staring endlessly at a cow tied to a post; when the bull's attention shifts, it causes earthquakes and disasters.

Diin iyo Dawaco (The Tortoise and the Fox): Classic trickster animal fables where smaller, slower animals outwit swift predators through clever thinking.

Dad Qalato: General folklore descriptions of dangerous, isolated cannibalistic groups used to frighten travelers away from lawless frontiers.
