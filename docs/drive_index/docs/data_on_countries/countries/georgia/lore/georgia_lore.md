Amirani: The central figure of Georgian mythology, Amirani is a demigod and demigod of metalwork chained to the Caucasus Mountains with his dog Kersha as punishment for defying God. It heavily mirrors the Greek myth of Prometheus. [1]

Dali: A powerful, golden-haired goddess of the hunt who protects wild animals in the mountains.

The Argonauts & Golden Fleece: Anchored in the ancient western Georgian kingdom of Colchis, this Greek myth centers around Medea and Jason seeking the Golden Fleece.

Fairy-Tale Horses (Rashi): Winged, water, or fire-breathing horses that act as powerful magical helpers in folk tales.

The Altamaha-ha: Georgia’s equivalent of the Loch Ness Monster. It is a 20-to-30-foot serpentine creature said to inhabit the dark, winding waters of the Altamaha River.

Savannah Hauntings: Founded in 1733, Savannah is known as one of the most haunted cities in America. Legends include restless spirits, yellow fever victims, and shadowed phantoms in its historic squares.

Gullah-Geechee Culture: Rich coastal African-American folklore heavily influenced by West African traditions, including "Hoodoo" conjure magic, ring shouts, and stories of Brer Rabbit.
