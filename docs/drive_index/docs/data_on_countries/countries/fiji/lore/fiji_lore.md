Degei: The supreme creator god of Fiji, often depicted as a giant snake living deep within the Nakauvadra mountain range on the island of Viti Levu. According to legend, he hatched the first humans from eggs and is responsible for seasonal weather and earthquakes.

The Afterlife Journey: The folklore includes a complex soul's journey. Degei acts as the judge of the newly dead, who must pass through the caves of Cibaciba or Drakulu to either reach a paradise known as Burotukula or face punishment.

Dakuwaqa: Perhaps the most famous figure in Fijian mythology, he was a fierce shape-shifting shark god. He originally terrorized the islands until he was humbled in battle by a giant octopus guarding the island of Kadavu. In exchange for his life, he swore to protect Fijian fishermen at sea, which is why the people of Kadavu do not fear sharks.

The Sacred Turtles of Kadavu: The people of Namuana village hold a traditional ritual where they sing chants to call sea turtles up to the surface.

The Veli: These are the "little people" of Fijian folklore. They are mischievous, hairy forest-dwelling sprites that live in hollow trees. They are known to possess magical knowledge (like the secret of firewalking) but can be easily angered if travelers destroy their sacred trees or disrupt the forest.

Red Prawns of Vatulele: The legend of Vatulele tells of a beautiful chief's daughter who rejected a mainland suitor, ordering him to be thrown from a cliff. As he fell, his gift of cooked prawns transformed into the bright scarlet prawns found in the island's rocky pools today, which locals revere as sacred.

The Great Flood & Canoes: In addition to Degei's stories, there are accounts of a great flood and of the god of carpenters teaching the early people how to construct outrigger canoes.

Firewalking (Vilavilairevo): Originating from the island of Beqa, legend dictates that the Sawau tribe gained the supernatural ability to walk across burning hot stones after a chief struck a deal with a forest spirit (veli).
