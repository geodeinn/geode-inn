La Patasola: A terrifying, one-legged female monster who inhabits the dense jungles and mountains. She is known to lure men deep into the forest to disorient or kill them, often acting as a fierce protector of nature.

El Mohán (or Poira): A wild, hairy, and cigar-smoking jungle guardian who lives near riverbanks. He is known to protect the waterways and is notorious for luring young women into the forest.

La Madre Monte (Mother Mountain): A beautiful yet fierce spirit of the forest draped in moss and leaves. She punishes loggers, hunters, and those who destroy nature, often causing them to get lost in the jungle.

El Hombre Caimán (The Alligator Man): A legendary figure from the Magdalena River town of Plato. He was originally a fisherman who, after spying on bathing women, used a potion to turn into an alligator, but a mishap left him half-man and half-alligator.

La Muelona: A mesmerizing, beautiful woman with a hauntingly large set of teeth. She stalks rural roads at dusk, luring unsuspecting men to their demise with her beauty.

Much of Colombia's indigenous roots—such as the Muisca and Chibcha cultures—form the bedrock of the country's creation stories:

Bochica: A revered, bearded civilizing hero who taught laws, crafts, and morality to the Muisca people.

Bachue: The mother goddess of the Muisca. She emerged from the sacred waters of Lake Iguaque holding a small boy, who later became her husband, ultimately populating the world.
