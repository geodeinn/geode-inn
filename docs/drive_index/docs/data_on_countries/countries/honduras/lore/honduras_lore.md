El Cadejo: A legendary spectral dog that appears at night. The White Cadejo is a good spirit that protects travelers, while the Black Cadejo is an evil, demonic dog that attacks those who walk alone at night. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]

La Cigua / La Sucia: Often compared to La Llorona, she is a beautiful, long-haired woman who enchants men on dark nights, only to reveal a skull or horse's face once she has lured them into deep rivers or the jungle.

El Cipitío: A cursed ten-year-old boy with a protruding belly and backwards feet. He is mischievous but harmless, known for wearing a massive hat and eating ash.

El Sisimite: A large, ape-like creature akin to Bigfoot that lives in the dense, mountainous forests of Honduras.

Honduran traditional music relies on pre-Hispanic instruments like clay ocarinas, tortoise shells, and conch shells, alongside European instruments like the marimba, guitar, and accordion.

The Bananero: A famous traditional folk song and dance celebrating the joy of the banana harvest.

El Sueñito: A lively dance authenticated by the National Folklore Office, highlighting the cultural blend of Spanish and Indigenous movements.

The Fish Rain of Yoro (Lluvia de Peces): One of Honduras' most famous natural and folkloric phenomena. Occurring annually in the department of Yoro, residents believe it is a miraculous blessing rather than a weather event, celebrated widely by the community.
