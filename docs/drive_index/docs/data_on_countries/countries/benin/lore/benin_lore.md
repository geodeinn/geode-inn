The universe is ruled by Mawu-Lisa, the supreme creator. Mawu (the moon goddess) represents fertility and gentleness, while Lisa (the sun god) represents strength.

Aido-Hwedo: The great cosmic serpent that carried Mawu across the sky during creation. Where he coiled on the ground, mountains formed, and valleys were made where he turned.

Legba: The trickster god of chance, communication, and magic. He sits at crossroads, testing human resolve and acting as the messenger between humanity and the gods.

Voodoo (Vodun) is central to Beninese spirituality, shaping the way communities view the unseen world.

Sagbata: The earth god who controls illnesses and healing.

The Sacred Chameleon: In ancient lore, the chameleon was a guide for the first human families, changing colors to warn them of approaching danger.

Guelede: Heavily featured among the Yoruba near Porto-Novo, these wooden masks are used in ceremonies to honor the "Mothers" (the powerful female ancestors and spirits of the night) and promote fertility.

Joromi: A celebrated mythological warrior and wrestler in Edo folklore who defeated all mortal rivals. Emboldened by his strength, he ventured into the afterworld to challenge the dead and was never seen again.

Egwi (The Tortoise): A staple trickster in ancient Edo (Benin Kingdom) folklore, often matching wits against personified plants and larger animals.

Leopard, Mudfish, and Bird of Prophecy: These three creatures are heavily revered in Benin Kingdom cosmology. They symbolize the mystical power of the Oba (king), who was historically believed to possess a god-like connection to the spiritual and natural world.

Why Death Exists: A timeless Beninese legend tells of a time when humans lived forever by shedding their old skin like serpents. After an old woman was terrified by a discarded serpent skin and cried out that it was "evil," the cycle of renewal stopped, introducing permanent death to humanity.
