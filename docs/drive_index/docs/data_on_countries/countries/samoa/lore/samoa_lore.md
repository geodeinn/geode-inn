Tagaloa: The supreme creator god who formed the islands from the sky and transformed worms into human beings.

Nafanua: The fierce warrior goddess and peace-bringer from Falealupo on Savaiʻi island.

Mafuiʻe: The subterranean deity responsible for causing earthquakes.

Saveasiʻuleo: The ruler of Pulotu, the spiritual underworld and domain of ancestors.

Sina and the Eel: Explains the origin of the first coconut tree after a young girl named Sina plants the buried head of a magical eel.

The Tattooing Arts: The twin sisters Taema and Tilafaiga swam from Fiji to Samoa carrying the traditional knowledge and songs of the tatau.

The Turtle and the Shark: A classic tale of a grandmother and granddaughter from Savaiʻi who leaped into the sea during a famine and transformed into marine animals.

Aitu: Complex ancestral or localized spirits tied to the jungle and lava rock who enforce sacred taboos (tapu).

Teine Sa: Sacred or forbidden ethereal women of the villages who protect cultural spaces and punish transgressions.

Telesa: A fierce guardian aitu associated with protecting Samoan women, famously tied to warnings against leaving hair down at night.
