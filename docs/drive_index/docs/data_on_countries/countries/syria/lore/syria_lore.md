he Trickster and Moral Archetypes: Tales collected from urban centers like Aleppo (such as those compiled in Folktales from Syria) feature recurring archetypes like the sly fox, wicked stepmothers, and wise fools. [1, 2]

Mythical Beings: Oral lore warns of ghouls (shape-shifting spirits of the desert and ruins) and jinn, blending pre-Islamic desert myths with regional Abrahamic traditions.

Legendary Figures: Historical rulers like Queen Zenobia of Palmyra persist as folk symbols of female strength, resilience, and leadership against overwhelming odds.

Folk Instruments: Traditional acoustic expression relies heavily on the oud (lute ancestor), ney (reed flute), and hand percussion like the darbouka or daf.

Nomadic Traditions: Bedouin regional music highlights the use of the rababah (one-stringed fiddle) and wind instruments like the mizmar.
