La Loogaroo (Loup-garou): A shape-shifting witch or vampire-like entity from Creole folklore. People historically believed she shed her skin at night to turn into a ball of fire and suck the blood of victims, a belief so strong it caused widespread panic in past decades. [1, 2, 3]

Touni Minuit: Translating roughly to "naked at midnight," this phantom entity or panic-inducing urban legend gripped the collective Mauritian psyche in the 1990s, causing residents to lock their doors early.

The Mermaid of Poste La Fayette: A coastal spirit said to have the upper body of a beautiful woman and the lower body of a fish, luring unwary fishermen into the northern waves with her enchanting songs.

The Seven Colored Earths of Chamarel: Creole oral tradition states that the dunes of Chamarel were shaped by ancient earth spirits. Each colored layer of soil represents an elemental emotional or physical force left behind by these unseen guardians.

The Legend of Pieter Both Mountain: The iconic mountain with a giant boulder balanced on top is said to be a giant who helped villagers but was turned into stone as punishment for breaking a sacred promise. Folklore warns that whistling near the peak will cause the "head" to fall.
