Hairouna (Yurumein): The Kalinago and Garifuna ancestral name for Saint Vincent means "Land of the Blessed," viewed in lore as a sacred spiritual homeland gifted by higher spirits. [1, 2, 3]

Spirit Luo: Early creation lore tells of the powerful spirit Luo who rose from the endless sea to shape the mountains and valleys of the islands with invisible threads of life.

Anansi Stories: Inherited from West Africa, these trickster tales feature Anansi the spider outwitting larger animals, emphasizing cleverness and oral performance.

Superstitions and Obeah: Belief systems include traditional African-derived spiritual practices, protective charms, and a lingering cultural respect for folk magic and local spirits.

Big Drum Music: Traditional rhythms performed on hollowed tree trunks or rum kegs accompanied by community dancing, historically reserved for milestone cultural events and weddings.

Petroglyphs: Ancient rock carvings left by early Amerindian inhabitants on basalt boulders reflect deep cosmological worldviews and adaptation to the volcanic islands.
