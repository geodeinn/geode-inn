The Fang people of the mainland share mythological traditions with neighboring Gabon and Cameroon.

Nzame: The supreme creator god. In some traditions, Nzame is recognized as a trinity of deities alongside Mebere and Nkwa.

Creation of Humanity: According to some tales, the creator sent the Chameleon to earth with a message that humans would never die. However, the message was reversed by a bird, which led to mortality and the natural cycle of life and death.

The Mvét Epics

The mvét is both a storytelling tradition and an eight-stringed musical instrument played by traveling troubadours.

The Èkáng Clan: Mvét performers sing epic, musical stories about the Èkáng clan—mythical, immortal representations of the Fang people.

Mythical Heroes: Heroes such as Mefoumou Mba Foumou embark on grand quests. In one famous episode, the hero travels across a bridge made of twisted pythons and uses magic to conjure an ebigane (a chimera-like beast with bat wings, fangs, and a buffalo's body) to fight his battles.

The Tortoise and the Leopard: The Tortoise is a beloved, clever trickster who relies on wisdom, patience, and foresight, whereas the Leopard represents unchecked pride and cruelty. The tales frequently show how the small and wise outsmart the strong and arrogant.

Why Goats Became Domestic: According to one tale from Corisco Island, goats were once wild and aggressive forest dwellers. The Goat's unchecked boasting and pride drew the envy of the Leopard. Only through human protection and community did the goat survive, sealing the enduring bond between humans and domesticated goats.

Bokona and the Talking Hogs: A unique myth originating from the deep forests where a hunter named Bokona hears hogs speaking the language of humans. The tale serves as a lesson of humility, reminding humanity that the natural world holds deep mysteries.

The Ndòwé (Benga or Playeros) people of the coastal regions have elaborate folklore regarding the cosmos and the sea.

Star Lore: Ndòwé skylore includes intricate dramas of the heavens. Celestial bodies represent members of a single, sprawling family—such as how Jupiter, Sirius, and Polaris are represented by distinct stars that reflect their brightness and position in the sky.

Storytelling is often combined with rhythmic music, traditional puppetry, and communal dance to invoke ancestral spirits.

The Byeri (Cult of the Dead): The Fang people practice a traditional ritual of ancestor worship that heavily features music, dialogue with the deceased, and stylized puppet figures. During initiations, traditional healing plants (like iboga) are sometimes consumed to enhance visions of ancestral apparitions.

The Abira: Celebrated by the Bubi people of Bioko Island, this is a community-wide gathering and ritual dance performed to cleanse the community and drive away evil spirits.
