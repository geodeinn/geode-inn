Before the world, there was only a cosmic sea. Ilmatar, the spirit of the air, descended into the water, and a teal laid an egg on her knee, which shattered to form the earth, sky, sun, moon, and stars. Instead of a harsh pantheon of warrior gods, Finnish folklore centers on benevolent nature deities.

Ukko: The supreme god of thunder, weather, and the harvest.

Tapio & Mielikki: The god of the forest and the goddess of the hunt, respectively, who were prayed to for safe passage and bountiful trips.

Ahti: The temperamental god of the sea and ruler of the aquatic realm.

Haltija: Helpful, elf-like household or land guardians.

Näkki: Shapeshifting, dangerous water spirits known to lure children into drowning beneath bridges or deep waters.

Otso (The Bear): The king of the forest, treated with supreme divine reverence as a sacred ancestor.

Revontulet (Fox Fires): The Northern Lights, which, according to myth, are caused by an arctic fox swishing its brushy tail across snowy fells and sending sparks into the sky.

Väinämöinen: The eternal bard, a powerful tietäjä (shaman/healer) who sang the world into existence and guided his people through the power of magical song.

Ilmarinen: The eternal, peerless blacksmith who forged the vaulted sky and the Sampo.

The Sampo: A magical, ornate artifact created by Ilmarinen that supposedly brings endless grain, salt, and gold to its owner. It became the root of legendary wars between the peoples of Kalevala (Väinölä) and the dark northern land of Pohjola.
