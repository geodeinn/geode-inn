Samodiva / Samovila: Wild, beautiful forest fairies possessing glowing eyes and a magical dress made of flowers. They live in the Balkan Mountains, dance barefoot in meadows, and can entrance or kill mortal men. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]

Zmey (Dragon): Unlike standard fire-breathing monsters, they are deeply connected to the weather. Some are protective guardians of a specific village, while others are known to fall in love with human women.

Lamia: A multi-headed, monstrous serpent that frequently battles the hero to control water sources or golden apples.

Karakondjul: A mischievous, shape-shifting demon that roams during the 12 days of Christmas, often jumping on the backs of unsuspecting travelers.

Talasum: A spirit formed when a building or bridge is built on a human shadow; they can be mischievous or protective.

The Little Brother & Golden Apple: The "nameless little brother" frequently embarks on harrowing quests to defeat dragons or retrieve the Golden Apple to save his kingdom.

Sly Peter (Hitar Petar): The most popular collective hero in Bulgarian humor. He acts as a witty, kind, and cunning peasant who constantly outsmarts rich merchants, priests, and even the devil.

Magical Beasts: Native animals often take human traits. Kuma Lisa (the cunning fox) and Kumcho Vulcho (the foolish wolf) represent the clever surviving against the strong.

Kukeri: An ancient spring ritual where men wear enormous, menacing wooden masks and heavy sheepskin coats. They jump and ring copper bells attached to their waists to ward off disease, bad weather, and evil spirits.

Nestinarstvo: A unique, mystical fire-dancing ritual practiced in specific villages (like those in the Strandzha Mountains), where dancers, holding icons, step barefoot onto glowing red coals without burning themselves.

Baba Marta (Grandma March): Celebrated on March 1st, people exchange and wear Martenitsi—red and white woven bracelets or tassels—to bring health and signify the coming of spring.
