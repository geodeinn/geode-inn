The Ao Fish (Aoyu): Originating from Huangshan and Tangmo villages, this legendary creature possesses a dragon’s head and a fish’s body. Folklore says a massive Aoyu carries Mount Tai on its back, and spotting a floating Aoyu lantern in the night sky brings peace and good fortune.

Zhong Kui: Hailing from Lingbi county, Zhong Kui is a fierce deity from Chinese mythology famous for vanquishing ghosts and evil spirits. In Anhui folklore, his legend is intrinsically tied to local craftsmanship, as local artisans immortalize his image by carving him out of rare Lingbi black stones.

Mount Huang's Immortals: The Yellow Emperor (Huangdi) is said to have ascended the peaks of Mount Huang to gather medicinal herbs and successfully forge pills of immortality.

Wuwei Fish Lanterns: Celebrated for over 600 years, this provincial-level Intangible Cultural Heritage features glowing, colorful fish-shaped lanterns parading through the streets, especially during the Spring Festival and Lantern Festival.

Taige Parades: A spectacular folk tradition historically performed in Shouxian County. It involves adults and children in elaborate makeup and costumes balancing on moving, elevated platforms to act out dramatic scenes from Chinese lore.

Huaguxi (Flower-drum Opera): Originating primarily from the northern city of Bozhou, this traditional folk opera features playful local dialects, songs, and dances that tell stories of rural life, harvest, and love.

Huizhou Architecture: Ancient Anhui villages—such as Hongcun and Xidi—are deeply rooted in Fengshui and Taoist harmony with nature, seamlessly blending mountain landscapes with curved, flying-eaves rooflines.

Four Treasures of the Study: Anhui's folklore and arts are connected through the invention of traditional calligraphy tools. The story goes that craftsmen in ancient Xuancheng invented Xuan Paper to perfectly preserve ink, allowing artists to record myths and local folklore across generations.
