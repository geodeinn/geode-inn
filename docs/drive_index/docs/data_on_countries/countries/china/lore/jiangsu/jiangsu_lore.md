Wu Cheng'en and Journey to the West: Born in Huai'an, the legendary Ming dynasty author Wu Cheng'en drew heavily from local river-town storytelling and regional myths to weave the epic tale of the Monkey King. [1]

Madam White Snake (Baishe Zhuan): One of China's Four Great Folktales is deeply rooted in the Zhenjiang region. The tragic romance of Lady White Snake and Xu Xian features iconic local folklore locations like the Jinshan Temple.

The Jiangshi (Hopping Ghosts): Deeply rooted in Taoist funerary traditions, the jiangshi (hopping ghost or Chinese vampire) is a staple of regional folklore. Traditionally, Taoist priests were believed to reanimate corpses, guiding them to hop along mountain roads so they could be buried in their hometowns.

Baojuan (Precious Scrolls): In towns like Changshu, the recitation of prosimetric "Baojuan" texts remains a prevalent folklore tradition. These vernacular stories are chanted during communal temple festivals to honor tutelary local deities.

Qiantong Boat Gathering: Held annually during the Qingming Festival in Jiangyan, this vibrant water activity sees hundreds of colorful wooden boats racing to drumbeats, celebrating the unity and courage of the northern Jiangsu water town people.

Shan'ge (Mountain Songs): Traditional folk singing from southern Jiangsu's Wu-speaking region. Historically sung by farmers and boatmen, these tunes encompass labor songs, romantic ballads, and historical legends.
