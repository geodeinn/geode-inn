The name "Heilongjiang" translates to "Black Dragon River". Local legend holds that a malevolent white dragon wreaked havoc and caused suffering. A brave young man transformed into a black dragon to fight the white dragon, eventually defeating it. The black dragon died in the conflict, and its body became the mighty river that brings flourishing natural resources and luck to the region.

One of the most famous figures in Northeast China's folklore is "Bald-Tailed Old Li". According to the tale, he was born as a black dragon in Shandong to the Li family, but had his tail severed by his frightened father. He flew north to Heilongjiang, where he helped local migrants defeat a wicked white dragon and became the region's benevolent river god. This legend reflects the historical "Migration to Northeast China" and embodies the nostalgia and regional identity of Shandong migrants.

The Hezhe people, an indigenous group residing where the Heilong and Songhua rivers meet, have a UNESCO-listed oral tradition called Yimakan. This art form involves a mix of speaking and singing without musical accompaniment. Yimakan stories are passed down through generations to preserve the history, spirituality, and culture of the clan.

Mergen Heroes: The tales focus on heroes called Mergen, symbols of wisdom and unmatched skill in fishing and hunting.

As the historical heartland of the Manchu, Heilongjiang is home to a vast collection of myths detailing creation, clan migrations, and ancestral spirits. Manchu epic storytelling features composite narrative structures with themes ranging from cosmic battles of three goddesses to the adventures of tribal heroes.
