Mazu (Goddess of the Sea): The most iconic figure in Fujian mythology. Born on Meizhou Island, she is revered as the protector of fishermen and sailors. Her cult has spread globally from this coastal epicenter. [1, 2]

Sun Wukong (The Monkey King): Beyond the classic Journey to the West, Fujian houses numerous regional temples dedicated to the Monkey King, elevating him from a literary character to a formally worshipped folk deity.

You Shen (游神): A centuries-old Daoist and local folk tradition where elaborate statues of deities are paraded through village streets to bring prosperity, health, and protection to the community.

Hakka Tulou: The folklore of the Hakka people is embedded in their Tulou—massive, circular, fortified earthen buildings. These structures were built to foster community and protect the clan, inspiring legends of unity and resilience.

Li Ji Slays the Great Serpent: A famous southern Chinese folktale (often associated with ancient Fujian) about a brave village girl who sacrifices herself to slay a giant, man-eating serpent, reflecting themes of courage and female empowerment.

Liuyue Liu (The 6th of June Festival): A traditional celebration deeply tied to local legends, particularly observed by the She minority ethnic groups living in the mountainous regions of Fujian.

Songbooks and Puppetry: Fujian is famous for "Dongshan Songbook" (Dongshan Gece) and shadow/glove puppetry, which have been used for centuries to pass down tragic, romantic, and heroic local sagas (such as tales of the region's ancient generals).

Oral Histories of Women: Scholars have compiled collections of village folktales (such as Imagining Women: Fujian Folk Tales) that reveal the everyday lives of common people, providing an alternative perspective to male-dominated Confucian lore.
