The Butterfly Mother: In Miao folklore, all living things and the ancestors of the Miao were birthed by the Butterfly Mother, who hatched from a maple tree.

The King of Yalu: A massive oral epic of the Miao, focusing on the origins of the world, epic migrations, and mythological battles.

Lusheng & Song: The Dong people are famous for their Kam Grand Songs (polyphonic choir music with no director). They are performed in Drum Towers, which are traditional wooden structures that act as community centers.

Textile Epics: Because many ancient minority groups lacked a written language, they recorded their myths and family histories through intricate silverwork and embroidery.

Sister's Meals Festival: A major Miao festival centered around courtship and love, celebrated in the spring with young women offering traditional colored rice to suitors.

Dragon Dance with Molten Iron Fireworks: Celebrated around the Lantern Festival in Puding County, generations have passed down this tradition where performers brave molten iron sparks as a blessing for good harvests.

Dixi (Ground) Opera: Known as the "living fossil" of Chinese drama, this masked opera is heavily tied to the ancient customs of the Han and Yi populations in Anshun, acting out myths and historical battles.
