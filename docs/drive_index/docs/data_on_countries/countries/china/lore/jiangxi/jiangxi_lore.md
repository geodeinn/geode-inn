The Jiangshi (Chinese Hopping Vampire/Zombie): While a broad Chinese legend, the Jiangshi ("stiff corpse") or Jiangxi monster, features heavily in local village lore. These reanimated corpses are said to suck the qi (vital energy) of the living and travel by hopping stiffly across the landscape. Local tales suggest their stiff movements were inspired by ancient "corpse drivers" who used to bounce bodies across rugged terrain using bamboo rods.

Nuo Dance (Ghost Drama): Originating in areas like Pingxiang, this is one of the oldest dance types of the Han Chinese. Performers wear terrifying wooden masks and engage in ritualistic "ghost dramas" to worship gods and ward off plagues and evil spirits.

Jingdezhen Porcelain Lore: Known as the "Porcelain Capital of China", the city has fascinating tales dating back to the Song Dynasty (960–1279 AD). Potters historically told stories of the fierce devotion required to master their craft, with some myths attributing the flawless blue and white glazes to the magical properties of the local kaolin clays.

Strange Stories from a Chinese Studio (Liaozhai Zhiyi): Many of Pu Songling’s famous tales of the supernatural—including the ghost of ghosts (Jian) and shape-shifting fox spirits—are heavily tied to the philosophical and mystical settings inspired by Jiangxi's Millennium Zen temples and academies.
