Home of Fuxi: Tianshui in Gansu is historically revered as the birthplace of Fuxi, the legendary first emperor of Chinese mythology who is credited with inventing writing, fishing, and trapping.

The Mother of Mankind: Legend associates Mount Niutou in Gansu with Nuwa, the goddess who molded humanity from clay and patched the heavens.

Nine-Colored Deer: Originating from a famous mural in the Mogao Caves in Dunhuang, this tale follows a magnificent deer who saves a drowning man. The man betrays the deer to the king for a reward, but the deer magically exposes the betrayal, teaching a lasting moral lesson about gratitude and greed.

Flying Apsaras: The enchanting flying devatas (angels) painted on the ceilings of Dunhuang are central to Gansu's folklore. They symbolize the intersection of Indian, Central Asian, and Chinese Buddhist beliefs.

Hua'er Folk Songs: A celebrated musical art of Northwest China, these high-pitched, spontaneous folk songs are passed down orally. They are frequently sung in the mountains as romantic expressions or laments about rural life.

Baima Tibetan Traditions: The Baima people of southern Gansu are renowned for their unique, mask-based animist dances (like the Tsangba Dance) that honor nature spirits and ancestors, accompanied by unique Tibetan lute and flute music.

Thangka Painting: Tibetan communities in Gannan preserve the intricate art of Thangka. These are scroll paintings of Buddhist deities, and mastering them takes monks decades of practice.

Yugur Shamanism: The Yugur people—a unique ethnic minority in Sunan—have a rich tradition of oral folklore filled with animal fables. They historically practiced shamanism, using trees, blue and white wool, and specific rituals to bridge the human and spirit worlds.

Hexi Baojuan (Precious Scrolls): Originating from Dunhuang, these storytelling performances alternate between prose and verse. They act as a mix of Taoist parables, folktales, and morality plays passed down orally through the Hexi Corridor.
