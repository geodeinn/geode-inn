The Butterfly Lovers (Liang Zhu): Originating in Ningbo and Hangzhou, this is one of China's Four Great Folktales. It follows Zhu Yingtai, who disguises herself as a male scholar to get an education. She falls in love with her classmate Liang Shanbo. Forbidden to marry, Liang dies of a broken heart. When Zhu's funeral procession passes his grave, it opens, and the two lovers’ souls emerge as butterflies, flying away together.

The Legend of the White Snake (Baishe Zhuan): Set primarily at Hangzhou's West Lake and the Leifeng Pagoda, this tale features a white snake spirit who transforms into a beautiful woman named Bai Suzhen. She falls in love with a mortal man, Xu Xian. Their romance is constantly disrupted by a conservative Buddhist monk, Fahai, who eventually imprisons Bai under the pagoda.

Legends of Great Yu & Fangfeng: Deep in the mountainous terrain of northern Zhejiang lies the legacy of Yu the Great, who tamed ancient floods. Local folklore heavily features the myth of Fangfeng, an ancestral giant and tutelary spirit of the region. Historically, Yu executed Fangfeng for arriving late to a flood-control assembly, but local temples in Zhejiang revere Fangfeng as an exonerated local hero.

Sericulture and the Legend of Leizu: The Jiangnan region (including Zhejiang) is the heartland of Chinese silk production. Folklore credits Empress Leizu, the wife of the Yellow Emperor, with discovering silk when a silkworm cocoon dropped into her tea. This legend birthed a rich tapestry of local sericulture taboos and rituals practiced in Zhejiang's silk towns.

Dragon Worship & Dances: Rooted in the Xin'an River Basin, "Loong" (dragon) culture isa massive part of Zhejiang folk tradition. Colorful festivals frequently feature unique local performances like the grass loongs, bench loongs, and fish lantern loongs.

Local Diet & Customs: Seasonal folk rituals heavily dictate life. During Lixia (the start of summer), locals practice folk customs centering around specific foods, such as eating wumifan (black glutinous rice) to protect against summer heat and illnesses.

Nature Spirits: Rooted in early localized folklore, everyday physical landmarks in Zhejiang (such as rivers, mountains, and trees) are believed to harbor their own ancient spirits, deities, or "Yaoguai" (monsters), which demand reverence and respect.
