Xintianyou: A genre of mountain folk songs requiring a clear, sincere voice. These are passed down orally and tell the real-life struggles of farming, poverty, and love.

Sanxian Shu: A Northern Shaanxi storytelling art where performers sing and recount legends while playing a three-stringed lute (sanxian).

Shadow Puppetry: Intricate, hand-carved leather figures acting out legendary Chinese tales—like The Monkey King—accompanied by local music.

Shehuo: An ancient, elaborate folk performance originating from sacrificial ceremonies. Performers wear terrifying, vibrant, traditional masks (such as "Horse Ladle" facial makeup) intended to ward off evil spirits and bring good harvests.

Paper-Cutting (Jianzhi): Widely practiced in rural communities like Yanchuan County, women pass these detailed, symmetrical designs through generations to decorate windows and celebrate the seasons.

Embroidered Patchwork: Vibrant textiles, including tiger-head shoes and pillows, made to protect children and invite blessings.

The "Ten Weirdos of Guanzhong"

Central Shaanxi (the Guanzhong Plain) is famous for its humorous folklore known as the "Ten Weirdos." This oral tradition playfully highlights the unique local climate, geography, and diet:

Noodles like belts: Belt-wide biangbiang noodles.

Houses like half houses: Slanted, single-slope roof structures.

Girls not walking out in public: Headscarves used to shield women from loess dust.

Crying, not singing, for marriage: Traditional wedding laments.
