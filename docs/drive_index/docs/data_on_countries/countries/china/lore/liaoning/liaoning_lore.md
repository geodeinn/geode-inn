The Fox Spirit: Often viewed as clever, magical, and capable of taking human form.

The Weasel (Yellow Rat): Believed to be highly spiritual and capable of either bringing good fortune or wreaking havoc if angered.

The Snake (Long): Associated with protection, healing, and water.

The Hedgehog & Gray Rat: Revered in domestic settings, sometimes possessing household shrines (Xiantang or Tangzi).

"The Princess of the Moon": Predating the classic Chang'e legend, this iconic Harqin Left Wing Mongolian folklore traces the history, culture, and nomadic lifestyles of Manchurian, Mongolian, and Han groups in western Liaoning.

The Hongshan Culture: Centered around Chaoyang, this 6,000-year-old prehistoric civilization is famous for unearthing the "Jade Dragon" and "Jade Owl," which gave rise to dragon and animal worship folklore still prevalent throughout China today.

Shuo Bu: Epic Manchu oral narratives and heroic legends, traditionally sung with drums, narrating the origins of the Manchu people and their transition from hunting to agriculture.

The geographical and environmental landscapes of Liaoning—spanning the Bohai Sea, coastal wetlands, and the Changbai Mountains (locally known as Dongshanli)—have birthed regional legends. Stories frequently focus on water deities, animal protectors of the forest, and mythological manifestations like the Kui Niu (a powerful, one-legged mythical creature that symbolizes strength and protection).

Folklore in Liaoning is actively preserved through regional performance arts and rituals, these include:

Yangge Dance: A vibrant, rhythmic, and acrobatic stilt and group dance used to invoke gods and celebrate bountiful harvests (such as the Quanbao Cunqiao Yangge in Benxi).

Shadow Puppetry: Traditional folk dramas and folktales told using intricate shadow puppets, heavily popular in cities like Shenyang and Liaoyang.

Suona and Drum Music: Traditional wind and percussion music used in both celebratory and sacred folkloric ceremonies.
