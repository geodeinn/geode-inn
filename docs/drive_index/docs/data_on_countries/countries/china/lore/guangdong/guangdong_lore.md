Wong Fei-hung: The most famous martial artist and folk hero of the region. A master of Hung Ga kung fu, he is immortalized as a symbol of righteousness, medicine, and Southern Chinese pride.

The Ten Tigers of Canton: A legendary group of ten top martial artists who defended the province against corruption and foreign invaders during the late Qing Dynasty.

General Yuan (Yuan Chonghuan): The Ming Dynasty general who defended the region against the Manchus. His famous battlefield catchphrase, "Diu nei maa, ding ying soeng!" (an incredibly fierce Cantonese exclamation), is deeply ingrained in Cantonese cultural defiance.

The Southern Dragon: Unlike the imperial dragons of the north, Guangdong folklore features the dragons of the South China Sea. They are seen as patrons of the local fishermen and bringers of both rain and devastating typhoons.

Black Carp (Kuang Yu): Deeply revered in Guangdong ponds, the black carp is a symbol of immense perseverance, strength, and overcoming adversity.

Amah Rock (Mong Fu Shek): Located in Hong Kong (part of the historical Canton region), this is a famous stone formation. According to legend, it is the petrified body of a loyal woman holding her baby, gazing out to sea, forever waiting for her fisherman husband to return.

Yingge Dance: Originating in the Chaoshan (Teochew) region, this vibrant, 300-year-old folk dance translates to "Hero's Song". Performers wear elaborate, dramatic face makeup and portray the 108 rebel heroes from the classic Chinese novel Outlaws of the Marsh.

The Lion Dance: The Cantonese Lion Dance is famed globally. In folklore, the "lion" is a mythical beast believed to dispel evil spirits and bring good luck, prosperity, and rain to the harvests.

Mid-Autumn Fire Dragon Dances: Performed throughout Guangdong (especially in Tai Hang), these massive, glowing dragons are paraded through the streets to rid villages of plagues and bad luck.

The Goddess Mazu: Given the province's vast coastline, Mazu (the Goddess of the Sea) is the most prominent deity in Guangdong. Fishermen pray to her for safe voyages and abundant catches.

Zhuji Lane Legend: Many Cantonese people trace their ancestral roots back to a specific migration point in northern Guangdong (Nanxiong) during the Song Dynasty. Legends of leaving this ancestral home frame much of the province's culture of diaspora, loyalty, and migration.
