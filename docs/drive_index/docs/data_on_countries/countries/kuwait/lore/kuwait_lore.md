Hemarat Al-Gayla: A infamous half-woman, half-donkey creature (literally translating to "female donkey of noon"). Parents used her myth to scare children into staying indoors during the scorching summer afternoons to prevent sunstroke.

Tantal: A Boogeyman figure described as a tall man with disheveled hair and heavy footsteps. Elders used the Tantal legend to frighten children away from wandering the streets alone after dark.

Bu Darya: A marine djinn—half human, half fish—who terrorized pearl divers and sailors. He would shriek in the middle of the night acting as if he were drowning; if sailors attempted to rescue him, he would pull them down into the depths.

Al-Seolu: A towering figure with elongated teeth, said to kidnap and cannibalize children who strayed far from home.

Um Al-Sa'af Wa-Alleef: A grotesque, unkempt woman who could fly using palm fronds. She was primarily used by parents to terrify misbehaving children into obeying requests.

Al-Duaidea: A supernatural mirage that conjures a glowing, moving ember; it would mysteriously change locations whenever someone approached to pick it up.

Before the discovery of oil, Kuwait's economy was anchored by pearl diving and ocean trade. This seafaring life birthed a wealth of maritime folklore, chants, and sea shanties. The nahham (traditional singer) played a vital role, singing call-and-response chants to keep the crew's spirits and energy high while pulling sails and hauling up heavy anchor lines.

On land, Bedouin tribes developed their own specific forms of folklore, celebrated through rhythmic, communal dances and poetry. Traditions like the Al-Qalta, Al-Feraisni, and Al-Dahha involve rhythmic clapping, foot-stomping, and call-and-response poetry used to show solidarity during weddings, childbirth, and national celebrations.

Another major cornerstone is Al-Sadu weaving. This intricate, traditional Bedouin craft uses camel and sheep wool to create vibrant, geometric patterns on tents and rugs. The patterns often reflect the desert environment and local flora.
