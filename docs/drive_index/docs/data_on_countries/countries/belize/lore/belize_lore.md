Tata Duende (El Dueño del Monte): A cunning, roughly 3-foot-tall dwarf with feet pointing backward to mislead travelers. He has no thumbs and wears a large straw or red hat. He protects the forest but will kidnap children and cut off the thumbs of those who do not hide them.

Xtabai (La Xtabai): A beautiful seductress from Maya folklore. She appears at night under Ceiba trees, luring men into the jungle with her silky hair and hypnotic beauty. If a man approaches, he meets a grisly fate, often emerging in the morning covered in fatal, venomous thorns.

Sisimito (or Sisimite): Belize's version of Bigfoot. A short, hairy ape-like creature that lives in deep caves. They are known for kidnapping women to live in their caves, having their feet on backward to confuse trackers, and having no knees.

Cadejo: A mythical dog wrapped in a chain. It has burning red eyes and goat hooves. The white Cadejo is a benevolent spirit that protects drunk men on their way home, while the black Cadejo is a malicious, evil entity.

La Llorona (The Crying Woman): A tall, slender woman with long hair who lost her children near a river and seeks revenge by wandering late at night, luring disobedient children and men into the jungle.

Alux (Aluxo'ob): Small, knee-high spirits from Maya mythology resembling miniature people in traditional dress. They act as good caretakers of the land and crops, but play mischievous tricks on those who disrespect nature.

La Chatona: A 7-foot-tall dancing mannequin made of bayal (a tropical vine), traditionally danced through the streets of towns like Benque Viejo Del Carmen during September celebrations.

Good Friday Taboos: Local mothers warn their children that swimming in rivers or the ocean on Good Friday is strictly bad luck, as they will turn into mermaids or fish.

Finados: A Belizean celebration (similar to Día de los Muertos) where food is left out for the dead, and elders share supernatural folklore.
