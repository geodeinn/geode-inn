The Seven-Headed Dragon: A Pamir Mountain legend about a beast whose heart must be pierced beneath its scales to be defeated.

The Sleeping Knights: A tale of heroes put into deep sleep in the Fan Mountains, awaiting a time of need to protect the land.

The Walnut Tree Taboo: A widespread belief that sleeping under walnut trees invites jinns or spirits that can cause paralysis.

The Yeti (Snow Man): Pamiri mountain lore features enduring accounts of large, wild bipedal humanoids roaming high-altitude snowline borders.

Shah-nameh Tradition: Storytelling (shohnomakhoni) derived from Ferdawsi's Book of Kings, detailing cosmic battles between good and evil.

Dastans of Rustam: Heroic, theatrical oral recitations following ancient Persian legendary warriors.

Falak: Traditional, soulful mountain folk music characterized by high-pitched, expressive vocal solos dealing with destiny, love, and longing.

Shashmaqom: A complex, classical 6-mode musical tradition featuring Sufi-inspired Persian poetry.

Folk Instruments: Performance uses the dutar (two-stringed lute), rubab, doira (frame drum), and nay (flute).

Nowruz: The ancient Persian New Year on March 21–22, marking spring planting with house cleaning, communal bonfires, and traditional games like Buzkashi.

Fire Jumping: Pre-Islamic rituals involving fires used to ward off evil or devils in remote valleys.
