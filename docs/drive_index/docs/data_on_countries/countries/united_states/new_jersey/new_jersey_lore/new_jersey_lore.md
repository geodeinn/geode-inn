New Jersey's folklore is rich with cryptids, ghosts, and eerie mysteries. The crown jewel is the Jersey Devil, a winged, hooved beast said to terrorize the 1-million-acre Pine Barrens. Legend claims Mother Leeds cursed her 13th child in 1735, causing the baby to transform into a monster and fly away into the swamps.

Other notable Garden State legends include:

Clinton Road (Passaic County): Known as one of the most haunted roads in America. Folklore includes tales of a ghost boy on the bridge who returns coins dropped into the water, phantom vehicles, and connections to mafia body-dumping.

The Blue Hole (Winslow): A deep, eerie, indigo-colored pond in the Pine Barrens. Local mythology claims it has no bottom, acts as a vortex, and is home to the Jersey Devil or other sinister water spirits.

The Pig Lady of Mount Misery (Burlington County): A haunting spirit tied to "Pig Lady Road," said to be the ghost of a woman who roams the isolated woods seeking revenge for brutal ax attacks.

The Black Doctor of the Pines: The spirit of James Still, a 19th-century self-taught African American herbalist who lived in the Pine Barrens. Legend says his ghost still appears to aid lost or injured travelers.
