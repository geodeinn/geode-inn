Cryptozoology & Monsters

Bear Lake Monster: A serpentine, 50-foot creature with legs and a cow-like head. The first reports originated in the 1860s in the border town of Garden City. [1, 2, 3]

Skinwalker: Rooted in Navajo tradition, Skinwalkers are malevolent, shape-shifting witches capable of taking the form of animals. Sightings are deeply feared in the Uintah Basin and around the Four Corners area. [1, 2]

Utah Lake Water Babies: In Ute and Great Basin Native folklore, these are small, spiritual humanoids believed to inhabit Utah Lake, sometimes said to lure people into the water.

Haunted Locations & Cures

Allen Park

Once owned by a local eccentric, the site is populated by peacocks and rustic cottages. Local legend dubbed it "Hobbitville," claiming it was inhabited by a community of little people.

Dead Horse Point

A dramatic overlook with a legend of a ghostly, wild white horse said to haunt the precipice after the tragic death of a herd.

Lost Treasures & Historical Myths

Montezuma’s Treasure: A legend claiming immense Aztec gold was smuggled into Utah's Red Rock country. Generations of prospectors have dug through the canyons near Kanab, convinced it is protected by ancient booby traps.

The Dream Mine: An ongoing, century-old prophecy involving a gold mine in Salem, Utah, famously dug by locals acting upon a dream of John H. Koyle, who prophesied it would save people during an upcoming economic collapse.

Native American Legends & The Great Basin

Fremont and Ute Rock Art: The deep, mysterious pictographs and petroglyphs carved into canyon walls across the state, such as those at Nine Mile Canyon, represent ancient religious spirits and hunting chronicles that folklorists continue to study.

Gadianton Robbers: A band of secret, shadowy individuals mentioned in the Book of Mormon, often woven into local folklore as supposedly roaming and hiding out in the Wasatch Mountains.
