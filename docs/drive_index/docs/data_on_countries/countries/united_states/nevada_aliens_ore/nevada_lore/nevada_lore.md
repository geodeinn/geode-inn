The Water Babies of Pyramid Lake

Deep in Pyramid Lake, the "Water Babies" are the restless spirits of premature or ill-formed infants. According to Northern Paiute tradition, these infants were cast into the water to avoid hardship. Today, local folklore warns that these angry spirits lure fishermen to their deaths, and visitors camping near the shore still report hearing the faint cries of babies echoing across the water.

The Si-Te-Cah Giants of Lovelock Cave

Paiute oral tradition tells of a fierce race of red-haired, cannibalistic giants known as the Si-Te-Cah. These giants were eventually driven into Lovelock Cave and suffocated by the Paiutes. In 1911, miners digging for bat guano unearthed mummified human remains in the cave, adding real-world fuel to the myth.

Tahoe Tessie and Cecil the Serpent

Nevada's lakes are famously tied to monster legends. "Tahoe Tessie" is a plesiosaur-like creature reported to swim the depths of Lake Tahoe, rooted in Washoe tradition and numerous modern boater sightings. Down south, Walker Lake is home to "Cecil the Serpent," a 30-foot, crocodile-like snake said to have terrorized indigenous tribes.

The Cactus Cat

A uniquely Southwestern cryptid, the Cactus Cat is a wild, ferocious feline covered in sharp spines, with branching, antler-like thorns on its head. Folklore describes it roaming the Mojave Desert, slashing cacti to drink their sap and letting out blood-curdling shrieks to intimidate prospectors and campers.
