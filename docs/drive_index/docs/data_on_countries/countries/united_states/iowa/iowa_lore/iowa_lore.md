Cryptids & Monsters

The Van Meter Visitor: In 1903, residents of Van Meter reported encounters with a winged, half-human, half-bat monster that emitted a horrible stench and shot a blinding light from a horn on its forehead. [1, 2]

Pepie: The legend of a lake monster, similar to Nessie, living in the deep waters of West Okoboji Lake in northwest Iowa.

The Ioway Monster Tales: Indigenous folklore, specifically from the Ioway (Baxoje) people, includes mythical figures like Sharp-Elbows (a man-eating ogre) and Shunka Warak'in (a wolf-like monster).

Ghost Stories & Urban Legends

The Children of Iron Hill: Legend claims a fiery train crash in the early 1900s killed orphaned children traveling through Charles City. Today, locals and visitors along the Charlie Western Trail report hearing the cries of children and seeing ghostly figures in the woods.

The Mathias Ham House: Built in 1856 in Dubuque, this documented historic site is famous for tales of flickering lights, cold breezes, and the sound of an unplayed organ.

Historical Folklore

Kate Shelley: One of Iowa’s most celebrated real-life folklore heroes. In 1881, a 15-year-old girl named Kate Shelley crawled across a flooded, storm-ravaged railroad trestle to warn an oncoming passenger train of a collapsed bridge near Moingona, saving countless lives.
