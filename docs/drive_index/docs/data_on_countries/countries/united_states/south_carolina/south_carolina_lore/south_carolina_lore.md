Cryptids & Monsters

The Lizard Man: A 7-foot-tall reptilian creature with searing red eyes and three-clawed fingers, first sighted in Lee County in 1988 after it allegedly attacked a teenager's car.

Third-Eye Man: A phantom reported by students in 1949 who claimed to see a man with an extra eye roaming the "catacombs" beneath the University of South Carolina in Columbia.

Hound of Goshen: A spectral "happy dog" that reportedly tracks down the killers of its owner through Union and Newberry counties.

Ghostly Phenomena

The Gray Man: A ghostly figure seen wandering Pawleys Island before major hurricanes; seeing him is said to spare your home from the worst of the storm's destruction.

The Ravenel Lights: Mysterious, otherworldly lights that appear near ancient Baptist churches and train tracks in Ravenel, sometimes accompanied by strange winds and hand-shaped dents on nearby cars.

Old State Road Legends: In Cayce, a local urban legend claims that if you put flour on your car bumper and stop on the railroad tracks past a church, ghostly handprints will appear to push your car to safety.

Gullah & Coastal Folklore

Boo Hags: From Gullah mythology, these are skinless spirits that steal human breath and ride horses at night. They can be warded off by putting salt or brooms with many bristles by your doorways.

Blue Bottle Trees: Used across the South Carolina lowlands, placing blue glass bottles on branches is a Gullah tradition meant to trap evil spirits or "hags" that come out at night.

Dr. Buzzard: A well-known historical figure and voodoo doctor from St. Helena Island who used roots, herbs, and hexes to practice hoodoo.

Historical Haunts & Curses

Lands End Ghost: A legendary figure who wanders the Sea Islands of South Carolina, usually spotted walking along coastal roads.

Julia Legare: An Edisto Island legend of a woman mistakenly buried alive. Her tomb was sealed, but later found open from the inside, with handprints remaining on the mausoleum doors for years.

Old Exchange Dungeon: In Charleston, this colonial-era prison is famously haunted by the ghosts of pirates, prisoners, and British soldiers from the American Revolution.
