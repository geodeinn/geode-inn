The Pennsylvania Dutch heavily influenced local folklore, blending European fairy tales with the rugged Appalachian landscape. Belief in "powwowing" (a form of faith healing and folk magic) was once widespread.

Hex Signs: These geometric patterns painted on barns are deeply rooted in folklore as symbols meant to bring good fortune, ward off evil spirits, and protect livestock.

The Squonk: Hailing from the hemlock forests of northern Pennsylvania, this famously ugly creature is so ashamed of its appearance that it hides and weeps constantly. If cornered by a hunter, the Squonk will dissolve entirely into a pool of tears and bubbles.

Ghosts, Monsters, and Urban Legends

The state's long industrial history and deep forests have given rise to various cryptids, spirits, and urban legends:

The Green Man (Raymond Robinson): Based out of the South Park area near Pittsburgh. Robinson was severely disfigured by an electrical accident as a child and took to walking the roads at night. He became a celebrated local legend, with generations of Pittsburghers searching for "The Green Man" into the 1950s and beyond.

Raystown Ray: Pennsylvania's own lake monster, said to lurk in the depths of Raystown Lake, with sightings stretching back decades.

The Easton Monk: A notoriously dark tale from Easton tells of a greedy, corrupt monk who was hanged for beating an elderly woman to death, only for his body to spring back to life as a monstrous ghoul.

The Kecksburg UFO (1965)

In December 1965, a massive fireball streaked across the sky, crashing into the woods of Kecksburg, PA. Military cordons quickly locked down the area, sparking decades of folklore surrounding a crashed Soviet satellite or an alien spacecraft bearing Egyptian-like hieroglyphics on its hull.
