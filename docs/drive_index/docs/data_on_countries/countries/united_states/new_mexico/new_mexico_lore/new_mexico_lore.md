1. La Llorona (The Weeping Woman)

Deeply embedded in the Hispanic oral tradition across the state, this spirit is a tragic mother who drowned her own children in a fit of jealousy. She is said to perpetually roam the rushing waters of the Rio Grande and the Pecos River, clad in a flowing white dress and loudly crying out for her lost children.

2. Skinwalkers

Derived from Navajo traditions, skinwalkers (yee naaldlooshii) are human witches who have attained dark, supernatural powers. They are capable of shape-shifting into animals like coyotes, wolves, owls, or crows, and are known for their terrifying, glowing eyes and uncanny speed.

3. Brujas (Witches)

New Mexican colonial history is filled with legends of brujas. Folklorist accounts note stories of witches who could make themselves invisible or even shape-shift into owls. Local superstitions warn that if you see a tecolote (owl) in a tree and it asks for salt the following day, you have encountered a witch.

4. The Taos Hum

Located in the northern region of the state, the Taos Hum is a persistent, low-frequency buzzing noise that a small percentage of residents and visitors claim to hear. Despite scientific investigations, its exact origin remains completely unexplained.

5. The Roswell Incident

New Mexico is an international hub for UFO and alien lore, anchored by the infamous events of July 1947. While the military claimed a weather balloon crashed, local folklore and global conspiracy theories continue to insist that an extraterrestrial spacecraft and its alien crew were recovered near Roswell.
