Indiana folklore is a rich tapestry of Native American legends, midwestern tall tales, and chilling urban legends, from the ghostly shores of Lake Michigan to the dense, rural woods.

Haunted Places & Local Legends

Diana of the Dunes (Indiana Dunes State Park): A popular legend on the shores of Lake Michigan describes a mysterious, ethereal woman roaming the beach and running into the lake. The legend is inspired by the real-life Alice Mabel Gray, a brilliant socialite who abandoned Chicago in 1915 to live as a hermit in the dunes.

100 Step Cemetery (Brazil): Located in Clay County, this cemetery features a crumbling staircase. Urban legends warn that if you climb and count the steps at midnight, the number will change on the way back down, or you will see a vision of your own death from the old caretaker.

House of Blue Lights (Indianapolis): Once located in the northeast side of Indianapolis, this sprawling estate belonged to eccentric millionaire Skiles Test. Throughout the 20th century, locals told stories of the property being endlessly decorated in strange, glowing blue Christmas lights, sparking rumors of hauntings and secret cults.

Cryptids & Monsters

Beast of Busco (Churubusco): In 1949, a local farmer claimed to see a colossal, boat-sized snapping turtle in Fulk Lake. Dubbed "Oscar," the turtle caused a media frenzy with locals attempting to drain the lake and trap the beast. Churubusco still celebrates its legendary turtle today with an annual "Turtle Days" festival.

Crawfordsville Monster: In 1891, terrified residents reported a floating, headless, glowing creature flapping through the night sky. While it caused mass panic at the time, many historians and investigators later attributed the sightings to a frightened, migrating flock of killdeer.

The Ohio River Monster: Originating in 1955 near Evansville, locals reported a human-sized, green-skinned creature with hairy arms and claws lurking in the waters, allegedly attempting to pull swimmers under.
