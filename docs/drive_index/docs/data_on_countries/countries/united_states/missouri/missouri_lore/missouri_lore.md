Momo (The Missouri Monster)

First gaining widespread attention in July 1972, this Bigfoot-like creature was famously spotted by two children in the small town of Louisiana, Missouri. Witnesses described Momo (short for Missouri Monster) as a 7-foot-tall, incredibly hairy bipedal creature with a pumpkin-shaped head, a terrible odor, and a habit of carrying dead dogs.

The Hornet Spooklight

Also known as the Joplin or Ozark Spooklight, this mysterious atmospheric ghost light has been documented for over a century on the Devil's Promenade, a rural road near the Missouri-Oklahoma border. The nocturnal anomaly appears as a bouncing or floating orange ball of fire that can separate, split, and dance down the road before disappearing entirely.

The Ozark Howler

A legendary creature purported to live deep in the wilderness of the Ozarks, stretching from southern Missouri to northern Arkansas. Described as a bear-sized, shaggy black cat or "nightshade bear" with glowing eyes and horns, it is famous for its terrifying cry, which is said to sound like a mix of an elk bugle, a wolf howl, and a human scream.

The Bald Knobbers

Unlike the mythical cryptids of the region, the Bald Knobbers were a real, documented group of 1880s vigilantes in southwest Missouri. Originating in Taney County, these masked night-riders wore intimidating black horned hoods and used heavy-handed, sometimes deadly, tactics to enforce their own brand of frontier law and morality.
