Indigenous & Lakota Legend

The White Buffalo Calf Woman

A cornerstone of Lakota lore. According to tradition tied to Wind Cave near Hot Springs, she brought the sacred pipe and ceremonies to the people, promising abundance.

Sica Hollow

Located in northeastern South Dakota, this heavily forested ravine was deemed sacred and "evil" by the Sioux (meaning they refused to hunt there) due to its crimson-running water.

A Dakota legend tells of a young boy who spotted enemies disguised as wolves. The warriors avenged the women and children by trapping and defeating the enemies in this exact lake.

Cryptids & Spooky Folklore

The Badlands Banshee: A wailing ghost haunting the rugged, eroded terrain of the Badlands.

Walking Sam: A shadowy, tall, and thin figure who stalks the Black Hills.

The Jackalope: This mythical horned rabbit is famously celebrated as a plains "fearsome critter" from the Dakota frontier.

Wild West Legends & Outlaws

Jesse James and Devil's Gulch: Located in Garretson, this 60-foot canyon is the site where the notorious outlaw Jesse James reportedly leapt his horse across an 18-foot gap to evade a posse.

Potato Creek Johnny: A famed local folk hero and prospector of the Black Hills who discovered one of the largest gold nuggets in the region (7.5 troy ounces) in 1929. He is buried in Deadwood's Mount Moriah Cemetery (cityofdeadwood.com). [1, 2, 3]

Hugh Glass: The ultimate frontier survival legend. Attacked by a grizzly bear along the Grand River, he famously crawled hundreds of miles across the South Dakota wilderness to seek revenge on the trappers who abandoned him.

Geographical Tall Tales

Paul Bunyan in the Black Hills: Logging tall tales place the giant lumberjack Paul Bunyan in the Dakotas, claiming he and his blue ox Babe traversed the state and created the Black Hills.

Spirit Mound: Located near Vermillion, early legends told of tiny, protective creatures living here, a place Lewis and Clark visited in 1804 when investigating the mound.
