Massachusetts folklore blends Wampanoag traditions, colonial history, and maritime myths. The most famous legends include the 1692 Salem Witch Trials, the haunted Hoosac Tunnel, the 1892 Lizzie Borden ax murders in Fall River, and the Pukwudgies—sinister, small gray humanoids from Native lore.

Haunted Places & Local Legends

The Hoosac Tunnel (Florida & North Adams): Dug through the Berkshires between 1851 and 1875, this train tunnel is infamously dubbed the "Bloody Pit." Over 200 workers died during its construction, and legends claim their ghosts still whisper in the dark.

Boston Common (Boston): America's oldest public park was once a site for public hangings, including the Great Elm. Spectral figures, like the pirate Rachel Wall, are tied to the park's centuries-old history.

Lizzie Borden House (Fall River): The site of the unsolved 1892 murders of Andrew and Abby Borden. Today, it operates as a bed and breakfast, offering overnight stays where guests report hearing phantom footsteps.

The Bridgewater Triangle: An approximately 200-square-mile area in southeastern Massachusetts known for high concentrations of strange phenomena, ranging from UFO and Bigfoot sightings to poltergeist activity in the Hockomock Swamp.

Cryptids & Mythical Beasts

Pukwudgies: Wampanoag legend tells of these diminutive, porcupine-quilled humanoids that inhabit the swamps of eastern Massachusetts.

The Gloucester Sea Serpent: Dating back to 1817, numerous sightings were reported in Cape Ann of a massive 60-to-100-foot-long aquatic serpent swimming in the bay.

Maritime & Historical Mysteries

Dighton Rock (Berkley): A massive boulder carved with mysterious, overlapping petroglyphs. Explanations range from ancient Viking or Portuguese explorer inscriptions to Native American artwork.

The Mary Celeste: Though an Atlantic maritime mystery, Captain Benjamin Briggs and his ill-fated crew hailed from Marion, Massachusetts. The ship was found entirely abandoned in 1872 with its cargo and food supply intact.
