Florida’s folklore blends Native American myths, Spanish colonial legends, and Southern pioneer tales. Famous lore includes the Skunk Ape (Florida’s swamp-dwelling Bigfoot), the paranormal mysteries of Cassadaga (Florida's psychic capital), and the phantom cars rolling uphill on Lake Wales' Spook Hill.

Florida boasts a distinct regional mythology shaped by its massive swamps, diverse cultural influences, and isolated pioneer settlements. The legends often reflect the wild, untamed nature of the peninsula:

The Skunk Ape: The South’s version of Bigfoot. This large, hairy, foul-smelling cryptid is said to inhabit the remote wetland forests of the Everglades and Big Cypress. Sightings date back decades, with many locals claiming to have encountered the bipedal beast.

Spook Hill (Lake Wales): One of Florida's oldest magnetic hill illusions. Folklore claims that a Native American chief and a giant alligator clashed on the hill long ago. Today, drivers put their cars in neutral and swear an unseen supernatural force pushes their vehicles uphill.

Cassadaga Spiritualist Camp: Founded in 1894, this small town in Volusia County is renowned as the "Psychic Capital of the World". Its folklore is filled with tales of séances, Victorian-era spirits, and the infamous "Devil's Chair"—a brick bench where it is said the devil himself appears at midnight.

The Fountain of Youth: The most enduring historical legend, tied to Spanish explorer Juan Ponce de León’s 1513 expedition. While Ponce de León never actually sought out an age-reversing spring in his journals, the myth remains deeply ingrained in St. Augustine's identity and Florida’s broader lore.

The St. Johns River Monster ("Johnnie"): Florida's very own Loch Ness Monster. For generations, residents along the St. Johns River and in nearby Jacksonville have reported seeing a massive, horned serpentine beast or a "shrimp-colored" sea serpent.

The Ghost Village (Everglades): Deep in the Everglades lies "The Lost City," an abandoned three-acre site wrapped in tall tales of hidden Confederate gold, Prohibition moonshine, and use by Al Capone.
