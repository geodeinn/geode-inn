Lake Monsters (Champ): Perhaps Vermont's most famous folklore entity is Champ, the legendary serpent said to inhabit Lake Champlain. Sightings date back to Native American traditions and early explorer Samuel de Champlain himself. Another monster, "Memphre," is said to prowl the deep waters of Lake Memphremagog.

The Glastenbury Triangle: Located in the rugged wilderness surrounding Glastenbury Mountain, this area is tied to several unexplained disappearances in the 1940s and 50s. Local legends warn of a "man-eating rock" and various Bigfoot sightings.

The Pigman of Northfield: A staple of 1970s urban legend, the Pigman is said to be a human-pig hybrid who roams the woods near Norwich University and Northfield High School. He is the subject of local late-night dares and campfire stories.

Witch Windows: Distinct to 19th-century Vermont farmhouses, these windows are rotated roughly 45 degrees into the roof slope. Folk legend claims witches cannot fly their broomsticks through tilted glass, though the practical reason was to squeeze light into cramped gable spaces.

The Spanish Silver Mine of Wallingford: Local lore tells of a lost 18th-century Spanish crew who supposedly found an exposed vein of silver ore near White Rocks. To this day, treasure hunters explore the area, though the vast silver stash has never been found.
