Legendary Creatures & Spirits

The Rougarou: Rooted in French loup-garou tales, this shapeshifting, wolf-headed beast is said to hunt the swamps at night. The legend was famously used by parents to keep misbehaving children in line and enforce religious practices like Lent.

Feu Follet: Also called fifollet, these are mysterious, glowing orbs of light floating over the marshes. Local lore suggests they represent the spirits of the lost or serve as mischievous tricksters guarding hidden treasure.

The Grunch: Exclusive to New Orleans urban legend, these are small, deformed, vampiric creatures that allegedly inhabit the shadowy, overgrown edges of the city.

Folk Heroes & Characters

Bouki and Lapin: Found deeply in Creole and African-American folktales, Bouki is a dull-witted, gullible hyena/alligator figure, while Lapin (the rabbit) is the clever, wily trickster who always outsmarts him.

Traiteurs (Folk Healers): A significant part of Cajun culture, traiteurs are traditional folk healers who utilize prayer, laying on of hands, and herbal remedies to cure ailments.

Voodoo & Historical Legends

Marie Laveau: The revered, historically documented 19th-century Voodoo Queen of New Orleans. Her legend is surrounded by mysticism, with stories claiming she could heal the sick, cast powerful gris-gris charms, and even influence the weather.

Madame LaLaurie Mansion: A notorious New Orleans historical legend. Located in the French Quarter, the 1830s home is tied to tales of severe cruelty committed by Delphine LaLaurie. It is widely considered one of the most haunted sites in the country.
