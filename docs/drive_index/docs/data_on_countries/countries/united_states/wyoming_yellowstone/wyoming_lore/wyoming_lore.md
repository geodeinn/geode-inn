Native American Legends

Devils Tower: According to the oral traditions of the Arapaho, Cheyenne, and Sioux tribes, the spectacular rock formation was created to save seven young girls from a giant bear. As the bear chased them, the girls climbed a low rock and prayed to the Great Spirit. The rock rose high into the sky, and the bear clawed the sides, creating the vertical striations we see today.

The Nimerigar (Little People): Shoshone and Crow folklore tells of the Nimerigar, a race of hostile, pygmy-like people who lived in the Pedro Mountains. In 1932, a mummified body of a tiny human was discovered in the region, fueling speculation that these tales were based on a real, ancient species of humans.

Water Babies: According to Shoshone tradition, these childlike spiritual beings dwell in the rivers and lakes of the Wind River region. They are said to lure people to the water by mimicking the cries of infants.

Wild West & Pioneer Tall Tales

Jim Bridger’s Tall Tales: Famed mountain man and scout Jim Bridger is legendary for his tall tales. He famously told stories of a "petrified forest" where petrified birds sang petrified songs, and a river that ran so fast over rocks that it generated friction and boiled the water.

The Headless Bride: A popular ghost story tracing back to an old Cheyenne boarding house. The bride was murdered on her wedding night, and it is said her spirit wanders the historic buildings searching for her lost head.

The Ship of Death: A legendary ghost ship that supposedly haunts the North Platte River. Appearing out of a thick, ominous mist, it is crewed by frosted phantoms who are said to gather and reveal the identity of loved ones who will die that day.

Cryptids & Natural Mysteries

Lake Desmet Monster: Named after Father Pierre Jean De Smet, this large, serpentine creature is said to inhabit Lake De Smet in Sheridan County. Early Native Americans and settlers alike claimed to see it, and locals noted horses would become highly uncontrollable near the water.

The Vedauwoo Monster: Nestled in the Laramie Mountains, the Vedauwoo recreation area is named after the Arapaho word for "earthborn spirits". Local lore speaks of a shapeshifting monster that lures unsuspecting campers by mimicking the sound of an owl calling out in the night.
