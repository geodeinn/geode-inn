Alaska’s lore spans ancient Indigenous mythology and frontier mysteries. From shape-shifting tricksters and menacing sea-ice monsters to modern wilderness enigmas like vanishing ships and Bigfoot-like creatures, the 49th state’s stories are shaped by its vast, unforgiving landscape.

Mythological Beings & Monsters

The Raven: In many Native Alaskan traditions, the Raven is the ultimate creator and a clever trickster who brought light to the world and helped shape the first people.

Kushtaka (Land Otter Man): Found in Southeast Alaskan Native lore, these shape-shifters resemble half-man, half-otter sirens. They are said to mimic the cries of babies or women to lure weary travelers away from camp and steal their souls.

Qalupalik: A haunting figure in Inuit folklore, this sea monster steals disobedient children who wander too close to the water's edge.

Lake Iliamna Monster: Deep in Southwest Alaska, tales persist of "Iliamna Nessie"—a giant, silver-scaled, fast-moving creature reportedly seen by bush pilots and fishermen in Alaska's largest lake.

Frontier & Historical Lore

The Alaskan Triangle: An area stretching between Anchorage, Juneau, and Utqiaġvik notorious for unexplained disappearances, plane crashes, and compass anomalies. [1]

Soapy Smith: During the Klondike Gold Rush, Jefferson "Soapy" Smith ran Skagway as a legendary con artist and crime boss, maintaining the illusion of an upstanding citizen until a famous shootout ended his reign.
