The Logging Era and Tall Tales

Paul Bunyan & Babe the Blue Ox: The legendary giant lumberjack and his massive blue companion are heavily tied to Minnesota. While popularized by early 20th-century journalists, you can see massive 26-foot and 10-foot statues of the duo standing outside the Bemidji Tourist Information Center in northern Minnesota.

Cordwood Pete: The fictional, smaller brother of Paul Bunyan who primarily chopped cordwood around Fosston, Minnesota.

Indigenous Legends and Cryptids

The Wendigo: Originating in Ojibwe and Algonquin lore, this tall, gaunt, and terrifying spirit embodies greed, famine, and cannibalism. Deeply feared in the isolated winter wilderness, it remains one of the most famous cryptids in northern Minnesota.

Lake Pepin Serpent (“Pepie”): The Mississippi River has its own version of the Loch Ness monster. Sightings of a 30-foot serpentine creature in Lake Pepin date back to the late 1800s, fueling local tourism in nearby towns like Lake City.

Other Cryptids: Deep northern swamps and forests are still hotbeds for reported cryptids, including Bigfoot near Two Harbors and dogmen sightings throughout the Chippewa National Forest.
