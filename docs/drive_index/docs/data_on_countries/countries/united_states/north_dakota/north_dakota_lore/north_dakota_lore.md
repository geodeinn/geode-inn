The Miniwashitu

Originating in Dakota and Mandan oral tradition, the Miniwashitu is a fearsome, red-furred serpent that dwells in the Missouri River near Bismarck. Described as having a single glowing eye, a horn on its forehead, and sharp spines, the creature is tied to violent spring floods and breaking ice. According to legend, merely looking upon the beast in daylight brings certain death within a year.

The Tagus Stairway to Hell

Perhaps North Dakota’s most infamous urban legend centers on the ghost town of Tagus in Ward County. The site of a former Lutheran church that burned down in 2001, local rumors claimed the ruins were the epicenter of dark cult activities and cannibalism. A mythical "stairway" is said to lie beneath the ashes, leading to the underworld.

The Rougarou

Shared with French-Canadian and Ojibwe heritage, the Rougarou is a monstrous werewolf-like creature that haunts the Turtle Mountains. Brought to the region by French fur trappers, the beast was famously used by elders to warn children to behave and observe Lent.

Wrong Side Up

One of the most famous pioneer folktales tells the story of an 1880s settler breaking the pristine prairie sod for wheat. An Indigenous elder watched him plow, picked up a clod of dirt, and told the pioneer he had it "wrong side up" before walking away. The settler laughed, but the legend became a somber truth—turning the native grass under left the topsoil vulnerable to the wind, leading to massive soil erosion in the region.
