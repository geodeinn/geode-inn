Key Mythical Figures

Johnny Kaw: Created in 1955 by Kansas State University professor George Filinger, Johnny Kaw is the mythical founding father of Kansas agriculture. Standing as tall as the sunflowers, this giant pioneer reportedly dug the Kaw River with his bare hands, kneaded giant biscuits, and created the state's sweeping wheat fields.

The Hamburger Man: A grim urban legend centered near Hutchinson’s "Hamburger Hill". This terrifying disfigured farmer is said to grind up lost hikers and campers, serving them to unsuspecting locals.

Famous Haunted Locations

Stull Cemetery: Located just outside Lawrence, this rural graveyard is famously rumored by students and ghost hunters to be one of the "Seven Gateways to Hell". Legends claim the devil appears there twice a year, and that a local stone church once contained portals to the underworld.

The Blue Light Lady (Hays): Elizabeth Polly, a 1860s nurse during a cholera outbreak at Fort Hays, is said to walk the limestone hill overlooking the fort. Witnesses claim to see her ghost glowing with a blue light.

Takaluma (Saline River): According to local Native American lore, the spirit of Takaluma haunts the banks of the Saline River, eternally searching for his father’s skull which was stolen by settlers.

Weather & Prairie Superstitions

Pioneers and farmers generated their own rich body of folk beliefs to survive unpredictable storms and harsh droughts.

Early Kansans relied heavily on weather signs, such as interpreting the thickness of corn husks or the behavior of migrating birds, to anticipate devastating blizzards or tornadoes.

Countless superstitions regarding luck, romance, and crops were collected by folklore scholars, such as sleeping with an egg filled with salt to dream of your future spouse.
