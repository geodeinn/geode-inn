1. Cryptids & Monsters

The Hodag (Rhinelander): Wisconsin’s most famous folklore creature. It has the head of a frog, the face of an elephant, a dinosaur's back, and spears on its tail. It is still the beloved mascot of Rhinelander today.

The Beast of Bray Road (Elkhorn): Since the late 1980s, drivers in rural Walworth County have reported seeing a large, fur-covered, wolf-like humanoid walking on its hind legs.

The Wendigo: Rooted in Ojibwe and Ho-Chunk Native American traditions, this fearsome, gaunt ice giant embodies insatiable greed and cannibalism, said to stalk the deep Northwoods and Madeline Island.

The Wisconsin Manbat: Also known as the Lake Michigan Mothman, this cryptid has been reported in areas like Kenosha, taking the form of a large gargoyle or winged entity.

2. Haunted Locations & Ghost Lore

The Pfister Hotel

Known as one of the most famously haunted hotels in America, it is often cited by visiting Major League Baseball players who claim to experience unexplained noises, flickering lights, and apparitions in their rooms.

The Witch's Tower

Also known as the Starin Park water tower, this stone structure is a focal point of local legends, which claim the area is a center for the occult and the site of a buried "witches' circle."

Death Song Cave

A chilling Winnebago (Ho-Chunk) legend about a cave near the Wisconsin River. Warriors and hunters who entered the cave vanished, accompanied by a strange, mournful melody.

3. Lumberjack & Cultural Tall Tales

Before it became the Dairy State, Wisconsin was the "mother of all circuses" and a hub for lumbering.

Lumberjack "Fearsome Critters": Loggers in Wisconsin camps entertained themselves with tales of bizarre animals, such as the Hidebehind (a sneaky creature that hides behind tree trunks), the Hodag itself, and the Agropelter (which hurls branches at passersby).

Paul Bunyan: Many of the tales surrounding this legendary lumberjack were popularized in Wisconsin logging camps.
