Urban Legends & NYC Folklore

The Van der Voort Sisters: The ghosts of two sisters who died in 1880 are said to haunt Central Park’s Wollman Rink, gleefully ice-skating in red and purple dresses without their feet ever touching the ice.

The Mole People: An urban legend describing uncontacted communities, said to live in the abandoned subway tunnels and sprawling sewer systems beneath New York City.

Staten Island Boat Race: A popular myth claims the ownership of Staten Island was decided by a 24-hour boat race around the island, won by sea captain Christopher Billop.

Upstate Haunts & Cryptids

The Headless Horseman: Washington Irving’s legendary tale is rooted in regional Dutch ghost stories and rumors of a phantom Revolutionary War soldier.

Champ (Lake Champlain Monster): Often described as a long, serpent-like cryptid with horns, this friendly water monster is said to inhabit Lake Champlain in upstate New York.

Fort Niagara Hobgoblin & Henri the Headless: At Old Fort Niagara in Youngstown, the ghost of a French soldier named Henri is said to wander the basement wells searching for his head, alongside a mischievous hobgoblin.

Unexplained Phenomena

The White Lady of Durand Eastman Park: Found in Rochester, local tales claim the ghost of a grieving mother wanders, searching for her missing daughter.

The Marysburgh Vortex: Known as a watery graveyard for shipwrecks, this area of eastern Lake Ontario is fraught with legends of strange disappearances and supernatural turbulence.
