1. Famous Cryptids & Monsters

The Mothman: The state's most famous legend. First sighted in Point Pleasant in 1966, this winged, humanoid creature with glowing red eyes is famously tied to the tragic 1967 collapse of the Silver Bridge.

The Flatwoods Monster: A 10-foot-tall metal alien with glowing red eyes and a spade-shaped head, reportedly seen in Braxton County in 1952.

Sheepsquatch & Snarly Yow: Sheepsquatch is a woolly, white, fanged beast first recorded in local ghost tales, the Snarly Yow is a terrifying phantom dog said to roam the Blue Ridge mountains.

The Grafton Monster & The White Thing: Shadowy, phantom cryptids often reported in the deep, remote woods of the Tygart and Kanawha Valleys.

2. Historical Tall Tales & Heroes

John Henry: The ultimate steel-driving man. Legend dictates that he challenged a steam-powered rock drill to a contest while constructing the Chesapeake and Ohio Railway's Big Bend Tunnel, dying with his hammer in his hand.

Johnny Appleseed: John Chapman, who planted his famed apple nurseries across the Ohio Valley, spent significant time in the region, spawning decades of tall tales and frontier myths.

3. Spooky Legends & Hauntings

The Trans-Allegheny Lunatic Asylum: Located in Weston, this immense 19th-century psychiatric hospital is the subject of countless ghost stories, with reports of shadowy figures and cries echoing through its abandoned halls. [1, 2, 3, 4]

The Wizard Clip: A strange and enduring late-1790s haunting in Middleway (Jefferson County) where scissors and household objects mysteriously flew through the air and chopped fabric after an early settler refused to call a Catholic priest for a dying stranger.

4. Appalachian Superstitions & Omens

A bird flying into the home or a dog rolling on its back foretells a death in the family.
