Illinois folklore is a rich, spooky tapestry woven from Native American mythology, French exploration, and Midwestern urban legends. Spanning from the deep south to Chicago, it blends tales of ghostly hitchhikers, bizarre cryptids, and haunted historical sites.

👻 Chicago & Northern Ghosts

Resurrection Mary: The most famous ghost in Illinois lore. Mary is a young woman in a white dress and dancing shoes who reportedly flags down drivers along Archer Avenue in Justice, Illinois, asking for a ride before vanishing near the gates of Resurrection Cemetery.

Bachelor's Grove Cemetery: Tucked away in the Rubio Woods near Midlothian, this abandoned 19th-century graveyard is considered one of the most haunted places in the Midwest, known for phantom vehicles, hovering orbs, and a glowing "vanishing house."

Hull House Devil Baby: During the early 1900s, Jane Addams' famous settlement house in Chicago was the epicenter of a rumor about a born-to-human-parents Devil Baby—complete with pointed ears, cloven hooves, and a tail—who was supposedly left on the doorstep and later raised in the attic.

🦖 Cryptids & Monsters

The Piasa Bird: One of Illinois' oldest legends, stemming from a massive painting on the bluffs of the Mississippi River near Alton. Documented by French explorers in 1673, it was described by the Illini people as a giant, man-eating, dragon-like bird with the head of a man.

Big Muddy Monster: In the early 1970s, the rural woods and river bottoms near Murphysboro (Southern Illinois) were terrorized by sightings of a tall, foul-smelling, hairy cryptid, prompting massive local citizen search parties.

The Mad Gasser of Mattoon: In 1944, a series of bizarre attacks swept Mattoon, Illinois, wherein residents reported an invisible assailant spraying a strange, sweet-smelling gas into their bedroom windows, causing temporary paralysis and nausea.

🏚️ Historic & Southern Lore

Crenshaw House (The Old Slave House): Located in rural Equality, this notorious 1830s mansion features a third-floor "slave attic". Local folklore insists that owner John Hart Crenshaw used the house to kidnap free African Americans and sell them into slavery, and that the attic still holds the tormented spirits of those imprisoned there.

Cato the Witch (Carmi): A localized Southern Illinois legend centered around an early 19th-century woman in Carmi who locals suspected of practicing dark magic and herbal witchcraft.

🪦 Bizarre Curses & Mummies

Deaf Bill (Alton): Local lore in Alton recounts the story of "Deaf Bill," a mute, local fisherman who died in the early 1900s. Because his body went unclaimed and the funeral home needed an exhibit to pay for expenses, his mummy was placed in an upright closet in the Bower Funeral Home for eight decades, where neighborhood kids used to dare each other to poke him.
