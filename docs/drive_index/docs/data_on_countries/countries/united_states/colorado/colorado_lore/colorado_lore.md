Legendary Creatures

The Slide Rock Bolter: A massive, whale-like creature said to inhabit steep mountain slopes in Southwest Colorado. Legend holds it uses a hooked tail to anchor itself to mountain peaks before dropping down to swallow tourists whole.

Tommyknockers: Brought by Cornish miners during the Gold Rush, these are mischievous yet superstitious pixie-like entities. Miners believed they lived in the deep, dark underground tunnels, warning men of cave-ins by knocking on the rock walls, or playing tricks if treated disrespectfully. Today, the lore lives on at the Tommyknoocker Brewery in Idaho Springs.

Eerie & Haunted Histories

Located in Estes Park, this historic hotel is globally famous for inspiring Stephen King’s horror novel, The Shining. It is deeply tied to paranormal folklore, with guests and staff regularly recounting tales of ghostly piano music and apparitions from its early 1900s founders.

The Vampire Of Lafayette

Buried in the Lafayette Municipal Cemetery, Theodore Glava was an Eastern European coal miner who died during the 1918 influenza pandemic. Local hysteria led to a dark urban legend that he was a vampire, a rumor that was fueled by the bizarre iron cross structure erected over his grave to "keep him down."

Cheesman Park is one of Denver’s most beautiful green spaces, but it was established on land that was originally Prospect Hill Cemetery. In the late 1800s, the city tried to relocate the bodies, but a botched, macabre process resulted in thousands of remains being left behind beneath the park grounds. This dark history continues to fuel local ghost stories.

Tales from the Western Frontier

Silver Heels: A legendary dancehall girl from the 1860s mining camp of Buckskin Joe. According to folklore, she nursed miners through a devastating smallpox outbreak while wearing a mask to hide her pox-scarred face. When the epidemic ended, she disappeared without a trace, and the miners named a mountain peak, Mount Silverheels, in her honor.
