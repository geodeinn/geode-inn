The Michigan Dogman

First reported in Wexford County in 1887, the Dogman is described as a 7-foot-tall, bipedal canine with the torso of a man and a piercing human scream. According to local legend, this fearsome beast emerges from the woods of the northern Lower Peninsula every 10 years during years ending in a 7.

The Nain Rouge of Detroit

Known as the "Demon of the Strait," the Nain Rouge (Red Dwarf) is a small, glowing-eyed creature whose appearance supposedly forewarns Detroit of impending disasters. According to local legends, the creature was spotted before the 1763 Battle of Bloody Run, the 1805 fire, and the 1967 riots. Today, Detroiters celebrate the annual Marche du Nain Rouge to chase the creature away and usher in a prosperous spring.

Mishipeshu (The Great Lynx)

Deeply embedded in the folklore of Great Lakes Native American tribes, Mishipeshu is a supernatural water panther with scales, serpent-like qualities, and sharp horns. Said to guard the rich copper deposits in Lake Superior, this powerful spirit is believed to create whirlpools, violent storms, and treacherous waters to protect its domain from intruders.

Maritime Mysteries

With thousands of shipwrecks scattered across the Great Lakes, Michigan has no shortage of nautical lore. The 1679 disappearance of Le Griffon—the first European vessel to sail the upper Great Lakes—is famous in the region, with some attributing its loss to a Native American prophet's curse.
