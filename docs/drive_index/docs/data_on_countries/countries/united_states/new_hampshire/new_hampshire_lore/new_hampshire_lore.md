The Curse of Mount Chocorua

According to legend, Chief Chocorua of the Pennacook tribe left his son behind with a white settler family. When the boy accidentally drank a poisonous mixture intended for wolves, the settler killed Chocorua's son in retaliation. Before plunging to his death from the summit of Mount Chocorua, the chief cursed the white settlers and their land—a curse said to have caused a mysterious disease that wiped out the area's cattle.

Oceanborn Mary

Perhaps the state's most famous historical ghost story involves Mary Wallace, born aboard a ship captured by pirates in 1720. According to the legend, the pirate captain spared the vessel on the condition the baby be named Mary after his mother. She grew up to live a long life in Henniker, New Hampshire, and after her death, a lingering myth emerged that her spirit—and the ghost of the pirate captain—haunted her old home.

The Danville Devil Monkey

In the early 20th century, and famously in the 1950s and 1980s, southern New Hampshire became a hotspot for cryptid reports. Witnesses described a creature that moved like a monkey, was the size of a large dog, and possessed a long tail. The legend of the Danville Devil Monkey persists alongside other regional cryptid tales, such as the mysterious "Wood Devils" of the White Mountains and the Monster of Dublin
