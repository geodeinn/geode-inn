Indigenous Mythology & Landscapes

Mt. Rainier and Mt. Adams: Known as Tahoma (or Tacoma), Mt. Rainier was often viewed as a sacred, towering, and volatile home to powerful spirits, with its eruptions and avalanches explained as spirit battles.

Coyote Stories: Coyote is the central figure of Pacific Northwest trickster lore. Stories from the Klickitat, Nez Perce, and Chinook people detail how Coyote stole fire, shaped the course of the Columbia River, and defeated the monster beaver, Wishpoosh, at Lake Cle Elum.

Cryptids & Monsters

Sasquatch (Bigfoot): Washington records more Bigfoot sightings than any other state. The lore spans from Indigenous oral traditions to modern-day sightings near Mount Rainier, Snoqualmie Pass, and Skamania County.

The Lake Chelan Dragon (N’hah’hahat’q): Indigenous cultures share legends of a long, scaly, alligator-like monster hiding in the deep underwater cave systems of Lake Chelan. Settler stories later claimed the beast hatched from a Scottish pirate's egg in 1812.

Batsquatch: A lesser-known winged cryptid with leathery wings and glowing red eyes, said to inhabit remote volcanic mountains like Mount St. Helens.

Caddy (Cadborosaurus): A sea monster spotted off the Washington and British Columbia coast, described as having a long neck and the head of a horse.

Modern Legends & Tall Tales

The Pacific Northwest Tree Octopus: A famous internet-era tall tale and urban legend stating this cephalopod lives in the rainforests of the Olympic Peninsula, using its tentacles to camouflage in the trees.

Ape Cave Lava Tubes: Located near Mount St. Helens, these caves get their name from a 1920s local lore that an ape-like monster inhabited them
