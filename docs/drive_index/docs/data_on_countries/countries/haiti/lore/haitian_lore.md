Damballah and Ayida-Wedo: The divine serpent couple associated with creation, wisdom, and the rainbow, which is viewed in Haitian lore as a colossal serpent quenching its thirst in the sea. [1]

Agwé and La Sirène: The aquatic rulers. Agwé is the powerful sea spirit who protects sailors and fishermen, while La Sirène is a mermaid who lures humans into the deep to teach them dark magic or drown them.

Papa Legba: The master of the crossroads and communicator between the living and the spirit world.

Lougawou: A terrifying shapeshifter and werewolf who transforms into animals like cats, snakes, or birds to roam the night, serving both as a myth and a mechanism for social control.

Zonbi (Zombie): Deeply rooted in the brutal history of slavery, this concept originally refers to a soulless body forced into labor by a sorcerer (a Bokor) rather than the flesh-eating monster of modern pop culture.

Mèt Minwi: A towering, mythical giant who roams the streets at night, punishing those who are out past curfew.

Bouki and Ti Malice: The most famous duo in Haitian fables. Bouki is the gullible, hard-working, and often clumsy donkey-like figure, while Ti Malice is the cunning underdog who consistently outwits him.

Kont: Stories and fables used to teach life lessons about betrayal, innocence, and survival.

Pwovèb: Proverbs that act as the moral compass of the culture.

Devinèt: Riddles used for evening entertainment.
