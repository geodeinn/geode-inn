Ra: The sun god and first pharaoh, who sailed across the sky by day and battled the chaos serpent Apophis in the underworld at night.

Osiris, Isis, and Horus: A foundational myth cycle. Osiris, god of the dead, was murdered by his brother Set, but resurrected by his wife Isis. Their son, Horus, fought Set to claim the throne of the living.

Anubis: The jackal-headed god of embalming who guided souls to the afterlife.

Monsters and hybrid animals were central to explaining cosmic forces and daily life.

Sphinx: The benevolent guardian with a lion’s body and a human head.

Ammit: The dreaded "devourer of the dead" with the combined body of a lion, hippopotamus, and crocodile.

Bennu: A gray heron that served as the inspiration for the mythological phoenix.

Alongside the gods, Egyptian folklore encompasses a rich array of supernatural entities and localized legends that evolved through the centuries.

El Naddaha: A legendary, alluring female spirit (siren) who calls out to men by the banks of the Nile River, luring them into the water.

Jinn and Afarit: Pre-Islamic and Islamic supernatural beings that inhabit the unseen world, capable of both malicious and benevolent deeds.

The Zār: A traditional ritual and healing ceremony practiced in Egypt to appease spirits or demons (often of Ethiopian or Sudanese origin).
