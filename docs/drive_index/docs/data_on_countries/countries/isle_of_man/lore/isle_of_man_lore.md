Manannán mac Lir: The island's legendary namesake and a Celtic sea god. He is said to protect the Isle of Man by shrouding it in his magical cloak—bringing down the mist to hide the land from approaching enemies. [1, 2, 3, 4]

Finn mac Cumhaill (Finn McCool): Irish mythology claims the island was formed when the giant Fionn scooped up a chunk of land to throw at a fleeing Scottish rival. He overshot, and the hunk of earth landed in the Irish Sea, creating the Isle of Man.

The Moddey Dhoo (Black Dog): A ghostly black hound that is said to haunt the corridors of Peel Castle. According to local legend, a guard in a drunken stupor once defied the creature, resulting in his being stricken with terror and dying shortly after without being able to speak again.

The Phynodderee: A hairy, giant goblin-like creature. Though incredibly strong and capable of doing a farmer's field work overnight, it can easily take offense and leave if it is gifted clothes, resulting in a lifelong grudge.

The Buggane: A malevolent, shape-shifting goblin. The most famous tale involves a Buggane tearing the roof off St. Trinian's Church in a fit of rage.

Hop Tu Naa: The Manx version of Halloween, celebrated on October 31st. Children traditionally carve turnips instead of pumpkins and sing traditional Hop Tu Naa songs, which are based around the legend of Jinny the Witch.

Gef the Talking Mongoose: A famous 20th-century paranormal legend from Dalby. The Irving family claimed a small, intelligent, talking mongoose lived behind the wooden partition of their farmhouse, speaking, singing, and even hunting rabbits for them.
