El Silbón (The Whistler): A tall, spectral figure from the plains (Llanos) who carries a sack of bones and whistles a chilling musical note that foreshadows death.

La Sayona: A vengeful female phantom in a long white dress who appears to unfaithful men to terrify or punish them.

Florentino el que Cantó con el Diablo: A legendary llanero singer who famously defeated the Devil in an improvised folk singing duel (contrapunteo).

María Lionza: A major nature goddess and queen of a prominent spiritual cult, often depicted riding a tapir and revered at Sorte Mountain.

Amalivaca: The creator god of the Warao and Tamanaco peoples who shaped the Orinoco river landscape.

Makunaima: A trickster and culture hero central to the mythology of the Pemón indigenous people.

Joropo: The national upbeat folk dance and musical style originating from the plains.

Dancing Devils of Yare (Diablos Danzantes de Yare): A traditional Corpus Christi festival where dancers wear vibrant red costumes and masks to symbolize the triumph of good over evil.
