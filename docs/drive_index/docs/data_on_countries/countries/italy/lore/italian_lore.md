The Monaciello (Naples): A famous, short, stocky sprite dressed as a monk. He is known to be mischievous but can secretly leave money or bring good luck to households.

La Befana (National): A beloved, kind-hearted witch who rides a broomstick and delivers gifts to children on Epiphany Eve (Jan 5th). She fills stockings with candy for good children and coal for the naughty.

The Anguana (Alpine regions): Elusive freshwater nymphs that inhabit mountain springs and waterfalls, often bringing blessings or mischief to travelers.

The Mazapégul (Romagna): A nocturnal elf or sprite known for disrupting the sleep of young women and tangling horses' manes after sunset.

Romulus and Remus: The ultimate founding myth of Rome. Twin brothers were abandoned, raised by a she-wolf, and eventually went on to establish the great city (with Romulus becoming its first king).

Aradia (Witchcraft): A central figure in modern Italian-American witchcraft lore, famously positioned as the goddess and messenger of the "old religion," sent to teach witchcraft to the poor and oppressed.

Il Malocchio (The Evil Eye): A deeply rooted belief that an envious glance can cause bad luck, illness, or financial ruin.

The Cornicello: The primary charm used to ward off the evil eye. Often worn as a necklace, it is a twisted horn made of red coral, gold, or silver.

Cimaruta: A traditional silver amulet representing a sprig of rue (an herb), often adorned with magical symbols like a key, a moon, or a fish, used to protect against negativity.
