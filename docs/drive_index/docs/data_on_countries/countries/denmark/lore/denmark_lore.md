Nisse: A mischievous but loyal farm spirit. No taller than a child and wearing a traditional red cap, he protects the household if treated with respect but wreaks havoc if offended. [1]

Trolls: Enormous, ancient beings made of stone and earth who live deep in the forests or beneath hills. They are famously afraid of daylight and church bells, which turn them into stone.

Elverfolk (Elves): Seductive, ethereal dancers who inhabit mounds and misty bogs at night. Humans are warned not to join their magical dances, which can cause them to lose their minds or be trapped in the earth forever.

Valravn: A monstrous shape-shifting raven born on blood-stained battlefields. It possesses molten gold eyes and can mimic human voices or transform into a human. It feeds on greed and human sorrow, preying on lost travelers.

Kirke-Grim (Church Grim): A guardian spirit (often a black dog or pig) attached to local churches. It protects the building and graveyard from dark spirits and watches over the parish.

King Skjold: The mythical founder of the Danish royal lineage (the Scylding dynasty). According to legend, he arrived as an abandoned baby on a boat filled with treasures and grew up to become a mighty king.

Gefjon: A powerful goddess who tricked the Swedish king into giving her land. She plowed the Swedish earth with her four ox-sons to carve out the large island of Zealand, leaving behind Lake Mälaren.

Holger Danske (Ogier the Dane): Denmark's most celebrated legendary hero. According to the myths, he never truly died but rests deep in the casemates of Kronborg Castle. He sleeps, but will awaken and come to Denmark's defense in its darkest hour of need.

Denmark has a rich history of oral folklore, meticulously collected in the 19th century by scholars like Svend Grundtvig and E.T. Kristensen, which inspired world-famous fairy tales written by Hans Christian Andersen, such as "The Little Mermaid" and "The Steadfast Tin Soldier".
