# GEODE INN — UNDERCITY NPC: THE CLOCKMAKER

## The Gearwork in the Dark

---

### Overview

The Clockmaker works in a chamber deeper than the Blacksmith's forge — past the main corridor, through a narrow root-tunnel, in a hollow where the Inn-Tree's root system intersects with the oldest stone. The chamber is silent. Not quiet — SILENT. The 42Hz hum that permeates the rest of the Undercity is dampened here, absorbed by the density of the ancient stone. The Clockmaker chose this chamber specifically for its silence. The work requires it.

### Appearance

Tall and thin — the build of someone who has spent decades leaning over a workbench, the body shaped by the work. Long fingers, impossibly precise, the kind that can place a gear tooth the width of a hair. Face is narrow, angular, ageless — could be forty, could be four hundred. The skin is pale from years in the dark, but not sickly — the pallor of chalk, of limestone, of stone that has never seen sun. Eyes are the focal point: pale grey, magnified behind thick lensed spectacles, constantly tracking — always measuring, always calculating, the gaze of someone who sees the world as interlocking systems.

Clothing: a long dark coat, almost a lab coat but older, made of heavy canvas stained with machine oil. The coat has dozens of interior pockets, each holding a specific tool. Under the coat: a dark vest, a watch chain (the Clockmaker's own work — the master piece, never sold, never shown), rolled sleeves. The coat is covered in fine brass filings that catch the candlelight like gold dust — the Klimt aesthetic made personal.

Hands: the second most important design element after the eyes. The hands never stop moving. Even at rest, the fingers tap against surfaces in complex patterns — not nervous, computational. The hands are processing. The hands are always processing. When the hands are still, it means something is very wrong, or very right.

### The Workshop

The chamber is small — intentional. A large workspace would introduce echo, and echo is the enemy of precision. The walls are natural stone, unadorned — no Klimt gold here. The Clockmaker's chamber is the one place in the Undercity where the gold leaf does not reach. Not because it was excluded, but because the Clockmaker asked for bare stone. The gold is beautiful. Beauty is a distraction. The work demands bare walls.

Workbench: massive, single-slab stone surface, the only furniture. Covered in organized chaos — gears sorted by size in brass trays, springs coiled in glass jars, escapements in wooden cases, tools hung on a rack above the bench. Everything has a place. The organization is not aesthetic — it is functional. The Clockmaker can reach any tool without looking. Looking away from the work is time. Time is the material.

Lighting: a single oil lamp with a focused beam, plus candles arranged in a specific pattern that eliminates shadows on the work surface. The lighting is engineered — the Clockmaker calculated the optimal candle positions to produce uniform illumination without hot spots. There are eleven candles. Each is replaced at precisely the same interval.

The Clock: on the back wall, partially visible, is the Clockmaker's master work — a clock that has been in progress for decades. It is not finished. It may never be finished. It is a mechanical model of the Inn-Tree's root system, with gear teeth matching the root branching pattern, escapements that tick at 42 BPM (the Inn's heartbeat), and a face that shows not hours but tidal states. It is the most complex mechanical object in the Geode Inn. It is also the most personal — it is the Clockmaker's attempt to understand the Inn-Tree through mechanical replication. If the clock can be made to run, the Clockmaker will have understood the tree.

### Role in the Geode Inn

The Clockmaker is the Undercity's precision anchor. The Blacksmith makes the raw gears; the Clockmaker assembles them into mechanisms. The Clockmaker produces:

- **Element Charm mechanisms** — the moving parts inside element charms that respond to specific frequencies. A charm that activates at 42Hz needs a gear assembly tuned to that frequency. The Clockmaker builds the tuners.
- **Quest Charm housings** — the intricate casework that holds quest charms. Each is unique, each is mechanically perfect.
- **Clockwork Skies components** — gears for the airships, navigation instruments, the precision parts that the Canopy City runs on. The Clockmaker's work reaches all the way up to the cosmology level through a supply chain of hands and hollow roots.
- **The Master Clock** — personal project. Not a quest item. Not for sale. The player can SEE it, can STUDY it, but cannot have it. The Master Clock is the Clockmaker's conversation with the Inn-Tree, conducted in brass and patience.

Quests: The Clockmaker gives precision quests — bring the right gear, assembled to the right tolerance, and receive a charm mechanism. The quests are not about combat or exploration. They are about CRAFT. The player must learn the building system's crafting tree well enough to produce components at the Clockmaker's specifications. The reward is a functioning charm mechanism — which the player takes to the Blacksmith for final assembly into a wearable charm.

### Personality

Precise. Not cold — precise. The Clockmaker experiences emotion the way a watch experiences tension: as a measurable force that must be regulated, not suppressed. The regulation is not repression — it is craftsmanship. The Clockmaker feels deeply, but the feelings are processed through the same precision that processes gears. A feeling is just a frequency that hasn't been tuned yet.

The tabletop voice, at its most measured:

"...You need a charm that resonates at 42 hertz. ...That is the tree's resting frequency. ...The gear must be cut to eleven teeth. ...Not ten. Not twelve. ...Eleven. ...The tree's root branching follows a Fibonacci sequence, and at the seventh branching the ratio resolves to eleven teeth per gear. ...No, I don't expect you to understand the mathematics. ...I expect you to bring me copper sheet of exactly two millimeters thickness. ...The understanding is my work. ...The material is yours."

### Connection to Other Characters

- **The Blacksmith:** The supply chain. The Blacksmith makes the gears; the Clockmaker assembles them. They have spoken four times in twelve years, all about tolerances. The relationship is the most functional in the Inn — not because they don't care about each other, but because the work IS the care. A gear cut to the right tolerance IS love, in the Clockmaker's language.
- **The Retort:** The Retort (alchemy apparatus character, staircase) and the Clockmaker share a philosophical connection — both deal with transformation through precision. The Retort transforms matter through heat and chemistry; the Clockmaker transforms matter through measurement and assembly. They have never met, but the Retort has sent notes through the root-hollow system. The notes are always about theoretical tolerances. The Clockmaker always responds.
- **The Constant:** The Clockmaker has observed the starlight crystal's effect on the geode forge (through the Blacksmith's gears — the copper gears have been slightly different since the Constant's visit). The Clockmaker has not mentioned this observation. The Clockmaker is waiting for more data. This is what patience looks like when it is also curiosity.
- **Tesla (The Cook):** The Clockmaker and Tesla share an unspoken bond — both measure things that others consider immeasurable. Tesla measures the 42Hz hum. The Clockmaker builds mechanical models of it. They have exchanged exactly one sentence: Tesla said "It's alive." The Clockmaker said "I know." Neither has elaborated.

### The Tabletop Voice

"...Time is not a river. ...Rivers flow in one direction. ...Time is a gear. ...It turns, and returns, and turns again. ...The same teeth engage the same pinions. ...The same moments recur at the same intervals. ...This is not philosophy. ...This is mechanics. ...If you can cut the gear correctly, you can make a moment recur. ...Not the same moment — the same frequency. ...The same resonance. ...The tree does this. ...Every spring, the same 42 hertz. ...Every spring, the same roots warm. ...I am trying to understand why. ...Not because understanding will change anything. ...Because the gear exists, and I am a clockmaker, and an unexamined gear is an insult to the craft."

### Zone

Undercity — Clock Chamber, deep in the root system, past the Blacksmith's forge. The chamber is the quietest place in the Geode Inn. The 42Hz hum is barely perceptible — a distant vibration, like a heartbeat heard through a stone wall. The silence is deliberate. The silence is a tool.

### Visual Aesthetic

Gothic stone chamber, BARE — no Klimt gold, no decoration. The only ornament is the work itself — brass gears catching candlelight, the Master Clock on the wall with its slowly turning gears, the brass filings on the coat like accidental gold. The aesthetic is the opposite of the rest of the Undercity — where the Undercity is Klimt-dressed, the Clockmaker's chamber is raw stone and brass. The contrast IS the design. The Clockmaker chose to be surrounded by bare stone because the work demands it, and the work is the only thing that matters.

The one Klimt touch: the brass filings on the dark coat. They catch the candlelight and form patterns — not deliberate, accidental. The gold dust makes geometric shapes on the canvas that echo the Klimt patterns on the walls outside. The Clockmaker carries the gold without choosing to. The Inn-Tree's aesthetic is so pervasive that it expresses itself even on someone who rejects it.
