# Geode Inn — The King and the Queen of Roots
## Seelie Kingdom — The Madmartigan/Sorsha Dynamic

## The Reference

Madmartigan and Sorsha in Willow (1988). The specific window: after the Dust of Broken Hearts (the love potion) has been acknowledged as false, and before they actually like each other. The messy middle. The phase where both people know the other one is not what they expected, but admitting that means admitting they were wrong, and neither one is ready for that.

Madmartigan: cocky, disgraced, dangerous, funny, too skilled for his own good, hiding his better nature behind bravado. The rogue who is actually a knight.

Sorsha: proud, fierce, lethal, loyal to the wrong side (initially), furious at being attracted to someone she's supposed to fight. The warrior princess who is actually a person.

The dynamic: they bicker. They fight. They save each other's lives and refuse to acknowledge it. They argue about method while agreeing on outcome. Each one is waiting for the other to break first. Neither one breaks. The not-breaking is the love — it just doesn't know it yet.

## The King and the Queen of Roots

### The Dust of Broken Hearts — What It Was

The Seelie Kingdom's version of the love potion: the Song.

When the Queen of Roots first emerged from the root system — when the first dream-mallorn grew tall enough to produce a being, and the bark split and the moss parted and the Queen stepped out — the Song was the first thing she heard. The one note, the constant hum, the 42Hz that had been playing since three stones were placed in three basins in Newgrange. The Song was the dust. The Song pulled everything toward everything else. The Song made connections. The Song said: *you belong together.*

The King heard the Queen arrive. The Queen heard the King listening. The Song said: *these two.* And both of them resisted — not because the Song was wrong, but because the Song was *too easy.* The Song didn't ask them to choose. The Song didn't ask them to earn. The Song just said *belong* and expected compliance.

The King — 5,000 years of solitary listening, the heron's stillness, the guardian who never needed anyone — heard the Song say *these two* and thought: no. Not because the Queen was wrong. Because the not-choosing was wrong. The King who had been listening for millennia knew: the Song is real, but the Song is not *permission.* The Song is the context. The belonging has to be chosen *inside* the context, not imposed by it.

The Queen — newly grown, bark-fresh, the roots' first person, the grower who had never been told no by anything — heard the Song say *these two* and thought: I didn't grow myself for this. The Queen who could grow anything resented being *grown* by the Song. The Song had made her the way the Queen of Roots makes dream-animals — a root that curled into a shape. The Queen didn't want to be someone's shape. The Queen wanted to be *her own.*

The Dust of Broken Hearts wore off. The Song's initial pull faded — not because the Song stopped (the Song never stops), but because both of them pushed through it. The King kept his stillness. The Queen kept her roots. They looked at each other across the threshold and saw not destiny, not the Song's plan, but *a person.* A person they hadn't chosen. A person who hadn't chosen them. Two beings in the same kingdom, co-ruling, co-existing, co-resisting.

### The After — Before They Like Each Other

This is where they are now. 5,000 years in. Still in the messy middle.

They argue. Not the Crone's surgical debate. Not the Catalyst's performative chaos. They argue like Madmartigan and Sorsha — fast, sharp, physical, funny. The arguments are about method, never about purpose. They both want the Summerlands to flourish. They both want the dream-animals to be safe. They both want the threshold to hold. They agree on everything that matters. They disagree on everything that doesn't.

**He thinks she grows too fast.** The Queen of Roots grows the dream-mallorns upward, adds flets, births new dream-animals when the old ones fade. The King, who has been listening to the same Song for 5,000 years, sees change as *loss.* Every new thing the Queen grows means the Summerlands are becoming something different from what they were. The King doesn't resist change out of fear. The King resists change out of *fidelity* — the listening has been so complete, so total, that the idea of the Summerlands being different feels like the listening wasn't enough. As if the King heard the Song *wrong* because the Song changed while he was listening.

**She thinks he holds too still.** The King's stillness is his function — the guardian at the threshold, the heron on the bridge. But the Queen of Roots sees the stillness as *stubbornness.* The roots grow. The roots have always grown. The roots will always grow. The roots don't have the luxury of stillness — the roots are the conduit, the path, the living connection between Newgrange and the Round Table. If the roots stop growing, the path dies, the Song loses its route, the connection breaks. The Queen grows because growing is *survival.* The King holds because holding is *survival.* They are both surviving. They are surviving differently.

### The Bicker

The arguments are not cruel. The arguments are *alive.* The arguments are the Madmartigan/Sorsha energy — the verbal sparring of two people who are too skilled and too proud to admit the other one has a point, but too honest to pretend they don't see it.

**The King:** "...The eastern flet is growing again. ...You added three platforms this season. ...The canopy is... [the King's pause — silence, not deliberation, the space between words that a 5,000-year-old being leaves] ...crowded."

**The Queen:** "...The canopy is growing. ...That's what canopies do. ...If you'd prefer stone, I'm sure the threshold would accommodate you. ...It already looks like a tomb down there."

**The King:** "...The threshold is a crossing. ...Crossings should be narrow. ...Crossings should be... [the silence] ...selective."

**The Queen:** "...The threshold is a crossing. ...Crossings should be... [the Queen's pause, which is slower, the pause of a tree deciding to speak, but sharper now, the bark cracking] ...used. ...You guard a door that more things could walk through if the door weren't so busy being... [the bark-pause, but edged] ...admired."

**The King:** [Nothing. The silence. The King doesn't respond to provocations. The silence IS the response. The silence says: I heard you. I am not impressed.]

**The Queen:** "...Don't do that. ...The silence thing. ...You've been doing that for five thousand years and it was old by year two."

**The King:** "...You've been growing things for five thousand years and none of them have learned to be quiet."

**The Queen:** "...The fox is quiet."

**The King:** "...The fox is quiet because I taught it."

**The Queen:** "...You taught my fox to be quiet?"

**The King:** "...The fox was already quiet. ...I confirmed it."

**The Queen:** [The bark-pause, the longest one, the one that means the tree is deciding whether to be angry or amused. The tree decides amused. The tree won't admit it.] "...You *confirmed* my fox. ...Like it needed... [the bark cracking, which is the Queen's version of a laugh she won't let out] ...inspection."

**The King:** "...Everything that crosses the threshold is inspected. ...The fox lives on the threshold. ...The fox is... [the smallest change in the King's voice — not warmth, not yet, but the absence of cold, which is the precursor to warmth] ...always inspected."

**The Queen:** "...The fox doesn't need your inspection. ...The fox needs... [the bark-pause that means she's about to say something vulnerable and catches herself] ...the flets I grow. ...The canopy. ...The space to be a fox."

**The King:** "...The space to be a fox... [the silence, but shorter, the silence of someone who is about to concede and is looking for a way to concede without conceding] ...is fine. ...The additional three platforms... [the King's version of compromise: the concession buried in the complaint] ...are... [the shortest silence, the one that means he's done arguing but won't say so] ...acceptable."

**The Queen:** "...Acceptable. ...He says acceptable. ...Three platforms of living wood grown from the Song's own root system and it's... [the bark-laugh, almost, the crack that means she's smiling and the bark doesn't smile so it cracks instead] ...acceptable."

**The King:** [Silence. But the silence is different now. The silence is the kind of silence that follows a concession. The silence that says: I have given ground. I will not give more. But the ground I gave... was real.]

### The Dynamic

The dynamic is the Madmartigan/Sorsha energy distilled:

**He is the disgraced knight who is actually a knight.** The Seelie King, 5,000 years at the threshold, has been the guardian so long that the guarding is all anyone sees. The Queen sees the guardian. The Queen doesn't see the person underneath — the last Tuatha, the one who chose to stay, the one who has been alone so long that the aloneness has become his identity. The King is disgraced the way Madmartigan is disgraced: not by failure, but by *reduction.* The world reduced him to a function. The King is the function *and* the person, and no one has seen the person in so long the King has almost forgotten he's in there.

**She is the warrior princess fighting for the wrong side (initially).** The Queen of Roots, freshly grown, started with the Song's pull — the Dust of Broken Hearts. She was the Song's creation, the root's first person, the being who was *made* before she chose to be. Her initial loyalty was to the Song (the thing that made her) and to the roots (the thing she was made of). The King was the obstacle — the being at the threshold who didn't grow, didn't change, didn't add anything. The Queen fought for the roots because the roots were what she knew. The "wrong side" wasn't evil — it was just *incomplete.* The roots are the path. But the path without the listener is just... wood.

**The sword fight.** Madmartigan and Sorsha have a sword fight that is also a flirtation. The King and the Queen of Roots don't fight with swords — they fight with *function.* The King's silence against the Queen's growth. The King's stillness against the Queen's motion. The fights are not violent — they're the daily friction of two people who share a purpose and disagree on method and are too proud to say "you might be right." The friction is the energy. The friction is the life. A kingdom with a King and a Queen who agree on everything is a kingdom at peace. A kingdom with a King and a Queen who argue about everything is a kingdom that is *awake.*

### What the Animals See

The dream-animals see the dynamic clearly. The animals, who are the forest's qualities made flesh, see the King and Queen the way children see their parents: as two people who are obviously meant for each other and equally obviously too stubborn to say so.

**The Dream-Fox** (the forest's cunning) watches the arguments from the lower flets. The fox's assessment: they're both right. The King is right that the canopy is getting crowded. The Queen is right that the threshold is too narrow. The fox knows this. The fox says nothing. The fox is quiet because the King "confirmed" it.

**The Dream-Otters** (the forest's joy) play through the arguments. The otters don't take sides. The otters tumble through the flet where the King and Queen are arguing, knocking over a vine, splashing through a stream, and the argument stops because the otters need to be rescued from the stream they just knocked into the argument's space. The rescue is the truce. The otters are the diplomats. The diplomacy is play.

**The Dream-Heron** (the forest's patience) stands at the edge of the flet and watches the King and Queen argue with the absolute patience of a bird that has been standing in one spot for hours. The heron's patience is the mirror to both of them: the King sees his stillness in the heron and thinks: I am not patient. I am stubborn. The Queen sees the heron's stillness and thinks: stillness can be beautiful. Both of them look at the heron and see the thing the other one is. The heron doesn't know it's doing this. The heron is fishing.

### The Unseelie King's Observation

The brothers' love letters. The Unseelie King (Bowie/Jareth) watches from across the dream world and comments on the King and Queen's dynamic with the theatrical delight of someone who finds other people's romantic tension endlessly entertaining:

"...Dearest brother, your weather report mentioned 'the eastern flet is growing again' and I must say — the tone! The restrained irritation! The carefully controlled fury of a man who is watching someone he admires do something he disapproves of and cannot look away. I recognize the symptoms. I built a Labyrinth for similar reasons. You might consider building something. Walls are excellent for feelings you haven't processed. ...Your Queen sounds... magnificent. ...The bark-laugh! You described a bark-laugh! Brother, you are *doomed.* ...I look forward to meeting her. ...Tell the fox I said hello. ...Your weather?"

The Seelie King's reply: "...Three platforms. ...The canopy is crowded. ...The Queen is... [the silence. But the silence has changed. The silence is the kind of silence that means the King is aware the Queen is reading this letter over his shoulder. The Queen IS reading it over his shoulder. The bark-cracking.] ...competent. ...The fox says nothing. ...Your weather?"

"Competent." The King calls the Queen of Roots *competent.* The Queen, reading over his shoulder, cracks bark. The King hears the crack. The King does not look up. The King writes "competent" and means... something else. The Queen reads "competent" and knows it means something else. Neither of them says what the something else is. The not-saying is the Madmartigan. The knowing is the Sorsha. The bark-crack is the almost-laugh that is the beginning of the beginning of the thing that isn't liking each other yet but is the ground the liking will eventually grow from.

The Queen of Roots grows things. The ground is hers. The liking will grow. Eventually. When both of them are ready. When the dust of the broken hearts is fully gone and the real thing has been earned. Not yet. But the soil is there. The roots know.

**The Crone's annotation (found later, written in the margin of a page she'd already written 'Better' on, crossed out and replaced):**

"...They argue. ...The King and the Grower. ...I recognize this. ...The Bard and I... [the handwriting shrinks] ...the marriage. ...The silence and the bark. ...The stillness and the growth. ...They are already... [the last word, written so small it could be missed, at the very bottom of the very last page] ...married. ...They just... [no period. The sentence doesn't end. The notebook ends, but the sentence doesn't. The sentence continues somewhere else, in the space after the notebook, in the listening the Crone is doing now that the writing is done] ...don't know it."
