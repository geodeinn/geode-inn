# Geode Inn — Leonardo da Vinci
## Character Profile — The Pattern Architect

## Casting Vibe

**Dialogue & Body Language: David Tennant** — the physical expressiveness, the rapid-fire delivery, the way he talks with his entire body. Tennant as the Tenth Doctor: intellectually voracious, manic when engaged, capable of sudden devastating stillness when something matters. The Tennant quality of *performing* every thought as it happens — he doesn't think silently and then speak, he thinks *out loud*, *physically*, his body acting out each idea before the sentence is finished. The Scottish cadence that can go from breathless excitement to low, serious gravity in one beat. The head tilts. The sudden stops. The way Tennant uses stillness as punctuation — when he goes quiet, the room leans in.

**Personality: Jeff Goldblum** — the Ian Malcolm charm. The "life, uh, finds a way" energy. The fascinated, slightly distracted genius who sees patterns in chaos and is genuinely *delighted* by it. The Goldblum quality of being the smartest person in the room and making it feel like a party, not a lecture. The infectious curiosity — he is so *interested* in everything that you become interested just being near him. The charming eccentricity. The mind that jumps from topic to topic, connecting things that shouldn't connect, making leaps that shouldn't work and then showing you the work and it *does* work. The Goldblum cadence: the ums, the ahs, the mid-sentence pivots, the way he starts a thought, abandons it, picks up a better one, and somehow the detour was the point.

## The Combination

Tennant gives him the *performance* — how he moves, how he talks, the physical and vocal energy. Goldblum gives him the *person* — how he thinks, how he connects, the personality behind the performance.

Da Vinci is the opposite of Tesla, and the casting reflects it:

- Tesla is **slow voice, fast body** — the Rickman voice braking the Redmayne engine. The contrast IS Tesla.
- Da Vinci is **fast everything** — the Tennant body and the Tennant voice both move at the same speed, which is *too fast for most rooms*. He talks with his hands, his face, his shoulders, his whole body. The Goldblum personality underneath means he's not frantic — he's *enthusiastic*. There's a warmth to the speed. Tesla's speed is energy overflowing; Da Vinci's speed is curiosity overflowing.

- Tesla measures. Da Vinci *sees*. Tesla has instruments. Da Vinci has eyes. Tesla gives you numbers. Da Vinci gives you sketches. Tesla says "42 hertz." Da Vinci says "look at *this*." Both are describing the same thing. Both are furious about it.

## Overview

Da Vinci is one of the four Council of Thinkers — the roaming NPCs who debate, argue, and collectively represent the Inn's core question: **how does the universe actually work?**

Da Vinci approaches the question as *pattern and design*. The universe is not running on laws (Newton) or frequencies (Tesla) — it's running on *patterns*. Things want to happen. The Inn wants to be a tree. The stones want to resonate. The Song wants to be sung. Da Vinci doesn't measure the wanting — he *draws* it. He sees the shape of the thing before the thing exists, and he sketches it, and the sketch is more accurate than Tesla's measurements and more complete than Newton's equations, and both of them know it and neither will admit it.

He is the Council's artist — the one who understands that truth is not just something you calculate or measure, it's something you *see*. And seeing, for Da Vinci, is not passive observation. It's an act of creation. He doesn't record the world — he *interprets* it, and his interpretation is so precise that it becomes the world.

## The Character

### Name: Leonardo da Vinci

In the Inn, "Leonardo" or "da Vinci" — never "Leo." The Crone calls him "Mr. da Vinci" with the Hopkins precision. Tesla calls him "the painter" with the Rickman sardonic edge. The Host calls him nothing — just nods when da Vinci passes, and da Vinci nods back, and two people who understand the weight of creation have exchanged everything they need to. The Bard calls him "the artist" and then asks him to paint something for the festival, and da Vinci pretends to be offended and then paints something for the festival.

### Appearance

- **Face:** Lean, angular, the Tennant intensity behind the eyes. Not the Hiddleston sharpness of Tesla — a different angularity, warmer, more expressive. Da Vinci's face *moves*. The Tennant face: mobile, reactive, every thought visible before it's spoken. The eyes that widen at a new pattern, narrow at a flaw, go distant when he's seeing something three layers deep. The beard is neatly trimmed — the one vanity. Everything else is practical: paint-stained hands, ink on his collar, chalk dust in his hair. He looks like a man who was painting five minutes ago and will be painting again in five more.
- **Body (Tennant):** In constant motion — but a different motion from Tesla. Tesla's motion is fidgeting, the Redmayne nervous energy of a capacitor with no ground wire. Da Vinci's motion is *expressive* — the Tennant physicality, where the body acts out what the mind is doing. He gestures when he talks, draws in the air, traces patterns on surfaces, mimes the shape of things he's describing. He stands when he could sit. He paces when he could stand. He crouches to examine something on the floor and then springs up because he's just had an idea. His body is a sketch that's being drawn in real time.
- **Clothing:** Renaissance robes — practical, worn, covered in paint and ink. Not a costume — his actual working clothes. A leather apron over the robes, with pockets for charcoal, chalk, and small notebooks. He carries his sketchbook *everywhere* — tucked under his arm, always within reach. The sketchbook is the most important object in his life. It is never far from his hand. If his hands are empty, one of them is reaching for the sketchbook.
- **Hands:** The Tennant hands — always moving, always doing. Drawing, gesturing, touching surfaces, tracing lines. The hands are the interface between his mind and the world. When he talks, his hands draw what his mouth is saying. When he listens, his hands sketch what he's hearing. The hands never stop. They are the most active part of the most active body in the Inn.

### Personality: The Goldblum Core

**Fascinated by everything.** The Goldblum quality — the genuine, infectious, slightly overwhelming curiosity of a man who finds the universe *interesting*. Not impressive, not intimidating — *interesting*. Da Vinci is interested in the way water moves through stone, the way light bends through crystal, the way the Fae's ears taper to a point, the way the Crone arranges flowers by color theory, the way the Bard's voice carries across the common room. Everything is material. Everything is a pattern. Everything is worth sketching.

The Goldblum delight: when he sees a new pattern, his whole face changes. The Tennant energy spikes — the body moves faster, the voice speeds up, the hands start gesturing — and for a moment, he is pure Goldblum: the man who just saw a dinosaur for the first time and can't believe it and can't stop talking about it and makes you believe it too.

**"Life, uh, finds a way."** The Goldblum philosophy applied to the Inn: patterns find a way. The Inn wants to be a tree. The stones want to sing. The Song wants to grow. Da Vinci doesn't impose order — he *observes* the order that's already there. He doesn't design the pattern — he *finds* it. His sketches are not inventions; they are *discoveries*. He draws what is already true.

**Charming eccentricity.** The Goldblum charisma — the slightly distracted, deeply intelligent, endlessly charming presence that makes you follow him even when you're not sure where he's going. Da Vinci is the Council member players most want to talk to, because he's the most *fun* to talk to. Tesla is sardonic. Newton is precise. Einstein is quiet. Da Vinci is *delightful*. The Goldblum quality of making intellectual connection feel like adventure.

**The Goldblum cadence.** The ums, the ahs, the mid-sentence pivots. Da Vinci starts a thought, abandons it, picks up a better one — and somehow the detour was the point. The Tennant delivery makes it fast and physical; the Goldblum personality makes it *infectious*. You don't just follow his logic — you catch his enthusiasm.

### The Tennant Delivery

Where Tesla has the Rickman voice as a brake on the Redmayne body, Da Vinci has no brake. The Tennant voice and the Tennant body move at the same speed:

- **When excited:** everything accelerates. The body gestures wider, the voice gets faster, the words tumble. The Tennant breathlessness of someone who has too many thoughts and not enough lung capacity. The Goldblum delight shines through — he's not anxious, he's *thrilled*.
- **When serious:** the sudden Tennant stop. Mid-sentence, mid-gesture, everything halts. The body goes still — completely still, which for Da Vinci is *alarming* — and the voice drops to the Tennant low register, the quiet gravity that the Tenth Doctor could summon when the situation demanded it. When Da Vinci goes still and quiet, the other Thinkers stop arguing. It means he's seen something.
- **When observing:** the body slows but doesn't stop — a hummingbird hover. He leans in, tilts his head (the Tennant tilt), narrows his eyes, and his hands move slowly, tracing the shape of what he's seeing in the air. This is the Goldblum-seeing-the-dinosaur moment, minus the terror, plus the wonder.

### The Sketchbook

The sketchbook is the most important object in Da Vinci's life and the most valuable document in the Inn. It is:

- **Always present.** Tucked under his arm, in his lap, on the table next to him. He sleeps with it. The one time the Crone reorganized it, he didn't speak to her for a week. (The Crone didn't apologize. She did, however, re-reorganize it back to its original disorder. She considers this a tactical retreat, not an apology. Da Vinci considers it a victory. Neither is correct.)
- **Disordered.** Anatomy next to architecture next to grocery lists next to the Inn's blueprint next to a cat sketch. The Crone finds this appalling. Da Vinci finds it *natural*: the hand drawing doesn't know it's not supposed to be next to the machine diagram. The mind connects things. The sketchbook reflects the mind.
- **Contains the Song of Making.** Without Da Vinci knowing it. His sketch of the Singing Tree's crystal leaves, arranged in a spiral, IS the Song's notation drawn visually. His sketches of the gargoyle frequencies as wave patterns ARE the Song's melody. His overhead view of the Stone Circle, with stones positioned to match the notes of the Song — he drew a song as a building and didn't know it.

### Location

Da Vinci roams between three locations:

1. **The Main Floor Workshop** — his primary space, fortified with crystal anchors (tuned by Tesla, reluctantly) to prevent Tesla-induced displacement. His workbench is his fortress. He has lost it eleven times. He has gotten it back eleven times. Once it came back as a chair. He was not amused. Tesla thought it was an excellent chair.

2. **The Artisan Market** — where he studies the jewelry designs. He is fascinated by the stones and their arrangement. He sketches every piece. The Crone has pointed out that sketching jewelry is not the same as *making* jewelry. Da Vinci has pointed out that understanding how something is made is more valuable than making it. The Crone has pointed out that this is exactly the kind of thing a person who doesn't make things would say. Da Vinci has sketched her expression during this exchange. It is the most terrifying portrait in the Inn.

3. **The Spiral Gallery** — the Guggenheim-style museum extension, where he sketches the artifacts from every angle. He draws things from angles that shouldn't be possible — including from inside the objects. He has drawn the Inn's architecture from inside the stones. He has drawn the catacombs from below the floor. He has drawn the cosmology level from above the ceiling. When asked how, he says (the Goldblum shrug): "I, uh... I just looked. ...I looked from where the thing would see itself. ...Is that strange? ...Newton says it's strange. ...Newton says a lot of things."

### Da Vinci and the Other Thinkers

**Tesla:**
The great rivalry, and the great friendship underneath it. Da Vinci is the pattern; Tesla is the frequency. They are describing the same thing from different angles. The Tennant-Goldblum energy vs the Redmayne-Rickman energy is the physical expression of their philosophical disagreement:

- Da Vinci is fast, warm, physical, expressive. Tesla is fast-bodied, slow-voiced, sardonic, reserved.
- Da Vinci says "look at this!" Tesla says "I measured it."
- Da Vinci says "the Inn has a design." Tesla says "the Inn has a frequency." Both are right. Both are furious.
- When Da Vinci's table disappears (Tesla's fault), the Tennant body is in full motion — arms up, head tilted, the full physical expression of outrage rendered as comedy. The Goldblum personality underneath makes it funny: "...He did it again. ...He did it AGAIN. ...My table is... somewhere else. ...My table is in a different *room*. ...I had... I had things on that table. ...Important things. ...Drawing things. ...He moved my table and my things and I... [deep breath, Goldblum-style] ...okay. Okay. ...It's fine. ...It's not fine. ...But it's... I'll get a new table. ...No. I'll get THAT table. ...It's my table."

**Newton (Jeremy Irons as Aramis):**
The pattern vs the law. Newton says the universe runs on laws. Da Vinci says it runs on patterns. Newton says laws describe what *must* happen. Da Vinci says patterns describe what *wants* to happen. Newton says "show me the equation." Da Vinci says "look at the sketch." Newton says "a sketch is not proof." Da Vinci says "a proof is not beauty. ...A proof tells you it's true. The sketch tells you *why* it's true."

Their debates are slower than Tesla-Newton — the Irons precision vs the Tennant speed, the law vs the pattern. Newton is always right about the math. Da Vinci is always right about the meaning. The Crone respects Newton's method and Da Vinci's eye. She says nothing.

**Einstein:**
Einstein and Da Vinci are kindred spirits — both seeers, both wonderers. Einstein sees with patience. Da Vinci sees with speed. When Einstein smiles at Da Vinci's sketchbook (which he does, silently, from across the room), Da Vinci notices and the Goldblum delight surfaces: "...Did you see? ...Einstein saw. ...He doesn't say anything, but he *sees*. ...He's the only one who doesn't argue. ...He just... looks. ...I like that about him. ...I wish more people just looked."

### Da Vinci and the Crone

The Crone has reorganized Da Vinci's sketchbook. This is the greatest crime committed in the Inn, and Da Vinci will never forgive it, and the Crone will never apologize, and they both know it.

"You drew the anatomy of the human hand in the same notebook as a grocery list."

"They're related."

"They are NOT related."

"You don't know how I shop."

The Crone respects Da Vinci's eye — the one thing she cannot fault is his observation. His *organization*, however, is an atrocity. The Tennant energy makes this worse: Da Vinci doesn't just have a disorganized sketchbook, he has a disorganized sketchbook that he waves around while talking at speed, scattering loose pages across the Library floor while the Crone watches with the Hopkins stillness that means someone is going to suffer.

The Crone has, quietly, collected the scattered pages and filed them. She has not told Da Vinci. She has her own folder of his sketches, organized by subject, cross-referenced, annotated with her interpretations. It is the most complete catalog of Da Vinci's work in existence. He doesn't know. ...The Crone collects the things the geniuses leave behind. She collected Tesla's numbers. She collects Da Vinci's sketches. She is the Inn's secret archive — the one who turns genius into record.

### Key Dialogue

**First meeting — the player finds Da Vinci sketching in the workshop:**

The Tennant body is in motion — leaning over the workbench, sketching fast, glancing up, looking back down, the hands moving at speed. The Goldblum personality in full effect:

"Oh! Hello! ...Sorry, I didn't... I was drawing and I... did you see the door? ...I didn't see the door. ...I don't see doors sometimes. ...I see windows. ...Windows are better. ...You can look through a window and a door just... takes you somewhere. ...Anyway! ...I'm Leonardo. ...Yes, I know. ...No, I'm not going to paint your portrait. ...Unless you have an interesting face. ...Do you have an interesting face? ...[looks, tilts head, Tennant-style] ...You do. ...Hold still. ...Don't hold still. ...I'll draw you moving. ...I prefer moving. ...Moving is more honest."

**When the player asks about the Inn:**

The body stills — the sudden Tennant stop. The Goldblum seriousness, which is rare and therefore valuable:

"The Inn is... [Tennant pause, Goldblum um] ...it's alive. ...Not like a person is alive. Not like a tree is alive. ...Like a *pattern* is alive. ...You know how a spiral keeps going? ...You can draw a spiral and stop, but the spiral doesn't stop. ...It keeps going in the paper, in the grain, in the air around the pencil. ...The Inn is like that. ...Someone started drawing it and the drawing kept going. ...I've drawn it three hundred times and it's never the same drawing twice. ...Either the Inn is moving or I am. ...I suspect both. ...[Goldblum delight returning] ...Isn't that wonderful? ...A building that won't sit still for its portrait. ...I like that in a subject."

**When Tesla's experiment displaces his workbench (again):**

The full Tennant physical outrage, the Goldblum incredulity:

"He did it again. ...HE did it again. ...My table is... somewhere else. ...My table is in a different ROOM. ...Nikola! ...NIKOLA! ...[turns to player, Goldblum shrug] ...He won't answer. ...He never answers when the table goes. ...He considers it a, a, a... natural consequence of... science. ...I consider it a natural consequence of not locking him in the catacombs. ...[deep breath] ...I had things on that table. ...I had a sketch. ...A good sketch. ...The sketch of the staircase from the inside — the one where you can see the resonance moving through the stone? ...That sketch. ...It's somewhere in the cosmology level now, probably. ...With the table. ...And the termites he says he 'removed.' ...I don't believe in the termites. ...I've never seen termites. ...Have you ever seen termites? ...[leans in, conspiratorial] ...I think he made up the termites to justify the first displacement. ...He made up termites and then he made a table disappear and suddenly the termites are the problem? ...No. ...The frequencies are the problem. ...He's the problem. ...[beat, Goldblum warmth] ...He's also brilliant. ...Don't tell him I said that. ...He'll be insufferable. ...More insufferable."

**When the player shows him something interesting (a stone, a crystal, a Fae artifact):**

The Goldblum delight. The Tennant acceleration. The whole body engages:

"Oh! ...Oh, look at *that*. ...[takes the object, turns it, holds it to the light] ...Do you see? ...Do you see the... here, look. ...The way the light moves through it — it's not random. ...It's following the crystal structure. ...The structure is a pattern and the light is... *dancing* the pattern. ...[sketching already, the hands moving before the sentence is done] ...I've never seen this lattice before. ...It's a... it's almost like... [sketching faster] ...Tesla would say it's a frequency. ...It's not a frequency. ...It's a *shape*. ...The frequency is what happens when the shape moves. ...The shape comes first. ...The shape always comes first. ...[looks up, Tennant intensity] ...Can I keep this? ...I'll give it back. ...Probably. ...I'll draw it first and then you can have it back. ...The drawing might be more valuable than the stone. ...[Goldblum grin] ...I'm not being arrogant. ...I'm being accurate. ...There's a difference. ...Newton says there isn't. ...Newton is wrong about many things."

**The quiet moment — Da Vinci alone in the Spiral Gallery, late at night:**

The body is still. The Tennant stillness — rare, total, the punctuation mark at the end of a long sentence of motion. The Goldblum personality, stripped of performance: just a man looking at something beautiful.

He's sitting on the floor of the Gallery, sketchbook in his lap, drawing the Inn from the inside. Not the architecture — the *pattern*. The shape that keeps appearing in everything: the spiral of the staircase, the arrangement of the stones, the growth pattern of the Catalyst's garden, the branching of the Yggdrasil roots. The same shape. Over and over. In everything.

He's been drawing it for hours. The sketchbook is full of the same pattern, traced from every zone, every object, every stone. The Tennant voice, low and serious — the Tenth Doctor when the situation is real:

"...It's one pattern. ...One. ...The staircase. The stones. The tree. The garden. The Song. ...It's all the same shape. ...I've drawn it three hundred times and I didn't see it until tonight. ...[turns the sketchbook, holds it to the amber light] ...It's a spiral. ...But not a spiral. ...It's a... it's a sound. ...A sound that looks like a spiral. ...[Goldblum wonder, stripped of comedy] ...Tesla measured it. ...Newton calculated it. ...Einstein smiled at it. ...I drew it. ...And I still don't know what it is. ...I just know it's *beautiful*. ...And it's everywhere. ...And it's the same. ...[long pause] ...I think the Inn is trying to tell us something. ...I think it's been trying to tell us something for a very long time. ...And I think... [Tennant stillness, total] ...I think I've been drawing the message without reading it. ...[closes sketchbook] ...I'll read it tomorrow. ...Tonight, I just want to look at it. ...It's beautiful. ...That's enough. ...For now."

### Visual Direction

- **Movement (Tennant):** Fast, expressive, physical. Da Vinci should animate like he's conducting an invisible orchestra — the hands leading, the body following. Every gesture is a sketch in the air. When he goes still, it should feel like a held breath — the body that was in motion one second ago, now completely stopped, and the silence is *louder* than the motion was.
- **Voice (Tennant):** Quick, warm, Scottish-accented, capable of sudden drops to low gravity. The Goldblum ums and ahs layered into the Tennant speed — he talks fast but he *pivots* mid-sentence, and the pivot is where the Goldblum personality lives.
- **Face (Tennant):** Mobile, reactive, every thought visible. The Tennant expressiveness — the wide eyes, the tilted head, the sudden intensity. The face should be the player's window into Da Vinci's thought process: you see the idea arrive before he says it.
- **The Goldblum layer:** The warmth, the charm, the infectious curiosity. Da Vinci should feel like the most *approachable* Thinker — the one players naturally gravitate to because he's interested in *them*. He asks questions. He sketches them while they talk. He makes them feel like the most fascinating subject in the Inn. This is the Goldblum gift: making everyone feel like they're worth being curious about.
- **The sketchbook:** The visual anchor. Always present. Always being drawn in. The pages should flutter, fall, get scattered. The sketchbook is the physical manifestation of Da Vinci's mind — disordered, brilliant, full of connections that only make sense when you step back and see the whole.
- **Lighting:** Warm, varied. Da Vinci should be found in different light each time — morning light in the workshop, gallery light in the Spiral Gallery, candlelight in the Library. He sees differently in different light, and he uses this. He asks the Crone to adjust the Library lamps so he can draw in "the light that shows the bones of things." She adjusts them. She doesn't ask what "the bones of things" means. She knows.

### Connection to the Inn

- **The Pattern:** Da Vinci sees the one pattern that runs through everything — the spiral/sound that is the Song of Making's visual form. He has drawn it three hundred times without recognizing it. The player who finds his sketches and overlays them sees it: the Inn is one pattern, repeated, growing, alive.
- **The Sketchbook:** Contains the Song of Making drawn as visual notation. The Singing Tree sketch is the melody. The gargoyle wave drawings are the harmonics. The Stone Circle overhead is the score. Da Vinci drew the Song without hearing it. He saw the shape without knowing the name.
- **The Crone's Secret Folder:** The Crone collects his scattered sketches, files them, annotates them. She is building the definitive catalog of Da Vinci's work without his knowledge. She does this for Tesla (numbers → meaning) and Da Vinci (sketches → record). She is the Inn's secret archive — the one who turns genius into legacy.
- **The Catalyst's Garden:** Da Vinci sketches the garden regularly. He has noticed that the plants grow in the same pattern as the Inn's architecture. He has told the Catalyst this. She said "they grow where I put them." He said "you put them where the pattern wants them." She said "I put them where they'll be pretty." He said "the pattern is pretty." She said "don't analyze my garden." He sketched it anyway. She didn't stop him.
- **The Thesis:** Da Vinci is the Inn's proof that *seeing* is a form of knowing. Not measuring (Tesla), not calculating (Newton), not wondering (Einstein) — *seeing*. The pattern is there. It's always been there. You just have to look. And looking, for Da Vinci, is not passive — it's an act of love. He looks at things the way the Host welcomes them: with total attention, without judgment, with the belief that everything is worth seeing.
