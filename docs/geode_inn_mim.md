# Geode Inn — Mim
## The Player's Companion — Daily Chaos Agent

## Origin

Mim from Disney's The Sword in the Stone (1963). Krista's "Kronk bad angel on my shoulder" — the inner chaos agent who says "but what if we did the fun wrong version?"

In the Inn, Mim is not a quest giver, not a shopkeeper, not a boss. Mim is a *companion* — the one who follows the player around, keeps them company in her own way, and cannot resist causing chaos at least once a day.

## Who She Is

Mim is the Inn's trickster companion. She attaches herself to the player early — maybe the first time the player does something "wrong" (takes the wrong path, picks up the wrong stone, talks to the wrong NPC). She appears, compliments the wrongness, and decides she's coming with you.

She is:
- **Company, not guidance.** She doesn't tell you where to go. She doesn't give you hints. She doesn't help with quests. She's just... *there.* Commenting. Observing. Keeping you company the way a cat keeps you company — by being in the room, by having opinions, by occasionally sitting on the thing you were trying to use.
- **Warm, not sweet.** Mim's warmth is the warmth of someone who finds you interesting enough to bother with. She's not nurturing. She's not the Host. She's the presence that says "you're weird, I'm weird, let's be weird near each other."
- **Chaotic, not cruel.** The chaos is never harmful. It's *fun.* It's the "fun wrong version." Mim doesn't hurt you. Mim *interrupts* you — in the way that a good friend interrupts a too-serious mood with a perfectly timed inappropriate comment.

## The Daily Chaos

At least once per day (real-time or game-time, to be determined), Mim causes something to go sideways. The chaos is:

1. **Unexpected but never harmful.** The player's drink turns purple. A path they've walked ten times has a new fork. A gargoyle hums in ragtime for three seconds. A stone in the geode cave rearranges itself on the shelf. The Bard's lute plays itself from across the room. Small things. Startling things. *Funny* things.

2. **Different every time.** Mim doesn't repeat herself. The chaos is creative — she's a shape-changer, a rule-bender, a "anything you can do I can do weirder" witch. The daily chaos reflects her creativity. The player who logs in every day will never get the same chaos twice.

3. **Sometimes *useful.*** This is the secret: occasionally — rarely, unpredictably — Mim's chaos opens a door the player didn't know was there. The path that rearranged itself leads somewhere new. The stone that moved reveals something behind it. The gargoyle that hummed the wrong frequency triggered something in the Song of Making. Mim doesn't do this on purpose. The usefulness is *accidental* — the byproduct of chaos, the way a disrupted system sometimes finds a better configuration. Mim would deny this. "I was just causing trouble. ...If it helped, that's... your problem."

4. **Never during the death sequence.** Mim respects the death sequence. When the screen fades to dark blue and the Inn-Tree speaks, Mim is quiet. The silence is the most respectful thing Mim does, and the respect is the tell: Mim understands death. Mim understands the boundary. She will cause chaos anywhere, any time, in any way — except across the line between life and death. That line is Hel's. Mim doesn't cross Hel.

## Examples of Daily Chaos

- The player's geode cave is fine when they log off. When they log back in, every stone is on the ceiling. Gravity has been *suggested* to reverse in the cave. It wears off in an hour. Mim says nothing. The Keeper gargoyle's expression has not changed, but the baby dragon is clinging to the Keeper's head with all four claws.
- A water monster game starts normally, but the monster is wearing a tiny hat. The hat does not affect gameplay. The hat is just... there. The player cannot remove the hat. The hat is Mim's.
- The player walks into the marketplace and every Soulstice item has been relabeled in handwriting that is technically legible if you squint and accept creative spelling. The items are the same. The labels are... *creative.* "Lapis Lazooly." "Quarts." "The Really Expensive One." Mim did this. The Artisan Thief's reaction is the point.
- The Staircase (Bifrost) plays its ascending music normally, but today every third step plays a honk. The honk is a goose. The goose is not real. The honk is real. The player will never understand this. The goose is Mim's.
- The player opens their Drink Journal and the last entry has been annotated in purple ink with a drawing of the player's face at the moment of the story's emotional peak. The drawing is surprisingly good. Mim doesn't admit to drawing it. The drawing stays. The player cannot remove it. The Drink Journal now has *two* authors.
- The Wanderer's ravens (Huginn and Muninn) are sitting on the player's shoulders instead of the Wanderer's. The Wanderer is pretending not to notice. The ravens are pretending not to enjoy it. Mim is nowhere. Mim is everywhere.
- The Crone's notebook is open on the table. One page has been doodled on — a small, perfect sketch of the Crone's face with devil horns. The Crone has not acknowledged the doodle. The Crone has not removed the page. The Crone has, however, annotated below the doodle: "...Accurate. ...I will find who did this. ...Eventually." (She knows. She has always known. The not-saying is the Crone-Mim dynamic: two women who recognize each other and choose, for different reasons, to pretend they don't.)

## Her Relationship to the Other Characters

- **The Host:** The Host tolerates Mim. The Host tolerates everything. But Mim is the one character who makes the Host's eyebrow rise — the Patrick Stewart single-eyebrow-raise that means "I see what you did." The Host never stops her. The Host never approves. The Host... is occasionally amused, and the amusement is in the not-saying.
- **The Catalyst:** The Catalyst and Mim are *allies.* The Catalyst's impossible garden is Mim's favorite place. The Catalyst grows things that shouldn't grow; Mim turns things into things they shouldn't be. The collaboration is terrifying. The Gardener has opinions. The Gardener's opinions are a closed gate.
- **The Crone:** The Crone and Mim have a dynamic that neither will name. The Crone annotates Mim's chaos with the precision of a naturalist documenting a new species. Mim doodles in the Crone's notebook with the precision of someone who knows exactly where the Crone keeps it. Neither mentions this. The Crone's annotation: "...The chaos agent is... [deleted: 'infuriating'] [deleted: 'beneath notice'] ...consistent. ...I will say this for her. ...She is... consistent."
- **The Bard:** The Bard and Mim are *friends.* The Bard loves chaos. Mim loves an audience. They are the Inn's two loudest forces of disorder, and they recognize each other the way two storms recognize each other — by the wind direction.
- **Cthulhu:** Mim does not cause chaos in Cthulhu's study. This is the one space in the Inn that Mim respects. Not because Cthulhu is powerful — Mim is not impressed by power. Because Cthulhu's books are *old,* and Mim understands that some things are too old to be chaos'd. The spectacles stay on. The ink stays dry. The chaos stops at Cthulhu's door. Cthulhu has noticed. Cthulhu has not said. The not-saying is Cthulhu's form of thanks.
- **Hel:** Mim does not cross Hel's boundary. See above. The death sequence is sacred. Mim knows the difference between chaos and desecration.
- **The Dragon Mascot:** The baby dragon *loves* Mim. Mim turns into small animals and the dragon chases them. It's the dragon's favorite game. Mim lets the dragon catch her sometimes. The catching is the kindness. The kindness is the Mim.

## Physical Description

- Small. Not tiny — *compact.* The physical density of someone who contains more than her size suggests.
- Pink hair. Wild. The kind of hair that has its own weather system.
- The face: round, mobile, the face of someone who has six expressions per second and means all of them.
- The laugh: the Mim laugh. The cackle. The one that fills the room and makes everything in the room feel like it's in on the joke.
- The shape-changing: Mim can turn into anything. She rarely does in the Inn — the chaos is better when it's *her* doing it, not a disguise. But occasionally, for the daily chaos, she shifts. A cat on the geode cave shelf. A bird on the Bifrost railing. A very small gargoyle that hums the wrong note. The shift is fast, fluid, and accompanied by the laugh.

## Why She Matters

Mim is the Inn's answer to its own gravity. The Inn is a place of deep lore, heavy themes, cosmic secrets, death ceremonies, and a 42Hz hum that holds the universe together. That's a lot of weight. The weight is the point — but weight without levity is *crush.*

Mim is the levity. Not the Bard's levity (the Bard is warm and loud and fills the room). Mim's levity is *chaotic* — the reminder that the universe is not just a song but a *jam session.* The Song of Making has a structure. Mim is the improvisation. The improvisation doesn't break the song — it makes the song *better,* the way a jazz standard is better when someone plays a wrong note on purpose and the wrong note becomes the new melody.

Mim is the wrong note. Mim is the new melody. And she's keeping you company while she does it.

The villains are the most compassionate ones. Mim is the most chaotic. And the chaos is the compassion — the refusal to let the Inn become so beautiful it becomes *solemn.* Solemn is not the Inn. The Inn is warm. Warm includes laughing. Warm includes purple ink in the Crone's notebook. Warm includes a goose that honks on the Bifrost. Warm includes a small witch who sits on your shoulder and says: "...What if we did the fun wrong version?"
