# Geode Inn — The Bead Composer
## System: The Digital Jewelry Studio
## Access: Undercity → Master's Bench + Campgrounds → Solstara's Studio
## Prerequisite: Level 4 (Campgrounds) reached + at least 10 stones collected

### Overview

The Bead Composer is the Geode Inn's commercial tool — the bridge between the digital game world and Krista's physical jewelry craft at Soulstice Jewelry. The Composer is a digital bead board where players design custom jewelry pieces from collected stones, hear the sonic composition of their design, and submit the design as a production order to Soulstice.

The Composer is NOT just a game mechanic — it's a product configurator. The vision: customers design custom pieces digitally, hear the sonic composition (each stone produces a frequency — the arrangement is a melody), and submit designs as real production orders. The digital design becomes a physical "Wearable Song."

The Composer is the Inn's most direct connection to Soulstice's real-world business. Every stone in the game has a real-world mineral counterpart. Every frequency in the game corresponds to a real mineral property (Mohs hardness → frequency mapping). The Bead Composer is the place where the game's fiction and the craft's reality meet.

## The Interface

### The Board
The Bead Composer's workspace — a wooden tray (three wood types: cocobolo, olive, ebony) with rows of circular dips for bead placement. The board is Krista's physical booth process, digitized: two-sided tape becomes snap-to-grid, artsy paper becomes background texture selection, matte laminate becomes the finish selector.

### Stone Palette
The player's collected stones, displayed as beads in three sizes (small, medium, large). Each stone-bead shows:
- **Color** (from the real mineral's color range)
- **Frequency** (from the Sonic Mineral Codex)
- **Mohs hardness** (real geological data)
- **Source** (which zone/quest the stone came from)

### Metal Palette
The structural metals — the wire and findings that hold the beads together. Each metal has a frequency contribution:
- **Copper:** 79Hz (warm, grounding — the oldest worked metal)
- **Silver:** 80Hz (bright, clear — the reflective metal)
- **Gold:** 82Hz (rich, resonant — the noble metal)
- **Alloys:** The Blacksmith can create intermediate frequencies by alloying metals (e.g., copper-silver = 79.5Hz)

### Audio Engine
The Bead Composer's core feature — real-time audio playback of the design. As the player places beads on the board:
1. Each bead produces its frequency (a sustained tone based on the stone's mineral properties)
2. The metal wire produces its drone (the structural metal's frequency, constant beneath the beads)
3. The arrangement produces a MELODY — the beads' frequencies, ordered by position on the board
4. The complete piece produces a HARMONIC — the interaction between the beads' frequencies and the metal's drone

The audio engine is the Composer's unique feature — the ability to HEAR a jewelry design before it exists. The player is not just designing visually — the player is composing a piece of music that happens to be made of stone and metal.

### The Finish
The final step — the player selects a finish (matte, polished, oxidized, textured). The finish affects the visual appearance and the audio quality: matte dampens the frequencies (softer sound), polished amplifies them (brighter sound), oxidized darkens both the color and the tone, textured adds a subtle roughness to the audio.

## The Workflow

### Step 1: Select the Board
Choose the tray material (cocobolo, olive, or ebony). Each wood has a different resonance — cocobolo is warm and deep, olive is bright and clear, ebony is neutral and balanced. The wood's resonance affects the overall sound of the composition.

### Step 2: Select the Metal
Choose the structural metal (copper, silver, gold, or alloy). The metal is the DRONE — the constant frequency beneath the beads' melody. The metal also determines the visual color of the wire and findings.

### Step 3: Place the Beads
Drag stone-beads from the palette onto the board. Each bead snaps into a circular dip. The arrangement is free-form (the player chooses the order) but follows design principles:
- **Balance:** symmetric arrangements produce harmonious sounds; asymmetric arrangements produce complex, interesting sounds
- **Spacing:** beads placed close together create interference patterns (close frequencies interact); beads placed far apart produce cleaner individual tones
- **Size:** large beads produce louder, longer tones; small beads produce quieter, shorter tones
- **Hardness:** harder stones (higher Mohs) produce higher frequencies; softer stones produce lower frequencies

### Step 4: Listen
Press the play button. The Composer plays the arrangement as a composition:
1. The metal drone begins (sustained, constant)
2. Each bead's tone enters in sequence (left to right, or in a pattern the player defines)
3. The tones interact — harmonics, interference, resonance
4. The complete piece plays as a short composition (10-30 seconds, depending on bead count)

The player can adjust, rearrange, and replay until the composition sounds right. The EAR is the final judge — not the eye.

### Step 5: Finalize
Select the finish. The finish is applied. The piece is saved to the player's portfolio.

### Step 6: Submit (Real-World Bridge)
The player can submit the design as a production order to Soulstice Jewelry. The submission includes:
- The digital design (bead arrangement, metal, finish)
- The sonic composition (audio file)
- The stone list (real mineral names and quantities)
- The player's contact information

Krista receives the order at Soulstice and crafts the physical piece. The digital "Wearable Song" becomes a real, physical piece of jewelry — the same stones, the same metal, the same arrangement. The frequency that played in the game is the frequency the real stones carry in the real world.

## Design Principles (Taught by Solstara)

Solstara (the Campgrounds jeweler) teaches the player the principles of sonic jewelry design through the Display Board (her finished pieces) and the Commission Book (NPC requests):

1. **The Drone is the Foundation** — the metal wire's frequency is the piece's base. Everything else builds on it. Choose the metal first.
2. **Contrast Creates Interest** — beads of similar frequencies produce a drone (boring). Beads of very different frequencies produce chaos (unpleasant). Beads with COMPLEMENTARY frequencies (harmonic intervals) produce beauty.
3. **Hardness is Pitch** — harder stones sing higher. Softer stones sing lower. A piece with all hard stones is shrill. A piece with all soft stones is muddy. Mix.
4. **The Arrangement is the Melody** — the order of the beads is the order of the notes. A piece played left-to-right tells a story: beginning, middle, end. A piece played center-out tells a different story: origin, expansion, return.
5. **The Silence Between** — the empty dips on the board are RESTS in the music. Not every dip needs a bead. The silence is part of the composition.

## The Commission System

NPCs in the Inn can commission custom pieces from the player. Each commission has requirements:
- **The NPC's frequency** — the piece must include the NPC's personal frequency
- **The NPC's zone** — the piece must include at least one stone from the NPC's home zone
- **The NPC's story** — the piece must tell the NPC's story (the arrangement must follow a narrative arc — beginning, middle, end — that matches the NPC's background)

Fulfilling commissions rewards:
- **Reputation** with the NPC and their zone
- **Unique stones** not available through normal gameplay
- **The Commission Book entry** — the completed piece is documented in the Living Book

## Cross-Platform Pairability

The Bead Composer is designed for maximum cross-platform pairability:
- **Web:** Full interactive Composer (drag-and-drop, real-time audio)
- **Mobile:** Touch-optimized Composer (simplified interface, same audio engine)
- **Steam/Godot:** The Composer as an in-game workstation (the physical bench in the Undercity)
- **Physical Jewelry:** The submitted design becomes a real piece from Soulstice

The same design, the same stones, the same frequencies — playable on every platform, wearable in real life. The Bead Composer is the Inn's bridge between digital and physical — the place where the game's fiction becomes the craft's reality.
