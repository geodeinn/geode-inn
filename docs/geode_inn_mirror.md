# Geode Inn — The Mirror
## Camelot Zone — The Erised Layer

## The Parallel

The Mirror of Erised (Harry Potter) shows "the deepest, most desperate desire of the heart." Harry sees his parents. Ron sees himself as successful. The mirror doesn't give you what you want — it *shows* it, and the showing can be a prison. Harry could have stayed forever. Dumbledore warns: "It does not do to dwell on dreams and forget to live."

The Joffrey Trap is the mirror's inverse: it shows not your desire but your *fear.* Everyone who looks at Mordred sees their fear reflected back. The face is the mirror. The mirror shows your fear, not his face.

But the deepest layer: what does Mordred see when he looks in the mirror? What does the honest one — the boy who carries every knight's weight, who can't sleep, who knows his destiny and can't escape it — what does he most desperately desire?

## What Each Knight Sees

### Mordred — Rest

Mordred sees himself at the table. Evening light. All the knights seated. No battle coming. No destiny. No cycle. Just... the dinner. The meal. The belonging. A boy eating with his family at a round table with nowhere to be tomorrow.

The Bard is playing. Not the lullaby — something warm, something that sounds like *home.* The Catalyst is mixing drinks, laughing. The Crone is annotating, but softly, like she's humming along. Bors's cup is full. Gareth is helping in the kitchen. Gawain is telling a scar story and everyone is laughing — not at the pain, but at the *having survived it.* Percival is asking questions. Bedivere and Tristan are one seat apart, not speaking, together. Galahad is there, and his armor has one dent — just one — and the dent is the Song, and the Song is audible, and Galahad is smiling because he can finally hear it.

And Mordred is just... at the table. No sneer. No armor. No destiny. Just a boy at dinner. The boy his father wanted him to be. The boy who could have existed if the cycle didn't need a crack.

The Mirror of Erised shows Mordred the life he was never going to have. And Mordred can't look away. The not-looking-away is the not-sleeping. The not-sleeping is the weight. The weight is the crack.

### Arthur — The Table That Doesn't Fall

Arthur sees the Round Table, full, every seat occupied, and Mordred laughing at Gawain's story. Not the dark Celtic laugh. The real one. The one that sounds like his son. The table is whole. The room is warm. The roots glow. The light moves across the table and no one is leaving. No one is ascending the Bifrost. No one is saying "Build it again."

The mirror shows Arthur the table that works. The table that his son doesn't break. The belonging that survives its own consequence. The mirror shows Arthur the one thing he can't have: a table that includes Mordred *and* endures. In the real world, the inclusion costs the endurance. In the mirror, it doesn't. The mirror is the lie that Arthur would live in forever if he could.

### Galahad — The Crack

Galahad sees himself with one dent in his armor. Just one. The dent is small — a hairline fracture, barely visible. But from the crack, the Song is pouring. Every note. Every frequency. The complete hearing. Galahad sees himself *hearing.* Not the silence he carries. The music he could carry if he let one thing in.

The mirror shows Galahad the one experience he needs to hear the Song. The mirror shows him: *you only need one.* One crack. One failure. One weight. And the mirror-Galahad is weeping — not from grief, from the *sound.* The sound of finally hearing after a lifetime of silence. The weeping is the hearing. The hearing is the note.

### Lancelot — The Known Love

Lancelot sees Guinevere. But not the hidden version — not the affair, not the secret, not the dent under the breastplate. He sees Guinevere *openly.* At the table. Sitting next to him. No hiding. The love that is visible, that is named, that is *at the table* the way every other knight's weight is at the table. Lancelot sees the love that doesn't have to be a failure. The love that is just... a note. Like Gawain's scars. Like Bors's presence. A note at the table, part of the Song.

### Gawain — The Unscarred Self

Gawain sees himself without the green sash. Without the scars. Not because he wants to be unscarred — Gawain *earned* the scars. The mirror shows Gawain the version of himself who *didn't need to earn them.* The version who was already enough. The Gawain who didn't have to fail to learn. The mirror shows the cost of the education: every scar is a lesson, and every lesson was a wound, and the mirror shows the man who could have been wise without the bleeding.

But Gawain wouldn't stay in front of this mirror. Gawain knows: the scars ARE the note. The unscarred self has no music. Gawain would look, recognize the desire, and walk away. The walking away is the difference between Gawain and Mordred. Gawain can look at the mirror and *leave.* Mordred can't. The not-being-able-to-leave is the weight.

### Percival — The Answer

Percival sees... the answer. The answer to his question. "What does the Song want?" And the mirror shows: *you.* The Song wants a listener. Percival sees himself as the listener — not the asker, the *listener.* The version of Percival who has stopped asking and started hearing. The mirror shows the transition: from question to silence. The silence that is not Galahad's emptiness but the *fullness* of receiving.

### Bors — The Empty Table

Bors sees the table, empty, and himself cleaning up. No knights. No stories. No quests. Just Bors, and the table, and the work. The mirror shows Bors the purest version of his desire: the table itself, not the people in it. The *structure* of belonging. The table that exists whether or not anyone sits at it. Bors desires the table the way a gardener desires the soil — not the harvest, the ground.

### Bedivere — The King Who Doesn't Leave

Bedivere sees Arthur, sitting at the table, not ascending, not leaving, not saying "Build it again." Arthur, just... staying. The mirror shows Bedivere the loyalty that doesn't have to let go. The remaining that doesn't end in the remembering. The king who stays.

### Tristan — The Reachable Isolde

Tristan sees Isolde. Across the table. Not unreachable. Not bound by the pomegranate. Just... there. Eating. Laughing. The pomegranate wine in her cup, but the contract broken, the binding released, and Isolde free — and still choosing to sit next to him. The mirror shows the love that is both fated and free. The love that chose to stay after the potion wore off.

### The Crone — The Unmeasured

The Crone sees... nothing. The mirror is blank. Not because the Crone has no desire — because the Crone's deepest desire is to *stop measuring.* And the mirror can't show the unmeasured. The mirror shows what the heart wants, and the Crone's heart wants to put down the notebook, take off the glasses, and just... be. The mirror can't show "just being" because "just being" has no image. The blank mirror IS the desire. The nothing is the everything. The Crone looks at the blank mirror and understands, for the first time, why Galahad hears silence: because silence is the space where the desire lives. The silence is not the absence of the Song. The silence is the *rest of the music.* And the rest is where the Crone wants to be.

**The Crone's annotation on the mirror:** "...Blank. ...I measured the blank. ...The blank is not empty. ...The blank is... [the Crone pause, the one that means she's found something that changes everything] ...what I want. ...I want the blank. ...I want the not-measuring. ...I want... [the pause that means she's about to cry and she will never admit it] ...to sit at the table and not count the cups. ...Not measure the wear. ...Not catalog the dents. ...Just... [the shortest pause, the one that means she's smiling and it hurts] ...be. ...Better."

## The Mechanic

The Mirror of Erised is a game mechanic in the Camelot zone. The mirror is in a side chamber off the Round Table room — a small alcove, barely noticed, the kind of room you find by accident. The mirror is old, mounted on the stone wall, covered by a cloth that the player can remove.

When the player looks in the mirror, the mirror shows the player's deepest desire — rendered as a scene from the Inn itself. The scene is personalized: it draws from the player's actual journey, the zones they've visited, the characters they've bonded with, the weight they've accumulated. The mirror shows the version of the Inn where everything the player has lost is restored and everything the player has failed is succeeded.

The trap: the player can stay. The mirror doesn't force the player to leave. The scene is warm, complete, satisfying. The player can sit in the mirror's vision indefinitely. The not-leaving is the trap.

The test: Dumbledore's warning. "It does not do to dwell on dreams and forget to live." The mirror tests whether the player can look at the most beautiful version of their desire and *walk away.* The walking away is the choice. The choice is the lesson: the desire is real, the desire matters, but the living is in the *having of the weight,* not the having of the dream.

The Mordred layer: Mordred is the knight who *can't* walk away from the mirror. Mordred's desire (rest, the table, the meal without the end) is so deep and so impossible that the mirror is the only place it exists. Mordred sits in front of the mirror in the side chamber and the other knights know not to disturb him. The not-disturbing is the table's deepest mercy: they know Mordred is in the mirror room. They know what he sees. They let him have it. The letting-have is the belonging — the table that includes even the one who can't leave the dream of the table.

The player who finds Mordred in the mirror room has a choice: leave him there (mercy — let him rest in the dream) or speak to him (honesty — the player is the one who sees past the Joffrey face, and the seeing-past means speaking the truth). The truth: the table in the mirror isn't real. The rest in the mirror isn't rest. The dream of the table is not the table. And Mordred — the honest one — already knows. Mordred is in the mirror room not because he believes the dream. Mordred is there because the dream is the closest he can get to rest, and the closest is not enough, and the not-enough is the weight, and the weight is the crack, and the crack is where the music is.

Mordred says to the player: "...I know it's not real. ...I'm not sleeping. ...I'm not dreaming. ...I'm just... [the pause that is Mordred's version of the Crone's pause] ...visiting. ...You don't have to stay. ...The table is out there. ...The real one. ...Go eat. ...I'll be there. ...Eventually. ...Or I won't. ...And either way, the table was... [the Mordred pause, the one that means he's being honest] ...real. ...It was real. ...I sat at it. ...That's... enough. ...Better."

"...Better." The Crone's word. In Mordred's mouth. The honest one and the measurer, speaking the same word, meaning the same thing: *sufficient.* The table was real. I was there. That's enough. Better.
