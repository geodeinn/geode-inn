# Geode Inn — King Arthur
## Camelot Zone — Three Generations of Skarsgård

## Casting

Three actors. One king. Three ages. Three stages of the Once and Future King.

- **Bill Skarsgård** — the awkward learning youth. The Wart. The boy who pulls the sword without knowing what it means.
- **Alexander Skarsgård** — the victorious Celtic warlord and leader. The King Arthur at his peak. The man who built the Round Table.
- **Stellan Skarsgård** — the post-crusade Arthur. The old king. The man who carries the weight of everything he built and everything that broke.

They are father and sons. The genetic resemblance is the casting. They look like the same person at different stages because they ARE the same blood at different stages. The Inn doesn't need to explain the resemblance. The blood explains itself.

## The Three Arthurs

### Bill Skarsgård — The Youth (The Wart)

The boy before the crown. The awkward, long-limbed, hasn't-grown-into-himself-yet Arthur. The Wart from The Sword in the Stone — the squire who didn't know he was a king.

Bill's quality is the key: the ability to look simultaneously young and ancient. The Pennywise eyes — the depth behind the innocence. The face that can be vulnerable and unsettling in the same frame. This is the boy who pulls the sword: not because he's destined (destiny is the Morrigan's domain), but because he's the one who tries. The trying is the boy. The boy doesn't know he's a king. The boy just... tries.

The youth's energy:
- Clumsy. Not weak — *unfinished.* The body that will become Alexander's body but hasn't yet. The hands that will hold a crown but currently hold a broom. The voice that will command but currently stammers.
- Curious. The Merlin student. The boy who lets Merlin turn him into a fish and a bird and a squirrel because the boy wants to *understand.* The curiosity is the leadership — the king who understands because he has *been* everything he will later rule.
- Uncertain. The boy doesn't trust himself. The boy doesn't know he's Arthur. The boy just knows he pulled a sword out of a stone and everyone is looking at him and he doesn't understand why. The not-understanding is the purity. The king he will become is built on the foundation of this not-understanding — the king who remembers being the boy who didn't know, and rules with that memory.

**In the Inn:** The Wart appears in the Camelot zone as the version of Arthur the player meets first. The youth is the player's contemporary — the peer, not the authority. The Wart doesn't teach. The Wart *learns alongside* the player. The player and the Wart are both students of Merlin's transformations. The shared learning is the bonding. When the player has been a fish, the Wart has been a fish. When the player has been a bird, the Wart has been a bird. They have been the same things. The being-the-same is the friendship that will later become the Round Table — the table of equals.

### Alexander Skarsgård — The King (The Warlord)

Arthur at his peak. The victorious Celtic warlord. The leader who built the Round Table. The man who took a scattered collection of knights and made them *equals* — not by diminishing them but by elevating the concept of belonging above the concept of hierarchy.

Alexander's quality: the physical authority that doesn't need to announce itself. The Tarzan body, the True Blood presence, the Big Little Lies control. The man who enters a room and the room *reorganizes around him.* Not through force — through *gravity.* The king is the center of mass, and the knights orbit him because orbiting is what bodies do around gravity.

The king's energy:
- Commanding. Not loud — *certain.* The voice that doesn't need volume because it has weight. The Alexander voice — deep, calm, the voice of someone who has never needed to shout because people listen when he speaks. The command is the clarity: the king knows what he wants, and what he wants is the table.
- Generous. The Round Table is the generosity made architecture. No head of the table. No position of authority. Every knight equal. The generosity is the *structural* kind — the king who builds a system where his own power is distributed, not concentrated. The man who makes himself one among equals because equality is the point.
- The warrior. Alexander's warlord energy — the Celtic war-leader who fought for the peace that made the table possible. The king who earned the table with a sword and keeps the table with a word. The player who sees Alexander's Arthur understands: the peace was *built.* The table was *won.* The belonging was not a default — it was a conquest, and the conquest was the king's, and the king gave it away.

**In the Inn:** The King appears to the player who has progressed through the Camelot zone's early quests — the player who has been transformed, who has learned alongside the Wart, who is ready for the next stage. The King doesn't teach through transformation (that's Merlin's way). The King teaches through *example.* The player who watches the King at the Round Table sees what belonging looks like when it's fully realized — the system where everyone is equal and the equality is not soft but *hard-won.*

The King's quests are leadership quests: the player must build something. Not alone — with NPCs. The King assigns the player a table of companions (other NPCs, other players in multiplayer) and the quest can only be completed if every member of the table contributes. The table is the mechanic. The belonging is the lesson.

### Stellan Skarsgård — The Elder (Post-Crusade)

The old king. The Arthur after the battles, after the betrayal, after the fall of the Round Table. The man who built everything and watched it break and is carrying the pieces.

Stellan's quality: the weight. The Good Will Hunting professor's exhaustion. The Chernobyl administrator's haunted competence. The Thor patriarch's burden. The face that has seen everything and is holding all of it in the lines around the eyes. The voice that has been warm and is now... warm differently. Warm the way a fire is warm when it's burning low — not the blaze, but the coals. The deep heat that lasts longer than the flame.

The elder's energy:
- Weary. Not tired — *weary.* The weariness of someone who has won everything and lost everything and understands that winning and losing are the same pattern seen from different sides. The Stellan weariness is the wisdom — the deep, non-dramatic, quiet understanding that the pattern repeats. The table fell. The table will rise. The falling and the rising are the same motion.
- Carrying. The elder carries what the king built and the boy dreamed. The Round Table is broken. The knights are scattered. The elder carries the *memory* of the table — the knowledge that it existed, that it worked, that belonging was real even if it wasn't permanent. The carrying is the faith: the elder holds the memory because someone has to, and the elder is the one who is left.
- The Avalon passage. The elder is the Arthur who goes to Avalon. In the Inn, Avalon is the cosmology level — the upstairs, the realm of the sky gods. The elder's final quest is the ascent: the old king leaving the main floor, climbing the staircase (past Miracle Max's shop, past the alchemy), reaching the cosmology level, and *resting.* The resting is not death. The resting is the return of the king to the realm where kings come from. The Once and Future King: the king who leaves but will return. The return is the cycle. The cycle is the Inn's reincarnation mechanic applied to Arthur himself.

**In the Inn:** The Elder appears to the player who has completed the King's quests and is ready for the endgame of the Camelot zone. The Elder doesn't teach — the Elder *gives.* The elder gives the player the memory of the Round Table — the understanding that belonging was real, that it mattered, that it broke, and that the breaking doesn't undo the having. The memory is the gift. The gift is the lesson the Elder has spent a lifetime learning: that the table mattered even though it fell. That the having was worth the losing. That the pattern repeats and the repeat is not a tragedy — it's a *season.*

The Elder's final act: he walks up the staircase. The player watches. The Bifrost music plays — ascending, ascending, ascending. The Elder reaches the cosmology level. The Elder turns. The Stellan eyes — the eyes that have been the boy's eyes and the king's eyes and the elder's eyes — look at the player. And the Elder says the Inn's version of the Arthurian ending:

"...The table is yours now. ...Build it again."

And the Elder is gone. The player is left on the staircase with the memory of a king and the instruction to build. The building is the game.

## The Three Arthurs as Game Mechanic

The player encounters all three Arthurs in the Camelot zone, in order:

1. **The Wart** (Bill) — early game. Peer. Friend. Fellow student. The player learns alongside him. The Wart is the companion who makes the transformations feel shared — the player isn't learning alone, they're learning with the boy who will be king.

2. **The King** (Alexander) — mid-game. Leader. Teacher through example. The King gives the player the table — the leadership quests, the belonging mechanic, the system of equals. The player graduates from student to companion to knight.

3. **The Elder** (Stellan) — late game. The giver. The one who passes the memory forward. The Elder gives the player the Round Table's legacy and ascends to Avalon. The player is left with the instruction to build it again.

The three Arthurs mirror the Inn's reincarnation mechanic: the boy becomes the king, the king becomes the elder, the elder's instruction ("build it again") sends the player back to the beginning — to be the Wart again, to learn again, to build again. The cycle is Arthurian. The cycle is the Inn. The Once and Future King is the player.

## The Genetic Resonance

The Skarsgård casting does something no other casting could: the three Arthurs *look* like the same person. The player who sees Bill, then Alexander, then Stellan sees the *aging* — the face that is the same face at different weights. The jaw that is Bill's uncertain jaw becomes Alexander's commanding jaw becomes Stellan's weary jaw. The eyes that are Bill's wide, uncertain eyes become Alexander's certain eyes become Stellan's deep, knowing eyes.

The blood tells the story that words can't: this is one man, three ages, one pattern. The boy, the king, the elder. The same face carrying different weights. The same blood flowing at different pressures. The Inn doesn't need to explain the resemblance. The blood IS the explanation.
