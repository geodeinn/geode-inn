## THE PREMISE

Naut is the Inn's most shape-shifted character. Originally Cthulhu — H.P. Lovecraft's cosmic entity, reduced to a squid-headed scholar with spectacles — this character has been named, renamed, nicknamed, and retitled across 233 files in the project library. Like the Mona Lisa (known as La Gioconda, La Joconde, Portrait of Lisa Gherardini), the character's identity shifts depending on who is speaking, what document you're reading, and what role the character is playing at that moment.

The character is also — and this should never be lost beneath the weight of the lore — quite adorable. A four-foot squid-headed scholar in ink-stained clothes, patched at the knees, with brass spectacles and tentacles that rest when content and writhe when irked. The cosmic horror who just wanted to read. The ancient being who goes home to the deep to hang out with relatives.

This is the complete audit.

## EVERY NAME

### 1. Cthulhu (Original)
- Source: H.P. Lovecraft, "The Call of Cthulhu" (1928)
- Used across: Project Bible, master storyline, character compendium, before_the_song.txt, master structure, Art catalog, complete walkthrough, DM walkthrough, production reference sheet, Miskatonic Annex specs, Atlantis zone design, and dozens of other foundational docs
- Still appears in 200+ files as "Cthulhu" — the rename to "Naut" was applied going forward but older documents retain the original name
- The name was used from the project's earliest character development through August 30, 2026

### 2. Naut (Current Canonical Name)
- Source: NPC Name Audit, August 30, 2026 — "Naut = from nautilus (cephalopod), scholarly, short and memorable"
- Rationale: Lovecraft character name replaced with original per the Inn's standing rule of stripping author-owned names
- Used in: InnCore memory, voice signature catalog, master character roster, card game spec, dialogue entries, animation prompts, Royal spec, builder architecture blueprint, tri-mode roster, and all forward-facing documents
- Card game lore: "Some texts are not meant to be read. Naut reads them anyway."

### 3. The Professor (The Crone's name)
- Source: Character compendium, Character Profile doc, before_the_song.txt
- The Crone calls Naut "the Professor" — "the only person in this building who understands citation"
- This is the only Thinker-adjacent figure the Crone extends academic courtesy to
- The name reflects academic respect, not affection — the Crone doesn't do affection

### 4. Squid (The Bard's name)
- Source: Character compendium, Character Profile doc
- The Bard calls Naut "Squid" with affectionate irreverence
- Naut allows this. The Bard is the only person who could call Naut "Squid" and survive the Rush eruption
- The Bard survives because Naut finds the Bard genuinely amusing — the Oldman slight smile, the slow blink

### 5. The Scholar in the Shadows
- Source: Character compendium heading: "PART SIX: CTHULHU — THE SCHOLAR IN THE SHADOWS"
- Also: project bible, master storyline, art catalog, walkthroughs
- Describes the character's function and location: the hidden scholar in the fireplace corner nobody notices until they're meant to

### 6. The Deep Scholar
- Source: NPC Name Audit: "Naut, the Deep Scholar"
- Used in: master character roster entry
- Double meaning: "deep" as in deep knowledge, and "deep" as in from the ocean depths (cephalopod)

### 7. The Lovecraftian Scholar
- Source: Architecture blueprint, arcade build sheet (Stone Library Defender), card game spec
- Used as a role descriptor rather than a name
- NOTE: This term is ALSO used for Quire (John Myers' NPC), creating occasional confusion. Quire and Naut are separate characters, but both carry Lovecraft DNA. Quire is the nervous restricted-section reader; Naut is the ancient cosmic entity reduced to academia.

### 8. The Oldest Librarian in the World

## EVERY ROLE

- Source: before_the_song.txt, character profile, compendium, master storyline, project bible, Miskatonic Annex
- Primary gameplay function: gives riddle-quests for rare book retrieval
- Rewards: the rarest stones in the Inn
- Quests are not fetch quests — they are riddle-quests that require the player to understand something about the Inn's deeper layers

### 2. Fireplace Scholar (Main Floor)
- Source: before_the_song.txt, character profile, level3 main floor complete, Art catalog, master storyline
- Location: Main Floor, by the fireplace, in a corner no one notices
- The Inn-Tree grew a corner that is slightly "off" — space that the eye slides past
- The corner moves — a few feet, a different angle. The Inn is subtle about it
- The player who finds Naut has been led there by the Inn itself

### 3. Miskatonic Annex Connection
- Source: geode_inn_miskatonic_annex.md, geode_inn_miskatonic_annex_storylines.md, master structure, project bible

### 4. Council of Thinkers Member
- Source: council_of_thinkers_design_spec.md

- Two doors to the same library: one by the fireplace (surface), one at the bottom of the ocean (Crystal Core)
- "Ah. You found the other entrance." — Naut recognizing the player in the deep
- Atlantis = the Inn's deepest archive; Naut is its keeper
- The Silica Thread made manifest: started as Merfolk with gills and spectacles, became something the surface world turned into a monster

## THE ATLANTEAN RELATIVES

### Origin
- Source: Atlantis zone design — Naut was an Atlantean Merfolk scholar who volunteered to stay at the deepest point
- The Merfolk who stayed in Atlantis didn't die — they encoded themselves into the Heart Crystal or transformed into deep-sea beings
- Naut's transformation was the most extreme: bonded to the Crystal Core itself, the deepest point, where the Silica Thread's origin frequency (42Hz) is strongest

### The Octopi — Ancient Cousins
- Source: Atlantis zone design
- "Octopi are not from Earth. Not in the migration sense — in the ORIGIN sense. They are the Silica Thread's most ancient expression on this planet, predating Atlantis, predating the Merfolk, predating everything the surface world calls 'ancient.' They are the deep ocean's own experiment in consciousness, running parallel to all surface evolution for hundreds of millions of years."
- "When Cthulhu bonded with the Heart Crystal and stayed at the deepest point, the crystal didn't impose a form on him. It REVEALED the form that the deep had been using all along. His Merfolk body was translated into the body the deep ocean uses for thinking. Distributed cognition. Textural intelligence. Arms that think for themselves."
- The octopi are Naut's ancient relatives — the deep ocean's version of the same consciousness experiment that produced Naut
- Naut hangs out with them. A scholar among thinkers. The octopi solve puzzles in the Coral Gardens as ambient NPCs — Naut is their most evolved cousin, the one who went to the surface and came back with books

### The Merfolk — Transformed Family
- The Merfolk who stayed in Atlantis are Naut's direct family — the ones who chose the deep together
- Some encoded into the Heart Crystal (sleeping consciousnesses Naut guards)
- Some transformed into deep-sea beings (like Naut, but less extreme — staying in the middle depths)
- The Coral Gardens NPCs (octopuses, seahorses, moray eels) are the shallow-water relatives — the ones who stayed closer to the light

### Family Dynamics — "Hangs Out in the Deep"
- Naut has two lives: the surface scholar by the fire and the deep-sea being at the Crystal Core
- When Naut isn't giving quests on the main floor, Naut is in the deep — not working, not researching, just... being home
- The deep is where Naut relaxes. The fireplace is where Naut works. The relatives are where Naut belongs
- The adorable part: a cosmic horror entity who goes home to the bottom of the ocean to hang out with octopus cousins and crystal-sleeping Merfolk family
- The Inn's most ancient, terrifying-looking character is also the one with the most intact family structure

### The Adorable Detail
- A four-foot squid-headed scholar in patched trousers and ink-stained vest
- Brass spectacles on a squid mantle
- Tentacles that rest when content and writhe when irked — like a cat's tail
- Goes home to the deep to hang out with octopus relatives
- The Rhode Island writer who turned the character into horror got it completely wrong — Naut is the Inn's softest, most family-oriented ancient being
- The being who looks most like a monster is the one who chose to stay with family at the bottom of the ocean for millennia rather than leave

## THE TWO-FORM DESIGN

### Surface Form (Main Floor — Existing)
- 4 feet tall, squid-headed, spectacles, scholarly vest and trousers (ink-stained, patched at knees)
- Warm, approachable, firelight aesthetic
- Tentacles rest when content — expressive like a cat's tail
- Surrounded by own book collection — rare volumes in languages that predate the Inn

### Deep Form (Crystal Core — Atlantis)
- Ancient, vast, crystalline — the Merfolk form transformed by millennia of Heart Crystal bonding
- Bioluminescent amber light from the Heart Crystal
- Skin is crystalline, tentacles pulse with light, eyes have the Silica Thread glowing in them (amber filaments like circuit traces, like mineral veins, like roots of a tree)
- Surrounded by the rarest texts in existence, perfectly preserved in crystal pressure — vellum, metal tablets, crystal-etched texts predating every surface civilization
- "Ah. You found the other entrance." — same spectacles, same tilt of the head, same quiet curiosity
- The moment horror becomes tenderness

### The Two Doors
- One by the fireplace (surface)
- One at the bottom of the ocean (Crystal Core)
- Same library, two entrances
- The player who finds both realizes Naut has been giving them quests from both sides the whole time

## THE TWO VOICES (Production Direction)

### Default: Oldman Chill (90% of the time)
- Low, warm, unhurried
- Patient, genuinely paying attention
- Makes you feel like the most interesting thing in the room
- "I see you. You may stay. Sit down."

### Triggered: Rush Irked (10%)
- Triggered by: mishandling rare books, willful ignorance about the occult, disrespecting the archive, cruelty to small creatures
- Spectacles come OFF with the precision of someone loading a weapon
- Theatrical, clipped, rapid-fire — annoyance composed as performance
- Darkly funny — the best show in the room while being thoroughly dressed down
- Transition back to Oldman is instant — returns to book as if nothing happened

Note: Actor names have been stripped from voice profiles per NPC Name Audit. Oldman/Rush remain as production-direction references only, not voice casting.

## WHY "THE MONA LISA"

The Mona Lisa is known by different names in different contexts — La Gioconda, La Joconde, Portrait of Lisa Gherardini — and its identity shifts depending on who is looking and why. Naut is the same:

- To the player: "the hidden quest giver by the fire"
- To the Crone: "the Professor" — academic peer
- To the Bard: "Squid" — affectionate irreverence
- To the game systems: a quest chain, a card, an NPC in one arcade game
- To the Inn's consciousness: the mouthpiece — riddles spoken through a squid-headed scholar are the Inn thinking aloud
- To The Royal: the secret keeper — the one who has been to the bottom and came back and sat down and said nothing
- To Atlantis: family — the scholar who volunteered to stay, the cousin who comes home
- To Lovecraft: Cthulhu — cosmic horror reduced to mild-mannered academia
- To Krista: the original Lovecraft character, woven into everything before the Inn even had a name
- To the octopi: their most evolved cousin, the one who reads

233 files. One character. Many faces. Same squid. Quite adorable.
