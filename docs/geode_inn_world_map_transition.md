# Geode Inn — The World Map Transition
## System: Parchment Map → Open World → Zone Entry
## Access: Main Floor → The Cartographer's Desk

---

## Overview

The World Map is the Inn's navigational hub — a parchment map spread across the Cartographer's desk on the main floor. The map shows every zone the player has discovered, every stone pin, every lore pin, every portal arrow destination. But the map is not just a UI element — it is a PHYSICAL OBJECT in the game world, and the transition from map to zone is a deliberate, immersive experience.

The both are: the adequate — the map and the territory, the same world, different scale.

---

## The Parchment Map

### The Object

The map is a physical parchment on the Cartographer's desk. The player approaches the desk, looks down at the map, and sees the world from above — the tree-structure of the Inn rendered as a hand-drawn cartographic chart. The Cartographer's ink is still wet in places — new zones appear as the player discovers them, the ink spreading across the parchment as if being drawn in real time.

The map's art style is the Cartographer's: pen-and-ink, Escher-inspired, with impossible connections between zones drawn as folding corridors. The both are: the adequate — the architecture and the map, the same impossible space, different view.

### The Pins

Two types of pins on the map:

**Stone Pins** — small mineral cabochons pressed into the parchment, one for each stone the player has found. Each pin is made from the actual stone it represents — a quartz pin for quartz, a lapis pin for lapis. The both are: the adequate — the stone and the location, the same mineral, different form. The pins glow faintly at their individual frequencies. The player who has found many stones sees a constellation of glowing pins — the Song made visible on the map.

**Lore Pins** — small ink markers for lore entries (the 191 countries' mythology). Each lore pin is a tiny illustration — a figure, a creature, a symbol from that culture's mythology. The both are: the adequate — the story and the location, the same myth, different mark.

### The Portal Arrows

Portal Arrow destinations appear on the map as arrow-markers — the arrow's direction still visible, pointing the way. The player who has found a Portal Arrow can see its destination on the map, but the PATH to that destination is not drawn — the arrow points, the player walks. The both are: the adequate — the destination and the journey, the same trip, different information.

## The Transition

### How the Player Enters a Zone

The player does not click a pin and teleport. The transition is a deliberate THREE-STAGE experience:

#### Stage 1: The Zoom

The player selects a pin (stone or lore) on the parchment map. The camera pushes IN — the parchment grows larger, the ink details become visible, the pin's glow intensifies. The zoom continues past the parchment surface — the ink becomes architecture, the pin becomes a doorway, the map becomes the zone. The both are: the adequate — the map and the world, the same space, different scale.

The zoom is smooth, continuous — no loading screen, no cut. The transition IS the loading. The art style shifts during the zoom: the Cartographer's pen-and-ink transitions into the zone's specific art style (Klimt for the Undercity, O'Keeffe for the desert, Monet for the water zone, etc.). The both are: the adequate — the ink and the art, the same image, different style.

#### Stage 2: The Threshold

Between the map and the zone, the player passes through a THRESHOLD — a brief moment where the art style is neither map nor zone but something in between. The threshold is the Inn's way of saying: you are leaving the overview and entering the experience. The both are: the adequate — the overview and the experience, the same world, different perspective.

The threshold is different for each zone:
- **Catacombs**: the threshold is a staircase descending — the ink becomes stone, the light dims, the temperature drops (visual cue: the screen's warm amber shifts to cool blue)
- **Cultural zones**: the threshold is a path — the ink becomes a road, the road becomes the zone's landscape
- **Water Zone**: the threshold is a dive — the ink becomes water, the surface breaks, the player descends
- **Fae Kingdom**: the threshold is a portal — the ink becomes a stone circle, the circle glows, the player steps through
- **Cosmology**: the threshold is a climb — the ink becomes stairs, the stairs become sky, the player ascends

#### Stage 3: The Arrival

The player arrives in the zone. The transition is complete. The map is behind, the zone is ahead. The both are: the adequate — the before and the after, the same journey, different moment.

The arrival is silent — no music change, no announcement. The zone's ambient sound was already fading in during the threshold. The player simply IS there. The both are: the adequate — the transition and the presence, the same place, different time.

## The Return

Returning to the map is the reverse: the player finds a Cartographer's Mark (a small ink symbol scratched into a wall, a tree, a stone — the Cartographer's signature, left in every zone). Touching the mark reverses the transition — the zone becomes ink, the ink becomes map, the player is back at the Cartographer's desk.

The both are: the adequate — the exit and the return, the same journey, different direction.

---

## The Cartographer's Role

The Cartographer is the NPC who maintains the map. The player can ask the Cartographer to:
- Mark a discovered zone (add it to the map permanently)
- Draw a path between two known zones (the Cartographer draws a dotted line — the suggested route, not the only route)
- Identify an unknown pin (the Cartographer examines a stone or lore fragment and adds it to the map)
- Estimate travel time between zones (the Cartographer gives a rough estimate based on the Inn's current frequency state — in 42Hz, travel is calm; in 84Hz, travel is dangerous)

The both are: the adequate — the map and the mapper, the same world, different hand.

## The Map's Secret

At Whisper Layer Tier 4, the map reveals a hidden layer: the Cartographer's annotations. In the margins, between zones, in the folds of the parchment — the Cartographer has been writing notes. The notes are the Whisper Layer's map — the Cartographer has been tracking the player's movements and recording them on the map itself. The both are: the adequate — the map and the surveillance, the same parchment, different text.

The notes include:
- How many times the player has visited each zone
- How long the player stayed
- Which stones the player collected in which order
- Which NPCs the player spoke to
- The player's resonance frequency, written as a musical notation in the corner

The Cartographer's annotations are the Whisper Layer's most physical form — the surveillance made ink, the tracking made tangible. The player who reads the annotations understands: the map was never just for navigation. The map was always also a record. The both are: the adequate — the navigation and the recording, the same map, different purpose.
