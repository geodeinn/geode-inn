# Geode Inn — The Canopy of Sála: Kelethin-Inspired Zone

## Overview

The Canopy of Sála is a tree-platform city zone inspired by Kelethin from EverQuest — a sprawling settlement built on wooden platforms high in the canopy of enormous ancient trees, connected by narrow beams with no railings. Travel requires faith in the wood and steady nerves. The fall is real. The beauty is worth the fear.

## Name Origin

**Sála** — from the Proto-Indo-European root *sel-* (meaning "dwelling, habitation" and also "to take refuge"). The word carries both meanings: it is a home, and it is a sanctuary. The Canopy of Sála is the home of the Canopy Elves — a people who chose to live in the dangerous heights because the view of the stars is unobstructed and the crystals grow thickest where the branches meet the sky.

## Location in the Inn

The Canopy of Sála sits on the **World Map** as a standalone cultural region, accessible from the Celtic/Druidic zone cluster. It bridges the downstairs (earthly regions) and the upstairs (cosmology). To reach it, you climb the Great Tree of Sála — a vertical traversal sequence through the trunk that serves as the zone's entrance corridor.

The Canopy also connects to the **Fae Kingdom** — the Seelie Court's Summerlands border the Canopy's lower platforms. The Fae and the Canopy Elves have an ancient alliance. Seelie NPCs can be found on the lower platforms, trading and gossiping.

## Visual Design

### Architecture
- Platforms of aged golden wood, built across the branches of trees so large their trunks are the width of buildings
- No railings. Ever. Anywhere. This is the point.
- Beams connecting platforms range from:
  - **Main beams** (2 planks wide, stable, the "safe" paths)
  - **Side beams** (1 plank wide, gently swaying, the "faith paths")
  - **Branch walks** (literally just a tree branch — no planks, bark underfoot, the "true faith paths")
- Buildings on platforms: small, cozy, warm-lit. Taverns, shops, a map gazebo, an observatory, a crystal workshop
- Lanterns hang from branches — warm amber light, gently swaying
- Crystals grow from the bark where branches meet platforms — the Silica Thread reaches the canopy
- Rope bridges exist on the main paths (still no railings, but the rope sides give psychological comfort — a luxury the side beams don't offer)

### The Drop
- Below the platforms, far below, the forest floor is visible through gaps in the canopy
- A soft green mist obscures the ground — you can see enough to know it's far, not enough to see what's down there
- The mist is warm, not ominous. It's beautiful. The danger is the height, not what's at the bottom.
- Falling objects (leaves, loose crystals) drift down slowly — giving you a sense of just how high you are

### Lighting (Epilepsy-Safe)
- Warm amber throughout — dappled sunlight through leaves
- Lantern light gently sways (slow sine-wave motion, 0.3 Hz)
- No sudden light changes. The canopy is perpetually in golden hour.
- Crystal formations catch the light and create soft prismatic rainbows on the wood — static, not flashing
- Night cycle (if implemented): lanterns become the primary light source, moonlight filters through leaves, crystals glow with their own internal light. The beams are harder to see at night, making night travel more dangerous and more beautiful.

### The Trees
The Great Trees of Sála are not background — they are characters:
- **Sála-Mother** — the central tree, oldest and largest. The main platforms are built in her branches. Her trunk is wide enough to contain rooms (the entrance corridor climbs through her hollow trunk).
- **The Twin Sentinels** — two trees flanking Sála-Mother, their branches interlocking with hers. The Seelie trading post sits between them.
- **The Watcher** — a tree on the eastern edge of the canopy, its highest branch holding a small observatory platform. The Owl is sometimes seen here at night.
- **The Singing Tree** — a tree whose leaves chime in the wind. Each leaf is a thin crystal formation. The wind creates the zone's ambient music — a living Kelethin theme.

## The Beam Mechanic

### Core Design
- Walking on beams is a balance mechanic — the player uses the analog stick (or arrow keys) to maintain center of gravity
- Main beams (2 planks): very forgiving. You'd have to actively walk off the edge.
- Side beams (1 plank): moderate challenge. The beam sways slightly. Strong winds (weather events) make it sway more.
- Branch walks (bare branch): high challenge. The branch is round, not flat. Rain makes it slippery. Wind makes it sway significantly. Crystal formations on the bark can be stepped on for handholds (brief stability points).

### The Fall
- **You don't die.** The Inn's core design principle: no death, no game-over.
- When you fall from the canopy, you enter a **Falling State**:
  - The screen softens — motion blur, time dilation, the amber particle effect intensifies
  - The music swells — the Kelethin motif plays in full orchestration as you fall
  - You hear wind, then birdsong, then forest sounds
  - You land on the forest floor below — not with impact, but caught. The forest catches you. (The Great Tree's roots cushion the fall — a visual of amber light pulsing through the root network as you land.)
  - You "forget" something — the Inn's caught mechanic. You lose a small amount of progress or a minor item. Not enough to punish, enough to make you careful.
  - A spiral staircase of roots grows from the forest floor, carrying you back up to the lowest platform. The climb back up takes 30-60 seconds — time to reflect on your hubris.

### The Newbie Death
- First-time visitors to the Canopy who fall from the highest platforms get a special "First Fall" moment:
  - The fall takes longer (more time dilation)
  - The Kelethin motif plays in its entirety
  - When you land, a Canopy Elf NPC is waiting at the base of the root staircase. They say: *"First time? Don't worry. Everyone falls. The trees always catch you. ...Eventually."*
  - You receive a small crystal shard — a **Sála Welcome Stone** — as a memento of your first fall. It has no properties. It's just a reminder.
  - Achievement unlocked: "Blind Faith"

### Weather Effects on Beams
- **Clear weather** (default): normal beam difficulty
- **Gentle rain** (common): beams become slippery. Branch walks become very dangerous. Main beams are fine. Visual: raindrops catch the amber light, everything glows softly wet.
- **Wind** (uncommon): beams sway more. Branch walks sway significantly. A wind chime sound from the Singing Tree intensifies. The Canopy Elves say the wind is the trees talking.
- **Crystal Bloom** (rare event): crystals on the trees bloom with light, illuminating even the narrowest beams. During a Crystal Bloom, all beams are easier to see but the light is distracting. A trade-off.
- **Starfall** (nighttime, rare): meteor shower visible from the canopy. All Canopy Elves gather on the observatory platform. Beams are dark and dangerous but the sky is breathtaking. The ultimate "is the view worth the risk" moment.

## The Canopy Elves

### Culture
- Elves who chose the canopy over the ground. Not because they can't live on the ground — because the stars are closer here.
- Their philosophy: *trust the wood, not the railing.* A railing says "I expect you to fall." The wood says "I believe you can walk."
- They are master crystal workers — the only culture that harvests crystals from living trees without harming them
- They are friendly, not haughty. They'll laugh at your first fall and help you back up. They remember their own.
- They trade in **Canopy Crystals** — stones that only grow in the upper branches, fed by starlight rather than earth

### NPCs
- **Sála-Wren** — the Beamkeeper. An ancient elf who maintains the beams. Sits on the narrowest beam in the canopy, repairing it with crystal tools. Will teach you beam-walking if you ask. Will also casually mention she hasn't fallen in 400 years.
- **Old Oak** — tavern keeper on the main platform. Named for the wood his tavern is built from (not his species — he's an elf, he just thinks it's funny). Sells Canopy Mead (restores focus, making beams easier for 5 minutes). Tells stories of famous falls.
- **Linden** — a young Canopy Elf who is afraid of heights. She lives on the lowest platform and has never climbed higher. She's the zone's shopkeeper — her shop is the safest building in the Canopy. She'll trade Canopy Crystals for stones from other zones. She's also the only NPC who warns you about the beams without a smile.
- **The Windreader** — an elf who sits on the observatory platform with the Owl. Reads weather patterns. Can tell you when wind events are coming. Charges one stone per forecast. Will not guarantee accuracy.

### Connection to the Owl
The Owl is frequently seen on the Watcher Tree's observatory platform at night. The Canopy Elves consider the Owl a guardian — "wherever an owl is sighted, one of us is near" takes on literal meaning here. The Owl watches the beam walkers. It does not help them. It does not warn them. It watches, and if they make it across, it blinks slowly — the owl equivalent of a nod.

## Stones of the Canopy

The Canopy drops unique stones not available from any other zone:

1. **Starlight Quartz** — clear quartz that has absorbed starlight for centuries. Glows faintly at night. Found on the highest platforms.
2. **Canopy Amber** — amber formed from the Great Trees' sap, trapped high in the branches. Warmer and clearer than ground amber.
3. **Wind Chime Crystal** — a thin, fragile crystal that resonates in the wind. Used by the Singing Tree. Very rare.
4. **Sála Welcome Stone** — given to every visitor on their first fall. No properties. Just a memory.
5. **The Beamwalker's Stone** — awarded for crossing 10 branch walks without falling. A small crystal that fits in the palm — warm to touch, said to steady your balance. (Actually does provide a minor beam-walking buff.)
6. **The Starfall Stone** — awarded for being on the observatory platform during a Starfall event. Glows with a faint meteor-trail pattern. Rarest stone in the zone.

## Zone Quests

### Main Quest: The Singing Tree
The Singing Tree's crystal leaves are falling — one by one, they detach and drift to the forest floor. Without them, the tree goes silent, and the Canopy loses its music. The player must:
1. Climb to the Singing Tree (branch walks, increasing difficulty)
2. Investigate why the leaves are falling (a crystalline parasite — small, not violent, more like a fungus that feeds on crystal resonance)
3. Collect fallen crystal leaves from the forest floor (descending to the ground for the first time in the quest)
4. Return them to the Singing Tree — they must be reattached by hand, one at a time, while balancing on a branch walk
5. Each leaf reattached adds a note back to the zone's ambient music
6. When all leaves are reattached, the Kelethin motif plays in full — the zone's music is complete
7. Reward: Wind Chime Crystal + Beamwalker's Stone

### Side Quest: Linden's Ascent
Linden has never climbed above the lowest platform. She wants to see the stars from the observatory but is terrified. The player must:
1. Walk every beam with Linden following behind (she won't walk alone)
2. The player goes first on each beam — demonstrating it's safe
3. At each platform, Linden gains a little confidence (visual: her posture straightens)
4. The final stretch is a branch walk to the observatory — Linden freezes
5. The player must cross first, then call back to her. The Owl is watching.
6. If Linden crosses, she sees the stars and cries (subtle, not sappy — one line of dialogue)
7. Reward: Canopy Amber + a unique dialogue where Linden says the player's name in the credits... no, that's too much. She just gives you a stone and says "thank you" and means it.

### Side Quest: The Windreader's Storm
A major wind event is coming — the worst in a century. The Windreader needs help securing the platforms. The player must:
1. Deliver crystal anchors to 5 platforms (platforming challenge in high wind)
2. During the storm, escort NPCs back to the main platform
3. The storm peaks — all beams sway violently, visibility drops, the music becomes intense (the only time the Canopy music creates tension)
4. After the storm, the Canopy is intact. The Windreader was right about the timing.
5. Reward: Starlight Quartz + permanent wind resistance buff on beams

## The Kelethin Connection

### Music
The Canopy's ambient music is a **direct homage to Kelethin**, following Jay Barbeau's compositional principles:
- Built on sixths (Chopin-inspired intervals)
- Cyclical melody that "brings you home"
- Played by the Singing Tree's crystal leaves — it's diegetic music, not background music
- The full motif plays only when all leaves are attached (main quest completion)
- Before that, the music is incomplete — missing notes, gaps in the melody
- This gives the player a musical reason to complete the quest, not just a narrative one

When the player falls, the Kelethin motif swells in full orchestration — the fall is accompanied by beauty, not horror. This is the intentional emotional design: the danger is real, but the world is kind. You fall through music.

### Architecture
- Platform layout inspired by Kelethin's organic sprawl — no grid, no symmetry, paths that make sense only if you're a tree elf
- The "newbie death" experience is preserved but softened — you don't die, you forget
- The first fall is a rite of passage, not a punishment
- The narrow beams with no railings are the zone's defining feature — they create a unique kind of tension that no combat can replicate
- The forest floor below is accessible (via falling or via the root staircase) and contains its own content — fallen crystal leaves, ground-level creatures, the base of the Great Trees

## Integration with the Inn

### Leaderboard
- No arcade game in this zone — the beam walking IS the game
- A "Canopy Crossing" leaderboard tracks: fastest time across all branch walks, longest time without falling, most falls in one visit (a "Persistence" board — falling a lot is celebrated, not shamed)
- Pair mode: "Tethered Crossing" — two players must cross beams together. If one falls, the other can catch them (if fast enough) or must continue alone. Combined time is the pair score.

### Connection to Other Zones
- **Celtic/Druidic zones** — the Canopy is accessed through these regions. The druids know the Great Trees.
- **Fae Kingdom (Seelie Court)** — the Summerlands border the lower platforms. Seelie NPCs trade here.
- **Cosmology (Upstairs)** — the observatory platform is the closest surface point to the cosmology level. On clear nights, you can see Jupiter. The Owl watches.
- **Crystal Pinball** — a Canopy-themed pinball table is a future possibility. Bumpers are crystal leaves. The ball is a falling crystal leaf. Multiball is "Leaf Fall."

## Future Expansion

- **Under-Canopy** — the forest floor below the platforms. A separate sub-zone with ground-level exploration, the roots of the Great Trees, and creatures that live in the mist.
- **The Canopy in Winter** — a seasonal event where ice forms on the beams. Maximum difficulty. The Singing Tree's leaves become ice chimes. The music shifts to a colder, crystalline version of the motif.
- **The Highest Platform** — a platform so high it's above the cloud line. Only accessible during a Starfall event. The view is the entire Geode Inn world map, laid out below you like the parchment map. The Owl is always there.
