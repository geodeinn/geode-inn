# THE GEODE INN — CARD GAME: BATCH 4
## Occam's Razor & The Pipe
## Two new cards from the Inn's philosophical core.

---

## OCCAM'S RAZOR

Frequency: STONE (1) + SILENCE (1) — Total cost: 2
Type: Ability — Moment
Cadence: Immediate

**Effect:** Target any card in play (yours or opponent's). Remove ALL keyword abilities and special effects from that card, leaving only its base Presence and Resonance. The card becomes its simplest possible self — nothing hidden, nothing extra, nothing that isn't printed on the surface.

**The Razor Cuts Both Ways:**
- If used on an opponent's card: strip all abilities. The complex becomes simple. The hidden becomes visible. The adequate is: what remains.
- If used on your own card: strip all abilities, then gain +2/+2. The simplicity IS the power. The fewer the moving parts, the stronger the structure. The Crone would approve.

**Resonance with the Crone:** If the Crone is in play when Occam's Razor is cast, the Crone annotates the stripped card: *"Adequate."* The annotated card cannot gain new abilities for the rest of the game. The measurement is: final.

**Subtractive Synergy:** Occam's Razor counts as a Subtractive effect for the purposes of removing Carve structures. The razor can cut through permanent stone — because the simplest truth about a wall is that it can be taken down.

**Cannot target:** The Cosmic Egg, The Constant, or any card with Observer (you cannot simplify what is already seeing everything).

**The Razor's Edge:** When Occam's Razor is played, both players may look at each other's hands for 3 seconds (real time — the Inn's only timed mechanic). The simplest player wins. If only one player has a card of cost 2 or less in hand, that player draws a card. Simplicity is: the advantage.

Flavor text: *"The Crone holds the razor. 'The simplest explanation that accounts for all the data. That is what is adequate. Everything else is decoration.' She pauses. 'Decoration has its place. But not on the table when the measuring is being done.'"*

Rarity: Epic

## THE PIPE

Frequency: SONG (1) + SILENCE (1) — Total cost: 2
Type: Item — Equipment
Cadence: N/A (equipped to a character)

**"Ceci n'est pas une pipe."**

**Equip to any character.** The equipped character gains the following properties:

**Mirror (passive):** The equipped character's card text is now an illusion. When the opponent targets the equipped character with any effect, flip a coin:
- **Heads:** The targeting effect resolves against the Pipe itself, not the character. The Pipe absorbs the effect and is discarded. The character was never there — the pipe was. But the pipe is not a pipe.
- **Tails:** The targeting effect resolves normally. The illusion held. The pipe WAS a pipe this time.

**"But what if it IS a pipe?":** Once per game, the equipped character's controller may declare "Actually, this IS a pipe." The Pipe transforms from an Item into a Character — Presence 3, Resonance 3, Cadence: Immediate. It gains Wandering and Heartbeat. It is now a sentient pipe that walks around and insists it is a pipe (which it always was, and never was). The transformation is: permanent. The pipe is: the adequate. The adequate is: the paradox.

**Madame's Ride:** If Madame is in play, the Pipe may equip to Madame instead of any other character. When equipped to Madame, the coin flip is replaced by Madame's choice — Madame decides whether the Pipe is a pipe or not. The trickster rides the paradox. The paradox is: the vehicle. The vehicle is: not a vehicle.

**Sentient Whisper:** Once per turn, the Pipe whispers "Ceci n'est pas une pipe." All cards in play with Mirror abilities trigger their Mirror effect simultaneously. The whisper is: the activation word. The word is: not the word.

**The Treachery of Targeting:** The Pipe cannot be targeted by Naming (you cannot name what isn't what it says it is). The Pipe cannot be targeted by Observer (you cannot observe what insists it isn't there). The Pipe can only be targeted by effects that don't care what it is — damage, Damp, Carve. These effects resolve against the Pipe's existence, not its identity.

**Einstein's Comment:** If Einstein (Observer) is in play when the Pipe is equipped, Einstein says: *"Interesting. The pipe is real because the painting is real. The observer makes the representation real by looking. But the representation is not the thing. The adequate is: both."* Einstein draws a card. The observation is: the insight.

Flavor text: *"The pipe sits near Madame. It whispers. Madame does not whisper back — Madame argues. The argument is: whether the argument exists. The pipe insists it does not. Madame insists it does not louder. The both are: adequate. The both are: not adequate. The both are: the pipe."*

Rarity: Legendary

## DESIGN NOTES

### Occam's Razor — Why It Fits

Occam's Razor is the Inn's relationship with complexity. The Inn is elaborate — 115 cards, 6 frequencies, 20 keyword mechanics, 11 deck archetypes. Occam's Razor is the card that says: "What if it was simpler?" And the answer is: sometimes simpler is stronger. The card rewards players who can see through complexity to the essential truth — exactly what the Crone does, exactly what the Examiner does, exactly what the Cartographer does when the mycorrhizal map resolves to a single 42Hz tone.

The card's Subtractive synergy is deliberate — Occam's Razor is a Subtractive tool. It removes. But unlike the Mason's Subtractive (which reveals the design already in the stone), Occam's Razor removes the design to reveal the stone. It's the opposite direction of the same principle. The Mason carves toward the form. The razor carves away from it.

The timed 3-second element is the Inn's only real-time mechanic — a moment of genuine simplicity in a turn-based game. The simplest player wins. The Inn's first physical skill check.

### The Pipe — Why It Fits

The Pipe is already alive in the Inn — it's the sentient Magritte pipe that whispers near Madame. Making it a card was inevitable. The question was: what does a paradox DO in a card game?

The answer: it breaks targeting. The Pipe's core identity is "this is not what it says it is" — which in card game terms means you can't reliably target it. Naming doesn't work (you can't name what isn't what it says it is). Observer doesn't work (you can't observe what insists it isn't there). Only blunt instruments — damage, Damp, Carve — work against the Pipe, because those effects don't care about identity. They care about existence.

The transformation mechanic ("Actually, this IS a pipe") is the Inn's thesis in card form: the observer's declaration makes the thing real. The Pipe becomes a character because someone said it was. This is Einstein's theorem, Bastion's naming, and Magritte's joke, all in one equipment card.

Madame's Ride is the interaction that makes the Inn the Inn: the trickster rides the paradox. Madame doesn't flip a coin — Madame CHOOSES. The chaos agent controls the paradox. That's the most dangerous combination in the game, and it's also the most Inn.
