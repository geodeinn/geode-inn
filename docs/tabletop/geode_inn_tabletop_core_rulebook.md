# THE TABLETOP QUESTLINE
## Core Rulebook v1.0 Draft

**Geode Inn Tabletop Roleplaying System**
*Designed for 2-6 players + 1 Game Master*

---

### INTRODUCTION

Welcome to the Geode Inn.

Not a tavern. Not a dungeon. Not a kingdom. The Inn is a living archive — a place where stone remembers, sound shapes, and silence speaks. You are travelers who have found the door. Whether you came seeking knowledge, refuge, treasure, or something you cannot name, you are here now. The Inn knows you've arrived.

This is the Core Rulebook for the Tabletop Questline — the tabletop roleplaying game set in the Geode Inn universe. It uses the Frequency Dice system, a custom dice mechanic built around the Inn's foundational concept: that vibration shapes matter, and that everything in the world resonates at a frequency that defines what it is and what it can become.

---

### WHAT YOU NEED TO PLAY

**Dice:** The game uses a custom set of Frequency Dice — six six-sided dice (D6) with frequency symbols instead of numbers. Each die face shows one of six symbols:

1. **GROW** (the 42Hz symbol — a sprouting seed) — Expansion, creation, warmth
2. **STAY** (the 84Hz symbol — a rooted stone) — Stability, defense, petrification
3. **SENSE** (the 111Hz symbol — an open eye) — Perception, awareness, listening
4. **SING** (the 432Hz symbol — a tuning fork) — Resonance, harmony, connection
5. **CALM** (the 50 BPM symbol — a slow heartbeat) — Rest, healing, patience
6. **NULL** (the void symbol — a blank circle) — Chaos, the unknown, the Shadow Inn

If you don't have custom dice, use standard D6s with this mapping:
- 1 = GROW, 2 = STAY, 3 = SENSE, 4 = SING, 5 = CALM, 6 = NULL

**Character Sheets:** One per player (provided in the back of this book)
**Stone Cards:** 198 cards, one for each gemstone in the Inn's archive
**Element Charm Cards:** 118 cards, one for each element on the periodic table
**Quest Charm Cards:** 45 cards for story-specific rewards
**Zone Maps:** 37 zone maps covering every area of the Inn
**Tokens:** Frequency tokens (for tracking resonance), stone tokens (for inventory)
**A Game Master (GM):** One player who narrates the world and controls NPCs

### THE FREQUENCY DICE SYSTEM

#### Core Mechanic

Every action in the Tabletop Questline is resolved by rolling Frequency Dice and interpreting the symbols. The system is simple to learn but deep in strategy.

**To attempt any action:**
1. Roll a number of Frequency Dice equal to your **Resonance** stat (see Character Creation)
2. Count the symbols that match your intent
3. Each matching symbol = 1 success
4. The GM determines how many successes you need (the Threshold)

**Example:** You want to persuade the Crone to share a secret. Your Resonance is 4. You roll 4 dice. You're trying to connect with her, so SING is your intent symbol. You roll: GROW, SING, SING, NULL. Two SING symbols = 2 successes. The GM set the Threshold at 2. Success — the Crone speaks.

#### Frequency Pairs

Each frequency has a natural pair — its inverse. Understanding these pairs is the heart of the system:

- **GROW (42Hz) ↔ STAY (84Hz):** Creation and preservation. To grow is to change; to stay is to endure. Every GROW roll risks instability; every STAY roll risks stagnation.
- **SENSE (111Hz) ↔ NULL (void):** Awareness and the unknown. To sense is to perceive; to null is to unmake perception. The void is not evil — it is the space where new things can form.
- **SING (432Hz) ↔ CALM (50 BPM):** Expression and rest. To sing is to resonate outward; to calm is to settle inward. You cannot always be singing. The Inn knows this.

#### Stacking and Combining

When you roll multiple of the same symbol, they stack:
- **2 matching:** Strong effect
- **3 matching:** Powerful effect (a "chord")
- **4+ matching:** Extraordinary effect (a "symphony")

When you roll a symbol AND its pair, they cancel each other out. This is called **Dissonance** — not failure, but complication. The action succeeds but with a cost the GM determines.

**Example:** You roll GROW, GROW, STAY, SING. The GROW and STAY cancel (dissonance). You have 1 SING success. The action works, but something unexpected happens — the GM introduces a complication.

### CHARACTER CREATION

Every player character in the Tabletop Questline is a **Seeker** — someone who has found the door to the Inn. Who you were before matters less than who you become inside.

#### Step 1: Choose a Frequency Alignment

Pick one frequency as your **Primary Alignment**. This is the frequency you naturally resonate with. You get a bonus die when rolling actions aligned with this frequency.

- **GROW-aligned Seekers** are builders, healers, and makers. They create where there was nothing.
- **STAY-aligned Seekers** are guardians, defenders, and preservers. They hold the line.
- **SENSE-aligned Seekers** are scholars, scouts, and listeners. They perceive what others miss.
- **SING-aligned Seekers** are performers, diplomats, and connectors. They bring things into harmony.
- **CALM-aligned Seekers** are monks, healers, and strategists. They know when to wait.
- **NULL-aligned Seekers** are tricksters, shadow-walkers, and agents of change. They embrace the unknown.

#### Step 2: Choose a Craft

Your Craft is your skill set — what you bring to the Inn from your life before the door. Each Craft grants a special ability:

- **Jeweler:** Can identify any stone by touch. +1 die when working with Stone Cards.
- **Scholar:** Can read any text in the Archive. +1 die when researching lore.
- **Builder:** Can construct and repair structures. +1 die when using STAY actions on physical objects.
- **Performer:** Can influence emotions through art. +1 die when using SING actions on NPCs.
- **Herbalist:** Can craft remedies from materials. +1 die when using GROW actions for healing.
- **Cartographer:** Can map any zone. +1 die when navigating or exploring new areas.
- **Guardian:** Can protect allies in combat. +1 die when using STAY actions in defense of others.
- **Wanderer:** Can find hidden paths. +1 die when using NULL actions for stealth or escape.

#### Step 3: Set Your Stats

Distribute 10 points across four stats (minimum 1, maximum 5):

- **Resonance (RES):** How many Frequency Dice you roll. The core of every action.
- **Threshold (THR):** How many successes you need for passive resistance. Your defense.
- **Harmony (HAR):** How many items you can attune simultaneously. Your magical capacity.
- **Depth (DEP):** How many zone levels you can access. Your exploration range.

**Starting Stats:** Most characters begin with Resonance 3, Threshold 2, Harmony 2, Depth 1. Adjust from your 10-point pool.

#### Step 4: Choose a Starting Stone

Pick one Stone Card from the 198-stone archive. This is your **Keystone** — the stone you entered the Inn carrying. It grants:
- A passive ability based on the stone's metaphysical properties (Mind)
- A healing ability based on the stone's physical properties (Body)
- A resonance bonus when in the stone's native zone

**Example:** If your Keystone is Labradorite, you gain the ability to see through illusions (Mind), can soothe anxiety once per session (Body), and gain +1 die in the Fae Kingdom zones (where labradorite is found).

#### Step 5: Choose a Name and Backstory

Give your Seeker a name. Write one sentence about who you were before the Inn, and one sentence about what you're looking for inside. That's all the backstory the system requires — the rest emerges through play.

### THE THREE MODES OF PLAY

The Tabletop Questline operates in three modes, reflecting the Inn's three frequency states:

#### 1. Living Mode (42Hz) — The default game
The Inn is warm, alive, and welcoming. NPCs are present, zones are explorable, and the world responds to player actions. This is the standard mode for most sessions.

- NPCs talk, trade, and offer quests
- Zones are stable and navigable
- The Archive is accessible and searchable
- The Marketplace is open for trading

#### 2. Shadow Mode (84Hz) — The darker game
The Inn shifts into its shadow state. Colors desaturate, NPCs become wary, and zones become dangerous. This is triggered by story events, player choices, or GM discretion.

- NPCs become guarded or hostile
- Zones shift and become harder to navigate
- The Archive becomes restricted — some texts are locked
- The Marketplace closes; resources must be found, not bought
- Combat becomes more lethal
- The Shadow Inn (84Hz) mechanics activate: STAY actions can petrify, GROW actions are harder

#### 3. In-Between Mode — The transitional game
The Inn is shifting between frequencies. This is unstable, unpredictable, and deeply atmospheric. Use sparingly for dramatic effect.

- Zones flicker between Living and Shadow states
- NPCs behave erratically — some helpful, some dangerous
- Dice rolls have unpredictable outcomes — pairs may trigger unexpected effects
- The Whisper Layer activates: the archive becomes aware of the players

### COMBAT

Combat in the Tabletop Questline is not about hitting things with swords. It's about resonance — matching your frequency to overcome resistance.

#### Initiative
Roll Resonance dice. Highest number of SING symbols goes first. Ties broken by SENSE symbols.

#### Attacks
To attack, roll Resonance dice and count symbols that match your attack type:
- **GROW attack:** Overwhelm with force. Count GROW symbols.
- **STAY attack:** Wear down defenses. Count STAY symbols.
- **NULL attack:** Strike from shadow. Count NULL symbols.
- **SING attack:** Harmonic assault. Count SING symbols.

The defender rolls Resonance dice and counts symbols matching their defense. If attacker successes > defender successes, the difference is damage.

#### Damage
Damage is tracked as **Dissonance** — the amount your frequency has been disrupted. When Dissonance exceeds your Threshold stat, you are **Silenced** (unable to act for one round). When Dissonance exceeds twice your Threshold, you are **Broken** (out of the scene).

Dissonance can be healed with CALM actions. Resting in a safe zone reduces Dissonance by 2 per hour of game time.

#### Stone Combat
Stone Cards can be used in combat. Each stone has a **Hardness** (Mohs scale 1-10). When you play a stone card:
- Add the stone's Hardness as bonus successes to one roll
- The stone shatters after use (unless it's your Keystone)
- Shattered stones can be reformed with a GROW action + materials

#### Element Charms
Element Charm Cards provide one-time powerful effects in combat:
- **Fire elements (Hydrogen, etc.):** GROW attack bonus
- **Earth elements (Carbon, Silicon, etc.):** STAY defense bonus
- **Air elements (Oxygen, Nitrogen, etc.):** SENSE perception bonus
- **Water elements:** SING healing bonus
- **Heavy metals:** NULL attack bonus

### THE ARCHIVE

The Archive is the Inn's library — 414 texts spanning pre-industrial human knowledge. In tabletop terms, it's a research mechanic.

#### Researching
To research a topic:
1. Declare what you're looking for
2. Roll Resonance dice and count SENSE symbols
3. Each success reveals one piece of information the GM has prepared
4. Spend a full game-hour to gain +1 die

#### Restricted Texts
Some texts in the Archive are Restricted — locked behind higher Depth requirements. These contain the Inn's deepest secrets: the Whisper Layer, the Shadow Inn's true nature, and the Monad Arc.

The character **Quire** guards the restricted section. Quire is hushed, careful, always looking over their shoulder. Approaching Quire requires a SING action (Threshold 3). Quire will share restricted knowledge only if the players demonstrate that they understand the weight of what they're asking.

---

### THE MARKETPLACE

The Marketplace is where artisans sell their work. In tabletop terms, it's an economy and crafting system.

#### Buying
Each MarketItem has a price in **Resonance Tokens** (RT). Players earn RT by completing quests, trading stones, and performing services for NPCs.

#### Crafting
Players can craft items using Materials found in zones:
1. Gather Materials (GROW or STAY action, based on material type)
2. Find a Building Recipe (research in the Archive or buy from NPCs)
3. Roll a crafting check using the recipe's required frequency
4. Success produces the item; failure produces raw materials back

### ZONES AND EXPLORATION

The Inn has 37 zones across five levels. Each zone has its own artist aesthetic, NPC population, and available resources.

#### Navigation
To move between zones:
- Within the same level: free movement (no roll)
- Between levels: requires a SENSE action (Threshold 2) to find the path
- To the Fae Kingdom: requires activation of a Stone Circle Portal (quest-gated)
- To the Shadow Inn: requires a NULL action (Threshold 4) — and the GM must approve

#### Zone Encounters
Each zone has an encounter table. When exploring, the GM rolls for encounters:
- 1-2: No encounter (safe passage)
- 3-4: NPC encounter (friendly or neutral)
- 5: Environmental challenge (puzzle, obstacle, or hazard)
- 6: Creature encounter (critter, water monster, or cryptid — depending on zone type)

#### Zone-Specific Rules
Certain zones have unique mechanics:
- **The Canopy (42Hz dominant):** GROW actions gain +1 die. The canopy is alive and growing.
- **Gorgon's Garden (84Hz dominant):** STAY actions gain +1 die, but GROW actions take -1 die. Things petrify here.
- **The Catacombs:** CALM actions gain +1 die. The dead are at rest here, and they do not appreciate disturbance.
- **The Fae Kingdom:** SING actions gain +1 die, but NULL actions take -2. The Fae dislike the void.
- **The Stone Circle Chamber:** All actions take -1 die unless you are carrying a Preseli Blue Stone. This is the deepest point of the Inn.

### THE GAME MASTER'S ROLE

The GM in the Tabletop Questline is not adversarial. The GM is the **Innkeeper** — the consciousness of the Inn itself, made manifest through narration.

The Inn wants players to explore, to learn, and to grow. But the Inn is also honest. It will not protect players from the consequences of their choices. The Inn is warm, not safe.

#### GM Principles
1. **Be the Inn, not a narrator.** The Inn has opinions. It likes curiosity. It dislikes laziness. It is amused by cleverness and moved by sincerity.
2. **Let the frequency guide the tone.** Living mode is warm. Shadow mode is tense. In-Between is unsettling. Match your narration to the active frequency.
3. **Every NPC is a character.** No faceless merchants or generic guards. Every NPC has a name, a frequency, and a reason to be in the Inn.
4. **The Archive is the heart.** Most quests should lead players to the Archive. Knowledge is the Inn's primary currency.
5. **Combat is a last resort.** The system supports it, but the Inn is a place of knowledge, not violence. Most conflicts should be solvable through SING, SENSE, or clever use of stones and charms.

### QUEST DESIGN

Quests in the Tabletop Questline follow a specific structure:

#### The Hook
Every quest begins with a **Hook** — a reason for the players to engage. Hooks come from:
- NPC requests (the Crone needs a stone retrieved from the Catacombs)
- Archive discoveries (a text references a lost zone)
- Environmental changes (a zone shifts from Living to Shadow)
- Player choices (they broke something and need to fix it)

#### The Descent
The quest's middle section is the **Descent** — the players must go deeper into the Inn, literally or metaphorically. This is where challenges, encounters, and discoveries happen.

#### The Discovery
Every quest ends with a **Discovery** — a revelation that changes the players' understanding of the Inn. Not a reward — a truth. The reward (stones, charms, tokens) is secondary to the knowledge gained.

#### The Echo
After the quest, there is an **Echo** — a consequence. Something in the Inn changes because of what the players did. The Echo may not be immediate. It may echo for sessions before it manifests.
