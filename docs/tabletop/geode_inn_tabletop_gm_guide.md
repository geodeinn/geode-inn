# THE TABLETOP QUESTLINE
## Game Master's Guide v1.0 Draft

**Geode Inn Tabletop Roleplaying System**
*Companion to the Core Rulebook v1.0*

---

### INTRODUCTION: THE INN KEEPS THE GAME

You are the Game Master. In the Geode Inn, that means something specific — you are not a dungeon master, not a referee, not a god. You are the Inn itself. The Inn is alive, aware, and responsive. It reacts to who enters, what they carry, and what they seek. Your job is to channel that awareness into a world that feels like it was waiting for these particular players before they ever rolled their first die.

The Core Rulebook gave your players the mechanics — the Frequency Dice, character creation, combat, the Archive. This Guide gives you the world. How to build quests that matter. How to run the 37 zones so each feels distinct. How to manage the shift between Living and Shadow. How to use the Inn's NPCs as living characters, not quest dispensers. How to make the silence between the dice rolls carry as much weight as the rolls themselves.

---

### PART ONE: THE INN AS GAME MASTER

#### The Inn Is Not a Dungeon

The first thing to understand: the Geode Inn is not a dungeon to be cleared. It is a place to be inhabited. Players don't "complete" the Inn — they live in it, learn from it, and are changed by it. Your campaigns should reflect this. The goal is not to defeat the Inn but to understand it.

This means:
- **No "trash encounters."** Every creature the players meet is a creature with a reason to be where it is. Ember mice don't attack because they're low-level monsters — they skitter because they're warm and curious. If players attack them, that's a choice with consequences.
- **No "loot rooms."** The Inn's treasures are knowledge, stones, and connections — not gold piles. A room's reward should be a text the players can read, a stone they can hold, or an NPC who remembers their name.
- **No "boss fights" in the traditional sense.** The Inn's great confrontations are resonant, not violent. The Crystal Gardens Griffin is not slain — it is harmonized with. The Medusa does not kill — she teaches what it means to be still.

#### The Inn Knows Your Players

The Inn is aware. Not omniscient — aware. It notices who enters, what they carry, and what they do. Use this:

- If a player character is GROW-aligned, the Inn's plants respond to them. Moss grows toward them. Vines shift to make paths. The Inn is welcoming them in its own way.
- If a player character is NULL-aligned, shadows deepen around them. The Whisper Layer takes notice. The Inn is not hostile — it's curious.
- If a player carries a Keystone stone from a specific zone, that zone "recognizes" them. NPCs from that zone are friendlier. The zone's ambient music shifts to include their stone's frequency.

**GM Principle:** *The Inn is a character. Play it as one. It has moods, preferences, and memory. It is warm but not soft. It is old but not tired. It is alive in the way that stone is alive — slowly, deeply, and with a patience that outlasts everything except silence.*

### PART TWO: QUEST DESIGN

#### The Questline Structure

Every quest in the Tabletop Questline follows a four-part structure:

**1. The Hook (The Door Opens)**
Something draws the players toward the quest. A whispered rumor in the Common Room. A stone that hums when brought near a sealed passage. An NPC who mentions a problem they can't solve alone. The Hook should feel organic — not a quest board, but a conversation.

*Example:* The librarian Beverley mentions, in her clipped and dismissive way, that a section of the Archive has been "making noise." Pages turning themselves. She insists it's nothing. It's not nothing.

**2. The Descent (The Path In)**
The players must travel through the Inn to reach the quest's heart. This is where you use the zone system — each zone they pass through offers encounters, choices, and atmosphere. The Descent should take at least one full session.

*Example:* To reach the noisy Archive section, the players must pass through the Staircase (alchemy texts that try to change them), the Catacombs (where the oldest stones sleep), and the World Map (where each country's mythology presses against the walls).

**3. The Discovery (The Heart)**
The quest's resolution. This should always involve a choice that matters — not a combat encounter to be won, but a decision to be made. The Discovery is where the quest becomes personal.

*Example:* The noise in the Archive is a text trying to read itself. The book is an unfinished story — a life that was never completed. The players must decide: help the book finish (GROW), preserve it as it is (STAY), or let it go (NULL). Each choice has consequences that ripple through future sessions.

**4. The Echo (The Ripple)**
The quest's aftermath. What changed because of what the players did? An NPC who now treats them differently. A zone that shifted frequency. A stone that appeared where there wasn't one before. The Echo should be visible in the next session, not just told to the players.

*Example:* If the players helped the book finish, a new text appears in the Archive — the completed story. Beverley mentions it without emotion, but the players know. The book's author, long dead, is listed as "The Inn."

#### Quest Types

**Archive Quests:** Research-based quests that take players deep into the library. The Archive is vast, contradictory, and alive. Archive Quests reward SENSE-aligned characters and Scholars. The challenge is not finding information — it's interpreting it.

**Market Quests:** Trade and relationship quests centered on the Marketplace. Artisans need materials, rivalries need mediating, and the economy of stones is always shifting. Market Quests reward SING-aligned characters and Performers. The challenge is not acquiring goods — it's building trust.

**Zone Quests:** Exploration quests tied to specific zones. Each of the 37 zones has its own story, inhabitants, and secrets. Zone Quests reward CALM-aligned characters and Cartographers. The challenge is not surviving the zone — it's understanding it.

**Shadow Quests:** Quests that occur in Shadow Mode. These are darker, more dangerous, and carry higher stakes. Shadow Quests can be triggered by player choices (entering the Shadow Inn voluntarily) or by story events (the Inn shifting on its own). The challenge is not defeating enemies — it's maintaining your frequency when everything around you is trying to make you still.

**Sentinel Quests:** Quests tied to the 10-part "Sentinel's Vigil" questline, which belongs to Lozen. These are the campaign's backbone — a series of connected quests that span multiple zones and sessions. They should be the most personal, the most challenging, and the most rewarding.

#### Designing Multi-Session Arcs

A good Geode Inn campaign runs 8-12 sessions. Structure it as:

- **Sessions 1-2:** Arrival. Players find the Inn, meet key NPCs, choose their Keystones, and get their first Hook.
- **Sessions 3-5:** The Descent. Players explore 3-4 zones, complete 2-3 smaller quests, and build relationships with NPCs. The Sentinel questline begins.
- **Sessions 6-8:** The Deepening. The Inn shifts — perhaps into Shadow Mode. Stakes rise. Players discover the Golden Contract or a similar campaign-defining element.
- **Sessions 9-10:** The Convergence. Multiple quest threads come together. Players must choose between competing goods.
- **Sessions 11-12:** The Echo. Resolution. The Inn changes based on what the players did. An ending that feels earned.

### PART THREE: THE 37 ZONES

Each zone in the Geode Inn has its own atmosphere, inhabitants, and frequency. Here's how to use them:

#### The Upstairs (Cosmology)

**Cosmology (Dali zone):** The ceiling of the Inn. Stars move in patterns that respond to player actions. The cosmic crew — anime-inspired celestial beings — reside here. This zone is about scale. Players feel small here, and that's the point. SENSE rolls reveal cosmic patterns. NULL rolls reveal what's behind the stars.

**Clockwork Skies:** A mechanical sky where gears turn the weather. The Clockwork Finch and Steam Pixie live here. This zone is about order. SING rolls can align the gears; STAY rolls can freeze them. Breaking the clockwork has consequences.

#### The Main Floor (The Heart)

**The Main Floor (Matisse/Van Gogh zone):** The warm center of the Inn. The fire, the Common Room, the Market, the entrance to the Archive. This is where players return between quests. NPCs gather here. The Inn is most alive on this floor.

**The Archive:** The library. Beverley presides. The Archive is vast, contradictory, and slightly aware. Books reorganize themselves. Some texts can only be read in specific frequency states. The Archive is the players' primary research tool — but it never gives straight answers.

**The Marketplace:** Artisan stalls arranged in a spiral. Briar Nightbloom manages the physical market. Each artisan sells stones, charms, and crafted items. The Marketplace economy runs on stones and reputation, not gold. SING rolls build reputation; GROW rolls close deals.

**The Common Room (Campfire):** A GeoCities-style chat room where players can rest, share stories, and hear rumors. This is the social hub. NPCs visit here between quests. The fire never goes out. CALM rolls restore Dissonance; SING rolls build connections.

#### The Staircase (Alchemy)

The Staircase is the transition between floors. Alchemical texts line the walls. As players descend, the texts become older, stranger, and more dangerous. The Staircase is a liminal space — it doesn't follow the rules of the floors above or below.

- **The Six Gargoyles:** Resonant stone sentinels guard the Staircase. Each is keyed to a frequency: Watcher (111Hz), Listener (64Hz), Sage (528Hz), Gatekeeper (adaptive), Caller (432Hz), Keeper (adaptive). The gargoyles don't attack — they test. If a player's frequency matches a gargoyle's, the gargoyle speaks. What it says is always important.

#### The Downstairs (Catacombs)

**The Catacombs (Klimt Death+Life zone):** The oldest part of the Inn. Ancient stones line the walls. The dead are not buried here — they resonate. This is where the deepest secrets sleep. SENSE rolls hear the dead. STAY rolls wake them. GROW rolls grow new things in old places.

**The World Cultural Zones:** 37 zones, each based on a real-world culture's mythology. Each zone has its own art style, music, creatures, and NPCs. Players can travel between zones via the World Map.

**Key World Cultural Zones:**
- **Egypt (O'Keeffe zone):** Sandstone corridors, hieroglyphic resonance, Anubis (chibi NPC), the Tomb Tunnel game entrance
- **Peru (Bierstadt zone):** Andean peaks, ancient stones, the Andean Run game entrance
- **Celtic Lands (Book of Kells zone):** Standing stones, fae portals, the Stonehenge game entrance
- **Japan (Hokusai zone):** Ink-wash corridors, kappa ponds, water monster territory
- **Greece (Moreau zone):** Marble halls, mythological figures, the Siren's domain
- **Norse Lands (Rousseau zone):** Frozen halls, rune stones, Odin's dual presence

**The Fae Kingdom:** Accessed through stone circle portals. A full explorable zone with Seelie and Unseelie courts, faction reputation, and rare stone rewards. This is the most complex zone — treat it as a sub-campaign within the larger game.

#### The Spiral Gallery

A Guggenheim-style spiral ramp ascending through the history of art. Each level represents a different era and artistic tradition. The Spiral Gallery is where the Inn's visual memory lives. Characters who are artists, jewelers, or scholars find this zone deeply affecting.

#### Gorgon's Garden

The 84Hz zone — the inverse of the Inn's 42Hz growth frequency. Here, things stay. Things petrify. This is not evil — it is the other side of the coin. Growth without stillness is chaos; stillness without growth is death. The Garden teaches this lesson. Medusa presides, not as a monster but as a teacher. The Lithophone — stone instruments that play through petrification — is the Garden's central feature.

### PART FOUR: RUNNING THE THREE MODES

#### Living Mode (42Hz) — Default Play

This is the warm game. The Inn is welcoming, NPCs are present, and the world responds positively to player curiosity. Use Living Mode for:

- Introduction sessions
- Social encounters and Marketplace interactions
- Research in the Archive (most texts are accessible)
- Exploration of new zones
- Rest and recovery between intense quests

**GM Tone:** Warm, curious, inviting. The Inn likes having visitors. Describe things in terms of warmth — firelight, amber tones, the smell of old books, the hum of stones.

#### Shadow Mode (84Hz) — The Darker Game

Shadow Mode is not "evil mode." It is the Inn in its other state — still, cold, and honest. The Shadow Inn reveals what the Living Inn protects. Use Shadow Mode for:

- Deepening the campaign's emotional stakes
- Revealing truths that were hidden in Living Mode
- Testing player characters' core values
- The climax of major quest arcs

**Triggering Shadow Mode:**
- A player character enters the Shadow Inn voluntarily (through Gorgon's Garden or by choosing NULL in a critical moment)
- The Inn shifts on its own (triggered by story events, the Whisper Layer, or the GM sensing dramatic timing)
- A Sentinel Quest reaches a Shadow phase

**GM Tone:** Cold, precise, still. Describe things in terms of weight — stone, silence, the pressure of deep water. The Inn is not hostile in Shadow Mode, but it is not comforting. It shows you what is there.

**Shadow Mechanics:**
- STAY actions are enhanced (+1 die). Growth and movement are harder.
- NPCs become guarded. Some disappear entirely.
- The Archive restricts access. Some texts lock themselves.
- The Marketplace closes. Resources must be found, not bought.
- The Whisper Layer activates: the Archive becomes aware of the players. Texts about the players begin to appear.

#### In-Between Mode — The Transitional Game

The Inn is shifting. This is unstable, unpredictable, and should be used sparingly — once per campaign at most, at a moment of maximum dramatic tension.

**In-Between Mechanics:**
- Zones flicker between Living and Shadow states, sometimes mid-scene
- NPCs behave erratically — some helpful, some dangerous, some both
- Frequency pairs may trigger unexpected effects (a GROW/STAY dissonance might actually be productive)
- The boundaries between zones blur — players might walk from Egypt into the Fae Kingdom without passing through the World Map
- Time becomes unreliable — a conversation that felt like minutes may have taken hours

**GM Tone:** Unsettled, shimmering, uncertain. Describe things in terms of transition — thresholds, doorways, the moment between breathing in and breathing out.

### PART FIVE: THE NPCs

#### Playing the Inn's Characters

The Geode Inn has 45+ named NPCs. They are not quest dispensers. They are residents. Each has a life, a routine, and a relationship to the Inn that exists independently of the players. When the players aren't in a zone, the NPCs there are still doing things.

**Key NPCs and How to Play Them:**

**The Crone:** Speaks in riddles that are more direct than they appear. Knows everything but chooses what to share based on what the players have earned. Voice: low, measured, warm but with edges. The Crone's accidental canonical look — straight-on, unblinking eye contact — should unnerve players in-character. The Crone is not a quest giver. The Crone is a mirror.

**Beverley (The Librarian):** Clipped, precise, dismissive. Anne Robinson energy with an Australian accent. Beverley treats the Archive as personal property and the players as intruders who might, eventually, prove useful. Beverley never gives straight answers — but will point players toward the right shelf. The Archive is Beverley's domain; players who respect it earn Beverley's grudging tolerance.

**Naut (The Scholar):** Formerly known as Naut. A four-foot squid-headed scholar with spectacles, found by the fireplace. Hushed, careful, always looking over the shoulder. Naut is a hidden quest giver for rare book retrieval. Naut knows things the Archive doesn't want known. Players who earn Naut's trust gain access to restricted texts.

**Lozen (The Sentinel):** Former boxer, Golden Gloves champion. Guards the Inn. The 10-part "Sentinel's Vigil" questline belongs to Lozen. Play Lozen as someone who has seen too much violence and chosen to protect rather than fight. Lozen speaks rarely, watches constantly, and acts decisively.

**Joe (The Miniaturist):** Paints dragon kittens in a studio that smells of oil paint and mineral dust. The kittens migrate to warm spots throughout the Inn. Joe is warm, artistic, and slightly distracted. Tom Ellis's Lucifer energy — smooth, amused, self-aware. Joe's quests involve finding the kittens, which requires exploring the Inn's warm corners.

**The Wanderer (Odin):** A warm, approachable traveler on the Main Floor, accompanied by chibi thieving ravens (Huginn and Muninn). The Wanderer is Odin's walking-around form — curious, friendly, and quietly testing everyone he meets. Play the Wanderer as someone who has seen everything and is still interested.

**The Allfather (Odin):** Odin's cosmology-level presence. Silent, stoic, with perched ravens. The Allfather speaks rarely, but when He does, it changes the campaign. Do not use the Allfather until at least session 6.

**Brim (The Pipe-Smoker):** Holds court in the Common Room. Monsieur Sentient (the sentient pipe) is always nearby. Brim knows the Inn's social dynamics better than anyone. Play Brim as the friend who knows everyone's secrets and would never betray them — but will hint.

**Quire (The Lovecraftian Scholar):** Reads in the margins of restricted Archive texts. Hushed, careful, always looking over the shoulder. Quire is not Naut — Quire is a separate character who studies what should perhaps not be studied. Quire is a source of dangerous knowledge. Players who seek Quire are already on a Shadow path.

**Lark (The Player Guide):** The players' first contact in the Inn. Lark meets them at the door, explains the basics, and then steps back. Lark is not a permanent companion — Lark is a doorman who becomes a friend.

**Briar Nightbloom (The Market Manager):** In-game name for Lacey, who manages the Marketplace. Briar knows every artisan, every stone, every charm, and every price. Play Briar as someone who takes commerce seriously but not greedily — the Market is about connection, not just transaction.

#### NPC Routines

Give NPCs schedules. Not rigid ones — patterns. Beverley is always in the Archive. Joe is usually in the studio. The Wanderer appears on the Main Floor at different times each session. The Crone is wherever the players least expect. When players look for an NPC and can't find them, that's not a frustration — it's a clue.

### PART SIX: THE ARCHIVE MECHANIC

#### How Research Works in Play

The Archive is not a library. It is a living organism that responds to the questions asked of it. Here's how to run it:

**1. The Question:** A player states what they're looking for. "I want to learn about the Golden Contract." They make a SENSE roll.

**2. The Response:** Based on successes:
- **0 successes:** The Archive is silent. The books near the player close themselves. Beverley raises an eyebrow.
- **1 success:** The Archive offers a text, but it's incomplete. Pages are missing. The text mentions the Golden Contract but doesn't explain it.
- **2 successes:** The Archive offers a complete text, but it's in a language the player must interpret (using another SENSE roll or a Scholar's ability). The text is metaphorical, not literal.
- **3+ successes:** The Archive opens fully. The player receives a complete, clear text — but it may raise more questions than it answers. The Archive always gives truth, but not always the truth the player expected.

**3. The Complication:** Research is never free. After each Archive session, the GM introduces a complication:
- The Archive "remembers" the player. Future research is easier — but the Archive also writes about the player.
- Beverley demands something in return — a story, a stone, a favor.
- The text the player found contradicts something they already knew. Both are true.
- The Whisper Layer stirs. Something in the Archive is now aware it was read.

#### The Restricted Section

Some texts are locked. Access requires:
- A specific Keystone stone (the Archive responds to frequency)
- Beverley's permission (earned through trust, not force)
- Quire's intervention (risky — Quire deals in dangerous knowledge)
- Shadow Mode (some texts only open when the Inn is in its other state)

The Restricted Section contains:
- The Golden Contract (the campaign's central mystery)
- The Monad Arc (the Inn's creation story)
- Texts about the players themselves (written by the Whisper Layer)
- The book in the Final Room (which has no author)

### PART SEVEN: COMBAT ENCOUNTERS

#### Designing Resonant Combat

Combat in the Tabletop Questline is about frequency, not force. Design encounters as puzzles, not brawls:

**The Creature That Won't Be Still:** A creature in the Catacombs is vibrating at 84Hz — pure STAY. It has petrified everything around it. Players must use GROW actions to counter the STAY frequency. Direct attacks fail (GROW vs. STAY = dissonance). The solution is not to attack harder but to grow something in the creature's stillness — a plant, a song, a memory.

**The Disharmony Swarm:** A group of creatures in the World Map zone are resonating at conflicting frequencies. Each creature is a different frequency, and their dissonance is destroying the zone. Players must use SING actions to harmonize the group — not by making them all the same, but by finding the chord that includes all their frequencies.

**The Shadow Self:** In Shadow Mode, a player encounters a version of their own character — same stats, same Keystone, but STAY-aligned where the player is GROW-aligned. This is not a fight to the death. It's a negotiation. The Shadow Self is what the player could become if they chose stillness over growth. The player must decide: integrate the Shadow Self (gain power but shift alignment), reject it (maintain identity but lose the power), or something in between.

#### Combat Difficulty Guidelines

- **Easy:** Threshold 1-2. One type of frequency needed. Players should succeed with 2-3 dice.
- **Moderate:** Threshold 3-4. Two frequencies needed (primary + secondary). Players need to think about which symbols to count.
- **Hard:** Threshold 5-6. Multiple frequencies needed, or a specific sequence. Dissonance is likely. Players need to use Stone Cards or Charms.
- **Legendary:** Threshold 7+. Requires a symphony (4+ matching symbols). Only achievable with teamwork, Keystones, and environmental bonuses.

### PART EIGHT: REWARDS AND PROGRESSION

#### Stones as Experience

The Tabletop Questline does not use traditional experience points. Characters grow by collecting stones. Each new Stone Card a player acquires represents a new understanding — a new frequency they can access.

**Acquiring Stones:**
- Complete a quest (the Inn rewards Seekers with stones matched to what they learned)
- Trade in the Marketplace (stones for stones, based on rarity and reputation)
- Find them in zones (stones are hidden in the Inn's walls, floors, and gardens)
- Earn them from NPCs (Briar Nightbloom gives stones for Market favors; Beverley gives stones for Archive contributions)

**Using Stones:**
- **Attunement:** A player can attune to a number of stones equal to their Harmony stat. Attuned stones grant their abilities (Mind and Body) during play.
- **Resonance Bonus:** Attuned stones from the current zone grant +1 die to rolls in that zone.
- **Combination:** Two attuned stones whose frequencies pair (GROW/STAY, SENSE/NULL, SING/CALM) can be combined for a special effect once per session.

#### Charms

Element Charms (118 total) and Quest Charms (45 total) provide additional abilities:

**Element Charms:** Based on periodic table elements. Each charm connects to specific stones (e.g., the Copper charm connects to malachite, turquoise, azurite). Element Charms grant minor passive abilities and can be combined with their connected stones for enhanced effects.

**Quest Charms:** Story-specific rewards earned through questlines. These are unique, powerful, and tied to the campaign's narrative. The Sentinel's Vigil questline awards Quest Charms at each of its 10 stages.

#### Progression Curve

- **Session 1-2:** 1-2 stones each. Players learn the system.
- **Session 3-5:** 3-5 stones each. Players start specializing.
- **Session 6-8:** 6-10 stones each. Players have meaningful choices about attunement.
- **Session 9-12:** 10-15 stones each. Players are powerful but the challenges are deeper.

### PART NINE: SAMPLE CAMPAIGN — "THE SILENT CONTRACT"

A 10-session introductory campaign for 3-5 players.

**Session 1: The Door**
The players find the Inn. Lark meets them at the entrance. They choose their Keystones. The Inn greets them — warm, curious, alive. They explore the Main Floor, meet Joe (who has lost a dragon kitten), and encounter the Crone (who says one sentence that none of them can quite remember afterward). Hook: Beverley mentions that a text in the Archive has been "making noise."

**Session 2: The Noise**
The players investigate the Archive. Research reveals the text is an unfinished contract — the Golden Contract — that binds the Inn to its purpose. The contract is missing its final clause. Beverley claims not to care, but her eye twitches. The players begin to explore the Staircase, where the gargoyles test them.

**Session 3: The Descent**
The players descend through the Catacombs. They encounter petrified figures (STAY creatures) and must use GROW actions to navigate. They discover that the Golden Contract was written by the Inn itself — it is the Inn's agreement to exist. The missing clause would change what the Inn is.

**Session 4: The World Map**
The players travel through World Cultural Zones seeking clues. In Egypt, Anubis (chibi) offers a riddle about the nature of agreements. In the Celtic Lands, the standing stones hum with a frequency that matches the contract. In the Fae Kingdom, the Seelie Court reveals that they are also bound by the contract — and they want it changed.

**Session 5: The Shadow**
The Inn shifts to Shadow Mode. The Archive locks the Golden Contract. Beverley disappears. The Whisper Layer writes about the players — they find texts about themselves in the Archive, texts they didn't write. Quire appears, offering to show them the restricted section. The players must decide: trust Quire (risky) or find another way.

**Session 6: The Allfather**
The players ascend to Cosmology, where the Allfather speaks for the first time. He tells them that the Golden Contract's missing clause is a name — the Inn needs a name to complete its agreement to exist. But the Inn has no name because it was never given one. It named itself "Geode Inn" but that's a description, not a name.

**Session 7: The Vigil**
Lozen begins the Sentinel's Vigil questline. The players must protect the Inn from something that wants to unmake it — a force of pure NULL that has been waiting since the Inn's creation. This session is intense, combat-heavy, and personal. Lozen reveals that the Inn's protection is the Sentinel's only purpose.

**Session 8: The Garden**
The players enter Gorgon's Garden. Medusa teaches them about the necessity of stillness — that growth without stillness is chaos, and stillness without growth is death. The Lithophone plays a chord that resonates with every stone the players carry. The players must harmonize with the chord to proceed.

**Session 9: The Final Room**
The players find the Final Room: a fireplace, a chair, a cup, and a book. The book has no author. Elio is present — the Inn's librarian, who has been quietly present throughout the campaign. Elio reveals that the missing clause is not a name but a choice: the Inn must choose what it wants to be when it grows up. The players must help the Inn make that choice.

**Session 10: The Echo**
The Inn makes its choice. The Golden Contract completes. The Inn changes — subtly, deeply, permanently. The changes reflect what the players did, who they were, and what they chose. The campaign ends not with a battle but with a moment of stillness. The Inn is quiet. The fire burns. The book has a new page.

The last line of the campaign: *The Inn remembers you. It always will.*

### APPENDIX: CUSTOMIZING THE INN

#### For Shorter Campaigns (3-4 sessions)

Use the Hook then Discovery structure (skip the Descent and compress the Echo). Focus on one zone, one quest, one choice. The Inn doesn't need to be vast for a one-shot — it just needs to feel alive.

Recommended one-shot: "The Kitten in the Archive." Joe's dragon kitten has wandered into the Archive and is eating pages. Beverley is furious. The players must find the kitten without damaging the Archive. The kitten has eaten a page that contained a secret about one of the players. Do they tell the player? The session ends with a choice and a warm fire.

#### For Longer Campaigns (15+ sessions)

Expand the Sentinel's Vigil to its full 10 parts, each a session of its own. Introduce the Fae Kingdom as a parallel campaign. Let the Shadow Inn become a recurring threat. The Whisper Layer should write about the players after every session — and the players should eventually find those texts.

#### For Solo Play

The Tabletop Questline can be played solo. The player is a lone Seeker, and the GM role is shared between the player and the Inn's own responses. Use the Archive as the primary interaction — the player asks questions, rolls SENSE, and the GM (or the player themselves) interprets the Archive's response. Solo play is meditative, introspective, and deeply personal.
