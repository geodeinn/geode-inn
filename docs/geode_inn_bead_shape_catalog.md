# Geode Inn — Bead Shape Catalog
## System: Bead Composer — Shape Library & Audio Mapping
## Status: Active development reference

---

## OVERVIEW

Every stone in the Geode Inn's database is tagged with three fields that determine its bead behavior:

- **`canFacet`** (boolean) — whether the stone can be cut into faceted shapes
- **`beadFinish`** ("smooth" or "faceted") — the display label
- **`opacity`** ("opaque" or "translucent") — visual rendering property

The Bead Composer reads these fields to filter which shapes are available for each stone. This document defines the complete shape library, size variants, and audio characteristics for each shape.

**Current database status:** 63 smooth stones, 101 faceted stones (164 total).

---

## THE RULE (Recap)

1. Opaque stones → SMOOTH only (exception: Black Onyx, all Tourmalines → faceted)
2. Translucent stones → FACETED (exception: all Opals → smooth; Fire Opal → faceted)
3. Fire Opal is the ONLY faceted opal

## SMOOTH SHAPES (canFacet: false)

These shapes are available to all 63 smooth stones. No flat facets — only curved, rounded, or naturally broken surfaces.

### S1. Round (Smooth)
The standard bead. Perfect sphere, drilled through center.
- **Sizes:** 4mm, 6mm, 8mm, 10mm, 12mm
- **Audio profile:** Clean, sustained tone. The sphere resonates evenly in all directions — the purest representation of the stone's base frequency. No shape-induced harmonics.
- **Best for:** Foundation beads in a composition. The sonic anchor.
- **Visual:** The stone's color at its most honest — no light refraction tricks, just the material itself.

### S2. Barrel (Smooth)
Cylindrical with rounded edges, drilled along the long axis.
- **Sizes:** 8×4mm, 10×6mm, 12×8mm, 14×10mm
- **Audio profile:** Warm, slightly dampened. The elongated shape stretches the tone — a fraction longer sustain, a touch softer attack. The barrel sounds like a round that's been held too long.
- **Best for:** Transitional beads between sections of a composition. The connective tissue.
- **Visual:** Color concentrates at the barrel's midpoint and fades toward the ends — a natural gradient.

### S3. Drum (Smooth)
Short cylinder, drilled through the flat face. Wider than it is tall.
- **Sizes:** 8×4mm, 10×5mm, 12×6mm, 14×7mm
- **Audio profile:** Percussive. Shorter sustain, sharper attack than a round. The drum is the Composer's rhythm section — it punctuates rather than sustains.
- **Best for:** Accent beads. Rhythmic emphasis in an arrangement.
- **Visual:** The flat faces show the stone's pattern at its widest — best for stones with banding or chatoyancy (Tiger's Eye, Malachite, Lapis).

### S4. Coin (Smooth)
Flat disc, drilled through the edge (not the face). The thinnest profile.
- **Sizes:** 8mm, 10mm, 12mm, 14mm (all ~3mm thick)
- **Audio profile:** Bright, ringing. The thin profile vibrates freely — the coin is the loudest smooth shape per unit of stone. Short sustain but high projection.
- **Best for:** High points in a melody. The stone's voice at its clearest.
- **Visual:** The face shows the stone's full color range at once — the widest display surface of any shape.

### S5. Freeform (Smooth)
Natural tumble-polished shape. No standardized dimensions — each bead is unique.
- **Sizes:** Small (~6-10mm), Medium (~10-16mm), Large (~16-22mm)
- **Audio profile:** Irregular. Each freeform bead has its own resonance based on its specific shape — unpredictable but characterful. The freeform is the only shape that produces a slightly different frequency each time it's placed.
- **Best for:** Solos. A single freeform in an arrangement of rounds creates a moment of wildcards — the one bead that won't be tamed.
- **Visual:** The stone as nature intended — organic, asymmetric, the raw personality of the mineral.

### S6. Rondelle (Smooth)
A flattened round — like a coin that kept some depth. Drilled through center.
- **Sizes:** 6×3mm, 8×4mm, 10×5mm, 12×6mm
- **Audio profile:** Bright but warm. Between a round's purity and a coin's ring. The rondelle is the versatile middle — it sits in any arrangement without dominating.
- **Best for:** Fill. The background singers of the composition.
- **Visual:** Shows the stone's color well while maintaining some depth for pattern visibility.

### S7. Tube (Smooth)
Long cylindrical, drilled through the long axis. The most elongated smooth shape.
- **Sizes:** 12×4mm, 16×6mm, 20×8mm
- **Audio profile:** Drone-like. The tube sustains longer than any other smooth shape — the elongated form holds the tone. Best used sparingly — a tube can dominate a composition.
- **Best for:** Bass notes. The foundation drone beneath a melody.
- **Visual:** Color concentrated in a thin line — the tube reads as a thread of color in the arrangement.

### S8. Teardrop (Smooth)
Asymmetric rounded teardrop, drilled through the narrow end.
- **Sizes:** 8×6mm, 10×8mm, 12×10mm, 14×12mm
- **Audio profile:** Two-part tone. The wider end resonates lower; the narrow end resonates higher. The teardrop is the only smooth shape that naturally produces a two-note chord from a single bead.
- **Best for:** Emotional accents. The teardrop carries narrative weight — best placed at the end of an arrangement as a closing statement.
- **Visual:** Shows the stone's color gradient at its most dramatic — the shape itself implies motion.

## FACETED SHAPES (canFacet: true)

These shapes are available to all 101 faceted stones. Flat facets cut at precise angles to maximize light return and refraction.

### F1. Round (Faceted)
Brilliant-cut sphere with 57-58 facets. The classic faceted round.
- **Sizes:** 4mm, 6mm, 8mm, 10mm, 12mm
- **Audio profile:** Brilliant, complex. The facets create micro-reflections in the tone — the faceted round sounds like a smooth round with harmonics layered on top. Brighter, more detailed, more shimmer.
- **Best for:** Centerpieces. The focal point of a composition — the bead everything else orbits.
- **Visual:** Maximum fire and brilliance. The stone's color shifts as the bead moves — every angle shows a different face.

### F2. Oval (Faceted)
Elongated brilliant cut. 57 facets stretched along the long axis.
- **Sizes:** 6×4mm, 8×6mm, 10×8mm, 12×10mm, 14×12mm
- **Audio profile:** Warm with shimmer. Between the faceted round's brilliance and the barrel's warmth. The oval has the most balanced audio profile of any faceted shape — bright enough to feature, warm enough to blend.
- **Best for:** Lead melody. The oval is the soloist — prominent without being overwhelming.
- **Visual:** Finger-sized fire. The oval is the most jewelry-traditional faceted shape — the one customers recognize from rings and pendants.

### F3. Cushion (Faceted)
Square with rounded corners. 57-58 facets in a pillow-shaped outline.
- **Sizes:** 6mm, 8mm, 10mm, 12mm, 14mm
- **Audio profile:** Soft brilliance. The cushion is the warmest faceted shape — the rounded corners dampen the highest harmonics, producing a tone that's bright but not sharp. The cushion is the faceted shape for players who want shimmer without harshness.
- **Best for:** Supporting melody. The cushion blends while still sparkling — the team player of faceted shapes.
- **Visual:** Vintage fire. The cushion has the most old-world feel — the shape reads as classic and timeless.

### F4. Marquise (Faceted)
Pointed oval — both ends sharp. 57 facets in a boat-shaped outline.
- **Sizes:** 8×4mm, 10×5mm, 12×6mm, 14×7mm, 16×8mm
- **Audio profile:** Sharp, projecting. The marquise is the loudest faceted shape — the pointed ends focus the tone into a narrow, intense beam. High projection, sharp attack, moderate sustain.
- **Best for:** Climax notes. The marquise is the composition's peak — the moment everything builds toward.
- **Visual:** Dramatic fire. The marquise looks larger than its carat weight — the elongated shape maximizes visual impact per unit of stone.

### F5. Pear (Faceted)
Teardrop with facets. Rounded at one end, pointed at the other. 57 facets.
- **Sizes:** 8×5mm, 10×7mm, 12×8mm, 14×10mm
- **Audio profile:** Directional. Like the smooth teardrop, the pear produces a two-part tone — but the facets sharpen both notes. The wide end is warm and complex; the pointed end is bright and focused.
- **Best for:** Closing statements and opening phrases. The pear is the punctuation mark — it starts or ends a section with intention.
- **Visual:** Elegant fire. The pear is the most emotionally expressive faceted shape — the teardrop form reads as intentional, designed, composed.

### F6. Trillion (Faceted)
Triangular cut with 43 facets. Three equal sides, slightly rounded corners.
- **Sizes:** 6mm, 8mm, 10mm, 12mm
- **Audio profile:** Angular, cutting. The trillion produces the sharpest tone of any faceted shape — the triangular form creates a three-part harmonic that's distinctive and penetrating. Not for blending.
- **Best for:** Contrast. The trillion breaks up a composition — the one bead that doesn't fit, making the others sound different by proximity.
- **Visual:** Modern fire. The trillion is the most contemporary shape — the triangle reads as deliberate, architectural, bold.

### F7. Square Princess (Faceted)
Square with sharp corners. 57 facets in a square brilliant pattern.
- **Sizes:** 6mm, 8mm, 10mm, 12mm
- **Audio profile:** Crisp, even. The princess is the most mathematically precise faceted shape — four-fold symmetry produces a balanced, clean tone. Neither warm nor sharp — neutral brilliance.
- **Best for:** Structural elements. The princess is the grid — the bead that organizes a composition by its presence.
- **Visual:** Geometric fire. The square reads as ordered, intentional, modern-classic.

### F8. Baguette (Faceted)
Long rectangular cut with stepped facets. 20 facets — far fewer than brilliant cuts.
- **Sizes:** 10×4mm, 12×5mm, 14×6mm, 16×8mm
- **Audio profile:** Clear, minimal. The baguette has the fewest facets of any faceted shape — the tone is clean and unadorned. No shimmer, no complexity — just the stone's base frequency at its most transparent.
- **Best for:** Bass lines and rhythm. The baguette is the metronome — steady, clear, foundational.
- **Visual:** Architectural. The baguette reads as structure — the shape of a building, not a decoration.

## SIZE & AUDIO RELATIONSHIP

All shapes follow the same size-to-audio principles:

| Size | Volume | Sustain | Projection | Board Dip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small (4-8mm) | Quiet | Short | Low | Smallest dip |
| Medium (8-12mm) | Moderate | Medium | Moderate | Medium dip |
| Large (12-16mm+) | Loud | Long | High | Largest dip |

Size affects the board layout — small beads can fit more per row, large beads take wider spacing. The board's dip pattern accommodates all sizes with snap-to-grid alignment.

## BOARD DIP MAPPING

The wooden tray's circular dips are sized to accommodate all bead shapes. Each dip snaps the bead to center and registers:
- **Stone ID** (which stone is placed)
- **Shape ID** (which shape was selected)
- **Size** (which size variant)
- **Position** (which dip on the board — determines melody order)
- **Orientation** (for asymmetric shapes like pear, teardrop, marquise — the direction the bead faces affects the audio)

The orientation feature is unique to asymmetric shapes — a teardrop placed narrow-end-left produces a different tone than narrow-end-right. The player can rotate beads after placement, micro-adjusting the composition.

## VISUAL RENDERING NOTES

### Opaque Stones (Smooth Shapes)
- No light transmission — color is surface-only
- The stone's color reads as flat, solid, confident
- Patterns (banding, spotting, chatoyancy) are the primary visual interest
- Polished finish adds a soft sheen; matte finish is velvet-deep

### Translucent Stones (Faceted Shapes)
- Light passes through — color has depth and dimension
- Facets create internal reflections — the stone glows from within
- Color shifts as the viewing angle changes — the stone is alive
- Polished finish maximizes fire; matte finish softens to a glow

### The Opal Exception (Smooth + Translucent)
- Opals are smooth but translucent — the only stones that combine both properties
- No facets, but the stone still has internal fire (play-of-color)
- The smooth surface shows the opal's natural flash without enhancement
- Visually unique — neither opaque-flat nor faceted-brilliant, but glowing and shifting
