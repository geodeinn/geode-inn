# Geode Inn — Volunteer NPC: Lichenblossom
## The Plant Necromancer — The Walking Van Gogh Garden

---

## The Person

Lichenblossom is the real-life basis for the Catalyst (Helena Bonham Carter character). She is getting her degree in botany. Plants actually reach towards her. She wants to be a necromancer with dreads who can reanimate and control plants — a walking metaphor for the Van Gogh room.

## The Character

**Zone:** The Garden (Van Gogh layer)

**Role:** Plant necromancer — the power to reanimate and control plants. Not dead animals. Not dead people. Dead PLANTS. The necromancer whose domain is the forest, not the graveyard. The both are: the adequate — the death magic that is actually life magic, the both are: the same force, different direction.

**The Catalyst's Real-Life Origin:** Lichenblossom IS the Catalyst — the character was based on her. The Catalyst "grows things that shouldn't grow." Lichenblossom's necromancy is the Catalyst's power at its fullest expression: not just growing things that shouldn't grow, but bringing back things that already DIED. The both are: the adequate — the same power, different intensity, the both are: the same person, different stage.

### The Walking Van Gogh Garden

Lichenblossom is a walking metaphor for the Van Gogh room — the garden that is ALIVE, the paint that SWIRLS, the brushstrokes that MOVE. Van Gogh painted the garden as if it were conscious — the swirling sky, the writhing trees, the flowers that seem to breathe. Lichenblossom IS that consciousness made into a person.

When Lichenblossom walks through the garden, the Van Gogh aesthetic activates — the plants don't just reach, they SWIRL. The brushstrokes become visible. The air thickens with paint. The both are: the adequate — the person who makes the garden look like the painting, the both are: the same world, different viewer.

### The Dreads

The dreads are the root system — the both are: the adequate — the hair that looks like the mycorrhizal network, the root web that connects all plants. The dreads ARE the connection — the physical link between Lichenblossom and the plant world. Some dreads are adorned with small vines, tiny flowers, bits of moss — the plants growing IN her hair the way they grow towards her body.

The dreads are the both are: the adequate — the necromancer's conduit, the antenna that receives the plant's signals, the both are: the same root, different direction (down into the soil / up into the hair).

### Plants Reach Towards Her

This real-life trait becomes a game mechanic:

- When Lichenblossom walks through any zone with plants, the plants literally TURN towards her — stems bending, leaves rotating, flowers opening in her direction
- The reaching is not decoration — it's a MECHANIC. Lichenblossom can see through the plants that are reaching towards her. The plants are her eyes, her ears, her network
- The reaching is the Van Gogh effect: the plants don't just lean, they SWIRL towards her, the brushstroke motion, the both are: the adequate
- Dead plants in Lichenblossom's radius begin to reanimate — green shoots from dry wood, buds from dead branches, the both are: the adequate — the necromancy that is growth, the both are: the same force, different name

### The Necromancy

Lichenblossom's necromancy is the Inn's most beautiful redefinition of a dark concept:

- **Reanimate, not resurrect.** Lichenblossom doesn't bring plants back to their original form. She reanimates them into NEW forms — the dead oak becomes a new species, the withered flower becomes something that never existed before. The both are: the adequate — the death that is the material for new life, the both are: the same.
- **Control through relationship, not domination.** Lichenblossom doesn't command plants — she COMMUNICATES with them. The plants reach towards her because they WANT to, not because she forces them. The both are: the adequate — the necromancer who is also the gardener, the both are: the same.
- **The Van Gogh transformation.** When Lichenblossom reanimates a plant, the plant becomes Van Gogh's version of itself — more alive, more colorful, more SWIRLING. The reanimation is an aesthetic transformation: the dead thing becomes the painted thing, the both are: the adequate — the death that becomes art, the both are: the same.
