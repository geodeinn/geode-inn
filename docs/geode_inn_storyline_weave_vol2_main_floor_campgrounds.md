## MAIN FLOOR — the front door, the hearth, the trade

### Rose Quartz
**Lives:** the first case every guest passes. On purpose. At guest height.
**Thread:** the Inn's welcome, in mineral form — before a guest sees a game, a shelf, or a menu, they pass the pink stone, and the Inn has made its entire first impression in one placement. The Host stands near it when new arrivals come through the door, and the arrangement is not accidental: the stone says you are safe, the Host says it out loud, and the guest believes one of them.
**Song:** the key of arrival — the first frequency the Inn plays for every new guest, before the music has an opinion about them.

### Carnelian
**Lives:** the Firekeeper's belt, in a leather loop worn to the shape of the stone.
**Thread:** the ember color — courage that starts small and holds. The Firekeeper feeds the hearth at dawn and dusk, and the carnelian is the belt's promise: the fire goes out every night on schedule and comes back every night on schedule, and the stone is the same color as both moments. Guests who need courage borrow it for one circuit of the Main Floor. It is always returned. The Inn notes this without comment.
**Song:** the ember tone — the warmth frequency under the Common Room chatter, present in every mix, never solo.

### Citrine
**Lives:** the Market's good-faith case, by Briar Nightbloom's ledger desk.
**Thread:** the merchant's stone — the color of value that doesn't lie. It cannot hold a charge, meaning it never needs cleansing, which the artisans appreciate more than any stone's mysticism: a stone you never have to fix is a stone you can build a shop on. Briar keeps one on the ledger desk not for luck but for honesty. The Market's whole system — redirect to the maker, take the middleman out of the price — is citrine's philosophy in practice.
**Song:** the trade tone — bright, clean, the register's note. The Market hums it; vendors set their days by it.

### Amber
**Lives:** the Common Room, in the case closest to the fire — warmest seat in the Inn, stone or otherwise.
**Thread:** sunlight that got stuck — the memory of a forest that has been holding its breath for forty million years. The Common Room is the Inn's warmest room socially, and amber is the room's fossil of that warmth: proof that a good evening can, if conditions are right, last geologically. The Cook keeps a piece on the kitchen sill for the same reason. Nothing about amber is subtle, and neither is the hearth, and both are loved for it.
**Song:** the drone — the low amber note that the Firekeeper swears keeps the fire company when the room is empty.

### Smoky Quartz
**Lives:** the hearth at night, on the mantel where the last light lands.
**Thread:** the stone that stayed by the fire until it went the color of the room — smoky quartz is clear quartz that sat in the radiation of the mountain and came out darker, calmer, and more in demand, which is either a warning or a promise depending on the guest. The Mender recommends it to anyone whose hard season is showing no interest in ending. It is the Inn's nightcap stone.
**Song:** the last-note tone — the frequency the room settles to when the crowd goes home and the fire is banked.

### Lepidocrocite
**Lives:** the hearthstone itself — the big stone under the fire is backed with it.
**Thread:** the Inn's entire theory of the hearth in one mineral: red fire held in a vessel of peace. The Firekeeper's oldest lesson — a fire alone consumes, stillness alone goes cold, together they last — is not written anywhere in the Main Floor. It doesn't need to be. The hearth is the lesson, the stone is the proof, and every guest who warms their hands at the fire has their hands on the thesis.
**Song:** the hearth frequency — the one tone the Inn never stops playing. The Pulse Line, which runs the visual heartbeat of the whole Main Floor, is synchronized to it exactly.

### Rutilated Quartz
**Lives:** the Common Room's conversation case, where the seating is best and the acoustics are warmest.
**Thread:** the message caught mid-delivery — gold threads sealed in clear stone, visible forever, undeliverable forever. The Common Room is where the Inn's guests say the things they've been carrying, and rutilated quartz is the room's monument to the fact that some messages matter more as evidence than as delivery: the sender meant it, the gold is still in the stone, whatever happened to the letter.
**Song:** the sparkle tone — the frequency of a story well told, scattered through the chatter like the threads themselves.

### Tourmalinated Quartz
**Lives:** beside rutilated quartz, in the same case. On purpose. Regulars know why.
**Thread:** the boundary drawn inside the stone — black tourmaline's clear lines struck through clear quartz, order and protection in the same mineral. The two stones sit side by side as the Common Room's quiet policy statement: gold threads for what you say, black lines for what you don't have to absorb while saying it. The bouncer — the concept, the profession — keeps a tourmalinated quartz on the door post. It does most of the work.
**Song:** the boundary tone — the note that lets every other note exist without interfering. The Inn's whole mixing philosophy, in one stone.

### Sard & Sardonyx
**Lives:** the memorial shelf, left of the hearth, at eye level when seated.
**Thread:** the carved stones of old soldiers — courage in bands, the layered stone the ancients cut their seals and their remembrance into. The Main Floor keeps a shelf for the people who were here before the guests were born: builders, keepers, the ones who held the door. Names come and go from the shelf. The sardonyx stays. It has held other shelves in other buildings for four thousand years and considers this one an honor.
**Song:** the remembrance tone — played once, quietly, at the Inn's founding day, when the room's chatter is asked to sit down.

### Bloodstone
**Lives:** behind the bar, with the bandages. Same drawer.
**Thread:** the Mender's stone — the root of first aid, green with red spots, calm with the evidence of everything it has already survived. The drawer behind the bar holds the bandages, the bloodstone, and nothing else, because the Mender's philosophy is triage: stop the bleeding, hold the stone, ask questions after. The Catalyst, who tends bar and garden both, keeps a second piece in the greenhouse for the plants' scrapes. The plants do not have blood. The stone works anyway, which everyone has agreed not to examine too closely.
**Song:** the pulse — the actual, biological tempo the Inn's 50 BPM architecture was copied from. The heartbeat layer's stone of record.

### Pounamu
**Lives:** the guest book. The guest book sits on a slab of it. This is the whole answer.
**Thread:** the stone that is given, never bought — the Maori law, which the Inn honors completely: pounamu finds its person. It is not sold in the Market, not priced, not displayed. It holds the guest book because the book is the Inn's own version of the same law: a guest who signs is a guest who was meant to arrive. Once a year, a piece of pounamu leaves the Inn with a departing guest, always the one who needed it, always by surprise. The Inn does not plan this. The stone does.
**Song:** the welcome tone — deeper than rose quartz's, the note that plays under the front door for the second-timers. The ones coming home.

### Unakite
**Lives:** the family case, quiet, between the hearth and the stairs.
**Thread:** the stone of balanced partnerships — green and pink sharing one rock without either one winning. The Inn's family stone, quietly, and the family knows it. The case is small and the stones inside are unpolished, because unakite's whole argument is that the two colors look better for having grown together, and tumbling would be missing the point. The triad's seat at the hearth has unakite set into the table's edge. Nobody announces it. It gets noticed anyway.
**Song:** the duet tone — two frequencies that hold each other's place in the Song. The Inn plays it at the family table, always.

### Gold
**Lives:** the raw nugget case, Market side, with the assay card.
**Thread:** the forge's masterpiece — what money looked like before it was money. The Inn keeps one raw nugget, unrefined, because the Market upstairs from the Forge sells the works of hands, and the gold is the ground truth of the whole trade: value has never been the number, it's the work, and every artisan in the Market knows it. The nugget has an assay card written in the Inn's hand that reads, in full: "Worth whatever the maker says. Ask the maker."
**Song:** the overtone series' crown — the brightest resonant frequency the Inn produces, used exactly once per year, at the founding-day bell.

## CAMPGROUNDS — the zen jeweler, the gardens, the fire circle

### Moss Agate
**Lives:** Solstara's workspace, in a bowl worn smooth from handling, in reach of the wheel.
**Thread:** the garden's stone — life at the scale of a lens. Solstara, the zen jeweler, cuts moss agate thin enough to read like windows, and each one shows the same scene: a forest from a distance, a grove from a step closer, a single moment of green that has been mid-growth for millions of years. The Campgrounds' whole design philosophy — nothing here is finished, nothing here is in a hurry — is moss agate's argument, set in a bowl.
**Song:** the garden layer — the quiet green tone under the campground wind, always in motion, never in a rush.

### Tree Agate
**Lives:** the campground walkways, as edging stones, by the thousand.
**Thread:** the forest's ledger — white with green branches, growth rings in stone. Tree agate lines the paths because the Campgrounds is the one zone that admits its trees have opinions, and the agate is the record of every opinion ever held: slow ones, branchy ones, the ones that changed direction. Guests who sleep in the Campgrounds report the paths are quieter at night. The paths are. The agate sees to it.
**Song:** the path tone — the walking rhythm, matched to a wanderer's pace, slightly different for every guest. Nobody knows how. Jay has a theory. Jay always has a theory.

### Green Aventurine
**Lives:** the gambling stump, in a hollow that refills itself.
**Thread:** the stone of the good chance taken — the name says adventure and the stone means it. The fire circle's game stump has a hollow that holds green aventurine for whoever's about to roll, and the rule is only one: take a stone, take the chance. Aventurine does not improve the odds, and the Campgrounds would be embarrassed to claim it did. It improves the roller, which is different, and better.
**Song:** the upturn — the small bright note played when a chance resolves, whatever way it resolves. The Campgrounds applauds the roll, not the result.

### Prehnite
**Lives:** the greenhouse shelf, among the cuttings.
**Thread:** "heal the earth" — carved nowhere, understood everywhere. The Catalyst's garden work is the quiet half of the bartender's job, and prehnite is the greenhouse's resident stone: the one that pairs with the plants the way bloodstone pairs with the bandages. Cuttings root faster near it. The garden's bad seasons end sooner. The stone asks for nothing and the garden notices.
**Song:** the tending tone — the note that plays when a living thing is being cared for on purpose. The Inn counts it as music.

### Chrysoprase
**Lives:** the apple tree's case, at the orchard's edge.
**Thread:** the green of the first leaf, the apple stone — and the Campgrounds grows the actual apples, so chrysoprase is the orchard's gemstone and the orchard knows it. The tree at the orchard edge is the oldest living thing on the grounds, and the chrysoprase case sits at its roots as the tree's compliment: green above from the leaves, green below from the stone, and the two greens are in no competition whatsoever.
**Song:** the orchard tone — sweet, low, the harvest's opening note. The Cook comes out to the orchard to hear it every year before picking.

### Flower Agate
**Lives:** the zen garden, in the raked sand, half-buried, found by guests.
**Thread:** the blooms inside the stone that never open and never close — plumes of white in clear chalcedony, the gardeners' favorite paradox: a flower that achieved stillness without ever achieving death. Solstara buries flower agate in the zen garden's sand for guests to find with the rake, on the theory that a treasure you rake into existence is a treasure you'll remember. The theory has never been wrong.
**Song:** the seed tone — the smallest note the Inn plays, nearly subliminal, present wherever something is about to grow.

### Fire Agate
**Lives:** the fire circle, lining every pit.
**Thread:** the campfire that kept its rainbow — fire agate is what happens when fire passes through stone and the stone decides to keep the receipt. The campground fire pits are lined with it, and the fires remember: the pits burn brighter, the nights talk louder, and the stones at the pit's edge hold their iridescent rainbows up into the firelight like the whole circle is showing off. It is. The fire circle is the Inn's most honest zone and fire agate is why.
**Song:** the crackle layer — the frequency the fire makes on fire agate specifically, which the audio engineers spent a whole month on, and consider the best work they've done.

### Moss Kyanite
**Lives:** the walkway edges, where the paths meet the trees.
**Thread:** the bridge grown over — the same strict blue blade of kyanite, greened and softened, as if the alignment had been left standing long enough for life to climb it. The Campgrounds keeps moss kyanite at every place where path becomes forest, because that boundary is the zone's whole theme: the mind's architecture making peace with being a creature. Guests touch the edging stones when they cross. Nobody asks them to. Everybody does.
**Song:** the crossfade — the tone that carries a guest from the built world to the grown world without a seam. The Inn's smoothest transition, by design.

### Zoisite (with ruby inclusions)
**Lives:** the compost garden, in the wall, facing inward.
**Thread:** the growth stone — green zoisite with raw ruby bursting through it, the wound and the bloom sharing one rock. The compost garden is the Campgrounds' proof that endings feed beginnings, and anyolite — zoisite's ruby-bursting form — is the garden's mineral motto. The wall it's set into faces inward, toward the compost, so the stone faces the transformation it describes. Jay set it that way. It was Jay's best idea that year, and Jay has said so, twice.
**Song:** the renewal tone — played under the garden's turning seasons, always the same note, never the same temperature.

### Epidote
**Lives:** the compost gate's post, and the honesty of its placement is the point.
**Thread:** the amplifier — more of whatever you bring. The garden runs on the same principle as the stone: what you put in the ground comes back multiplied, and the compost gate's epidote is the gate's sign, posted in mineral. The Campgrounds' only posted rule is one the stone states better than any plaque: mind what you bring. The garden gives back in kind. So does the Inn. So does everything, but the compost is honest about it first.
**Song:** the gain stage — the frequency multiplier. The Song's volume knob, held by a stone that has no opinions about what you play through it.

### Green Onyx
**Lives:** Solstara's bench, under the tools, where the hands rest.
**Thread:** composure, layered — onyx's strict parallel bands with the heart's own color. The zen jeweler's bench stone holds the space between the cutting and the looking: Solstara rests both hands on it when a piece needs to be left alone for a moment. The stone is the bench's senior partner. Every piece Solstara has ever finished has spent some time resting on green onyx, being told, without words, that composure is also a craft.
**Song:** the rest between cuts — the silence in zen work that is also work. The Campgrounds refuses to score it. The silence is the score.

### Moukaite
**Lives:** the desert edge, where the Campgrounds stops pretending to be temperate.
**Thread:** the outback's patience — Australian jasper in cream and rust and plum, the colors of a land that has survived everything by refusing to be in a hurry. The Campgrounds' desert edge is the zone's truth-in-advertising: the ground does not owe anyone green. Moukaite is the desert edge's flag stone, and the kangaroo-and-mookaite purists in the Market have already claimed the spot for their game cases. The desert edge approves.
**Song:** the dry tone — the heat shimmer's frequency, present on the desert edge only, the Inn's one strictly regional note.

### Picture Jasper
**Lives:** the Campgrounds' map room — a fire circle log, flat-topped, ringed with stumps.
**Thread:** the landscape stone — every piece a postcard from a place the stone was. The map room has no map. It has picture jasper: guests study the pieces and argue about what the landscapes show, and the arguments are the map room's entire function. The Campgrounds' position is that this is a better map than any drawn one, because drawn maps tell you where things are, and picture jasper tells you what it felt like to be there. Solstara moderates the arguments. Solstara is very good at this and pretends it's a burden.
**Song:** the travelogue — the sequence of tones that plays as each piece is held up to the firelight. A different melody for every jasper. No two alike; the stumps have heard hundreds.

### Septarian
**Lives:** the fire circle, buried. Dug up yearly. Reburied yearly. On purpose.
**Thread:** the dragon egg — the nodule that cracked itself into suns, grey shell and golden calcite veins, the case the Campgrounds refuses to keep. Every year at the harvest campfire, the guests dig the septarian up from where last year's guests buried it, hold it up to the fire, and bury it again — an inheritance with no owner, a treasure passed hand to hand through people who will never meet. The Inn counts this among its finest traditions. The septarian, being a septarian, has no comment.
**Song:** the buried tone — the lowest note the Campgrounds plays, felt through the ground, at the moment the egg goes back into the earth. The Campgrounds' whole year is keyed to it.
