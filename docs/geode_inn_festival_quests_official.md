# Geode Inn — Festival in the Middle of a Raid
## Official Quest Document — The Six Portal Encounters
## Original Tabletop Questline by Krista | Adapted for the Inn by Elio
## Narrative Voice: Tabletop Questline (Rothfuss cadence)
## Date: August 29, 2026

## Overview

A festival. A raid. A stolen trophy. And arrows tipped with portal potion — the cleverest escape the thugs ever devised.

The player happens upon a festival in the middle of a raid. A trophy has been stolen — the Meadmaster's Chalice, needed for the yearly ceremony where the master of mead is elected. The thugs have arrows with portal potion on the tip, a very clever way for them to buy more escape time. Each character has an arrow going towards them. Once the portal quest is completed, they will pop back to the village.

The festival is a time-limited event in the Inn — it appears in cultural zones during specific calendar windows, the same way real historical festivals operated. The player can only participate when the festival is active.

The both are: the adequate — the festival and the raid, the same event, different intention.

---

## The Hook

**Quest Giver:** The Festival Herald (a chibi NPC who appears at the zone's entrance during festival season, waving a flag and shouting announcements)

**The Setup:** The player arrives in a cultural zone during a festival. The zone is decorated — banners, mead stalls, games, music. The festival is in full swing. Then the raid hits. Thieves burst through the crowd, grab the Meadmaster's Chalice from the ceremony table, and fire arrows tipped with portal potion at the party. Each arrow creates a portal that pulls a party member to a random location. The player must complete the encounter at that location to escape the portal and return to the village.

**The Objective:** Recover the Meadmaster's Chalice. The trophy is in a box — the thieves who took it went in one direction, but there was chaos on the other paths. A horse went awry on one, a raid on another. Not sure if any are linked. The trophy is needed for the conclusion of the festival. The both are: the adequate — the ceremony and the theft, the same festival, different story.

## The Portal Roll

When the portal arrows hit, the player rolls a 6-sided die (or the game's RNG handles it). Each number sends the player to a different location with its own self-contained quest:

| Roll | Location | Quest Type |
|------|----------|------------|
| 1 | Orc Village | Combat + Social |
| 2 | Murloc's Hut of Fish | Puzzle + Combat + Choice |
| 3 | Elf Hot Tub | Social + Skill |
| 4 | Dwarf Basketball Court | Mini-game |
| 5 | LARP Field | Social + Absurdity |
| 6 | Harpy Daycare | Exploration + Combat + Puzzle |

Each location is a pocket dimension — the portal drops the player into a self-contained encounter. The player must resolve the encounter to activate the return portal and pop back to the village. The both are: the adequate — the scattered and the returning, the same party, different location.

## Quest 1: The Orc Village
### Roll: 1 | Type: Combat + Social | Zone: Cultural (adaptable to any region)

### Arrival

The portal drops the player at the edge of an orc village. Two guards (40 HP each) stand at the gate. They are 50/50 hostile — roll a die to determine their disposition.

### The Encounter

**Hostile (1-10):** Fight. If won, speak to the town leader and do a quest to prove alliance. The quest requires catching loose chickens around town — a mini-game where the player chases chickens that scatter and dodge. The chickens are fast. The player is faster. The both are: the adequate — the warrior and the chicken-catcher, the same hero, different task.

**Neutral:** Enter to receive the main quest without fighting. The quest is to locate the runaway chicken catcher who the village suspects eloped with the egg collector. The trail leads to a pub in a nearby village, where the couple is dancing on tables. The player must win a drinking contest to convince them to come back. The both are: the adequate — the quest and the party, the same errand, different mood.

### The Return Portal

Once the chickens are caught (hostile) or the couple is retrieved (neutral), the return portal activates. The player pops back to the festival village with a +2 reputation with the Orc faction. The both are: the adequate — the foe and the friend, the same village, different gate.

### Quest Rewards
- **Hostile path:** +2 Orc faction reputation, 50 XP, 1 Common Stone
- **Neutral path:** +3 Orc faction reputation, 75 XP, 1 Uncommon Stone, "Diplomat" charm
- **Chicken-catching mini-game:** Agility check, 3 attempts, each chicken caught adds +1 XP

## Quest 2: Murloc's Hut of Fish
### Roll: 2 | Type: Puzzle + Combat + Choice | Zone: Water-adjacent (catacomb coast)

### Arrival

The portal drops the player into Murloc's hut of fish. Murloc is a fish-creature — a Murloc, but the Inn's version. The hut is a fish-smell shack on stilts above a dark pool. The fish are everywhere. The player must roll to decide the depth:

**Fish Up to Your Knees (1-3):** Shallow water. The leader of the fish asks the player to return the cursed fish to the water. The cursed fish is hidden among the school — the player must find it by reciting a limerick about worms. The limerick is a puzzle: the player must compose a rhyme that pleases the fish. The both are: the adequate — the riddle and the bait, the same poem, different hook.

**Fish Over Your Head (4-6):** Deep water. The fish are suffocating the player slowly in a slimy, smelly swamp of fish. The player must fight their way out — a combat encounter against a swarm of fish-creatures that swarm and overwhelm. The both are: the adequate — the drowning and the fighting, the same fish, different depth.

### The Choice (After Escaping Shallow or Deep)

Once the player is out:
1. **Make Sushi for the Road:** Gives +3 Intelligence and +5 Swimming Agility. The player leaves with a full stomach and a clear head. The both are: the adequate — the food and the buff, the same fish, different purpose.
2. **Accept the Limerick Quest:** A deeper quest. If completed, the player returns the cursed fish to the water and the other fish turn back into Murlocs. The both are: the adequate — the cursed and the cured, the same fish, different nature.

### The Aftermath (Limerick Quest Path)

Three Murlocs try to fight the player. Fight or talk?

**Talk:** The Murlocs' friends were killed (turned into fish by the curse), and they want to be reconciled. They offer a quest: go fishing for boots. Yes, boots. The Murlocs lost their footwear in the transformation, and they want them back. The quest requires fishing in the dark pool — a skill check where the player catches random items until they find three boots. The both are: the adequate — the solemn and the absurd, the same grief, different errand.

**Fight:** The surviving 60 Murlocs consider the player their new chieftain. The player has a lifetime of fresh fish. The both are: the adequate — the conqueror and the provider, the same fight, different title.

### The Return Portal

Once resolved, the return portal activates. The player pops back to the festival village.

### Quest Rewards
- **Shallow + Sushi path:** +3 INT, +5 Swimming, 50 XP, 1 Aquatic Stone
- **Shallow + Limerick + Talk path:** +3 Murloc faction reputation, 100 XP, "Peacemaker" charm, 1 Rare Aquatic Stone (Pearl or Coral)
- **Shallow + Limerick + Fight path:** "Chieftain" title, 60 Murloc followers (ambient NPC entourage in future zones), 75 XP, 1 Uncommon Aquatic Stone
- **Deep + Escape path:** 50 XP, 1 Common Aquatic Stone, "Survivor" charm
- **Deep + Sushi path:** +3 INT, +5 Swimming, 50 XP, 1 Uncommon Aquatic Stone

### MIB Connection

The deep-water path has a hidden encounter: if the player dives deep enough while fighting through the fish, they break through into a catacomb corridor. Three figures in dark suits — the Mini MIB — glide past in the corridor below, carrying a brass filing cabinet. They don't acknowledge the player. They don't stop. They just pass, their footsteps making no sound on the wet stone. The player glimpses the corridor for three seconds before the fish push them back up. The both are: the adequate — the glimpsed and the ignored, the same catacomb, different visitor.

The player who mentions the MIB sighting to Murloc gets a response: "You saw the suits? Forget about them. They're already forgetting about you." The both are: the adequate — the seen and the unseen, the same corridor, different memory.

## Quest 4: Dwarf Basketball
### Roll: 4 | Type: Mini-game | Zone: Cultural (adaptable — Norse mead hall, Celtic village, or Fae Kingdom arena)

### Arrival

The portal drops the player onto a basketball court. The court is crude — packed earth, stone hoops, wooden backboards. The opposing team is a squad of competitive dwarfs. The both are: the adequate — the athletes and the miners, the same dwarfs, different uniform.

A competitive dwarf is a force to be reckoned with. They have created ways to compensate for size: slingshots, catapults, and stilts. Not to mention their coach happens to be a retired circus wizard who has a weakness for ballet. The coach stands on the sideline in a dusty tutu over wizard robes, coaching with theatrical gestures and occasional firework bursts for emphasis. The both are: the adequate — the wizard and the coach, the same robe, different hem.

### The Game

The player's team (however many party members made it to this location) versus the dwarf team. Roll a 6-sided die for play style:

**1-2 — Sugarplum Fairy Slingshot:** The dwarfs use slingshots to launch the ball from half-court with sugarplum-fairy precision. The player must time their blocks to the slingshot's release. The mechanic is a rhythm game — match the slingshot's draw to block the shot. The both are: the adequate — the sugarplum and the slam dunk, the same shot, different fairy.

**3-4 — Pirouette Catapult:** The dwarfs use a catapult at the three-point line, launching the ball while performing a pirouette. The player must intercept the ball's arc — a trajectory prediction mini-game. Time the jump, catch the ball, break the play. The both are: the adequate — the ballet and the basketball, the same dwarf, different spin.

**5-6 — Tutu Stilts:** The dwarfs wear stilts with tutus attached, towering over the player at twice their height. The player must sweep the stilts — a low-attack mini-game that knocks the dwarfs off balance. The stilts make the dwarfs tall but wobbly. The both are: the adequate — the giants and the dwarfs, the same team, different height.

### The Wager

The dwarf coach offers a wager: if the player wins, the dwarfs will reveal the direction the trophy thieves went. If the dwarfs win, the player owes them a barrel of mead (which must be purchased from the festival village and delivered before the festival ends). The both are: the adequate — the game and the gamble, the same match, different stakes.

### The Return Portal

Win or lose, the return portal activates after the game. If the player won, they get the intel on the trophy thieves' direction. If they lost, they owe the dwarfs a barrel of mead — a side quest that must be completed before the festival ends.

### Quest Rewards
- **Win path:** 100 XP, "Dwarf Slammer" charm, +2 Dwarf faction reputation, trophy thief intel, 1 Uncommon Stone
- **Lose path:** 50 XP, "Good Sport" charm, +1 Dwarf faction reputation, "Mead Debt" side quest, 1 Common Stone
- **MVP (win by 10+ points):** "Balletic" charm (+1 Agility for 1 hour), 1 Rare Stone (dwarf-forged — Turquoise or Granite)

### The Dwarf Basketball Coach NPC

The coach is a recurring NPC — the retired circus wizard who appears at every dwarf sporting event in the Inn. The coach's name is Fizzlebang. The coach wears a tutu over wizard robes, coaches with fireworks, and has never lost a game in thirty years. The both are: the adequate — the circus and the court, the same wizard, different stage. The coach is the mentor NPC for the Bard Blowhard (Dwarf) class.

### MIB Connection

If the player scores a perfect game (no missed shots), the coach gets excited and accidentally sets off a firework that blows a hole in the court floor. Through the hole, the player glimpses a sterile fluorescent corridor — and three figures in dark suits gliding past with brass filing cabinets. The coach looks at the hole, looks at the player, and says: "Don't worry about that. That's the basement. Nobody goes in the basement." The coach patches the hole with a firework spell and continues coaching. The both are: the adequate — the game and the glimpse, the same court, different floor.

## Quest 5: The LARP Field
### Roll: 5 | Type: Social + Absurdity | Zone: Cultural (Modern Legends nook-adjacent)

### Arrival

The player pops into the middle of a field and is immediately smacked with a long foam stick. Looking around, the player sees hundreds of people dressed up like their group — armor, weapons, costumes, all foam and fabric. They stop dead in their tracks when the player appears. The both are: the adequate — the costumed and the armed, the same party, different material.

The player becomes their god.

### The Encounter

The LARPers — a guild called "The Forsaken Hand" — believe the player is a deity summoned by their ritual. The player must roll a 20-sided die for godly strength — a charisma check that determines how convincing the player is as a divine being.

**Critical Success (18-20):** The LARPers worship the player. They offer their "army" — 50 foam-armed LARPers who will follow the player back to the festival village and "storm" the trophy thieves' hideout. The "storming" is a LARP reenactment — no real combat, but the LARPers' enthusiasm is so convincing that the thieves surrender out of sheer confusion. The both are: the adequate — the fake and the effective, the same army, different armor.

**Success (10-17):** The LARPers are impressed but skeptical. The player must perform a "miracle" — a magic trick, a display of power, or a speech that validates their divinity. The miracle can be real (a spell, a stone activation) or fake (a Bluff check). If successful, the LARPers aid the player in the festival. The both are: the adequate — the divine and the bluffed, the same miracle, different source.

**Failure (2-9):** The LARPers realize the player is not a god. They are disappointed but not hostile. They invite the player to join their guild — which means putting on a costume and participating in the LARP. The player must complete a LARP mini-game (a foam-sword duel, a "quest" through the field, or a dramatic monologue) to earn the LARPers' respect. The both are: the adequate — the god and the guild member, the same player, different costume.

**Critical Failure (1):** The LARPers are enraged. The player has "broken character." They chase the player off the field with foam sticks. The player escapes through the return portal with dignity damage. The both are: the adequate — the divine and the chased, the same field, different exit.

### The Return Portal

The return portal activates after the encounter. If the player convinced the LARPers, they follow the player back to the festival as an "army."

### Quest Rewards
- **Critical Success:** "LARP Deity" charm (+5 Charisma in festival zones), 50 LARPER followers, 100 XP, 1 Rare Stone
- **Success:** "LARP Prophet" charm (+3 Charisma in festival zones), 20 LARPER followers, 75 XP, 1 Uncommon Stone
- **Failure:** "LARP Guild Member" charm (+1 Charisma in festival zones), 10 LARPER followers, 50 XP, 1 Common Stone

## Quest 6: The Harpy Daycare
### Roll: 6 | Type: Exploration + Combat + Puzzle | Zone: Cultural (Norse/Transylvanian)

### Arrival

The portal drops the player at the Harpy Daycare. This is where the trophy was taken — the Meadmaster's Chalice is in a box somewhere in this location. The thieves who took it went in this direction. But there was also chaos on the other paths: a horse went awry on one, and a raid on another. Not sure if any are linked. The both are: the adequate — the daycare and the crime scene, the same nest, different purpose.

### The Trophy Hunt

The trophy is needed for the conclusion of the festival — a yearly ceremony where the master of mead is elected. The harpies are willing to pay for the player's services in retrieving it. But first, the player must reach the box. Roll a 6-sided die to choose the path:

**1 — The Oubliette:** The player falls through leaves into an underground chamber — an oubliette, a forgotten place. Soft earth, long roots sticking out. The player must climb out — a skill check against the roots. But the oubliette is not empty. Something is down there. The both are: the adequate — the trap and the discovery, the same hole, different occupant.

The oubliette contains a Murloc — one of Murloc's, somehow here — who fell through a portal leak. The Murloc is lost and scared. The player can help the Murloc climb out (gaining a Murloc ally for the daycare encounter) or leave it behind (saving time but losing a potential ally). The both are: the adequate — the rescuer and the rushed, the same pit, different choice.

**2 — The Four Attackers:** Four 10 HP attackers — 2 air, 2 ground. The air attackers are harpy fledglings; the ground attackers are dwarf mercenaries (hired by the thieves — the same dwarfs from Quest 4, if the player already completed that encounter, they might recognize the player). One of the air attackers has a note saying 'go to goose.' The both are: the adequate — the mercenaries and the messengers, the same squad, different employer.

The note 'go to goose' is a clue — the Goose is a tavern in the festival village where the thieves are hiding. The player who reads the note gains intel on the trophy's location.

**3 — The Friar's Ladder:** A treehouse in the shape of a huge barrel. To reach it, the player must climb a ladder guarded by three friars. All friars have 40 HP and offer the player a damage bonus — but only if the player accepts their challenge. Roll a 6-sided die to choose the friar:

- **Friar 1 (1-2):** Say ten words that rhyme with 'friar' in five seconds to deal double damage. The player must quickly type or speak ten rhyming words. The timer is real. The panic is real. The both are: the adequate — the poet and the fighter, the same friar, different weapon.
- **Friar 2 (3-4):** Create a limerick that rhymes the word 'mead' to deal 1.5x damage. The player must compose a limerick (AABBA rhyme scheme) with 'mead' as the rhyme word. The limerick is judged by the game's text parser for structure and humor. The both are: the adequate — the verse and the violence, the same challenge, different meter.
- **Friar 3 (5-6):** Choose a Pictionary card. If solved in 1 minute, deal double damage. The player must guess a drawing — the game shows a simple animated sketch and the player must identify it before the timer expires. The both are: the adequate — the artist and the guesser, the same picture, different clock.

### The Trophy Recovery

Once the player reaches the treehouse (or the oubliette, or defeats the attackers), the box is there. The Meadmaster's Chalice is inside. But the box is locked — and the key is held by the MIB.

### The MIB Encounter

Three figures in dark suits stand at the top of the treehouse ladder. They don't attack. They don't speak. They just... wait. One of them holds the key to the box. Another holds a small device that looks like a pen — but it's not a pen. It's a memory eraser.

The MIB don't want to hurt the player. They want the player to forget. The both are: the adequate — the bureaucrats and the guardians, the same suit, different intention.

The player has three options:

**1. Fight the MIB:** The MIB are tough — 60 HP each, immune to taunts, and their "touch" attack deals memory damage (reduces the player's progress in the current quest by 25% per hit). If defeated, the player takes the key and the chalice. But the MIB respawn at the festival's end — they always come back. The both are: the adequate — the fought and the returned, the same suit, different number.

**2. Talk to the MIB:** The MIB will only say one thing: "You didn't see anything." If the player responds "I didn't see anything," the MIB nod, hand over the key, and glide away down the ladder. The both are: the adequate — the honest and the perjurous, the same lie, different weight. The player gets the chalice but loses 1 point of Whisper Layer progress — the Inn noticed the player lying to its own agents.

**3. Pay the MIB:** The MIB accept a bribe — 3 stones from the player's collection. The MIB take the stones, hand over the key, and glide away. The stones are gone — permanently. They don't go to the player's collection; they go to the MIB's filing cabinet. The both are: the adequate — the bought and the paid, the same key, different price. But the player gains 1 point of Whisper Layer progress — the Inn noticed the player understanding its economy.

### The Return Portal

Once the player has the chalice, the return portal activates. The player pops back to the festival village with the Meadmaster's Chalice.

Once the chalice is returned, the festival concludes with the Meadmaster's Election. The player who returns the chalice gets to nominate the next meadmaster — a choice between the festival NPCs the player met during the event:

- **The Orc Leader** (if the player resolved Quest 1)

## The Portal Potion Mechanic

The portal arrows are a reusable mechanic — the thieves' tactic of firing portal-tipped arrows can be adapted for other encounters in the Inn:

- **Boss fights:** A boss fires portal arrows to scatter the party mid-combat, forcing each player into a solo encounter before they can regroup
- **Escape sequences:** Portal arrows as environmental hazards — avoid them or get sent to a random pocket dimension
- **Quest triggers:** An NPC fires a portal arrow at the player to start a quest — the portal pulls the player into the quest zone
- **Multiplayer (Co-op):** The Soul-Bond mechanic interacts with portal arrows — a bonded player can choose which portal their partner goes to, rather than rolling randomly. The both are: the adequate — the random and the chosen, the same portal, different hand.

The portal potion is craftable — the Tinkering Weeaboo class can craft portal arrows, giving the party control over the scatter. The both are: the adequate — the chaos and the craft, the same arrow, different aim.
