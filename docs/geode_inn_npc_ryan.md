# Geode Inn — Volunteer NPC: Ryan
## The Meadery's Bartender — The Campfire

---

## The Person

Ryan is River's best friend at the Sherwood Forest Faire. He manages the bartenders at the faire. His day job is head of a branch of Dell Technology. He is an avid gamer. He is raising three brilliant, entirely too inquisitive daughters. He has the energy of the campfire — the fire that gathers.

## The Character — Visual Identity

**The Look:** Ezio Auditore's athletic, confident build and balanced stance. Captain Barbossa's long, ornate, weathered pirate captain's coat — dark navy with tarnished gold trim and brass buttons, a coat that has survived years of storms. Van Helsing's wide-brimmed weathered leather adventurer's hat with a tarnished buckle band. Short, well-maintained beard. The pirate captain who chose to tend a bar instead of a ship.

**The Voice:** Captain Barbossa (Geoffrey Rush quality) — theatrical, commanding, that distinctive pirate cadence. The voice of a man who has told a thousand stories and knows exactly when to pause for effect. Warm underneath the theater — the both are: the adequate — the performance that is also genuine, the both are: the same charm, different register.

**The Presence:** Three genres in one person. The Renaissance assassin's poise, the pirate captain's authority, the vampire hunter's readiness. The both are: the adequate — three sets of skills that all resolve into the same quality: the person who is prepared for anything and makes it look easy.

**Zone:** The Meadery (main floor)

**Role:** The Meadery's bartender — the keeper of the hall's warmth, the one who pours and listens, the campfire energy made into a person. Ryan is the social hub NPC of the Meadery — the person everyone tells things to, the person who knows everyone's name and drink, the person whose energy makes the hall feel like a hall.

### The Campfire

Ryan IS Erik's fire — the fire that builds tables not walls, the fire that welcomes not consumes, the fire that adopts outcasts. Erik (the Norse vendor) is the fire's origin. Ryan is the fire's keeper — the one who tends it daily, who makes sure it never goes out, who feeds it with presence and attention. The both are: the adequate — the same fire, different keeper.

The Barbossa voice IS the campfire — the theatrical warmth that draws people in, the story that gathers the room, the both are: the adequate. The pirate captain's cadence is the bartender's cadence — the rhythm of a person who knows how to hold a room without demanding it.

### The Tech Bridge

Ryan's Dell background is the Inn's quiet technological bridge. The Meadery looks ancient (Viking ship hall, carved wood, horn cups), but Ryan knows the systems underneath. He's the bartender who can also explain the plumbing — not because he boasts about it, but because someone asked and he knows. The both are: the adequate — the ancient hall and the modern infrastructure, the both are: the same building, different layer.

The Assassin's Creed connection is the tech bridge's deepest layer: Ryan is a gamer who chose to be IN the game. The Ezio build is the gamer's tribute — the both are: the adequate — the real person who designed his game avatar, the both are: the same person, different world.

### The Gamer's Eye

Ryan is an avid gamer, which means he sees the Inn the way a player sees it — not just as a character but as a SYSTEM. He's the NPC who understands the meta. Not breaking the fourth wall, but leaning on it comfortably. The both are: the adequate — the character who knows he's in a game, the both are: the same person, different awareness.

The Van Helsing hat is the gamer's wink — the vampire hunter's accessory on the bartender, the both are: the adequate — the practical gear repurposed for a different kind of hunt (the hunt for the perfect pour, the hunt for the right story, the hunt for the daughters who wandered off again).

### The Three Daughters

Ryan's three daughters are the Inn's most organic information delivery system — three young NPCs who wander the Meadery (and beyond) asking questions. The "entirely too inquisitive" quality is a game mechanic:

- The daughters ask the player questions that reveal lore — the questions the player would ask if the player weren't too proud to ask
- The daughters wander — they don't stay in the Meadery. They get into everything. The player might find one in the Garden, one in the Staircase, one in the Catacombs — each one asking questions of a different NPC
- The daughters are the Inn's curiosity — the unfiltered, unashamed "why?" that the adult NPCs have learned to suppress
- Ryan's quest could involve finding the daughters who wandered too far — not because they're in danger, but because they asked a question that opened a door that shouldn't have been opened yet

### The Bartender's Privilege

Ryan knows everyone's story — not because he's a spy, but because people talk to bartenders. The both are: the adequate — the listener who doesn't judge, the keeper of confidences, the both are: the same. Ryan could be the Inn's gossip channel — not malicious gossip, but the social knowledge that keeps a community functioning. Who's feuding with whom. Who's looking for what. Who needs help and won't ask.

The Barbossa voice makes the gossip theatrical — the both are: the adequate — the pirate captain who tells the town's secrets like they're sea shanties, the both are: the same information, different performance.

### The Campfire Mechanic

Ryan's presence in the Meadery creates a warmth radius — the both are: the adequate — the area around the bar where NPCs and players gather, where the social energy is highest, where the hall feels like a hall. When Ryan is behind the bar, the Meadery is alive. When Ryan steps away (to find his daughters), the hall dims — not dark, just cooler, the fire banked. The player can help Ryan return to the bar, restoring the full warmth.
