That tension is the traveler's entire journey — reaching toward the Song without ever becoming the Song.

## Layer 4: The Timbre — Specific Gravity

Two stones with the same Mohs hardness share the same Solfeggio frequency. But they don't sound identical. The specific gravity (density) of each stone modifies the timbre — the tonal color, the harmonic content, the voice of the stone.

### How It Works

Specific gravity affects the stone's harmonic overtones. A denser stone (higher SG) produces darker, warmer overtones. A lighter stone (lower SG) produces brighter, more crystalline overtones. Same fundamental frequency, different voice.

The SG modifier is applied as an overtone series adjustment:

- **SG < 2.5** (light stones): +15% high harmonic content. Brighter, more crystalline, more "ringing." The stone sounds like a bell.
- **SG 2.5-3.5** (medium stones): balanced overtones. The stone sounds like a singing bowl.
- **SG 3.5-4.5** (heavy stones): +15% low harmonic content. Darker, warmer, more "humming." The stone sounds like a cello.
- **SG > 4.5** (very heavy stones): +25% low harmonic content, -10% high. Very dark, very warm, almost subsonic. The stone sounds like a drum.

### Examples: Same Frequency, Different Timbre

All quartz family stones are Mohs 7 = 528Hz. But they sound different:

| Stone | SG | Timbre | How It Sounds |
|-------|----|--------|---------------|
| Clear Quartz | 2.65 | Medium | A clear singing bowl. The reference tone. |
| Smoky Quartz | 2.65 | Medium | Same bowl, slightly darker (color-influenced EQ) |
| Amethyst | 2.65 | Medium | Same bowl, slightly warmer (purple = warmth) |
| Rutilated Quartz | 2.65 | Medium | Same bowl with metallic overtones (rutile inclusions) |
| Citrine | 2.65 | Medium | Same bowl, slightly brighter (yellow = brightness) |

The quartz family shares a frequency. The specific gravity is the same. The TIMBRE difference comes from inclusions, color associations, and mineral variations — the subtle modifiers that make each stone an individual voice rather than a note.

For stones with the SAME Mohs but DIFFERENT SG:

| Stone | Mohs | SG | Frequency | Timbre |
|-------|------|-----|-----------|--------|
| Quartz (SiO2) | 7 | 2.65 | 528 Hz | Bright singing bowl |
| Garnet (various) | 7-7.5 | 3.5-4.3 | 528-585 Hz | Dark cello hum |
| Tourmaline | 7-7.5 | 3.0-3.2 | 528-585 Hz | Warm viola |

Garnet and tourmaline share Mohs range with quartz but have higher SG. Same note, darker voice. The player learns to distinguish stones by ear — first by frequency (the Mohs note), then by timbre (the SG voice). A trained ear can identify a stone blind.

## Audio Engine Implementation

### Triple-Layer Synthesis

Each stone's audio is synthesized in three layers, mixed together:

```
Layer 1: 42Hz carrier (sine, continuous, low gain)
Layer 2: Solfeggio fundamental (sine, primary gain)  
Layer 3: Timbre overtones (determined by SG class)
         - Light SG: +2nd, 3rd, 4th harmonics (bright)
         - Medium SG: +2nd, 3rd harmonics (balanced)
         - Heavy SG: +2nd harmonic, sub-octave (dark)
         - Very Heavy SG: sub-octave emphasis, minimal highs
```

### Per-Driver Processing

Each IEM driver type applies different audio processing:

- **Bone Conduction:** Low-pass filter at 400Hz. The tone is felt, not heard. Reverb: long, dark, cave-like.
- **Dynamic Driver:** Full bandpass 200-2000Hz. Warm, present. Reverb: medium, room-like.
- **Balanced Armature:** Bandpass 500-3000Hz with slight HF shelf. Clear, precise. Reverb: short, minimal.
- **Electrostatic:** High-pass at 700Hz with air shelf at 8kHz. Airy, barely-there. Reverb: very short, almost dry.

### Volume Scaling

- 42Hz carrier: 0.03-0.05 gain (sub-perceptual)
- Solfeggio fundamental: 0.08-0.15 gain (audible, not overwhelming)
- Timbre overtones: 0.02-0.05 gain (subtle coloration)
- Total: never exceeds 0.25 gain (the Inn is not loud; the Inn is present)
