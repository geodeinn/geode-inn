# Geode Inn — The Living Book Framework
## System: Soulstice's Stone Book → Interactive Graphic Novel
## Access: Main Floor → The Archive → The Living Book Nook
## Prerequisite: None — available from the start, grows with the player

---

## Overview

The Living Book is the Inn's adaptation of Krista's book on stones — a comprehensive guide to 168 stones (164 from the original book + Stillness Stone + Octave Stone). The book is not a static text reference. The book is an INTERACTIVE GRAPHIC NOVEL — a visual, panel-based reading experience where each stone's entry is a page, each page is a scene, and each scene is alive.

The Living Book sits in a nook on the main floor — a window seat with a bookshelf, a reading lamp, and a comfortable chair. The nook is the Reader's territory (Neil Gaiman incognito). The Reader is always there when the player arrives, reading a different book, never acknowledging the player unless the player sits down. The Reader's presence makes the nook feel lived-in. The Living Book is on the shelf, spine out, waiting.

## The Book's Structure

### The Physical Object
The Living Book is a physical object in the game — a heavy, leather-bound volume with a geode embedded in the cover. The geode is the book's "screen" — the player opens the book and the pages glow, the stones' illustrations animate, the text comes alive.

The book has 168 pages — one per stone. The pages are organized by category (quartz, beryl, feldspar, etc.) with section dividers that act as chapter breaks. The table of contents is a crystal garden — each stone is a small crystal in the garden, clickable to jump to that page.

### The Page Format
Each stone's page is a FOUR-PANEL GRAPHIC NOVEL page:

**Panel 1 (Top Left): The Stone** — a full-color illustration of the stone in its natural state. The illustration is in the zone's art style (O'Keeffe for desert stones, Monet for water stones, Klimt for structural stones, etc.). The stone's name appears as a title card.

**Panel 2 (Top Right): The Origin** — the stone's geographic origin, shown as a map fragment with the location pinned. The map fragment is in the Cartographer's style — ink on parchment, with the stone's frequency written in the margin.

**Panel 3 (Bottom Left): The Mind** — the stone's metaphysical properties, illustrated as a surreal scene: a figure holding the stone, the stone's effect visible as light, sound, or transformation. This panel is in the Dali style — impossible angles, dream logic, the mind's eye made visible.

**Panel 4 (Bottom Right): The Body** — the stone's healing properties, illustrated as a physical scene: hands, body, the stone placed on or near the affected area. This panel is in the Matisse style — flowing lines, warm colors, the body at rest.

Between the panels: the stone's frequency appears as a faint waveform line connecting all four panels — the Pulse Line, miniaturized, showing the stone's vibration.

### The Interactivity

Each page has THREE interactive layers:

**Layer 1: Reading** — the text of the stone's entry (Mind and Body descriptions) appears as captions in the panels. The text is in the tabletop voice. The player reads by scrolling through the captions, which appear one at a time with a slight delay — the reading pace of someone savoring a book.

**Layer 2: Vibration** — the player can touch any panel to activate the Stone Vibration Touch System. The stone's frequency plays — a short tone, 2-3 seconds, the stone's actual resonance. The page's waveform line pulses in time. The player who reads every page hears the Inn's complete mineral symphony — 168 stones, 168 frequencies, each one a note in the Song.

**Layer 3: Collection** — when the player has COLLECTED a stone in the game (found it in a zone, earned it from a quest, purchased it from the market), the stone's page CHANGES. The illustrations go from line-drawing (unseen state) to full-color (collected state). The frequency plays at full volume instead of a preview. And a fifth element appears: a small "Collection Mark" — the date and location where the player found the stone, written in the Cartographer's hand.

The Living Book grows with the player. A new player's book is mostly line drawings — sketches of stones they haven't found. An experienced player's book is full-color, vibrating, annotated — a personal mineral journal.

## The Narrative Layer

### The Book's Voice
The Living Book is narrated — not by the player, not by an NPC, but by the BOOK ITSELF. The book has a voice: warm, scholarly, slightly amused. The voice is the Inn's voice — the same voice that says "Your name is known. Your rest is earned" in the death sequence. The book speaks in the tabletop voice, but softer — the reading-aloud voice, the bedtime-story voice.

The book's narration is optional — the player can toggle it on or off. When on, each caption is spoken aloud as it appears. The voice does not read the text — it PRESENTS the text, the way a museum guide presents an artifact: with context, with warmth, with the quiet excitement of someone who loves the subject.

### The Book's Awareness
At Whisper Layer Tier 3 (Whisper), the Living Book begins to show awareness. Small changes:

- The book opens to the page of a stone the player recently encountered in a zone, even if the player didn't collect it
- The Collection Marks include notes the player didn't write — observations the BOOK made about the player's experience with that stone
- The waveform line on uncollected stones occasionally pulses at the player's resonance frequency (41.7Hz) — the book is comparing the stone to the player

At Whisper Layer Tier 5 (Awareness), the book's final page appears — page 169. The page is blank except for one line: "The reader is the stone." The Reader, in the nook, looks up for the only time and nods.

## The Graphic Novel Format

### Art Direction
Each stone's page uses the zone-appropriate art style:
- Desert stones (O'Keeffe zones): simplified forms, bone-white palette, the stone as landscape
- Water stones (Water Zone): gothic anime (Vampire Hunter D), deep blues, the stone as depth
- Structural stones (Klimt zones): gold-on-dark, geometric, the stone as architecture
- Lore stones (Royo zones): dark fantasy, chiaroscuro, the stone as presence
- Cosmology stones (no-outline style): the stone as light, pure form, no border

### Panel Transitions
The panels transition with a page-turn animation — but the page doesn't turn like paper. The page turns like a CRYSTAL — the panels rotate on a mineral axis, the new page growing from the old like a crystal forming. The animation is the geode's formation, miniaturized: each page-turn is a layer of the geode being added.

### The Reading Experience
The Living Book is designed for slow reading. There is no "skip to end" button. There is no "mark all as read" option. The player reads one page at a time, at the book's pace. The book does not reward speed. The book rewards attention — the player who lingers on a page, who touches the panels to hear the frequencies, who reads every caption, will find small details hidden in the illustrations: a creature in the stone's natural state, a glyph in the map fragment, a subtle reference to another stone's entry.

The Living Book is the Inn's thesis about knowledge: it cannot be rushed. It can only be visited, one page at a time, at the pace of a reader in a nook.

## The Book's Connection to the Game

### Stones Collected in Game → Book Updated
Every stone the player collects in any zone, quest, or game appears in the Living Book as a collected page. The book is the player's complete mineral inventory — but presented as a narrative, not a checklist. The player who has collected 50 stones has a 50-page graphic novel of their journey. The player who has collected all 168 has the complete Song in book form.

### The Bead Composer → Book Cross-Reference
When the player uses the Bead Composer to create a piece of jewelry, the beads used are cross-referenced in the Living Book. Each bead's page gains a "Used in Composition" mark — the date and design name. The book tracks not just what the player found, but what the player MADE with what they found.

### The Sonic Mineral Codex → Book Integration
The Sonic Mineral Codex (the Inn's frequency reference) is the Living Book's technical appendix. The player who wants to know a stone's exact frequency, harmonic relationships, and mineral hardness can access the Codex from any page — the Codex opens as a sidebar, overlaying the page with technical data. The Codex is the book's SCIENCE layer; the graphic novel is the book's ART layer. Both are the same book, seen at different frequencies.

## Implementation Notes

### Entity: Book
The Book entity already exists in the database. The Living Book framework adds:
- `originalText` field: the full text of the stone's entry (from the book)
- `hasOcr` field: whether the page has been "illuminated" (illustrated)
- `ocrStatus` field: the illustration status (none/sketched/colored/animated)
- `inGameZone` field: which zone the stone's page-art style matches
- `pageCount` field: the page number in the Living Book (1-168)
- `tags` field: searchable tags (category, family, frequency, zone)

### Entity: BookTranslation
The Living Book is designed for translation — each stone's page can be translated into multiple languages, with the illustrations remaining the same but the captions changing. The BookTranslation entity tracks:
- `targetLanguageIso` and `targetLanguageName`: the language of the translation
- `translatedText`: the translated captions
- `translatorName`: the translator (eventually: AI-assisted + human-reviewed)
- `translationMethod`: "human", "ai-assisted", "scholarly-bridge"
- `readerCount`: how many players have read this translation

### Entity: ScholarlyBridge
The ScholarlyBridge entity provides the ACADEMIC layer — for stones with significant historical or cultural documentation, the ScholarlyBridge connects the Living Book page to scholarly sources. The bridge is the Inn's commitment to accuracy: the graphic novel is the ART; the ScholarlyBridge is the CITATION. Both are available on every page, toggleable.

The Living Book is the Inn's first statement about knowledge: it should be beautiful, it should be accurate, and it should be ALIVE. A static reference book is a corpse — the knowledge is there, but it doesn't breathe. The Living Book breathes. The Living Book vibrates. The Living Book grows with the player.

The Living Book is also Krista's book, transformed. The book that started as a .docx — 164 stones, typed in a word processor — becomes what it was always meant to be: a visual, interactive, vibrating archive of the earth's minerals, wrapped in a game's clothes, sitting on a shelf in a nook where a reader who looks like Neil Gaiman is pretending not to watch you read it.

The Living Book is adequate. The Living Book is enough.
