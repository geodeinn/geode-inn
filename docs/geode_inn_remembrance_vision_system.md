# Geode Inn — Remembrance Vision System
## Core Mechanic: "The Depth of Memory"
## Applies to: All Catacomb Zones (Ancient Civilizations)

### Design Philosophy

Every ancient civilization in the catacombs ends with a remembrance vision — a moment where the player briefly lives in that culture at its peak. This is the narrative spine's reincarnation mechanic operating at the zone level: you relive an existence, you see what was, and you carry that understanding forward.

The quality of the vision depends on how deeply the player engaged with the zone's puzzles, art, and symbols. This is not a scoring system. It's a memory system. Skim the surface, you get a glimpse. Truly read the space, you get the full memory.

This connects directly to the narrative spine: mastery of life patterns leads to cosmic awareness. Each catacomb zone is a life pattern. Read it deeply enough, and you don't just see the civilization — you start understanding *how civilizations rise and fall*. That understanding is what eventually unlocks the cosmology level.

## The Mechanic: Remembrance Depth

### Overview

Every catacomb zone has a hidden "Remembrance Depth" score that tracks *how* the player engaged with the content, not just *whether* they completed it. The score determines the quality of the final vision.

### What Increases Depth

| Action | Depth Gain | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Solve a puzzle through understanding (correct first attempt after examining clues) | +3 | The player read the culture |
| Solve a puzzle through retry (eventual correct answer) | +1 | The player persisted |
| Examine/read cultural artifacts (frescoes, seals, offerings, inscriptions) | +2 per unique artifact | The player is curious |
| Take the "wrong" path and discover a memory fragment | +2 | Exploration is rewarded |
| Complete a zone's hidden cultural challenge (optional, discovered through engagement) | +5 | The player went deeper than required |
| Listen to NPC dialogue fully (not skipping) | +1 per conversation | The player is present |
| Remain in a vision moment without moving (letting it play) | +2 per moment | The player is receiving |

### What Decreases Depth

| Action | Depth Loss | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Skip NPC dialogue | -1 | The player is not listening |
| Use a hint system (if implemented) | -2 | The player didn't read the culture |
| Rush through a room without examining artifacts | -1 | Speed without understanding |
| Solve a puzzle through brute force (trying every option rapidly) | -1 | The player is not thinking |

### Depth never goes below zero. The minimum vision is always a glimpse — never nothing.

## Vision Quality Tiers

### Tier 1: "A Glimpse" (Depth 0–30%)
**Duration:** 5 seconds
**Visual:** A single still image — a frozen moment of the civilization at its peak. Muted colors, like a faded photograph. No sound except a low ambient tone.
**Emotional register:** You saw something. You're not sure what. It's already fading.
**Example (Knossos):** A single frame — sunlight on the West Court. People as silhouettes. Still. Silent. Gone.

### Tier 2: "A Memory" (Depth 31–60%)
**Duration:** 15 seconds
**Visual:** A living scene — the civilization in motion, but only one location. Full color but soft focus at the edges. Ambient sound (footsteps, wind, distant voices) but no dialogue.
**Emotional register:** You were there, briefly. You remember the feeling more than the details.
**Example (Knossos):** The Great Hall — bull-leapers in motion, the crowd watching, the frescoes alive on the walls. No words. Just the rhythm of the leap and the sound of the crowd.

### Tier 3: "A Remembrance" (Depth 61–85%)
**Duration:** 30 seconds
**Visual:** Multiple connected scenes — the civilization unfolds across several locations. Full color, full detail. Sound design is complete: ambient, music, and one or two lines of dialogue spoken in the culture's language (reconstructed where possible, or in the Inn's narrative voice).
**Emotional register:** You lived there. You know the people. You can feel what was lost.
**Example (Knossos):** The full vision — corridors with people, bull-leaping in the Great Hall, priests in the Shrine Room, ships visible through a window. Ariadne's voice: "Now you have read the palace. You know what it was. You know what it lost."

### Tier 4: "A Life Lived" (Depth 86–100%)
**Duration:** 60 seconds
**Visual:** The civilization fully alive — the player can move within the vision (limited, walking speed, no interaction) and look around. Full sensory design: ambient sound, music, multiple voices, weather, time of day changing. The player briefly *is* someone in this civilization. They see through one person's eyes — a specific role based on what they engaged with most (the priestess, the bull-leaper, the scribe, the engineer).
**Emotional register:** You were someone. You remember their life. When the vision ends, the grief is real.
**Example (Knossos):** The player becomes a specific person — determined by which artifacts they examined most. If they spent time with the seal stones, they become a scribe in the workshop, watching the shipments come and go. If they engaged with the bull-leaping, they become a leaper, standing at the edge of the Great Hall, about to jump. The vision ends as the mountain begins to rumble.

## The "Reading" Check

At the end of each catacomb zone, after the vision, the player encounters a "reading" moment — a simple, wordless interaction that tests whether they understood the civilization, not just whether they solved the puzzles.

**Format:** The player stands before a blank wall (or a pool, or a fresco frame). Three symbols appear — drawn from the culture they just experienced. The player must choose the one that represents what the civilization *valued most*.

There is no "wrong" answer in terms of progression — the game doesn't block you. But the choice is recorded.

**What the reading does:**
- The player's choice determines what they carry forward — a "memory shard" keyed to that civilization's core value
- Memory shards accumulate across catacomb zones
- At the cosmology level, the collection of shards determines which cosmic gate the player can approach
- A player who collected shards from many civilizations has a richer set of cosmic options

**Example (Knossos reading):**
Three symbols:
1. The labrys (double axe) — represents *authority and the sacred*
2. The bull — represents *vitality and communion with the divine animal*
3. The dolphin — represents *the sea and connection to the wider world*

Each choice is valid. Each carries a different memory shard. Each opens a different path later.

## Zone-by-Zone Vision Framework

### Knossos (Greek)
- **Setting of vision:** The palace at its height, before the Thera eruption
- **Who you might become:** The bull-leaper, the scribe, the priestess, the engineer (based on engagement)
- **Ending trigger:** The mountain rumbles. The vision ends as the ash begins to fall.
- **Reading symbols:** Labrys, Bull, Dolphin
- **Memory shards:** Authority, Vitality, Connection

### Egypt (Hel gateway zone)
- **Setting of vision:** A temple complex at the height of a festival day
- **Who you might become:** The sistrum player (Bastet's Herding connection), the scribe, the priest, the embalmer
- **Ending trigger:** The sun sets. The vision ends as the first star appears — Sirius, the sign of the flood.
- **Reading symbols:** Ankh, Scarab, Sistrum
- **Memory shards:** Eternity, Transformation, Harmony

### Mesopotamia (Niflheim overlap)
- **Setting of vision:** A ziggurat city at dawn, the streets filling with people
- **Who you might become:** The astronomer, the brewer, the king, the farmer
- **Ending trigger:** The river changes course. The vision ends as the city's fate shifts.
- **Reading symbols:** Cuneiform tablet, Zigurrat, Cedar
- **Memory shards:** Knowledge, Ascension, Roots

### Norse/Jotunheim
- **Setting of vision:** A longhouse in winter, the fire burning, the story being told
- **Who you might become:** The skald, the warrior, the weaver, the shipwright
- **Ending trigger:** The fire burns down. The vision ends as the first snow of Fimbulwinter begins to fall.
- **Reading symbols:** Rune, Hammer, Web
- **Memory shards:** Memory, Force, Fate

### Celtic/Alfheim (Sacred Grove)
- **Setting of vision:** A grove at the turning of the year — Samhain, the boundary thin
- **Who you might become:** The druid, the bard, the smith, the healer
- **Ending trigger:** The veil closes. The vision ends as the grove goes quiet.
- **Reading symbols:** Ogham, Cauldron, Torc
- **Memory shards:** Language, Abundance, Sovereignty

### East Asian/Vanaheim
- **Setting of vision:** A mountain temple at the hour of the hare (dawn)
- **Who you might become:** The calligrapher, the gardener, the strategist, the monk
- **Ending trigger:** The cherry blossoms fall. The vision ends as the last petal lands.
- **Reading symbols:** Brush, Lotus, Mountain
- **Memory shards:** Expression, Enlightenment, Permanence

## The Accumulation: What Memory Shards Do

Memory shards are the player's accumulated understanding of human civilization. They are not a score. They are a *vocabulary*.

At the cosmology level (upstairs), the player approaches the cosmic gates referenced in the narrative spine:
- **Jupiter** — the gate of cosmic awareness
- **Mars** — the prison
- **Pluto** — the gate of transformation/death

The shards determine which gates the player can *understand*, not which they can *open*. A gate opens when the player has the right combination of shards — but the combination isn't a key. It's a *comprehension*. You can't walk through the gate of cosmic awareness if you don't understand what awareness means across human civilization.

**Example:** The gate of Pluto (transformation/death) requires the player to have experienced at least one civilization's ending (the vision's ending trigger) AND to have chosen a shard that represents transformation (e.g., the Scarab from Egypt, the Web from Norse, the Lotus from East Asian). The game doesn't tell you this — you discover it by trying to approach the gate and feeling whether you can enter.

This is the narrative spine made mechanical: mastery of life patterns leads to cosmic awareness. The life patterns are the civilizations. The mastery is the depth of engagement. The cosmic awareness is the ability to approach the gates.

## Technical Implementation

### Data Model (PlayerProgress entity additions)
```
remembranceDepth: {
  knossos: { depth: number, visionTier: number, readingChoice: string, shard: string },
  egypt: { depth: number, visionTier: number, readingChoice: string, shard: string },
  mesopotamia: { ... },
  norse: { ... },
  celtic: { ... },
  eastAsian: { ... }
}

totalShards: string[]
cosmicGatesAccessible: string[]
```

### Godot Implementation
- Each catacomb zone tracks player actions and calculates depth in real-time
- The depth score is hidden from the player — no HUD element, no progress bar
- The vision plays automatically at the zone's endpoint
- The vision tier is rendered based on depth score
- The reading moment is a simple UI interaction (three symbols, one choice)
- The shard is stored in PlayerProgress and persists across sessions
- The cosmic gate access check happens at the cosmology level, comparing shards to gate requirements

### Audio Design
- Tier 1: Single low tone (the zone's resonant frequency)
- Tier 2: Ambient soundscape, no music
- Tier 3: Ambient + zone-specific musical theme from the music direction
- Tier 4: Full soundscape + music + voice + the zone's resonant frequency as a bass note throughout
- All tiers end with silence — the silence after the vision is the most important sound in the game

## Design Principles

1. **The vision is earned, not given.** You get it by engaging, not by completing.
2. **Depth is invisible.** No progress bar, no score display. The player doesn't know their depth until the vision plays — and even then, they just experience the quality, they don't see a number.
3. **Every tier is valid.** A glimpse is still a real experience. The game never punishes the player for engaging less — it just rewards them for engaging more.
4. **The reading is personal.** There's no wrong shard. The choice reflects what the player valued, not what the game thinks they should value.
5. **The shards are vocabulary, not currency.** You don't spend them. You carry them. They determine what you can comprehend at the cosmology level.
6. **The silence after is sacred.** After every vision, there is silence. The player sits with what they saw. The game does not immediately push them forward. This silence is deliberate — it's the space where understanding forms.
7. **Reincarnation is understanding, not repetition.** Replaying a zone doesn't just let you try again — it lets you see more. The depth system means a second playthrough with deeper engagement yields a richer vision. You didn't "do it again." You "understood more."
