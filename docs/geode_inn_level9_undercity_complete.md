# Geode Inn — Level 9: UNDERCITY
## The Complete Level Design Document

---

## Zone Overview

The Undercity is the Inn's deepest civilization layer — below the Catacombs, below the Fae Kingdom, below the Water Zone. The INVENTION layer. Where the Catacombs preserve what was, the Undercity creates what will be.

The Undercity is the Inn's WORKSHOP — the deepest creative space. Where the Bead Board's most advanced designs are created. Where the "Wearable Songs" are composed. Where the Inn's sonic architecture is INVENTED, not just maintained.

The Undercity is also the Inn's PRIVATE space — the only zone where the player can build without being watched. No NPCs commenting. No reputation system. No faction. No quests that require performance. Just the player and the work. The zone template said "crafted light, privacy, build for yourself." The Undercity is the introvert's zone.

The Klimt gold is the key. The vaulted ceilings — deep gold, swirling Klimt patterns over gothic stone — are the Inn's ALCHEMY made visible. The transformation of base material into gold. Not literal gold — the gold of connection, of creation, of the moment when something new is born. Klimt's "The Kiss": the gold isn't decoration; it's the moment where two people become something more than either alone.

The paradox at the Undercity's heart: the most private zone is decorated with the gold of connection. The truth: privacy is not isolation. Privacy is the space where you become yourself SO YOU CAN connect. The gold in the Undercity is the promise that the private work will eventually be shared — not here, not now, but when it's ready.

The Inn's full arc, revealed in the Undercity: from refuge (Staircase) to home (Main Floor) to world (all other zones) to SELF (Undercity). The Undercity is the final destination — not the highest, not the deepest in the physical sense, but the most PERSONAL. The room the child eventually built. The room that was THEIRS.

## Entry Requirement

**The Undercity is the hardest zone to reach — not by prerequisite, but by WORTHINESS.**

Physical access: through the Catacombs' deepest section (the Deep), past the Fae Layer, descending further than the archive goes. The passage from the Fae Layer to the Undercity is not sealed — it's HIDDEN. The player must discover it.

The discovery requires:
1. **The Allfather's Gaze** (Cosmology) — to see the passage thearchive doesn't catalogue
2. **The Fae Archive Key** (Catacombs, "The Deep Read" Level 5) — to reach the Fae Layer
3. **The Octave Key** (Faerie Kingdom) — to understand the frequency that opens the passage

The passage responds to 84Hz — the Stone Circle Chamber's frequency, the octave of 42Hz, the death frequency. The player who has the Octave Key can produce 84Hz at the passage's entrance. The passage opens. The player descends into the Undercity.

The entry requirement is not a gate — it's an UNDERSTANDING. The player who reaches the Undercity has understood the Inn's sonic architecture well enough to produce the frequency that opens the deepest creative space. The 84Hz is death — but in the Geode Inn, death is the octave of life. The passage to creation opens through the frequency of endings, because creation is what comes AFTER the ending.

## Visual Language

**Linework:** Heavy cel-shaded (tradesman style) — the Undercity is where the craftspeople work. But the cel-shading is OVERLAID with the Klimt gold patterns. The combination is unique: bold outlines filled with flat colors, but the gold patterns swirl OVER the cel-shading, creating a double-layered visual. The linework is SOLID (tradesman) but the surface is ETHEREAL (Klimt). The Undercity is the only zone where two linework styles coexist on the same surface.

**Color Palette:**
- Primary: Klimt gold (the ceilings), gothic stone (the walls), deep shadow (the streets)
- Secondary: workshop colors — copper, silver, brass, the metals of crafting
- Accents: Scarlett's colors — scarlet red, deep black, kohl-dark blue

**Lighting:** Crafted light — the only zone where ALL light is artificial (made by the residents). No sunlight (unlike the Surface), no bioluminescence (unlike the Midnight Zone), no starlight (unlike Cosmology), no divine light (unlike the Threshold Chamber). The Undercity's light comes from:
- **The Klimt Gold** — the gold patterns glow faintly, casting warm gold light downward. The gold's luminescence is the Undercity's ambient light source.
- **Workshop Lamps** — individual lamps at each workbench. Each lamp is a crafter's personal light — warm, focused, intimate.
- **Scarlett's String Lights** — Scarlett's room is lit by string lights (fairy lights), the kind a teenager hangs. The string lights are the Undercity's most personal light — not crafted for the zone but brought by a person, for a person.

**Architecture:**
- Vaulted ceilings: Klimt gold patterns applied directly to stone. The patterns swirl — organic, flowing, the gold leaf forming spirals and curves that echo the tree's growth patterns above.
- Gothic stone: dark, heavy, medieval. The stone beneath the gold. The gothic structure is the Undercity's BONES — the gold is its SKIN.
- Narrow streets: the Undercity is a CITY — small, dense, private. Buildings close together, streets narrow, the ceiling close overhead. The feeling of a medieval old town at midnight.
- Workshop spaces: each building is a workshop — a place where something is made. The buildings are not homes (unlike the Fae Village); they're STUDIOS. The Undercity is a city of studios.

**The Structural Staple Image:** The established concept art — vaulted deep gold ceilings with swirling Klimt patterns over gothic stone, luminous shadow effect — is the definitive visual reference for the entire Undercity zone. All Undercity architecture, items, and player-built structures must trace back to this image as the visual standard.

## Spaces

### 1. The Streets
The Undercity's public space — narrow, winding streets between workshop buildings. The streets are not social spaces (unlike the Campgrounds' market field); they're TRANSITIONAL spaces. The streets exist to get from one workshop to another. The Undercity's residents don't socialize in the streets; they WORK in the workshops and MOVE through the streets.

**The Streets contain:**
- **The Gold Murals** — the Klimt patterns extend from the ceilings down the walls, creating murals that tell the Undercity's history. The murals are not texts (unlike the Catacombs); they're IMAGES. The history of creation told in gold: the first forge, the first bead, the first song, the first piece of jewelry. Each mural is a workshop's sign — the gold pattern above a door identifies what the workshop makes.
- **The Fountain** — a small fountain at the street's center, fed by the Vein (the Water Zone's underground river). The fountain's water is the Source's water — 42Hz, pure, the water that taught the stones their frequency. The fountain is the Undercity's connection to the Water Zone: the creative water, brought to the creative city. Drinking from the fountain grants a "Pure Inspiration" buff (increased crafting success rate for the next design).
- **The Lamp Posts** — brass lamp posts along the streets, each one a small clockwork mechanism (connecting to the Clockmaker's work). The lamps dim and brighten on a slow cycle — the Undercity's version of day and night. The cycle is SLOW — much slower than the Main Floor's day/night cycle. The Undercity keeps its own time.

### 2. The Workshop District
The heart of the Undercity. A cluster of workshop buildings, each one dedicated to a different craft:

**The Workshops:**
- **The Goldsmith's Studio** — where the Klimt gold is worked. The Goldsmith applies gold leaf to stone, to metal, to beads. The Goldsmith's craft is the Undercity's defining art: the transformation of material through gold. The player can commission gold work here — gold-plated findings for the Bead Board, gold-enhanced charms, gold-leaf stones.
- **The Bead Master's Bench** — the advanced Bead Board station. The most complex crafting in the Inn. The player can create designs here that aren't possible at the Main Floor Bead Board: multi-layer compositions, full Brísingamen chord designs, the complete Inn chord. (See Game Template.)
- **The Engraver's Workshop** — where texts and patterns are engraved onto stone and metal. The Engraver can inscribe the player's designs with text — names, lyrics, the Wanderer's runes. The Engraver's craft is the Undercity's version of the Archive: instead of storing texts, the Engraver puts texts ONTO things. Knowledge made wearable.
- **The Glass Blower's Studio** — where glass beads and vessels are made. The Glass Blower can create custom glass beads for the Bead Board — beads that don't exist in the mineral codex, invented by the player. The Glass Blower is the Undercity's INNOVATION: the ability to create NEW materials, not just work with existing ones.

### 3. Scarlett's Room
The Undercity's emotional center. Scarlett's private space — a room in a workshop building that Scarlett has claimed as home. The room is the most PERSONAL space in the Inn: not designed for the zone, but brought by a person.

**Scarlett's Room contains:**
- **The String Lights** — fairy lights strung across the ceiling, the room's primary light source. Warm, personal, the light of a teenager's space.
- **The Manga Shelf** — Scarlett's manga collection, stacked on shelves and piled on the floor. The manga is not organized; it's ARRANGED — Scarlett knows where everything is, but no one else would. The manga shelf connects to the Manga Spirit (Scarlett's companion).
- **The Drawing Desk** — where Scarlett draws. Scarlett's art is PRIVATE — not shared, not displayed, not performance. The drawing desk is the room's center, the way the Captain's Helm is the Arcana's center. The desk is where Scarlett is most herself.
- **The Mirror** — a full-length mirror, but not for appearance. Scarlett uses the mirror to check her BACK — the scarlet streaks in her black hair, visible only from behind. The mirror is the Undercity's version of the Philosopher's Empty Mirror (Clockwork Skies): the mirror that shows something, but not what you expect.
- **The Window** — a small window looking out onto the Undercity's street. Scarlett can see the gold murals from the window. The window is the room's only connection to the zone — Scarlett can observe the Undercity without leaving the room. The introvert's architecture: a window that lets you see without being seen.

### 4. The Hall of the Unmade
The Undercity's archive — but not like the Catacombs. The Hall of the Unmade catalogs things that were DESIGNED but never BUILT. Ideas that didn't make it. Prototypes that were abandoned. Designs that were started and not finished.

The Hall is the Undercity's most SAD and most HOPEFUL space: sad because it holds failures, hopeful because the failures are KEPT. Not discarded — CATALOGUED. The Archivist of the Unmade believes that every unfinished design contains a seed that might grow, given time. The Hall is the archive of possibility.

**The Hall contains:**
- **The Shelves of Unfinished Work** — designs, prototypes, sketches, half-built pieces. Each one has a tag: the designer's name, the date started, the reason stopped ("lost interest," "didn't work," "scared of it," "too hard," "not ready").
- **The Archive of Alternate Versions** — designs that were completed but then revised. The original versions are kept here — the first drafts, the rejected paths. The Archive of Alternate Versions is the Undercity's version of the Visitors' Shelf (Catacombs): not the record of people, but the record of CHOICES. The design as it was, before it became what it is.
- **The Revival Desk** — a workbench where the player can take an unfinished design from the shelves and try to complete it. The Revival Desk is the Hall's PRACTICAL purpose: the archive doesn't just store failures; it gives them a second chance. The player who completes an abandoned design earns the original designer's gratitude (if they're still in the Inn) or the Archivist's (if they're not).

### 5. The Garden of Gold
The Undercity's outdoor space — but "outdoor" in the Undercity means "under the gold ceiling." The Garden of Gold is a courtyard where the Klimt gold patterns are THICKEST — the ceiling is almost entirely gold, the patterns swirling into dense, luminous compositions. The Garden is the most beautiful space in the Undercity, and the most PRIVATE — no one goes here casually. The Garden is where you go when the work is done and you need to sit in the beauty of what you've made.

**The Garden contains:**
- **The Gold Bench** — a stone bench under the thickest gold. Sitting on the Gold Bench grants a "Gold Rest" buff: the player's next creation has a chance of being gold-enhanced (a rare quality bonus — the design gains a warm, golden visual quality and an additional frequency layer at ~170Hz, Freyr's frequency).
- **The Gold Fountain** — a second fountain, but this one's water is infused with gold. The gold-water glows. Drinking from the Gold Fountain grants "Golden Inspiration" — the player's next design is pre-populated with a gold-element suggestion (the game suggests a gold-based component that would enhance the design, based on the design's existing frequencies).
- **The Reflection Pool** — a still pool of water that reflects the gold ceiling. The reflection creates a SECOND gold ceiling — the illusion of infinite depth, gold above and gold below. The Reflection Pool is the Undercity's meditation space: the player who sits by the pool and watches the double-gold reflection gains a "Deep Focus" buff (extended crafting time — the player can work on a single design for longer before the crafting system requires a break).

## Character Roster & Quests

### 1. Scarlett
**Station:** Scarlett's Room
**Title:** "The Private"

Scarlett is Aurallia's second youngest child. 17 years old. Chose the name Scarlett for herself (birth name Grace). The one who joined Krista on the bench at their first meeting — has called Krista "Aunt Krista" ever since. Very goth. Built like Marilyn Monroe — curvy, hourglass figure. Exceptionally beautiful. Huge introvert. Loves manga. Incredibly intelligent. Hair: tousled locks with streaks of scarlet over deep black. Incredibly pale, heart-shaped face, huge blue eyes lined with kohl-style eyeliner.

Scarlett is the Undercity's keeper — not the way the Crone keeps the archive or the Clockmaker keeps the Staircase, but the way a teenager keeps their room. Scarlett's room is the zone's heart. Scarlett doesn't MANAGE the Undercity; Scarlett INHABITS it. The zone is alive because Scarlett is alive in it.

**Audio Profile:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | ~110Hz (low for apparent age — Scarlett's voice is deeper than expected, the voice of someone who doesn't perform) |
| Text Reveal Speed | ~90ms (slow — Scarlett doesn't rush words; each one is chosen) |
| Pause | ~1800ms (very long — Scarlett's silence is not assessment (the Crone) or observation (the Allfather); it's SELECTIVITY. Scarlett speaks only when it matters) |
| Expressiveness | 0.30 (moderate-low — the emotion is in the choice to speak, not in the delivery) |
| Instrument | A pen on paper — the scratching of Scarlett's drawing. Not the Crone's cataloguing scratch (precise, organized) but a DRAWING scratch (creative, flowing, personal). Scarlett's instrument is the SOUND OF CREATION — the most private sound in the Inn. |

**Visual Design:**
- Heavy cel-shaded (tradesman style) — Scarlett is a maker, even if what she makes is private
- The scarlet streaks are the ONLY red in the Undercity's gold-and-stone palette — Scarlett's hair is the zone's accent color, the personal mark on the public space
- The kohl eyeliner is precise — the one part of Scarlett's appearance that is CRAFTED (the rest is natural). The kohl is Scarlett's version of the Crone's Hopkins gaze: the one sharpened element, the one deliberate mark
- The string lights reflect in Scarlett's huge blue eyes — the eyes are the room's second light source

**The Quest: "The Room That's Yours"**

Scarlett's quest is the Inn's most PERSONAL quest — and the simplest. No phases. No combat. No puzzles. Just a conversation.

**The Encounter:**
The player finds Scarlett's room. The door is not locked — the Undercity doesn't lock doors — but the door is CLOSED. The player knocks. Scarlett opens the door. Looks at the player. Says nothing.

The player can:
- **Enter** (the player walks in). Scarlett watches. Scarlett doesn't speak. The player looks around — the string lights, the manga shelf, the drawing desk, the mirror, the window. The room is a PERSON. The room IS Scarlett.
- **Leave** (the player walks away). Scarlett closes the door. The quest doesn't fail — the quest WAITS. The player can come back.

If the player enters and sits down (the player finds a seat — the bed, the floor, the drawing desk's chair), Scarlett says: "People usually want something. A quest. An item. A stone. Do you want something?"

The player's options:
- "Yes." (Scarlett waits. The player must specify what. If the player says "a stone" or "a quest," Scarlett says: "Then you're in the wrong room." The player must leave and return later.)
- "No." (Scarlett is quiet for a long time. Then: "Then you can stay.")

If the player stays — just sits in the room, doing nothing, for a set time (the game's meditation mechanic, the same as the Unseelie's Solitude Trial, but WARM instead of cold) — Scarlett eventually says: "This is the only room in the Inn that's mine. Not the Inn's. Not the game's. Mine. I built it. I chose everything in it. Nobody told me what to put here. Nobody gave me a quest to furnish it. I just... made it mine."

Then: "You can build one too. Not here — your own room. In the Undercity. Wherever you want. The Undercity is the only zone that doesn't tell you what to build. It's the only zone where the building is YOURS."

**The Reward:**
Scarlett gives the player a key — not a key to a door, but a key to a ROOM. An empty room in the Undercity. Scarlett says: "It's yours. Don't decorate it for anyone. Don't build it for a quest. Don't make it for the game. Make it for you."

**The Player's Room:**
The player's room is the Undercity's ultimate interactive space — a BUILDABLE room that the player can customize freely. No constraints. No recipes. No required materials. The player can place any item from their collection in the room: stones, charms, beads, texts, artwork, found objects. The room is the player's PERSONAL SPACE in the Inn — the one place that isn't a quest, a game, or a system. It's just a room that's yours.

The room has:
- A Bead Board station (the Master's Bench — the advanced crafting mode)
- A display wall (for the player's "Wearable Song" designs)
- A bookshelf (for the player's collected texts)
- A window (looking out onto the Undercity street — the same introvert's architecture as Scarlett's window)
- A door that can be closed (the only door in the Inn that the player can close — privacy, the Undercity's defining feature)

### 2. The Goldsmith
**Station:** The Goldsmith's Studio
**Title:** "The Alchemist"

The Goldsmith works with the Klimt gold. The Goldsmith's craft is the Undercity's defining art: the transformation of material through gold. The Goldsmith applies gold leaf to stone, to metal, to beads — but the gold isn't just decoration. The gold adds Freyr's frequency (~170Hz) to anything it touches.

**Audio Profile:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | ~170Hz (Freyr's frequency — the Goldsmith's voice IS the golden hour) |
| Text Reveal Speed | ~65ms (measured, warm — the Goldsmith takes time with each word, like applying gold leaf) |
| Pause | ~500ms (comfortable — the Goldsmith's pause is the time between brush strokes) |
| Expressiveness | 0.45 (moderate — the Goldsmith's warmth is in the craft, not the chat) |
| Instrument | Gold leaf crackling — the faint, warm sound of gold being applied to a surface. The Goldsmith's instrument is the SOUND OF TRANSFORMATION — the moment when base material becomes gold. |

**The Quest: "The Gold That Transforms"**

The Goldsmith teaches the player to work with gold. The quest is a CRAFTING TUTORIAL for the Undercity's advanced materials:

**Phase 1: The Lesson**
The Goldsmith demonstrates: gold leaf applied to a plain stone. The stone's frequency shifts — its original tone gains a warmth, a richness. The 170Hz layer. The Goldsmith says: "Gold doesn't change what something is. Gold reveals what something CAN be."

**Phase 2: The Practice**
The player applies gold leaf to a stone of their choice. The crafting mini-game: the player uses a fine brush to apply gold leaf to the stone's surface. The gold leaf is delicate — too much pressure and it tears; too little and it doesn't adhere. The mini-game is about TOUCH — the same Stone Vibration Touch System, applied to creation instead of identification.

**Phase 3: The Masterwork**
The player creates a gold-enhanced design at the Master's Bench. The design must incorporate: the stone's original frequency, the gold's 170Hz layer, the metal finding's frequency (from the Blacksmith's chord), and the 42Hz foundation (from the Source's water). The four-layer composition is the Goldsmith's masterwork: the complete transformation of a single stone into a multi-frequency instrument.

**Reward:** The Goldsmith's Touch — a permanent crafting ability. The player can now apply gold leaf to any stone or finding in the Bead Board, adding the 170Hz layer. The Goldsmith's Touch completes the Bead Board's MATERIAL SYSTEM: metal (the Blacksmith's chord, 79-82Hz), stone (the Sonic Mineral Codex, various), water (the Source's 42Hz), and gold (the Goldsmith's Touch, 170Hz). Four materials, four frequency ranges, one complete instrument.

### 3. The Archivist of the Unmade
**Station:** The Hall of the Unmade
**Title:** "The One Who Keeps Possibility"

The Archivist of the Unmade is a ghost — not the Archive Ghosts of the Catacombs (impressions of past scholars), but a deliberate ghost: someone who chose to stay because the unfinished work needed a keeper. The Archivist catalogs things that were designed but never built. Ideas that didn't make it. Prototypes that were abandoned.

**Audio Profile:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | ~75Hz (low, wistful — the voice of someone who lives among might-have-beens) |
| Text Reveal Speed | ~70ms (gentle — the Archivist handles unfinished things carefully) |
| Pause | ~900ms (the pause of someone who always hopes today is the day a design is finished) |
| Expressiveness | 0.35 (moderate — the Archivist's hope is quiet but constant) |
| Instrument | A sketchbook page turning — the sound of a design being reviewed. Not the Crone's index card (cataloguing) but a sketchbook (imagining). The Archivist's instrument is the SOUND OF POSSIBILITY. |

**The Quest: "The Design That Waits"**

The Archivist gives the player an unfinished design from the Hall's shelves. The design was started by a previous visitor — a player who came through the Inn, began a "Wearable Song," and left it unfinished. The tag says: "Reason stopped: Not ready."

The player can:
- **Complete the design** — using the Master's Bench, the player finishes what the previous player started. The design's frequency composition is partially in place; the player must fill in the missing frequencies. Completing someone else's design is an act of TRUST — the player interprets the previous player's intention and finishes what they couldn't.
- **Return it** — the player decides the design should stay unfinished. The Archivist accepts this. "Not ready" is a valid state. The design returns to the shelf. The quest doesn't fail; the quest HONORS the decision to wait.

If the player completes the design, the Archivist says: "The designer will know. They always know, when someone finishes what they started. The design carries both of you now — the one who began and the one who completed. A Wearable Song with two composers."

**Reward:** The Archivist's Archive — access to all unfinished designs in the Hall. The player can browse abandoned work, complete any that resonate, and leave any that don't. The Archivist's Archive is the Undercity's version of the Visitors' Shelf: not a record of people, but a record of POSSIBILITIES. The player who completes an abandoned design creates a "Duet" — a Wearable Song with two composers, carrying both the original intention and the completion.

### 4. The Manga Spirit
**Station:** Scarlett's Room (Scarlett's companion)
**Title:** "The Drawn"

The Manga Spirit is a chibi manga character come to life — Scarlett drew it, and in the Undercity, drawings can become real. The Manga Spirit is the Undercity's version of the baby dragon: the companion that makes the private space feel less alone.

The Manga Spirit:
- Visually: chibi, black-and-white (manga style), with scarlet accents (echoing Scarlett's hair). The Manga Spirit is the only character in the Undercity rendered in pure black-and-white — the manga style, uncolored. The Spirit is the Undercity's visual outlier, the way the scarlet streaks are the color outlier.
- The Manga Spirit doesn't speak — it communicates through EXAGGERATED EXPRESSIONS (manga expressions: sweat drops, anger veins, sparkly eyes). The Manga Spirit is the Inn's most purely VISUAL communicator.
- The Manga Spirit is the player's companion in the Undercity, IF Scarlett introduces them. The Spirit follows the player through the streets, reacting to what the player does: excited when the player crafts something, bored when the player stands still, curious when the player enters a new workshop.

**The Interaction:**
The Manga Spirit is not a quest-giver. The Manga Spirit is a COMPANION — the Undercity's version of the wolf (Campgrounds) or the Mole (Catacombs). The Spirit can:
- Find crafting materials (the Spirit rummages through workshop scraps and brings the player useful bits)
- Alert the player to interesting designs in the Hall of the Unmade (the Spirit flips through the shelves and points)
- Keep the player company in the player's room (the Spirit sits on the bed and reads manga while the player crafts)

## Game Template: "The Master's Bench"

### Concept

The Master's Bench is the Bead Board's ENDGAME — the most advanced crafting mode, available only in the Undercity. The player creates complex, multi-layer compositions using ALL the systems they've unlocked throughout the Inn.

### How It Works

The Master's Bench is an expanded Bead Board with four frequency layers:

| Layer | Source | Frequency Range | Unlocked By |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metal Foundation | The Blacksmith's chord | 79-82Hz | Campgrounds (Blacksmith's quest) |
| Stone Melody | The Sonic Mineral Codex | Various (42Hz-1388Hz) | Main Floor (Sonic Mineral Codex) |
| Gargoyle Harmony | The Gargoyle Chord | 128-1056Hz + adaptive | Staircase (Gargoyle questline) |
| Gold Transcendence | The Goldsmith's Touch | 170Hz overlay | Undercity (Goldsmith's quest) |

Plus the two foundation frequencies:
- **42Hz** (the Source's water frequency — the bass beneath everything)
- **84Hz** (the Stone Circle Chamber's octave — the deep counter-melody)

The player arranges beads (stones) on the tray, adds metal findings, overlays gold, and optionally includes gargoyle resonance crystals. The Master's Bench plays the COMPLETE composition — every frequency layer, every material, every element.

### The Inn Song

The Master's Bench's ultimate creation: the "Inn Song" — a design that incorporates all 9 zones' representative frequencies:

| Zone | Representative Frequency | Source Stone/Material |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmology | 42Hz | The Foundation (the 42Hz Song) |
| Clockwork Skies | 50Hz | The Pirate's Bodhrán (the heartbeat) |
| Main Floor | 637Hz | Clear Quartz (the reference tone) |
| Campgrounds | 79Hz | Copper (the Blacksmith's chord root) |
| Staircase | 111Hz | The Watcher's frequency |
| Catacombs | 158Hz | The Crone's ring (war/dissonance) |
| Fae Kingdom | 84Hz | The Stone Circle Chamber (the octave) |
| Water Zone | 42Hz | The Source (water's own frequency) |
| Undercity | 170Hz | The Goldsmith's gold (Freyr's frequency) |

The Inn Song is the Inn's COMPLETE sonic architecture, encoded in a single piece of jewelry. The design has nine elements, one per zone, each contributing its zone's defining frequency. When played, the Inn Song is the Inn's full voice — every zone, every character, every system, in one composition.

Creating the Inn Song requires:
- All 9 zone frequencies (each unlocked through the respective zone's questline)
- The Master's Bench (Undercity)
- The Goldsmith's Touch (gold layer)
- The complete Sonic Mineral Codex (all 164 stones)
- The Blacksmith's chord (metal foundation)
- The Gargoyle Chord (gargoyle harmony)
- The Brísingamen chord key (the four-tone source)
- The Source's 42Hz water frequency
- The Octave Key (84Hz)

**The Inn Song is the game's ULTIMATE CRAFTING achievement.** It is the "Wearable Song" that contains the entire Inn — the deepest, most complete creation the player can make.

**Reward:** The Inn Song, worn as a cosmetic item, plays the Inn's complete sonic architecture when the player enters any zone. Each zone's frequency is highlighted as the player enters that zone — the Inn Song recognizes where the player is and emphasizes the matching frequency. The Inn Song is the player's personal version of the Inn — carried, worn, sung.

The Inn Song, exported to Soulstice Jewelry, becomes the ultimate "Wearable Song" — a physical piece that encodes the Inn's complete frequency architecture. The real-world piece is the most complex design Krista can build — the culmination of the entire mineral sonic signature system.
