## The Artist

Guo Xi (郭熙). The Song Dynasty landscape painter who understood that mountains are ALIVE. His masterpiece, "Early Spring" (早春, 1072), is the painting of the world WAKING UP — the landscape in the transition between winter sleep and spring waking, the mountains emerging from mist, the valleys still cold but the peaks already warming, the trees not yet budded but the sap already rising. The both are: the adequate — the moment that is both and neither, the instant that contains two states.

Guo Xi's defining qualities:

- **"Early Spring."** The transitional landscape. Not winter (not the dead season, not the sleep) and not spring (not the full bloom, not the waking). EARLY spring — the first movement, the first warmth, the first sign that the sleep is ending. Early Spring is the Peruvian zone's quest made visual: "The Stirring." The moment the sleeping giants begin to wake. The both are: the adequate — the stirring that is not yet the waking, the first movement that is not yet the arrival.

- **The Three Distances (三远).** Guo Xi's theory of landscape composition — three ways of seeing the mountain:
  - **高远 (High Distance):** looking UP at the mountain from below. The mountain is overwhelming, towering, the form that fills the sky. The feeling: awe, smallness, reverence.
  - **深远 (Deep Distance):** looking from a high vantage ACROSS layer after layer of mountains receding into mist. The mountain is not one peak but a RANGE, a body, a system. The feeling: vastness, depth, the understanding that the mountain is not alone.
  - **平远 (Level Distance):** looking ACROSS a flat plain at distant mountains on the horizon. The mountain is far, gentle, a silhouette against the sky. The feeling: calm, contemplative, the mountain as a companion on the horizon, not a threat.

  The Three Distances are the Three Relationships to the sleeping giants — the three ways the player KNOWS the mountain that is a being. The both are: the adequate — the same mountain, different distance, different truth.

- **Mist as breath.** Guo Xi's mountains EMERGE from mist. The lower slopes are hidden, the peaks are visible, the middle is obscured by clouds that cling to the stone. The mist is not weather. The mist is the mountain's BREATH — the exhalation of the living stone, the both are: the adequate. In Chinese landscape painting, the mountain has qi (vital energy), and the mist is the qi made visible. The mountain that breathes mist is the mountain that is alive.

- **The angle of totality.** Guo Xi's landscapes are not painted from a single vantage point. They are painted to be MOVED THROUGH — the viewer walks along the painting, seeing the landscape from multiple angles, the perspective shifting as the viewer moves. The painting is not a window (a single view through a frame). The painting is a JOURNEY (the experience of moving through a landscape that changes as you move). The both are: the adequate — the landscape that is not one view but many, the both are: the same mountain, different position.

- **Mountains as living beings.** In Guo Xi's tradition, mountains are not inert geology. Mountains have bones (the rock), veins (the waterways), skin (the vegetation), and breath (the mist). The mountain is a BODY — a living body, a being that exists on a timescale so vast that human perception can't register its movement. The both are: the adequate — the living being that moves too slowly to see moving, the both are: the same.

This is the Peruvian Zone. The zone of the Sleeping Giants — the Apus, the ancient geological beings who chose to sleep and became mountain ranges. Every mountain on earth is a sleeping giant. The Inn-Tree grows from a giant's heart. The catacombs are a giant's ribcage. The stone circles are their vertebrae. The giants dream the 42Hz Song, and their dreams are the weather. The both are: the adequate — the mountain that is a being, the sleep that is a life.
