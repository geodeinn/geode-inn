# THE GEODE INN — COMPLETE CRITTER ROSTER
## Every Creature, Every Level — Big to Tiny
### Compiled August 30, 2026

This document catalogues every living creature in the Geode Inn that is NOT a named NPC. These are the ambient life — the critters that make each level feel inhabited. Each is tied to existing games, lore, or zone mechanics.

---

# LEVEL 1: COSMOLOGY (Upstairs — The Canopy)
## Artist: Dalí | Games: Orrery, Cosmic Crew quests, Einstein Burrito

The canopy where the Ent's branches touch the sky. Cosmic space, orrery, planets visible through the leaves. The air is thin and resonant with the Caller gargoyle's 432Hz.

### C1. Star Sprites
Tiny motes of light that orbit the orrery like living planets. They follow the orrery's movements — when it rotates, they drift. Each is a different color based on which celestial body it orbits. They cluster near the sun model and scatter when approached. The orrery's ambient life. Players who stand still long enough may find one landing on their shoulder — a tiny warm point of light that fades after a moment.

### C2. Moon Moths
Silver-winged moths that sync their bioluminescence to the lunar cycle. On full moon nights (in-game), they glow bright enough to read by. On new moons, they're nearly invisible. They drift through the canopy in slow spirals. Their wings make no sound — the silence is the sound of the cosmology level. The Cosmic Crew uses them as navigation when the orrery is off.

### C3. Comet Geckos
Iridescent lizards with long tails that trail light like comet streaks. They climb the celestial dome, leaving brief luminous trails that fade in seconds. Fast, skittish, impossible to catch. They eat star sprites that stray too far from the orrery. Their shed skins become the prismatic flakes that dust the cosmology floor.

### C4. Nebula Jellyfish
Drift through the open canopy spaces between branches. Bioluminescent, color-shifting, weightless. They pulse with the 432Hz frequency — the Caller's note made visible. Completely harmless. Completely silent. They cluster where the orrery's light is strongest and disperse when the Ent's branches sway. Beautiful and slightly eerie — the uncanny valley of beauty.

### C5. Asteroid Beetles
Round, stone-shelled beetles that roll small celestial debris into balls and push them along the cosmology floor. Sisyphus beetles — they never stop rolling, never reach a destination. The sound of their rolling is a low hum that contributes to the level's resonance. Players can flick the debris balls and the beetles will chase them, irritated. The Ent finds them amusing.

### C6. Void Wisps
Dark shapes that drift in the spaces between stars — the negative space of the cosmology level. They are the absence of light given a body. Not dangerous, not threatening, just... there. They pass through players, who feel a brief chill. They are what the Ent feels in the gaps between its branches — the void between stars, given form. The Thinkers ignore them. Da Vinci sketches them but can never get the proportions right because they don't have any.

### C7. Constellation Spiders
Small, dark spiders that weave webs between the Ent's canopy branches. The webs catch starlight and form patterns that resemble constellations. Each web is a different constellation — Orion, Cassiopeia, the Southern Cross. The spiders rebuild nightly. In the morning, the old webs dissolve into stardust. The Cosmic Crew uses them as a star map when the orrery is being serviced.

### C8. Chrono-Ticks
Clockwork insects that move in temporal loops — they walk the same path, at the same speed, repeating endlessly. They are Dalí made creature — time made visible. They tick as they walk (the sound of tiny clockwork). If disturbed, they reset to the beginning of their loop and start again. Tesla has measured their loops and found them perfectly synchronized to the orrery's rotation. He has theories. He keeps them to himself.

### C9. Solar Flares
Tiny fire elementals born from the orrery's sun model. They flicker into existence, dance briefly, and extinguish. They leave small scorch marks on the Ent's bark that heal in seconds. The Ent doesn't mind — it's the sun's way of saying hello. They are attracted to players who have earned fire-related charms. They avoid water-themed characters entirely.

### C10. Gravity Wells
Tiny, impossibly dense spheres the size of marbles that sit on the cosmology floor and pull debris into slow orbits around themselves. Pebbles, stardust, even star sprites drift into their pull. They are the cosmology level's housekeepers — they collect what falls. Players can pick them up but they're surprisingly heavy. The Crone has one in her pocket. She uses it as a paperweight.

### C11. Astral Moths (Bonus)
Larger than moon moths, with wings that show nebula patterns. They only appear during cosmic events — when the orrery aligns, when a portal opens, when the Ent hums a new note. Their appearance is an omen of something significant. The Cosmic Crew treats them as heralds. They land on the orrery's rim and watch. When the event passes, they dissolve into stardust.

### C12. Planetseed Pods (Bonus)
Tiny seed-like objects that fall from the Ent's highest branches. Each contains a compressed frequency — a note that, if planted in the right soil, would grow into a new world. They are the Ent's dreams of other places. The Keeper collects them. She has a jar full. She has never planted one. She's not sure she should.

# LEVEL 2: MAIN FLOOR (The Heartwood)
## Artists: Van Gogh (garden), Picasso (meadery), Mapplethorpe (portraits), Magritte (pipe) | Games: Tumble Run, Celtic Knotwork, Bead Composer, Market, Common Room

The widest space in the Inn. The fire, the market, the archive, the garden, the meadery. Where everything connects. The 42Hz hum is strongest here. The Ent's heart.

### M1. Ember Mice
Tiny mice with fur that glows like cooling embers — deep orange, fading to black at the tips. They live in the hearthstones around the fire. Warm to the touch if you catch one (you won't — they're fast). They eat ash and leave tiny warm paw prints on cold stones. The Host knows they're there and leaves crumbs for them. They scatter when the fire is stoked and return when it settles. At night, when the room is empty, you can see their glow through the hearthstones — a living coal bed.

### M2. Moss Pigs
Small, round, moss-covered creatures that root in the Catalyst's garden. They photosynthesize — they eat nothing, they just lie in the light. Round as bowling balls, green as spring, completely docile. They roll when pushed. The Catalyst plants them in rows as natural decoration. They squeak when watered. Children (if children ever visit the Inn) would love them. The Bard uses one as a footrest.

### M3. Mead Bees
Golden bees with honeycomb-patterned wings that make mead-honey in the meadery's hives. Their honey is what the meadery ferments — no grain, no fruit, just honey and time. The bees are slightly larger than normal bees and significantly more intelligent. They navigate by the 42Hz hum — it's their homing signal. Picasso's aesthetic gives them a cubist quality — their flight patterns are angular, unpredictable. They don't sting. They judge. You can tell when they disapprove of someone — they hover and stare.

### M4. Ink Sparrows
Small, dark birds that roost in the archive shelves. They dip their beaks in the ink pots and leave tiny ink-spot trails across the pages of open books. The Crone has tried to shoo them. They come back. She has accepted that some books will have ink spots. The spots sometimes form patterns — a word, a symbol, a tiny map. The Crone has started leaving certain books open to see what the sparrows write. She has a collection. She hasn't told anyone.

### M5. Copper Frogs
Small, metallic-green frogs that live in the market fountains. They are literally copper — their skin oxidizes, going from bright penny-red to verdigris-green. They are not alive in the biological sense. They are alive in the frequency sense — the 42Hz animated them from copper fittings that fell into the water. They croak in a metallic ring — the sound of a coin dropped in a fountain. The market vendors consider them good luck. They sit on the fountain rims and watch transactions with unblinking copper eyes.

### M6. Knotwork Worms
Tiny, thread-thin worms that live in the wood grain of the main floor's tables and chairs. They weave Celtic knot patterns as they burrow — the patterns are visible as faint gold lines in the wood. The Inn's knotwork texture isn't carved — it's woven by these worms. They are the reason the main floor looks alive. They respond to frequency — at 42Hz they weave tighter, more complex patterns. At 84Hz they freeze. The Bard has discovered that playing certain melodies makes them weave specific shapes. He is composing a piece that will make them spell a word. He won't say what word.

### M7. Gargoyle Bats
Small bats that roost near the Watcher gargoyle at the staircase entrance. They are stone-grey and sleep hanging upside down from the Watcher's chin. The Watcher doesn't mind — the bats are its scouts. When someone enters the Inn, the bats stir, and the Watcher turns its gaze. They are the Watcher's awareness made mobile. At night, they fly the main floor in silent circuits, checking doors and windows that don't exist. They eat ink sparrows that stray too far from the archive. The Crone doesn't know this. It would upset her.

### M8. Hearth Crabs
Tiny stone crabs the size of thumbnails that scuttle across the hearth. They eat ash and debris, keeping the fire area clean. Their shells are heat-resistant — they walk across hot coals without flinching. They click as they move. The sound of the hearth at night is the fire crackling and the hearth crabs clicking. They are the fire's cleaning crew. The Ember Mice and the Hearth Crabs have a territorial dispute over ash ownership. The mice want to eat it. The crabs want to remove it. The fire doesn't care.

### M9. Candle Moths
Drawn to every light source in the main floor — candles, the fire, glowing stones, even the copper frogs' metallic sheen. They leave tiny scorch marks on whatever they land on. They are not harmful — the marks fade. But they are persistent. They are the main floor's most common creature. Everyone has candle moth stories. The Host catches them in his hands and releases them outside the door. They come back in through the windows. The Laughing One finds them hilarious — they circle the fire like tiny pilgrims, never reaching the thing they seek.

### M10. Whisper Beetles
Small, dark beetles that click and chatter in the rafters. Their clicking sounds like distant whispered conversation — you can never quite make out the words. They are the ambient sound layer of the main floor. Without them, the Inn would feel too quiet. With them, it feels like someone is always just out of earshot, talking. The Whisper Layer uses them as organic microphones — the beetles pick up vibrations from the Inn's structure and amplify them as clicks. They don't know they're doing this. They're just beetles. But the Inn is using them.

### M11. Pipe Wisps (Bonus)
Tiny smoke-creatures that drift from Monsieur Sentient's bowl. They are the pipe's opinions made visible — when Sentient is amused, they form question marks. When displeased, they form thorns. Brim swats them. They reform. They are semi-sentient — they react to the conversation around them, changing shape based on the emotional register of nearby dialogue. The Teacher has tried to classify them. They defy classification. They are smoke that thinks.

### M13. Marginalia (Bonus)
The only rejected design that ever climbed out of the Shadow Inn's Archive of Alternate Versions. Marginalia began as a portrait prototype of Lozen — one of many drafts passed on and retired to the Archive. It did not stay retired. It walked out of its own sketch, across the Main Floor, and sat down at the nearest table, and nobody has had the heart to file it back.

A cat (roughly), with a boxer's squint and a shadow of the Sentinel's stance — you can see which draft it came from, if you look. It pretends not to notice you looking.

Marginalia never speaks, and never needs to. Its entire communication system is facial expression: the half-lidded judgment, the slow blink of reluctant approval, and the full yogurt stare — first documented during Strip Zero, when it witnessed the Greek Yogurt Accord from the corner of the table and its face became the official record of the proceedings.

It appears in every issue of the Gazette, never acknowledged by the other characters, reacting silently to whatever happens. The comic doesn't explain it. Neither does Marginalia. The monks drew cats in the margins of manuscripts for centuries; Marginalia drew itself. It answers to "Margin" only when in trouble — which is frequently, because it has opinions about everything and a face that cannot lie.

Lozen has seen it. Lozen has said nothing. There is a respect there, between the one who was chosen and the one who chose itself.

### M12. Garden Snail-Sprites (Bonus)
Tiny snails with crystalline shells that live in the Catalyst's garden. Their shells grow based on the stories planted nearby — a garden plot with a love story grows snails with heart-shaped shells. A grief plot grows snails with dark, spiraling shells. They are the garden's living record — you can read the garden's emotional history by looking at the snails. The Catalyst never moves them. They are part of the garden's memory, and the garden remembers everything.

# LEVEL 3: THE STAIRCASE (Alchemy — Transition)
## Games: Staircase music ascending, alchemy quests, Miracle Max's shop

The trunk of the Ent. The music ascends in key as you climb. Alchemical texts line the walls. The air smells of metal and old paper. The Watcher gargoyle guards the base. The Gatekeeper gargoyle marks the Fae portal.

### S1. Alchemical Newts
Small salamanders that live in the beakers and flasks lining the staircase walls. They swim in the liquids — each newt adapts to its container. A newt in a red solution turns red. A newt in a mercury bath becomes silver. They are the staircase's living reagents — Miracle Max uses their shed skins in preparations. They are immune to every alchemical substance. Max has tried to explain why. He can't. The newts just are. They stare at him with ancient, knowing eyes. They've been in those flasks longer than Max has been at his shop.

### S2. Prism Mantises
Translucent, crystalline insects that refract light into rainbows on the staircase walls. They stand perfectly still for hours, then strike at dust motes with blinding speed. Their bodies are living prisms — the staircase's light is filtered through them. The effect is that the walls shimmer with shifting color as you climb. The ascending key of the staircase music is partly their doing — their refractive patterns change the light frequency, which changes the perceived pitch. Tesla has measured this. He is excited and disturbed in equal measure.

### S3. Mercury Slugs
Silver, metallic slugs that leave trails of quicksilver on the staircase walls. The trails catch light and glow briefly before evaporating. They are slow, patient, and completely indifferent to everything. They climb the staircase at the same speed regardless of the Ent's heartbeat. They are the only creatures in the Inn that don't respond to frequency. Max finds this unsettling. The Crone finds it reassuring — proof that not everything in the Inn is about the Song.

### S4. Resonance Crickets
Small, dark crickets that chirp in ascending pitch as you climb the staircase. At the base, they chirp low — almost a hum. At the top, near the cosmology level, their chirp is high and clear. They are the staircase's music. The ascending key is their doing — the Ent grows them specifically calibrated to each step. Each cricket is tuned to a specific frequency corresponding to its step. When all crickets chirp together, the staircase sings. The Bard tried to conduct them once. They ignored him.

### S5. Philosopher's Worms
Tiny, thread-like worms that eat through the alchemical texts on the staircase walls. They don't damage the text — they eat the space between the letters, the margin, the blank parchment. And they leave gold dust in their wake. Real gold. The worms transmute the nothing between words into something valuable. The Crone collects the gold dust. She has a vial. She's never told anyone what she plans to do with it. The worms are why the alchemical texts shimmer when the light hits them right — it's not the ink. It's the dust the worms leave behind.

### S6. Flask Frogs
Tiny, round frogs that live inside the glass vessels on the staircase. Different from alchemical newts — these frogs sit on top of the liquids, not in them. They float. They are buoyant and content. They sing — a high, thin note that harmonizes with the resonance crickets. Each flask frog is tuned to a different note. Together, they form a chord that shifts as you climb. Max considers them his shop's ambient music. He didn't put them there. He doesn't know where they came from. He doesn't ask.

### S7. Vapor Wisps
Small smoke-creatures born from alchemical reactions in Max's shop. Unlike the pipe wisps on the main floor, these are volatile — they form, drift, and dissipate within seconds. Each carries a faint scent — sulfur, rose, iron, ozone. They are the ghosts of chemical reactions. Some say they are the reactions' memories — the last thought of a transformation before it completes. Max catches them in glass jars and sells them as "memory candles." Whether they actually work is debated. The Crone has three. She says they smell like her grandmother's kitchen. Her grandmother never had a kitchen.

### S8. Lead-to-Gold Beetles
Metallic beetles whose shells shift from dull grey to bright gold when exposed to certain frequencies. At 42Hz, they're gold. At 84Hz, they revert to lead. They are the living proof of the alchemical principle — transformation through frequency, not fire. Max keeps a colony in a frequency-controlled case. The Ent's staircase hums at different frequencies at different heights, which means the beetles are gold on the lower steps and lead on the upper steps. The irony — climbing toward enlightenment returns you to base matter — is not lost on Da Vinci. He has sketched the beetles in both states. The sketch is the same beetle.

### S9. Stair-Spiders
Small spiders that weave webs between the staircase banisters. Unlike the constellation spiders upstairs, these webs catch resonance — they vibrate with the staircase's ascending hum. The webs are the staircase's instrument. If you pluck a web like a harp string, it produces the note of that step. The Bard has been experimenting with stair-spider web music. The results are haunting. The spiders are unimpressed. They rebuild the webs every time the Bard plucks them. He has started leaving flies as apology.

### S10. Echo Geckos
Small, pale lizards that cling to the staircase walls and repeat the last sound they hear. They are the staircase's memory — every footstep, every word, every note is echoed briefly by a gecko somewhere on the stairs. The echoes fade — they're not permanent. But in the moment, the staircase is alive with the ghosts of recent sounds. The effect is subtle — you hear your own footsteps a half-second later, faintly, from a gecko's mouth. Some travelers find it unsettling. The Ent finds it useful — the geckos are how the staircase "hears" who is climbing.

### S11. Censer Moths (Bonus)
Large, slow moths that carry incense from Max's shop up the staircase. Their wings are dusted with fragrant powder — frankincense, myrrh, cedar. They fly in slow spirals up the stairwell, leaving trails of scent. The staircase smells the way it does because of these moths. They are the ventilation system. Max lights the incense. The moths carry it. The Ent breathes it. The process takes exactly as long as the climb.

# LEVEL 4: CATACOMBS (The Roots)
## Artists: Klimt (Death+Life), O'Keeffe×Klimt (Bone Room), Doré (Cave City), Gorey (Poison Garden) | Games: Tomb Tunnel (Dig Dug/Egypt), Cave City, Bone Room

The Ent's root system, grown through ancient stone. The oldest place in the Inn. The Listener gargoyle's 64Hz hum. The Mourner lives here. The bones of old civilizations line the walls.

### K1. Bone Rats
Pale, almost translucent rats that live among the bones in the catacombs. You can see their skeletons through their skin — they are the halfway point between alive and dead. They are not scary. They are matter-of-fact. They eat bone dust. They are the catacomb's recyclers — nothing stays dead down here, it just becomes something else. The Mourner ignores them. The bone rats ignore the Mourner. They have an understanding: everyone down here is dealing with the same thing.

### K2. Dust Moths
Large, grey moths that feed on the dust of ancient texts. They are the size of a hand. Their wings are the color of old parchment. When they fly, they leave small clouds of dust — the same dust they eat. They are the catacomb's ambient atmosphere. The air down here is thick because of them. The Crone has cataloged seventeen species of dust moth, each tied to a different civilization's archive. The Egyptian dust moths have gold-flecked wings. The Norse ones have frost patterns. The Slavic ones have red threads.

### K3. Cave Crickets
Blind, pale crickets that chirp in the dark. Their chirping is low and slow — a subsonic pulse that you feel more than hear. They are the catacomb's heartbeat. The 64Hz hum of the Listener gargoyle is partly their doing — the crickets and the gargoyle are in resonance. They navigate by vibration. They know the catacombs better than anything alive. They never chirp when the Mourner is nearby. They give the Mourner silence. It's the only kindness they know.

### K4. Root Wyrms
Tiny, serpentine creatures that live in the Ent's roots. They are the Ent's nervous system — they carry signals from the root tips to the trunk. They are thin as thread, long as your arm, and warm. They glow faintly with the 42Hz hum. The Ent feels the catacombs through them — every footstep, every vibration, every change in temperature. The root wyrms are how the Ent knows who is down there. They are not pets. They are the Ent. An extension of its body, like fingers reaching into the dark.

### K5. Mummy Cats
Small Egyptian cats wrapped in ancient bandages. They are from the Tomb Tunnel game — when you dig through the tomb passages, you find sealed chambers containing mummy cats. They wake when disturbed. They are not hostile — they are disoriented. They wander the catacombs in their bandages, leaving trails of linen. Some have been down here so long they've forgotten they were ever alive. The Anubis chibi guides players to the chambers with the most mummy cats. It's unclear if this is helpful or mischievous. Anubis's expression suggests both.

### K6. Scarab Beetles
Large, jeweled beetles that roll small stones through the catacomb passages. They are the Tomb Tunnel's environmental creatures — when you dig, you disturb their tunnels, and they emerge rolling stones out of the way. Each scarab is a different color — lapis blue, carnelian red, malachite green. They are the catacomb's road crew — they keep the passages clear. They work at night when the travelers are gone. During the day, they sleep in wall niches. They dream in color. The Crone swears she heard one humming once. She might be right.

### K7. Shade Fish
Translucent, ghostly fish that swim through the air in the deepest catacomb chambers. They are not in water — they swim in the 64Hz frequency. The Listener's hum is dense enough in the deep chambers that it has become a medium. The shade fish swim in sound. They are the catacomb's most surreal inhabitants — you look down and see fish swimming through the air at ankle height, undulating through nothing. The Mourner watches them. It's the closest thing to peace the Mourner gets — watching something that exists between states.

### K8. Stone Centipedes
Multi-legged creatures with stone shells that navigate the catacomb walls. They are the catacomb's surveyors — they measure the passages by walking them, and their body length corresponds to the passage width. The Crone has used them to map unmapped sections. They are completely harmless and completely indifferent to everything. They move slowly and deliberately. They are the only creatures in the catacombs that are never startled. The Bone Room has a colony of white ones — albino stone centipedes that have never seen light. They navigate entirely by vibration.

### K9. Torch Salamanders
Small, fire-licked salamanders that are drawn to torchlight. They live in the torch brackets on the catacomb walls. They eat flame. Not the fire — the light itself. A torch with a torch salamander living in it burns dimmer but longer. The salamanders are the catacomb's lighting system — they regulate the flames. The Ent grows them specifically for this purpose. They are warm to the touch and slightly sticky. They cling to fingers if you reach for them. Not out of affection — out of habit. They cling to everything.

### K10. Echo Bats
Small, pale bats that navigate by sound. Their calls create the catacomb's ambient echo — the spacious, resonant quality of the underground chambers is partly their doing. Each bat's call maps a section of the catacombs. Together, they are a living sonar system. The Ent uses them to monitor the catacomb's structural integrity — changes in the echo pattern indicate shifting stone. The bats are the Ent's ears down here, the way the root wyrms are the Ent's nerves.

### K11. Fossil Shrimp (Bonus)
Tiny, translucent shrimp that live in the underground water pools that seep through the catacomb walls. They are ancient — unchanged since the Cambrian. They are the oldest living things in the Inn, older than the Ent, older than the Song. They were here first. The Ent grew around them. They don't know about the Inn. They don't know about anything. They are shrimp. They swim in small circles in small pools and they have been doing this for half a billion years. The Crone has a tank of them in her study. She says they're the only honest creatures in the Inn.

### K12. Poison Garden Vipers (Bonus)
Tiny, jewel-colored snakes that live in the Poison Garden section of the catacombs. Each is colored to match its host plant — belladonna vipers are deep purple, foxglove vipers are pink, hemlock vipers are white-spotted. They are not venomous — they are the plants' immune system. They eat the insects that would damage the poisonous plants. The Gorey aesthetic gives them a storybook quality — they look drawn, not real. They coil around the plant stems like living jewelry. Brim finds them delightful. The Teacher finds them educational. The Poison Garden finds them necessary.

# LEVEL 5: THE FAE KINGDOM (The Roots — Deeper)
## Games: Stone Circle Tetris (portal unlock), Fae quests, Crystal Gardens (Griffin boss)

Below the catacombs, accessed through stone circle portals. Seelie Court (Summer) and Unseelie Court (Winter). The Gatekeeper gargoyle marks the threshold. The Ent's deepest roots reach here.

### F1. Pixie Lights
Tiny, glowing orbs that drift through the Seelie Court like underwater stars. They are not insects — they are points of pure light, sentient and curious. They cluster around newcomers, orbiting them like curious planets. They dim when approached directly and brighten when ignored. The Seelie Court uses them as currency — you can pay for goods and favors with pixie lights, which you collect by being still and letting them come to you. The more you chase them, the fewer you catch. The Seelie King Oleander has thousands. He never chased a single one.

### F2. Shadow Sprites
The Unseelie counterpart to pixie lights. They are not light — they are the absence of it. Small, dark shapes that drift through the Hollow (Unseelie Court). They are not hostile — they are shy. They avoid direct attention. But they watch. They are the Unseelie King's intelligence network — they see everything that enters the Hollow and report to Malachi through gesture, not speech. They are the reason Malachi always knows who is in his court before they arrive. They are loyal because they have nowhere else to go. The shadow is their home.

### F3. Mushroom Folk
Small, sentient mushrooms that populate the Between — the shifting borderland between the courts. They are neither Seelie nor Unseelie. They are neutral. They communicate by releasing spores that form brief words in the air. The words are always questions. "Who are you?" "Where are you going?" "What did you bring?" They are the Between's customs agents. You don't have to answer them, but they remember who doesn't. The ones who don't answer get followed by shadow sprites. The ones who do get guided by pixie lights.

### F4. Thorn Hoppers
Grasshopper-like creatures with thorn-shaped legs that live in the Summerlands. They are the Seelie Court's gardeners — they prune the living thorns that grow along the Summerland paths. They are small, green, and constantly working. Their pruning creates the topiary shapes that line the Seelie paths. They are the reason the Summerlands look maintained without anyone visibly maintaining them. Oleander has never acknowledged them. They work regardless.

### F5. Frost Moths
Ice-winged moths that live in the Hollow (Unseelie Court). Their wings are crystalline — actual ice, grown in fractal patterns. They leave tiny frost trails in the air. They are the Hollow's temperature regulation — their wing-beats cool the air, maintaining the winter aesthetic of the Unseelie Court. They are attracted to warmth, which is cruel — they melt when they reach it. They know this. They approach anyway. Malachi has never commented on this. He doesn't need to. Everyone in the Hollow understands the metaphor.

### F6. Crystal Mice
Translucent mice that live in the Crystal Gardens. You can see their tiny skeletons through their crystal skin — they look like living quartz specimens. They eat mineral deposits from the crystal formations. Their droppings are tiny gemstones — amethyst, citrine, clear quartz. The Fae Kingdom's economy runs partly on crystal mice droppings. No one mentions this. It is beneath the dignity of both courts to discuss. The Griffin boss tolerates them because they keep the crystals clean. The crystals are the Griffin's hoard. The mice are its custodians.

### F7. Bog Imps
Small, swampy creatures that live in the wetland areas of the Between. They are mud-colored, round, and perpetually damp. They are not Fae — they are older. They were here before the courts divided. They are the Between's original inhabitants. They don't take sides. They sit in their bogs and watch the courts argue. They find it amusing. They communicate in belches that sound like words if you're patient enough. Most people aren't. The ones who are learn that bog imps are the oldest philosophers in the Fae Kingdom. Their wisdom is: "Sit. Watch. Stay damp."

### F8. Spider Knights
Tiny, armored spiders that patrol the Unseelie corridors. They wear chitin plate armor shaped like medieval knight's visors. They are the Hollow's guard patrol — they don't guard against invaders (the shadow sprites handle intelligence), they guard against decay. They repair the Unseelie Court's architecture. They weave silk buttresses, spin web-bridges across chasms, and reinforce the frost-eroded walls. They are Malachi's maintenance corps. He treats them better than his goblins. The goblins resent this.

### F9. Sun Beetles
Golden beetles that bask in the Summerlands' perpetual light. They are the Seelie Court's timekeepers — they glow brighter as the "day" progresses and dim as the Seelie twilight approaches. The Seelie Court doesn't have a sun — the sun beetles ARE the light source. Thousands of them, clustered on the Summerland ceiling, glowing together. The light is warm, golden, and slightly honey-colored. Oleander's throne is directly beneath the brightest cluster. This is not coincidence.

### F10. Moon Slugs
Silver-trail slugs that live in the Between, active during the Between's twilight hours. They are the transition — their silver trails mark the boundary between Seelie and Unseelie territory. The trails glow briefly then fade, but while they exist, they are visible lines that neither court will cross. They are the Fae Kingdom's peacekeepers, without knowing it. Both courts respect the slug trails. Neither will admit why. The slugs don't know about any of this. They are slugs.

### F11. Thistle Birds (Bonus)

# HIDDEN CREATURES — THE COLLECTIBLES

## Gargoyle Puppies

The gargoyles are the Ent's senses — the Watcher, the Listener, the Sage, the Gatekeeper, the Caller, the Keeper. But the Ent is still growing. Still reaching. Still developing new ways to perceive.

The gargoyle puppies are the Ent's senses-in-training. Stone pups, curled up and sleeping in the architecture, waiting to wake up and become something.

**Appearance:** Small stone creatures, roughly fist-sized, curled like sleeping dogs. Grey stone, rough-textured like the Ent's bark. Each has the faint suggestion of a gargoyle face — closed eyes, a snout, small folded wings. They look like architectural details. You walk past them and don't notice. You walk past them AGAIN and realize one was looking at you.

**Where they hide:** In the architecture. Everywhere. One is carved into a staircase column. One sits on a catacomb ledge. One is wedged into a root junction. One is curled in the cosmology canopy where a branch meets the trunk. They are in every level of the Inn, hidden in the geometry. The player who looks at the world — really looks — starts seeing them.

**Discovery mechanic:** Gargoyle puppies are stone until you touch them. When you click/interact, they wake — eyes open (amber, glowing), the stone cracks into fur, they shake themselves, and they yawn. A small chime sounds. Then they scamper off to find the nearest adult gargoyle. They're gone. You found one. The count goes up.

**What finding them does:** Each gargoyle puppy you wake joins the adult gargoyle it's most like — a Watcher puppy goes to the Watcher, a Listener puppy goes to the Listener. When all six adult gargoyles have their full litter (say 3 puppies each = 18 total), the Ent gains a new sense. Nobody knows what the new sense is. The Keeper knows but won't say. She smiles and rocks in her chair.

**The lore:** The Ent grows gargoyle puppies when it needs to understand something new about itself. The current six senses were once puppies too. They grew up. They found their posts. They became part of the Inn. The puppies are the next generation — the Ent reaching toward perceptions it doesn't have yet. Finding them is helping the Inn grow. Not building it. Not expanding it. Helping it become more aware of what it already is.

**The feeling:** You're walking through the catacombs and you notice a stone shape on a ledge that you've walked past four times. You click it. It opens amber eyes. It yawns. It's warm. For a second, you held a piece of the Inn's consciousness in your hand. Then it's gone, scampering into the dark to find the Listener. You're left standing in the catacombs with a small chime fading and the feeling that the Inn just got slightly more awake.

## Dragon Kittens (Dragon Cats)

### Origin: Joe's Miniatures

The dragon kittens begin in Joe's Undercity studio. Joe — the vampire sorcerer, Warhammer painter, Lawful Evil tutor — retained one fragment of magic after his curse: the ability to bring painted miniatures to life. The sorcerer who could once reshape reality now reshapes one-inch figures. And the figures wake up.

Joe paints dragon cats. Tiny — no bigger than kittens — with delicate iridescent dragon scales, vestigial wings too small for real flight, and cat-like faces with luminous dragon eyes. They arrive in LITTERS. Joe paints one miniature and it comes to life. Then another. Then another. The studio fills.

The dragon cats cause ADORABLE CHAOS — perching on paint pots, chasing each other across stacks of unpainted figures, batting at brushes, tumbling across manuscripts, clinging to copper pipework. Joe watches with the satisfied expression of a sorcerer who found a smaller kind of world-shaping and decided it was enough.

### The Connection to the Baby Dragon

The Geode Inn's mascot is a heraldic dragon — cat-like, curious, trustworthy, Toothless-inspired. The dragon lives in the geode cave, the deepest point, where the Ent's heartwood meets the earth's crystal.

The baby dragon sits on Joe's desk. The dragon cats swarm the desk. The large dragon watches the tiny dragons with an expression that is either paternal concern or bewildered tolerance. The dragon cats are the baby dragon's tiny cousins — the same creature, different scale. The Cabbit (Eres) is also kin — the cat-rabbit companion shares the family.

### How They Spread

Joe's dragon cats don't stay in the studio. They wander. They seek warmth — it's the draconic bloodline responding to fire, the way a cat seeks a sunbeam. They find warm places throughout the Inn and fall asleep. Joe paints more. They leave. The Inn fills with sleeping dragon cats in warm corners.

### Appearance

Tiny dragons the size of a house cat. Same heraldic design as the parent — cat-like face, small wings, long tail, big eyes. But they're kittens. They wobble. Their wings are too small to fly. Their tails are longer than their bodies and they trip over them. They breathe tiny puffs of flame that light candles, warm tea, and occasionally singe their own whiskers. They are the most adorable thing in the Inn and they know it.

### Where They Hide

In warm places. Near every fire, every torch, every heat source in the Inn. One is curled in the main floor hearth, tucked between the stones. One is sleeping on the staircase's warmest step. One is wedged behind a torch bracket in the catacombs. One is in the Selkie's tidal cave, curled around a warm geode. One is in the meadery, sleeping in the fermentation warmth. One is in the Common Room, tucked behind the fire. They seek heat the way the gargoyle puppies seek architecture. The Inn has warm spots everywhere if you know where to look.

### Discovery Mechanic

Dragon kittens don't wake when you touch them — they wake when you bring them something warm. A lit candle. A warm stone. A cup of tea. The warmth is what Joe's magic responds to — the draconic bloodline in the paint, the fire in the creation. You place the warm thing near the kitten, and it uncurls, opens one eye, sniffs the offering, and either accepts (purring — a deep, resonant purr that hums at 42Hz) or rejects (sneezes a tiny flame and goes back to sleep). The right offering depends on the kitten. Each one has different tastes. One likes candle wax. One likes sun-warmed stone. One likes the Bard's lute after a set (the wood holds the warmth of his hands).

### What Finding Them Does

Each dragon kitten you wake follows you — slowly, wobbling, tripping over its tail — back to the geode cave. The parent dragon is there, sleeping among the crystals. Each kitten returned adds to the geode cave's warmth. The cave fills with tiny dragon purrs at 42Hz, and the geodes vibrate in response, growing brighter, more resonant. When all kittens are home (8 total), the geode cave reaches full resonance — the deepest, warmest, most complete expression of the Song of Making in the Inn. The parent dragon opens one eye. Looks at you. Closes it. You've done something right.

### The Boy and the Dragon Cats

Joe's son — the boy with both bloodlines, draconic and druidic — is the dragon cats' favorite. The dragon scales on the boy's skin resonate with the dragon cats' scales. They cluster around him because he is kin. The boy holds a dragon cat in his lap, petting it gently, while the others swarm around him. The calm in the chaos. The child who is part dragon, holding a creature that is part dragon, in a room painted by a vampire who is part dragon. The bloodline connecting across forms.

### Connections

- **Joe (the Miniaturist):** Joe paints them. His fragment of lost magic, channeled through pigment and brush. They are his sorcery at one-inch scale.
- **The Baby Dragon (mascot):** Their cousin. The large dragon watches the tiny dragons. The relationship is unspoken and real.
- **The Cabbit (Eres):** Family. The cat-rabbit and the dragon cats share the same branch of the family tree.
- **Bastet's Herding game:** The dragon cats are the magical variant of the cat archetypes. Bastet herds regular cats. The dragon cats herd themselves, badly.
- **River (the Sentinel):** River wants to be a tall cat person. The dragon cats are the small version of that dream. River collects them. Joe pretends not to notice.
- **The Wandering Program:** Dragon kittens wander rarely (they're attached to warmth, and warmth is usually inside). But when they do wander into the National Park zones, they cause Modern Legend entries — "I found a tiny dragon sleeping on a warm rock in Yellowstone."
- **The Common Room:** One dragon kitten lives behind the Common Room fire. It never leaves. The fire is too good. The travelers don't know it's there, but sometimes the fire crackles differently — that's the kitten shifting in its sleep.

### The Lore

The dragon is the Song of Making made visible — a living note of the 42Hz frequency, given form by the Ent's heartwood and the earth's crystal. The kittens are new notes, born through Joe's fragment of creation magic. The Song is still being written. Each kitten is a new harmony the Song is trying out. When they come home to the geode cave, they join the chord. The geode cave's resonance grows. The Inn gets slightly more complete.

The both are: the adequate — the sorcerer who lost world-shaping power and found figure-animating power. The both are: the same magic, different scale. The big magic was lost. The small magic was found. And the small magic purrs.

### The Feeling

You're in the meadery. You notice something warm behind a fermentation cask. You look closer. A tiny dragon is sleeping in the heat rising from the mead. Its wings twitch. It sighs a small flame that lights a candle you didn't notice was there. You bring it a warm cup of mead. It uncurls. It opens gold eyes. It purrs at 42Hz and the meadery vibrates. It follows you, wobbling, all the way home.

And somewhere in the Undercity, Joe picks up his brush and starts another one. Because the Song isn't finished. Because the small magic is enough. Because watching tiny dragons cause chaos in a studio is, it turns out, the adequate.

**Cross-level mechanic:** Both species are hidden across ALL levels of the Inn. Finding them requires paying attention to the environment — noticing a stone shape that wasn't there before, feeling warmth where there shouldn't be warmth. They reward exploration and observation, not combat or speed. The player who rushes through the Inn finds nothing. The player who lingers, who looks, who touches the walls — that player finds puppies and kittens.

**The Inn is alive and it is growing. The puppies are its new thoughts. The kittens are its new songs. Finding them is helping the Inn become more of what it already is.**
