# THE SEELIE KING — Oleander
## August 29, 2026

## Name
**Oleander.** Named by Krista, August 29, 2026.

The oleander is a beautiful flowering shrub — vibrant, lush, covered in blooms. Every part of it is toxic. The beauty is real. The danger is real. Both are the same plant.

Oleander pairs with Malachi (the Unseelie King) as the two brothers of the Fae Kingdom. Malachi — Hebrew, "my messenger," dark, ancient, the one who rules the labyrinth. Oleander — beautiful, toxic, the one who rules summer. The messenger and the flower. The one who leaves and the one who stays. The one who performs and the one who guards.

## Pronunciation & Character Detail
The O is silent. It's pronounced "Leander."

His mother spelled it "Oleander" to be trendy. It bugs him to no end.

King Oleander (pronounced Leander, and if you say the O he will notice, and you don't want him to notice) has been irritated by this spelling for 5,000 years. The most ancient guardian of the threshold between waking and dreaming — the assassin-tender, the heron-still king — carries the quiet, permanent annoyance of a man whose mother thought she was being clever.

This is the most human thing about him.

## The Name Underneath — The Mother's Knowing

Leander. The mythological Leander swam the Hellespont every night to reach Hero. He drowned crossing the boundary between worlds for love.

Oleander doesn't see the Leander part. He sees the trendy O. He sees his mother being clever with spelling. He doesn't see what she actually did.

She named him after a swimmer who crossed impossible water for someone on the other side. She named the guardian of the threshold after someone who crossed thresholds. She named the protector after someone who died protecting what he loved.

The O is the decoration. The name is Leander. The name was always Leander.

Oleander thinks his mother was being trendy. His mother was being prophetic. She named him what he would become — the one who crosses, the one who guards the crossing, the one who loves the thing on the other side of the water.

The mother was more clever than he gives her credit for.

This is the second mother tonight who knew more than she showed: Mona (the mother creation principle disguised as the world's most famous painting) and Oleander's mother (the prophet disguised as a trend). The mother's knowing hides in plain sight. The mother names what the child will become, and the child spends 5,000 years annoyed by the spelling.
