## THE ARCHIVE — the library, the scholars, the aware

### Sodalite & Sodalite (African)
**Lives:** every reading table in the Archive. One per table. This is policy.
**Thread:** the working mind's stone — the friend who answers the phone. The Archive is the Inn's great quiet engine, and sodalite is its desk stone: deep blue with white logic running through it like the Inn's own linework, the stone that believes order and truth belong in the same sentence. Scholars who work the Archive long enough develop the habit of resting a hand on their sodalite while thinking, the way other people touch a worry stone — except this one gives back sense instead of smoothing. The African deposit is the rarer, deeper blue, reserved for the long-term tables. The regulars know which tables those are. The regulars would not tell you which tables those are.
**Song:** the study tone — the Archive's ambient register, a quiet thrum of concentration that Beverley the Librarian swears rises a quarter-step when the room is working well. It is the only measurable sound the Archive makes.

### Dumortierite
**Lives:** the restricted stacks, set into the shelf-ends, one at every row.
**Thread:** the scholar's armor — blue that took no prisoners, the hardest of the quiet stones, guarding the restricted wing where the Archive keeps what is kept for keeping's sake. Quire reads in the margins of those restricted texts, hushed and careful, and the dumortierite shelf-ends have watched every one of those sessions without a word, which is the entire job: guarding without judging, present without intruding. The Archive is aware of everyone inside it. The dumortierite is how the restricted wing says so politely.
**Song:** the hush-with-teeth — the restricted stacks' frequency: the Archive's quiet, one register lower, with the faintest edge of consequence in it. Quire says it is the most reassuring sound in the building. Quire is in a position to know.

### Lapis Lazuli (the working deposit)
**Lives:** the pigment case, in the conservation room, with the mortar that is older than the Inn.
**Thread:** the scribe's stone — ground into the ultramarine that painted the old books' heavens. The Archive holds the reading copies upstairs in the Cosmology and the Catacombs; the conservation room holds the working lapis, the small raw pieces that can be ground, because a library that preserves must also restore, and the blue of the restored pages must be the true blue. Beverley grinds it personally, once a year, for the wing that needs it. The Archive considers this the year's most important ceremony and invites nobody.
**Song:** the restoration tone — a small, precise figure that plays only in the conservation room. The Inn's only music written for a workbench. The mortar keeps the beat.

### Chinese Writing Stone
**Lives:** the great unsolved case, at the end of the Archive's central aisle, facing the whole room.
**Thread:** the untranslated script — white feldspar characters brushed across black limestone, strokes no human hand made, a message in transit for geological reasons. The Archive's position, stated on the case card and unchanged for years: the script has not been translated, but it is clearly trying to say something, and it is clearly not for us. Quire disagrees. Quire has been reading the margins of that stone for two years and will not report progress. The Archive permits this. The Archive and the stone have an understanding: the case stays open as long as the question does.
**Song:** the unsolved figure — a phrase with no resolution, cycling, patient, the Archive's one deliberate musical mystery. Composers are not permitted to resolve it. The stone hasn't.

### Hawk's Eye
**Lives:** the after-hours shelf, by the lamp the Archive leaves on.
**Thread:** the night reader — the blue tiger's eye, the eye that doesn't need good light. The Archive keeps its after-hours shelf for the scholars who cannot stop, and the hawk's eye is the shelf's stone: the seeing that does not require conditions. Quire holds it on the long nights. The Owl — the watcher, the bloodline's presence — has been observed perched on that shelf more than any other point in the Archive, which the Archive logs, quietly, in the same ledger where it records everything and explains nothing.
**Song:** the vigil register — the after-hours tone, played under the lamp's hum: the Archive's daytime study tone, dropped an octave, stripped to the sustained note only. The sound of a building that has not gone to sleep because someone is still reading.

### Lepidolite
**Lives:** the examination room — the small side room with the soft chairs, kept for scholars who have forgotten how to stop.
**Thread:** the lithium lavender — the calm study, the stone that quiets without sedating. The Archive loves its scholars and knows what it does to them: the marginalia that starts as notes and ends as a life's work at 3 a.m. The examination room is the Archive's mercy, and lepidolite is the room's resident: sit, hold the lavender stone, let the overworked mind stand down. Scholars emerge restored. Scholars are always surprised. The Archive is never surprised. The Archive has seen it ten thousand times and finds it a little wonderful every time.
**Song:** the decrescendo — the Inn's only piece composed to end quieter than it begins, written for this room only. It cannot be heard ending. That is the design. That is the mercy.

### Phantom Quartz
**Lives:** the restoration wing, in the open case, where every tour pauses longest.
**Thread:** the before-picture kept — the crystal that grew, paused, was dusted in chlorite, and then kept growing, sealing the old shape inside the new one as a ghost, visible forever inside the self it became. The restoration wing is the Archive's heart: the room that says nothing has to be erased to be continued. Every restored book, every repaired binding, every text saved from rot passes the phantom quartz on its way back to the shelves. The Inn's whole philosophy of preservation is in that case. The scholars know it. The books, the Inn maintains, also know it.
**Song:** the palimpsest tone — two melodies, one old, one new, played at once, neither masking the other. The restoration wing's signature sound and the Inn's most-played single composition. It is the Song's own theme, foreshadowed.

### Sugilite (the Archive's deposit)
**Lives:** the restricted wing's door, set into the lintel, facing outward.
**Thread:** the violet shield — the healer's alchemy, the protective stone, posted at the boundary of the wing that keeps the dangerous knowledge. The restricted wing's rules are posted beside the door, and the sugilite is the rules' enforcement, which is gentle, which is absolute: you may enter, you may read, you may leave; what you may not do is carry the wing's weight out with you. Set it down at the lintel. The Archive will hold it. The Archive has room. The Archive has always had room.
**Song:** the threshold tone — the ninth-landing note (the Staircase's guardian frequency), borrowed permanently by the Archive, played at the restricted wing's door. The Inn's thresholds all hum the same note. The sugilite is why.

### Howick Quartz
**Lives:** the reading nook — the smallest case in the Archive, the least visited, the most loved by those who know.
**Thread:** the quiet English quartz — unremarkable, essential, like the nook itself. Not the Archive's brightest stone, not its rarest, not its most storied: simply present, reliable, the stone that is always exactly where it was left, at the temperature of the room. Scholars who discover the nook tend to keep the discovery to themselves. The Inn keeps the nook supplied. This is the arrangement. It has never been discussed, and it has never been broken.
**Song:** the room tone — not music, exactly: the recorded ambient hum of the nook itself, held at the frequency of one quiet afternoon, preserved like everything else in the Archive. The Inn calls it the most honest recording it owns.

## THE SPIRAL GALLERY — the museum, the ramp, the view

### Picasso Jasper
**Lives:** the signature case, at the Gallery's entrance, first thing on the ramp.
**Thread:** the painter stone — the abstract that was always there. The Spiral Gallery mounts the Inn's art, and the Picasso jasper is the collection's thesis: the earth made abstraction before any gallery existed, and the Gallery's whole hanging is, the Curator maintains, an extended apology for taking so long to notice. Guests are invited to decide what the jasper shows. The Gallery's only rule: no answer is wrong, and none is final. The stone has outlasted every interpretation so far. The stone will outlast the next ten thousand.
**Song:** the improvisation — an unscripted figure the Gallery plays at the entrance, different every day, generated from the stone's banding by a process the composers describe as "reading it aloud." No two entrances have ever sounded the same. The Gallery considers this correct. The Gallery is a museum of the unrepeatable.

### Chrysanthemum Stone
**Lives:** the still-life room, the Gallery's quiet bend, where the ramp widens and the traffic slows.
**Thread:** the bloom that outlasted the still — white crystal blossoms scattered through black stone, opened at the exact moment the rock froze and open forever. The still-life room is the Gallery's meditation on permanence: paintings of flowers beside a stone that is one. Every painting in the room is temporary — pigment, varnish, time — and the chrysanthemum stone sits in the center of the room being the exception. The Curator calls it "the only still life that is also a portrait." The stone accepts the title. The stone accepted it a hundred million years ago.
**Song:** the held chord — the still-life room's single sustained harmony, unchanged since the Gallery opened. The Gallery's one piece of music that never moves, in the one room dedicated to never moving. Guests report it makes the paintings easier to look at. The stone could have told them that.

### Ammolite (the Gallery's deposit)
**Lives:** the color-theory case, in the Gallery's brightest bend, where the skylight lands.
**Thread:** the painting that became a gem — the color-theory case studies how color works, and the ammolite is the collection's final exam: an ammonite shell that turned opal, every hue at once, from a creature that has been dead longer than color has had a name. The Gallery hangs studies and charts and gradients by masters of pigment, and then the ammolite, which required no study, made no chart, and graded itself. The Curator calls it "the rainbow without the committee." The committee concurs.
**Song:** the spectrum sweep — the Gallery's only full-register piece, every frequency the Inn owns played once, in order, at the speed of light through shell. It takes ninety seconds. It is the only music in the building that contains all the other music. The Gallery plays it at opening. The building wakes up to it.

### Petrified Palm (the Gallery's column)
**Lives:** the calendar column — the rainbow palm, cut and polished, mounted at the Gallery's turn.
**Thread:** the rings of time, cut and mounted — the Gallery's calendar, counting seasons no one alive remembers. The column is the Gallery's only exhibit that is also a clock: each ring a year of a climate three hundred million years gone, each band of rainbow a season of light that fell on no eyes. The Curator hangs the museum's timeline beside it — all of art history, the whole human stretch — and the column's rings dwarf the timeline into a footnote. The Gallery finds this clarifying. The guests stand there the longest. The stone is very patient with them.
**Song:** the deep time — the Gallery's slowest piece: one note per ring, played at geological tempo, a piece that would take years to finish if anyone ever played it through. It is never played through. The Gallery plays one note per day. The column will outlast the concert. That is the composition.

### Bumblebee Jasper
**Lives:** the satire case, next to the entrance, angled so you cannot miss it on the way in.
**Thread:** the Gallery's joke — the artwork that knows exactly what it looks like. The satire case is the Gallery's honesty about itself: a museum with a sense of humor, mounted in a building that is a jewelry shop that is a game hub that is an archive, which is to say the Inn is the satire case of the internet, and the bumblebee jasper is the Inn's self-portrait. The Curator's card reads, in full: "We know. We did this on purpose. We would do it again."
**Song:** the gag figure — the Gallery's one comedy piece: a bright, busy, buzzing arrangement that resolves on the exact note the entrance improvisation began on, so the joke lands only if you heard both. Most guests do not hear both. The Gallery is at peace with this. The best gags reward the second visit.

### Zultanite (the Gallery's deposit)
**Lives:** the light room, on the rotating stand, beneath the Gallery's one adjustable skylight.
**Thread:** the collaboration — the color that changes with every light source, the Gallery's thesis stone: art is a collaboration with the light, and the light never performs the same show twice. The light room cycles its skylight through dawn, noon, dusk, and candle, and the zultanite produces four paintings, none of them repeated, all of them the stone. The Curator calls it "the only artist in the building with a perfect attendance record." The stone does not dispute this. The stone is busy being four colors.
**Song:** the modulation study — the Gallery's piece that changes key every sixty seconds, on the minute, matching the skylight's cycle. It never resolves. It was never going to resolve. The Gallery says resolution is a habit, not a law. The stone agrees, in four different keys.

## THE VAULT — the high cases, the crowns, the deepest guard

### The Four Crowns (Ruby, Sapphire, Emerald — and the Diamond's Floor)
**Lives:** the Vault's center, in the light the Inn saved for exactly this.
**Thread:** the classics, kept where the light is best. Ruby, the blood's bright argument. Sapphire, the sky's, compressed. Emerald, the green that royalty kept getting wars wrong about. The fourth crown, the Diamond, is not in the Vault — the Diamond is set into the Allfather's floor upstairs, unretrievable, on purpose — and the Vault's case says so, on the card, in the Inn's hand: "The fourth crown is the floor you are standing on. Go up and stand on it properly." The Vault is the Inn's most secure room and its most subversive teaching: the treasure was never behind the lock. The lock was the last test of whether you'd figured that out.
**Song:** the coronation register — the brightest full chord in the Inn's architecture, played at the Vault's threshold only, once per guest. It contains the ruby's pulse, the sapphire's depth, the emerald's hum, and one silence, tuned: the diamond's note, absent, felt through the floor. Every guest looks down. That is the coronation.

### Cat's Eye (Chrysoberyl)
**Lives:** above the Vault door, inside the lintel, facing out.
**Thread:** the watching stone — the chatoyant band that opens and narrows with the light, the guard that does not need to blink. The Vault's door is watched by the Cat's Eye the way the Catacombs' gate is watched by the tiger iron: the Inn believes in matching its doors to its staff, and the Vault's staff is the eye. Lozen, the Sentinel, has a standing arrangement with it — the eye watches, Lozen walks, and between them the Vault has never lost a stone to anything but its own lessons. The eye narrows at dusk. It has opinions about the dark. It has never once needed backup.
**Song:** the slit tone — a narrow, focused frequency the eye hums at the Vault's threshold: the guardian's note, the smallest sound in the Inn's security architecture. Lozen's leitmotif shares its root. The two guards have known each other for years.

### Spinel
**Lives:** the correction case, beside the crowns — the honest impostor, with the historical record.
**Thread:** the stone history kept confusing with ruby — the "Balas ruby" of the crown jewels, the red that wore the wrong name for centuries, the honest impostor who never claimed to be anything but itself and got miscatalogued anyway. The Vault's correction case is the Inn's museum of misattribution: the spinel displayed with every false name it ever carried, and its true one, at the end, in the Inn's hand: "Itself, the whole time." The Vault considers this the most important case it holds. Crowns are common. Being yourself for five hundred years while everyone calls you something else — that is rare.
**Song:** the true tone — the correction case plays one pure, unadorned note: the only unscored, unaccompanied, unprocessed frequency in the Inn. No reverb. No layering. Just the stone, audible. The composers call it "the spinel's confession." The spinel calls it Tuesday.

### Tanzanite (the Vault's deposit)
**Lives:** the rarity case, at the Vault's far end, with the geology report and nothing else.
**Thread:** one place on earth — the Vault's lesson in rarity without greed. The tanzanite is displayed with its geology report and no price, no provenance romance, no legend: the simple fact that the entire supply of this stone comes from four kilometers of mountain that are still rising, and that the mountain will decide when it is finished. The Vault, which could inflate the story, states it plainly: rare is not the same as hoarded, and the stone is not for sale, and the case is not locked, and the stone has never been taken, because the guests who reach this case have read all the other cases first. The Vault trusts the curriculum. The curriculum has never failed it.
**Song:** the single-source tone — the frequency the Vault plays at the rarity case: one note, from one stone, from one mountain, unmixed. The Inn's entire philosophy of rarity, in one held sound: it does not need accompaniment to be worth hearing.

## THE FORGE / UNDERCITY WORKSHOPS

### Copper (the Forge's deposit)
**Lives:** the anvil rail, the tool racks, the wiring of the whole workshop.
**Thread:** the forge's first friend — the Blacksmith's whole philosophy in a bar of metal: soft enough to shape with stone tools, generous enough to carry energy wherever asked, patient enough to turn green in old age without complaint. The Forge's tools are copper-riveted, the wiring is copper, the philosophy is copper. The Blacksmith, asked why the Inn's metalsmith works the way the ancients did, holds up the bar: "This one has been telling me the answer for twenty years." The question is never repeated.
**Song:** the working tone — the Forge's rhythm section: the hammer's pulse on copper, the original percussive loop the Inn's 50 BPM architecture was scored against. Every beat in the building traces to this rail. The Blacksmith knows. The Blacksmith has always known.

### Frost Iron
**Lives:** the cold rack, by the quench that steams in summer.
**Thread:** the cold-worked iron — the winter forge's edge, the metal that does its hardening in the cold, the Forge's proof that heat is not the only transformation. The Hollow, the Unseelie winter court, imports exactly one thing from the Undercity, and this is it: frost iron, for the winter edge, delivered annually with no paperwork and no explanation. The Blacksmith makes it. The Hollow collects it. Neither has ever discussed it. The Inn's opinion on this arrangement is that it is the single most stable trade agreement in the building, precisely because it has never been written down.
**Song:** the quench — the hiss of hot metal in cold water, the Forge's one dramatic sound, played at every hardening. The tri-mode's hard cut was scored from this: the sudden cold sound, the same shape as 42Hz into 84Hz. The composers came down here to record it. The Blacksmith obliged. The Blacksmith says it was nothing. The recording says otherwise.

### Hematite (the Forge's deposit)
**Lives:** the raw case — the ore's patience, before the art.
**Thread:** the raw case — the Forge's museum of itself: hematite before it becomes anything, the grey-silver heaviness that every blade and bearing begins as. The Blacksmith keeps the raw case stocked so that guests who watch the finished work forget its weight. Every horseshoe, hinge, and gargoyle bracket in the Inn began as this. The Forge honors its beginnings. The raw case is where the honoring is done. The Blacksmith adds a piece to it each year, unmarked. The case is the Blacksmith's diary. It reads well.
**Song:** the pre-tone — the Forge's lowest register: the sound of the anvil before the strike, the held breath of the workshop, played under every session. The silence in it is load-bearing. The Blacksmith taught the composers that. It is the lesson they quote most.

### Pyrite (the Forge's deposit)
**Lives:** the spark case, by the striker — the fire-starter, the warning, the joke.
**Thread:** the spark case — pyrite in the Forge is the working stone: the flint-and-steel spark, the fire that starts honestly, from actual work. The Staircase keeps pyrite as the beautiful lie; the Forge keeps it as the plain truth — the stone that makes fire when asked and asks nothing after. The Blacksmith's card, on the case, is the shortest in the Forge: "Upstairs, a lesson. Down here, a lighter." Both the alchemists and the Blacksmith consider their pyrite the real one. Both are right. That is the joke, and the case is where it is kept.
**Song:** the strike — the percussion of the striker on pyrite: the Inn's official sound of beginning. Every fire in the building, every forge session, every single session of every game the Inn has ever run starts with this recorded strike. The players never know. The spark knows. The spark is not telling.

### The Native Metals (Gold, Platinum, Copper, Frost Iron, together)
**Lives:** the family album — one case, the whole periodic table's metals wing of the Inn.
**Thread:** the Forge's family album — the metals displayed together the way the garnets got their wall: one family, many tempers. Gold, the masterpiece. Platinum, the one that never tarnishes and never mentions it. Copper, the first friend. Frost iron, the winter one. The Blacksmith keeps the album the way families keep theirs: rarely opened, never weeded, always accurate. Guests who ask which metal the Blacksmith loves most get shown the whole case and no answer. The whole case is the answer.
**Song:** the alloy chord — the metals' frequencies played as one: the Inn's densest harmony, the sound of a family that argues and holds. The Blacksmith hums it while working. The Blacksmith denies humming it. The workshop has recorded it anyway. The case stands by the recordings.

## THE SONG — the synthesis

The weave is finished, and the architecture it reveals is the Inn's oldest secret told plainly: every stone in the building is an instrument, and the building is the one instrument.

The frequency-keepers, in order of the descent:

The Octave Stone holds the turning point — the scale was never meant to end, only to turn around, and the Stone Circle Chamber's approval is on file. Clear Quartz holds the standard — the reference pitch, the A every other letter is measured from. Nuummite holds the floor — the deepest tone, the foundation that does not transform, felt in the feet before it is heard. Blue Hole Calcite holds the water's stillness — the one uninterrupted note in the Inn, the held tone with no known beginning. And the Stillness Stone holds the rest — the pause between the notes, which the Inn insists is part of the music, and which it has now, in five volumes, proven.

But the proof is in the crossing. The copper rail carries the Song from the Cosmology to the Catacombs. The tri-mode's hard cut was scored from the forge quench. The Whisper Layer's subtext runs at 12dB under the angelite. The garnets' seven-note motif resolves only in a single walk. The pearl sisters' chord stands alone at one moored point in the Cove. The phantom quartz plays the palimpsest theme — the Song's own theme, foreshadowed in a restoration wing, two melodies at once, neither masking the other. And the amethyst keeps getting stolen and coming home, which the Inn counts as the Song's proof performed on the guests: transformation is for traveling, and traveling is for returning.

The Tool lesson holds throughout: none of it layers. It fuses. The heartbeat, the hum, the tones, the thresholds, the silences — one sound the player cannot separate into parts. The math is the mysticism.

The final note belongs to the Inn's whole position, stated once, in stone, in five volumes, two hundred stones, and one Song:

Nothing has to be erased to be continued.

The weave is the proof. Ask any stone inside.
