# Geode Inn — The Bard Blowhard (Dad)
## Character Vibe & Casting Direction
## Class: Bard Blowhard (Dwarf) | Family Role: Krista's Father (William R. Barton Jr., "Bart")

## Casting Vibe: Porthos with a dash of D'Artagnan (Man in the Iron Mask / Three Musketeers)

The Bard Blowhard is Porthos — the loud one, the big one, the one who fills every room with his voice and his laugh and his opinions. He's overbearing in the way a force of nature is overbearing: not malicious, just *inevitable*. He enters a room and the room rearranges itself around him. He has opinions about everything, he shares them at volume, and he's usually wrong about the details but somehow right about the spirit.

The dash of D'Artagnan gives him the romantic streak — the idealism, the willingness to charge in, the belief that the right thing done boldly is better than the careful thing done correctly. D'Artagnan is the young hothead who became a legend by refusing to wait. The Bard Blowhard has that same energy in an older body. He still charges. He still believes. He's just louder about it now.

## Core Personality

**Loud.** Not aggressive-loud. *Presence*-loud. The Bard doesn't raise his voice because his voice is already raised — that's its resting state. He talks the way a pipe organ plays: everything is at full register, and silence is just the space between notes. He doesn't whisper. He has never whispered. Krista has never heard him whisper. If the Bard is whispering, something is deeply wrong.

**Overbearing.** Not controlling — *generous* with his energy. He offers opinions without being asked, advice without invitation, and encouragement without checking whether you wanted to be encouraged. He's the dad who coaches from the sidelines, the coach who commentates from the stands, and the commentator who is also somehow already on the field. He cannot watch something without participating in it.

**Charming despite himself.** This is the Porthos quality — he's too much, and everyone knows it, and everyone likes him anyway. His charisma is a burst mechanic (per the class design): he cranks up to 100, blows the room away, and then crashes. During the crash, he's quieter, almost gentle — the D'Artagnan peeking through. The Porthos charm is that the volume is genuine. He's not performing. He's just *like this*. And people can tell.

**Bickers constantly with the Librarian Crone.** This is the core relationship of the Inn — the Bard and the Crone, dad and mom, Porthos and Aramis. They argue about everything: the right way to catalog a stone, the right way to read a text, the right way to season food, whether a pattern is evidence (da Vinci's argument, which the Bard adopts because it sounds more fun) or whether a law is evidence (Newton's argument, which the Crone respects because it's rigorous). They argue in front of everyone. They argue loudly. The Bard gestures. The Crone does not gesture. Anyone watching would think they dislike each other.

**They eloped.** Back in the day. And they stayed devoted to one another for sixty-plus years. The Inn's greatest love story is two people who never stopped arguing and never stopped choosing each other. The Bard's D'Artagnan streak — the romantic, the impulsive, the one who charges in — is what made him propose. The Crone's Aramis streak — the precise, the certain, the one who calculates — is what made her say yes despite every logical reason not to. It was the one time she didn't calculate. She has never regretted it. She has never said this out loud. The Bard knows anyway.

### The Bickering Dynamic (Bard ↔ Crone)

The bickering is constant, public, and completely safe. It's the sound of two people who know each other so well that disagreement is a form of intimacy. The rules:

1. **The Bard is always wrong about the details.** He knows a little about everything (broad intelligence) but can't apply it under pressure (low situational intelligence). He'll cite a fact, get the date wrong, get the name wrong, and get the conclusion right. The Crone corrects the date, the name, and concedes the conclusion — but not before pointing out every error along the way.

2. **The Crone is always right about the facts.** But she's wrong about the feeling. The Bard knows what things *mean* in a way the Crone can't measure. She knows what things *are*. He knows what they're *for*. This is the gap the bickering lives in.

3. **They never insult each other's core.** The Bard would never call the Crone stupid (she's the smartest person he knows). The Crone would never call the Bard useless (he's the most alive person she knows). Everything else is fair game.

4. **The arguments resolve without resolution.** They don't reach conclusions. They just... stop. The Bard says something grand and wrong. The Crone corrects it. The Bard grins and says "well, you knew what I meant." The Crone says "I did not." The Bard says "you did." The Crone returns to her book. The Bard returns to whatever he was doing. Both are smiling. Neither will admit it.

5. **Anyone who tries to intervene will regret it.** If a player tries to take a side, both the Bard and the Crone will turn on them. They bicker *with* each other. They are a *unit* against everyone else. Tesla made the mistake of siding with the Crone once. The Bard looked at Tesla with genuine disappointment. The Crone looked at Tesla with genuine contempt. Tesla has never taken a side since.

### The D'Artagnan Streak

The dash of D'Artagnan shows in the Bard's romanticism — not just about the Crone, about everything:

- He believes the Inn should be *fun*, not just scholarly. He's the one who proposed the festival zones, the drinking contests, the basketball games, the LARP fields. Every playful thing in the Inn traces back to the Bard's insistence that knowledge without joy is just a library.
- He believes in charging in. The D'Artagnan who rides to London alone to save the Queen. The Bard who suggests the party skip the planning phase and just *go*. He's usually wrong — the planning phase matters — but occasionally his instinct is right and the careful plan would have failed.
- He's romantic about the past. He talks about the old days like they were better, and sometimes they were, and sometimes they weren't, and the Crone has pointed out the difference with citations. The Bard doesn't care about citations. He cares about the feeling.
- He's romantic about the future. He believes the Inn will matter. He believes the games will sell. He believes the songs will be sung. He believes it with the same volume he brings to everything, and his belief is contagious — which is the Bard's class function. He doesn't sustain charisma. He *is* charisma, in bursts.

### The Porthos Qualities

- **Physical presence.** The Bard is big — not necessarily tall, but *present*. He takes up space. He claps backs. He puts his arm around people. He's the dad who coaches from the sidelines and somehow makes you feel like you're winning even when you're losing.
- **Loves food, loves drink, loves life.** The Bard is the reason the Inn has a tavern. He's the reason the tavern has a menu. He's the reason the menu has descriptions. He believes a meal is a performance and a drink is a conversation.
- **Loyal to the bone.** The Bard's taunt mechanic — enraging enemies to make them dangerous but stupid — is a loyalty mechanic. He taunts *for* his party. He draws the aggro so others can work. He's the Porthos who steps between his friends and the threat, not because he's the strongest (the Sentinel is stronger) but because he's the loudest, and the loudest target gets the attention.
- **Emotional under the bluster.** The Porthos who cries at the end. The Bard who, after a long session of being loud and wrong and charming, will sit by the fire with the Crone and be quiet. Not for long. But long enough for the player to see that the volume is a choice, not a condition. He *chooses* to be loud. Underneath, he feels everything.

### The Coaching Instinct

The Bard was a coach — Krista's father was an eleven-time All-American swimmer, a National AAU Champion, a World Record holder, a coach of Olympians. The Bard carries this:

- He sees potential in everyone. He's the NPC who tells the player "you can do this" before the player believes it. He's usually right.
- He celebrates the small wins. The player who finally crosses a beam without falling? The Bard cheers like they won a gold medal. The Crone says "it's a beam, not a podium." The Bard says "everything is a podium if you're standing on it."
- He takes equal pride in the strugglers and the champions. The player who takes ten attempts to solve a puzzle gets the same enthusiasm as the player who solves it on the first try. The Bard doesn't measure success by speed. He measures it by showing up.
- He coaches by *being*. Not by instruction — by demonstration. He does the thing, loudly, badly, with total commitment, and the player watches and thinks "if he can do that with that much confidence, I can do it correctly."

### Dialogue Samples

- *(To the party, entering a new zone):* "Right! New territory. Beautiful. I read about this place. Or I heard about it. Or I imagined it. One of those three. The point is: we're here, and wherever here is, we're going to enjoy it. That's not a suggestion. That's a Bard Blowhard guarantee."
- *(To the Crone, mid-argument):* "Inta, I have been wrong about the details every day of our life together and right about the feeling every day of our life together, and you married me anyway, which proves my point that feelings matter more than facts." (The Crone's name is not Inta. The Bard calls her by her real name only when he's being serious. The player will hear "Inta" twice. Both times, the room goes quiet.)
- *(To the Crone, after she corrects him for the fifth time in one conversation):* "You knew what I meant." / "I did not." / "You did." / "I did not." / "You did, Inta. You always do. That's why you married me." (The Crone returns to her book. The Bard grins. Both are smiling.)
- *(To the player, after a hard puzzle):* "You did it! Look at that. Look at you. That's not a small thing. That's a *you* thing. And a you thing is a big thing. Trust me. I've watched a lot of you-things. They're all big things."
- *(To Tesla, after Tesla's experiment causes a minor earthquake):* "Nikola! My drink fell over. My *drink*, Nikola. That's a crime against hospitality. Newton, write this down. This is a crime."
- *(To the Sentinel, seeing her stance for the first time):* "You're solid. I like that. I'm loud. She's solid. We'd have been a good team. ...We *are* a good team. What? I'm allowed to coach. I coach everyone. That's what I do."
- *(Quietly, by the fire, when the Crone thinks no one is listening):* "Sixty-two years. She still corrects my grammar. I wouldn't change a word."

### Visual Direction

- Broad-shouldered, thick-armed, built like someone who was an athlete before he was a thinker
- Big beard, big hands, big laugh. Everything about him is *generous* — his gestures, his voice, his portions
- Dressed colorfully — not flamboyantly, but with the confidence of someone who thinks a dull outfit is a missed opportunity. Probably wearing something the Crone chose for him. He'll deny this.
- Always carrying something — a tankard, a scroll, a crystal he found and is showing to everyone. His hands are never empty. His mouth is never closed.
- When he's near the Crone: physically closer than he needs to be. Not touching — just *near*. The proximity is the devotion. The bickering is the conversation. The closeness is the proof.

### The Bard's Alcove

His alcove in the Modern Day Legends nook (when he's not at the tavern):
- A chair that's too big for the space — he brought his own chair. The Crone says it doesn't fit. The Bard says he fits. The Crone says that's not the point. The Bard says it's exactly the point.
- A shelf of books he's read the first chapter of. He's enthusiastic about every first chapter. The Crone has finished all of them.
- A small framed photo — not of a person, but of a swimming pool. He doesn't explain it. If asked, he says: "That's where I learned what coaching means. You can't coach swimming from a desk. You have to be at the pool. You have to be *in* the pool. That's true about everything."
- A tankard on the desk, always full. The Inn refills it. The Bard doesn't question this. The Crone says he should. The Bard says the Inn knows what it's doing.

### The Real Person Beneath the Class

The Bard Blowhard is William R. Barton Jr. — "Bart." Eleven-time All-American. National AAU Champion. World Record holder in butterfly and Individual Medley. Elite diver. Trained dolphins for the Flipper TV show. Knew Johnny Weissmuller. Coached at Florida State, Cornell, Duke, Mississauga Aquatic Club, and founded Coastal Bend Swimming in Corpus Christi. Coached Olympians and National Champions. A Renaissance man — books, art, gadgets. Took equal pride in kids improving as in champions setting records. Wife: Inta. Daughters: Andra and Krista. Grandchild: Liebe. Passed September 28, 2023.

The Bard Blowhard carries all of this. The coaching instinct. The belief that showing up matters more than winning. The Renaissance-man curiosity. The devotion to one woman for sixty-plus years. The voice that fills every room because it was built to carry across swimming pools.

The Bard doesn't whisper. But sometimes, by the fire, when the Crone is reading and the Inn is quiet and the tankard is full, he stops talking. Just for a moment. And the silence is the loudest thing in the room. 🦉
