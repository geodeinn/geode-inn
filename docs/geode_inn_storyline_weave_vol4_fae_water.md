## THE FAE KINGDOM — the courts, the circles, the Between

### Preseli Blue Stone
**Lives:** the Stone Circle Chamber — the deepest point of the Fae Kingdom — and every portal arch in the Between.
**Thread:** the chosen stone. Quarried from the Welsh mountains, hauled a hundred and fifty miles by hand to Salisbury Plain before the wheel existed, to stand at Stonehenge, where it still stands, still ringing faintly when struck. The Fae Kingdom's first key: winning the Standing Stones game awards a piece, and the piece opens the portal, because the Fae recognize the stone that was chosen when chosen things mattered. The courts disagree about everything except this. The Between's arches are mortared with Preseli dust and intention. Ask the stone inside. The door knows.
**Song:** the true tone — the frequency that opens the circles. The Inn will not print the number. It is struck, not played, and only by a guest who has earned the strike.

### Welsh Slate (the portal deposit)
**Lives:** the portal arches — the doorframes of every circle in the Between.
**Thread:** the ancient roof, promoted to doorframes. The same Welsh geology that raised the bluestones raised this slate, and the portal builders used both: bluestone for the key, slate for the lock's housing. The Fae consider this architecture of the highest comedy — the door and its key cut from the same mountain, so that the portal is, technically, the mountain coming to visit itself. The courts laugh about this. The courts laugh about almost nothing.
**Song:** the threshold hum — the frequency the arches hold while deciding whether to open. It sounds like held breath. It is.

### Fluorite Family (Green, Purple, Rainbow)
**Lives:** the Crystal Gardens, growing — actually growing, in rows, like a crop that knows it is being watched.
**Thread:** the Fae crystals. The Crystal Gardens are the Fae Kingdom's neutral ground — the deepest cultivation in the Inn, tended by both courts under the Griffin's ancient truce — and fluorite is the crop: green for the Seelie harvest, purple for the Unseelie, the rainbow pieces split custody down the seam. Fluorite is the most organized stone in the Inn and the most Fae about it: perfect cubes, impossible colors, and an insistence on being two things at once. The courts harvest it for their working magics. The Griffin harvests nothing. The Griffin is above the economy.
**Song:** the dual-tone — a chord that is one note to the Seelie ear and a different note to the Unseelie. Both are correct. The composers refuse to explain further.

### Labradorite
**Lives:** the Between, set into the path at intervals, like cairns that flash.
**Thread:** the Fae flash — the stone that shows its light only at the right angle, like the courts themselves, like the whole Kingdom. The Between is the shifting borderland, and its path is set with labradorite because the Between can only be walked correctly by someone willing to keep shifting their head. Travelers count the flashes. The number is never the same twice. The Fae consider this a feature of reality, not a flaw in the path, and they are very relaxed about which it is.
**Song:** the shift tone — the frequency of the angle changing. It plays at the exact moment a flash resolves, and the Fae dance to it, which surprises guests, because the Fae dance to almost nothing.

### Green Labradorite
**Lives:** the Fae reading nooks — the quiet alcoves off the Between, where the light is sideways.
**Thread:** the second-look stone — the famous family flash without the fame, holding its light at an angle nobody usually bothers with, for whoever finally does. The nooks are where the Kingdom keeps its slower guests and its faster secrets: the stones that reward the patient. A Fae who has lived three hundred years will sit in a green labradorite nook for a season, waiting for a particular angle of summer light. When it comes, the Fae says nothing. The stone says nothing. Everyone involved considers this a complete conversation.
**Song:** the patience tone — nearly subliminal, always present in the nooks, never once resolved. The Kingdom's calmest music. The courts both claim it. The nooks both allow this.

### Staurolite (the fairy cross)
**Lives:** the fairy roads, as mile markers — the crossing stones.
**Thread:** the meeting made mineral: two crystals that grew toward each other and met in the middle, both changed into the shape of the meeting. The old roads called them fairy crosses and pressed them into travelers' hands, and the Fae Kingdom honors the tradition at full value: the crossing stones mark every place where two fairy roads meet, and travelers who find one are told, by everyone — Seelie, Unseelie, and the Griffin, who does not usually tell anyone anything — to keep it. The crosses are the Kingdom's only universal superstition. The Kingdom denies having superstitions. The Kingdom keeps the crosses anyway.
**Song:** the intersection tone — two melodic lines meeting mid-phrase and becoming one line that is neither and both. The Inn's composers consider it the hardest thing they've written. The stone considers it obvious.

### Hackmanite
**Lives:** the Hollow — the Unseelie Court's deep winter — as the only clock.
**Thread:** the stone that keeps the dark's schedule: violet at rest, pale in the light, reversing honestly when the light changes back. The Hollow has no sun and wants none, so the Unseelie read hackmanite like a sundial, if the sun were the thing they were avoiding. When the stone goes violet, the Hollow is deep. When it pales, something has brought light in, and the Court adjusts its politics accordingly. The stone is the Hollow's whole calendar, weather report, and newspaper. It has never been wrong. It has also never been early.
**Song:** the inverse tone — 84Hz, the Shadow frequency, the Hollow's ambient hum. The tri-mode's hard cut lives here, cut into the Kingdom's score like a season.

### Blue Chalcedony
**Lives:** the Threshold — the entry meadow where the portal deposits newcomers.
**Thread:** the warding blue — the softest guard in the Inn. The Threshold is the Kingdom's front porch, and it is ringed with blue chalcedony because the Fae's first law of hospitality is also their last: the guest arrives protected, or the invitation was a lie. The stone does not fight what should not enter. It simply suggests, at a frequency and in a color, that elsewhere might be nicer. It works. The Threshold has never once had an incident. The chalcedony is never praised. This is the Threshold's second law: the best guards are the ones nobody mentions.
**Song:** the courtesy tone — the note that plays under every Fae greeting, the Kingdom's "welcome" in one frequency. It sounds like a door being held open. It is.

### Amazonite
**Lives:** the Summerlands' gaming tables, cut into the gaming stones themselves.
**Thread:** the gambler's luck, the Fae version — the Summerlands play for joy and take the games seriously anyway, and the tables are cut from amazonite because the Seelie prefer their luck in the color of rivers in sunlight. The house does not always win. The house is not really trying to win, and the tables are set so that the games themselves are the prize. The Unseelie, visiting, find this baffling. The Unseelie play anyway. The amazonite treats both courts' chips identically, which is the most diplomatic object in the Kingdom.
**Song:** the play tone — the flickering figure that runs under the Summerlands games, faster than the waltzes, brighter than the court's official register. The Summerlands' own music, kept deliberately unofficial.

### Charoite (the Kingdom's deposit)
**Lives:** the Stone Circle Chamber's walls, swirling at the deepest point of the Kingdom.
**Thread:** the swirl the Fae claim they taught the Staircase — the transformation stone, at the bottom of everything. The Circle Chamber is where the courts meet in truce and the Kingdom's deepest business is done, and the walls are charoite because the Chamber's whole function is transformation: enemies into guests, debts into stories, and once a season, at the full moon, the entire truce renewed. The stone swirls. The walls remember every renewal. The Fae do not keep records. The Chamber keeps them.
**Song:** the resolution — the seven courts' themes braided into one chord at the Chamber's center, the deepest sound in the Kingdom, played when the truce holds. It has always held. The stone expects it to.

## THE WATER ZONES — Andra's Cove, the Tidal Caves, Atlantis, the deep water

### Aquamarine
**Lives:** the Cove's keystone case, at the waterline, half in and half out.
**Thread:** the sailor's stone — the calm at the center of the cove. Andra's Cove is named for the water guardian who keeps it, and aquamarine is Andra's own stone: the beryl of the sea, the color of the water's best mood, kept at the exact line where the tide argues with the land. The case floods twice daily. The stone is calibrated for it. The Cove's guests learn the tide from the case before they learn it from the water, which Andra considers the correct order of education.
**Song:** the surface tone — the Inn's most-played water frequency, the note that is always in every water zone's mix, the sound of the top of the sea. Every other water stone tunes to this one first.

### Amazonian Aquamarine
**Lives:** the Cove's southern case, among the jungle stones.
**Thread:** the river's version — the same beryl from the river country, blue with green in it, the color of water that has somewhere to be. The Cove keeps it beside the ocean aquamarine as the water zones' gentle geography lesson: the sea and the river are the same stone with different schedules. The southern case was Andra's idea. The river guardian visits it. The two guardians are old friends, and the case is where they stand.
**Song:** the current — a flowing figure, faster than the surface tone, always going somewhere. The Cove's southern music. The river says you can tell where it's headed. The river is right.

### Larimar
**Lives:** Atlantis — the drowned wing of the water zones, where the light reaches everything.
**Thread:** the Atlantis stone — Caribbean blue, one mountain in the whole world, found on the one island the Inn considers the drowned city's closest shore. Larimar is the drowned wing's one survivor, and it remembers the water: it is calm the way only things that have already survived their flood can be calm. The Atlantis wing tells its story without pathos, which is the Inn's choice and the stone's example — the city ended; the stone carried on; the water is not the villain of the story. The water never is. The water zones say so at every turn.
**Song:** the sunken tone — the Inn's lowest water frequency, felt through the floor of the Atlantis wing, the note of the deep remembering the surface. The divers say they hear it at forty meters. The Inn believes them. The stone does too.

### Blue Opal (Peruvian)
**Lives:** the Andean shelf of the Cove, up in the dry gallery above the tide line.
**Thread:** the mountain's blue that never froze — Andean opal, the water stone that climbed. The water zones keep the shelf to make the point the sea itself makes at every coastline: the water goes up. Every Andean blue began as ocean, was lifted by the mountains, and stayed blue out of loyalty. The shelf is the water zones' proudest display and the mountains' quietest joke. Both parties find the arrangement correct.
**Song:** the altitude tone — a water frequency with the air in it, thinner and brighter, the sound the water zones play for guests who are afraid of the sea. It is the sea, from above. It usually helps.

### Water Opal
**Lives:** the tide pools, in the shallow cases that the tide reaches on the spring tides.
**Thread:** the stone that is mostly water, honestly — hydrophane opal, porous and clear, that turns translucent when soaked and milky when dry. The tide pools keep water opal because it is the pools' own mineral: a stone that changes with the water's schedule, in the water, on the water's clock. Children hold it under the sea and watch it clear. Adults do the same. The Inn has watched adults do this for hours and includes the detail in exactly no documentation, because some things should be found.
**Song:** the tide figure — the Inn's slowest melody, one full phrase per tide cycle, timed to the actual water. The composition is six hours long. The water has never once heard the end of it.

### Dreamtime Opal
**Lives:** the water country's case, on the Australian shelf.
**Thread:** the water country's opal — Australian opal from the artesian basin, the stone of the oldest continuous stories on earth, kept in the Inn's water zones because the water country insists: the stories and the water come from the same ground. The Dreamtime case is the only case in the water zones that faces the land. The Inn placed it that way after the first consultation, and it has never been questioned. Some stones face where they're from.
**Song:** the story tone — a figure the water zones play at dusk only, handed-down, unchanged, the oldest melody in the Inn's repertoire. The composers are not permitted to arrange it. The composers agree.

### Australian Boulder Opal
**Lives:** the reef shelf — the ironstone matrix displayed whole, sea and stone in one specimen.
**Thread:** the opal that stayed home — the fire running through the ironstone it was born in, the gem and its bedrock still one stone, refusing the idea that beauty must be extracted to count. The water zones keep the boulder whole on the reef shelf because the reef itself is the same argument in coral: the color belongs to the structure. Guests ask if the opal could be cut free. The shelf's answer is its entire inscription: "It could. It won't."
**Song:** the reef tone — two registers at once, the iron's low hum under the opal's bright figure, inseparable by design. The audio team calls this mix "the boulder." Every other duo in the Inn is scored against it.

### Sea Glass
**Lives:** the shore cases — the water zones' front door, inlaid in the threshold itself.
**Thread:** the sea's revenge on glass — the shore's patient recycling, the only stone in the Inn that people made, the sea finished, and both sign the card. The Cove's threshold is inlaid with sea glass because the water zones know what they are: the place where the human-made and the water-worn end up in the same case, tumbled into the same beauty. Every piece in the threshold was found, not made. The sea has been making them for as long as there has been glass to throw. The sea does not mind the work. The sea says the work is the point.
**Song:** the shore tone — the soft grinding figure, waves on sand, the frequency of the boundary where everything is smoothed. The water zones' welcome music. It sounds like forgiveness. It is.

### Abalone
**Lives:** the Cove's front door — the waterline lintel, every archway into the water zones, inlaid.
**Thread:** the shell's ocean — the wave-layered nacre, the iridescence that the shellfish spent a lifetime laying down one microscopic tile at a time, the water's own mosaic art. The water zones' doorways are inlaid with abalone because the zones' whole thesis is at the threshold: the sea is patient, the sea is generous, and the sea is the best decorator in the building, given time. Guests pass under the nacre and into the water's house. The water appreciates the manners. The shell appreciates the traffic.
**Song:** the shimmer — the highest overtone in the water zones' register, played at the exact pitch of light on nacre. The Inn's most beautiful frequency, by the vote of everyone who has heard it.

### Coral (black, branch) & Cyprus Coral Stone
**Lives:** the memorial wing — the quiet gallery of the water zones, kept dim, kept cool, kept honest.
**Thread:** the reef's bones, the tide's archive — coral that built cities while it was alive and keeps the architecture now that it's stone. The memorial wing is the water zones' grief for what the sea is still losing, and the coral is the wing's stone of record because coral is the grief that built something: the skeleton was the gift, the city outlasted the citizen. The wing does not scold. The wing remembers. The water zones consider this the strongest form of protest available to a stone.
**Song:** the archive tone — a slow pulse through the wing, at reef rhythm, the frequency of living coral's quiet music, kept playing so the stone remembers being alive. The divers call it the heartbeat. It is not a metaphor.

### The Pearl Family (Pearl, Freshwater, River, Maldivian, Mangrove, Coral Pearl)
**Lives:** the Cove's crown case, on the water itself — a floating case, moored at the center of the Cove.
**Thread:** the six sisters — the water's patience made solid, one grain at a time. The crown case floats on the Cove's still water and holds the family: the classic, the lake's, the river's, the atoll's, the mangrove's quiet blue-grey, and the coral's rare pink. Six waters, one answer: the nacre is laid down around the wound until the wound becomes the treasure, which is the pearl family's entire theology and the Inn's most borrowed lesson. The case is reached by a plank bridge. Guests go slowly. The pearls appreciate it.
**Song:** the sisters' chord — six pure tones, one per pearl, that combine into a single held note at the center of the Cove. The mooring lines were placed so the case sits at the exact point where the chord stands alone. The Inn calls this spot the Cove's ear. The pearls call it home.

### Blue Hole Calcite
**Lives:** the deep chamber beneath the Cove — the blue hole, the vertical water.
**Thread:** the deep blue hole, captured — the stone the Cove is named for, the calcite that glows the exact blue of the light at the bottom of a blue hole, the color the water keeps where the sun has mostly given up. The deep chamber is the Cove's vertical mystery: a shaft of still water that no current reaches, and the calcite sits at the bottom of it, glowing faintly, the water's own night-light. Guests are allowed to dive it. Andra goes first, every time. Andra has been going first for as long as the Cove has kept count, which is forever.
**Song:** the held note — the water zones' one truly static frequency, the blue hole's uninterrupted tone, the deepest sustained sound in the Inn's water architecture. It has never stopped. Nobody knows when it started. The composers call it "the calcite's decision."

### Lemurian Blue Calcite (Blue Onyx)
**Lives:** the drowned library — the Archive's underwater annex, at the edge of the Cove.
**Thread:** the drowned library — soft blue banded like light through deep water, holding the story about knowledge that sank before it was lost, and waited. The Archive upstairs keeps books; the annex keeps the ones the water claimed, and the Lemurian calcite is the annex's entire thesis in stone: some things are not destroyed by the flood, only filed beneath it, patient, still legible to whoever dives. The Siren is the annex's librarian, and the calcite is the reading stone. The Siren has read all of it. The Siren says the ending is good. The Siren refuses to say more.
**Song:** the hush — the muffled, underwater frequency of reading in an absorbed silence, played through the water only. Surface guests hear it as pressure. Divers hear it as music. Both are correct.

### Rhine Quartz
**Lives:** the riverbank cases, along the fresh-water channel that feeds the Cove.
**Thread:** the river stone — the Rhine's own clarity, the quartz that gave the river its legend of gold, kept along the fresh-water channel because the channel is the Cove's tributary memory: the water zones honor every river that ever carried something to the sea. The Rhine carried legends. The cases hold the quartz that watched them pass. The river's sirens, the Inn notes, were the original rough crowd — the Lorelei was a rock in that river, and the stone keeps a professional sympathy for everyone who ever sang on it.
**Song:** the current figure — the flowing melody of the fresh-water channel, an old Lorelei tune slowed to safe speed, the Inn's one rehabilitated danger-song. The river approved the arrangement. The river keeps the original on file.

### Vistula Agate
**Lives:** the riverbank cases, the Baltic reach of the channel.
**Thread:** the river agate — the Vistula's banded patient, the amber river's quieter sibling, carried from the mountains to the Baltic over a million patient years. The fresh-water channel keeps it beside the Rhine quartz as the water zones' lesson in river temperaments: some rivers carry legends, some carry amber, and the Vistula, carrying both at once and complaining about neither, is the channel's favorite. The agate's banding is the trip, recorded: every layer a stretch of the river, every line a year in the current.
**Song:** the layers — a slowly modulating figure, one shift per band, the stone's own itinerary rendered as music. The channel plays it as guests walk the riverbank. Everyone walks slower than they meant to. The agate has this effect. The channel finds it useful.

### Tannin Agate
**Lives:** the swamp reach — the dark-water bend of the channel, where the Cove admits it has a past.
**Thread:** the tea-dark water stone — the swamp's clear eye, banded in the browns and blacks of tannin water, the agate that formed in the water that stains everything and still came out with its clarity. The dark-water bend is the channel's honest stretch: not every water is bright, and the water zones refuse a wall of pure blues as a lie by omission. The tannin agate is the bend's proof — the clearest stone in the channel came out of the darkest water. The Bog visits this case regularly. The Bog says nothing on these visits. The Bog doesn't have to.
**Song:** the dark-water tone — the channel's low, warm register, the sound of water so brown it glows. It is not a threat frequency. It is the frequency of depth, kept honest.

### Aquaprase
**Lives:** the lagoon — the shallow bright reach of the water zones, the newest case, still being visited.
**Thread:** the lagoon that held still — blue-green chalcedony so clean it looks like captured lagoon water, discovered so recently the stone itself has barely been introduced. The lagoon is the water zones' newest feature and the aquaprase is its founding stone: the Inn is watching this one with interest, because new stones, like new guests, arrive knowing things. The lagoon's case has no inscription yet. The water zones are waiting to see what the stone says first. This is the Inn's oldest tradition with new arrivals, and the water zones keep it best.
**Song:** the new tone — a frequency the composers have not named, in a key the zones have not used before, played quietly, at the lagoon only. The stone is teaching it to them. They are learning it as fast as they can.

### Chrysocolla
**Lives:** the mediation table — the single slab of it, at the Cove's diplomatic reach.
**Thread:** the water teacher — the gentle blue-green, the wisdom stone that learned the better lesson: knowledge is common, but knowledge that speaks kindly is rare. The mediation table is a single slab of chrysocolla where the water zones settle their disputes — tides against walls, currents against schedules, the eternal gull disagreement — and every argument held over that table ends softer than it started, because the table is the wisest voice in the room and it never says a word. The Inn has considered installing chrysocolla tables everywhere. The chrysocolla has declined, politely, once per request.
**Song:** the counsel — the water zones' quietest melody, the frequency of a calm voice in a heated room, played whenever two parties sit at the table. The disputes end. The melody is never mentioned as the reason. The table prefers it that way.

### Chrysocolla Azurite
**Lives:** the treaty case, beside the mediation table.
**Thread:** the two blues in conversation — azurite's deep midnight, chrysocolla's gentle lagoon, grown into each other in the same copper vein, the treaty stone between the deep and the sky. The water zones keep it beside the mediation table as the proof that the method works: two blues that found each other underground and blended rather than competed. The case's inscription is the shortest in the Inn: "It can be done." The table concurs. The whole water architecture concurs.
**Song:** the duet — two blues, two voices, one song, the frequency that the water zones play for the signing of every agreement, anywhere, in any zone. The Inn's official peace music. Written by two stones, about each other.

### Ocean Jasper
**Lives:** the tide's promise case, at the Cove's far reach, facing the open water.
**Thread:** the orb stone from a shore only the tide can reach — ocean jasper, mined at low tide, from a cliff that the sea takes back twice a day, the stone that is only gatherable by the water's permission. The far reach keeps it as the water zones' promise case: the sea gives, on a schedule, and the stone keeps the record — every orb a day, every ring a season, the whole cliff's patience in a palm. The case faces the open water because that is who it is about.
**Song:** the gathering — the tide-cycle melody's bright companion, played at low tide only, the frequency of the water keeping its word. Twice a day, every day, the most reliable music in the Inn. The sea has never missed a performance.

### Sealite
**Lives:** the rocks — the haul-out shelf at the Cove's edge, where the sun lands first and the spray lands hardest.
**Thread:** the seal's stone, the shore's bright eye — the stone of the animals who chose both: the water and the land, the swim and the nap, the whole argument between the two worlds settled by an animal that simply lives in both. The haul-out shelf keeps sealite for the seals, who approved the arrangement by showing up. The seals' reviews of the shelf are all five stars. The seals cannot write. The stone writes nothing either. The shelf considers this arrangement ideal.
**Song:** the bark-and-lull — the water zones' most affectionate music, half splash, half nap, the frequency of the shoreline at its least serious. The composers wrote it watching. The seals took no notice. This was, the composers agree, the correct reception.

### Rainstone
**Lives:** the weather vane of the water zones, hung at the Cove's gate, in the weather, on purpose.
**Thread:** the stone that predicts weather by refusing to pretend it can't. The water zones hang rainstone at the gate the way harbors hang flags: a forecast is a courtesy, and the stone's has been accurate enough to embarrass instruments on three documented occasions. Guests ask how it works. The water zones' answer is the gate's entire signage: "It pays attention." The stone darkens before rain. The stone brightens before the clear. The stone has opinions about fog that the zones have learned to trust.
**Song:** the barometer — a shifting tone that follows the stone's mood, the only adaptive melody in the water architecture, scored by the weather. The composers wrote the frame. The sky does the rest.

### Cosmic Serpent Scale
**Lives:** shared custody — the water countries' creation case, argued over by the water zones and the Cosmology since the day it was catalogued.
**Thread:** the Rainbow Serpent's shed scale — the water countries' creation stone, the relic of the being whose dreaming shaped the rivers, and the Inn's most contested specimen: the water zones hold that a serpent that made the water belongs to the water zones; the Cosmology holds that a being that shaped creation belongs upstairs. The custody arrangement is the Inn's gentlest running argument. The scale itself tours annually between the two cases, traveling with an honor guard of one water zone escort and one Cosmology escort, who have become, the Inn notes, extremely good friends.
**Song:** the dreaming tone — the long arc, the deepest-to-highest sweep in the Inn's whole register, played only at the scale's arrival in each case. It takes four minutes. Both zones stop everything. Neither zone calls this worship. Both zones are wrong about that.

### River Amber
**Lives:** the riverbank cases' warm shelf, in the sunlight the channel catches at noon.
**Thread:** the river's sunlight — amber that traveled, resin to river to sea to shore, the sun's material carried by the water, the waterways' gold that isn't and is worth more. The warm shelf keeps river amber because the fresh-water channel deserves its own brightness: the channel carries everything to the sea, including the light, and the amber is the receipt. The water zones' youngest guests are told the whole journey, from tree to tide, and it is the first complete story the Inn ever tells them, because it has a hero, a journey, and a homecoming, and it's true.
**Song:** the carried light — the warmest frequency in the water register, the sound of sunlight that has been moving for a while, played on the warm shelf at noon. The channel calls it the amber hour. The amber calls it accurate.

### River Diamond
**Lives:** the patience case, at the channel's mouth, where the fresh water finally meets the Cove.
**Thread:** the river's cut — not diamond at all but river-tumbled quartz, worn brilliant by the water's own lapidary work, the patient proof-of-work the channel presents at its end. The patience case is the water zones' answer to every question about shortcuts: the river makes its diamonds by carrying them, for miles, for years, and the stone arrives at the Cove brilliant, and the river arrives with it, and both are the finished article. Guests who ask the Inn how anything gets done are walked to this case. It answers better than the Inn does.
**Song:** the finished note — the current figure's resolution, played where the channel meets the Cove, the sound of arrival. Every melody in the water zones resolves here, because every river does.
