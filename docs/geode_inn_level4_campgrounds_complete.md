# Geode Inn — Level 4: CAMPGROUNDS
## The Complete Level Design Document

---

## Zone Overview

The Campgrounds are the exterior of the Inn — the front porch, the market field, the tavern, the woods. The Inn's interface with the outside world. Where the archive meets the road. Where travelers arrive before they enter.

If the Main Floor is the Inn's body, the Campgrounds are the Inn's HANDS — the part that reaches outward, that touches the world, that makes things and trades things and welcomes people before they step inside.

The Campgrounds are a Renaissance Faire: canvas tents, wooden stalls, fire pits, banners, the smell of ale and forge smoke and grass. The sky is open here — the first time the player sees the full sky since entering the Inn. The transition from Main Floor to Campgrounds is the transition from home to market — from private to public, from enclosed warmth to open-air social energy.

The Campgrounds are the Inn's ECONOMY zone. The place where copper beads are earned and spent. Where the artisan craft lives. Where Krista's jewelry business connects to the game world — the physical booth experience translated into a digital Ren Faire.

---

## Entry Requirement

**The front door of the Inn.** The Campgrounds are accessible from the Main Floor at any time. No prerequisites. The player can walk out the front door from the very first moment.

This is deliberate. The Campgrounds are the WELCOME BEFORE THE DOOR — the social space that's easier to enter than the Inn. Some players will explore the Campgrounds before they feel ready to enter the Main Floor. The Campgrounds are the stepping stone.

---

## Visual Language

**Linework:** Heavy cel-shaded (tradesman style) — the Campgrounds are where the craftspeople live. Jay, Dani, the Blacksmith, the market vendors: all tradesmen, all rendered with bold 3-4px outlines and flat fills. The Campgrounds are the most visually SOLID zone — everything has weight, edges, presence. No ethereal no-outline here. No fragmented crisis lines. The Campgrounds are grounded because the people in them are grounded.

**Color Palette:**
- Primary: canvas tan, grass green, wood brown, sky blue
- Secondary: ale amber, forge orange, banner red, market-stripe purple
- Accents: copper (the market metal), steel (the blacksmith), leaf-green (the woods)

**Architecture:**
- Canvas tents and wooden stalls arranged in a loose semicircle around the Inn's front entrance
- The Huntsman Tavern: a larger wooden structure — not a tent, a proper building, but rustic. Log walls, a stone fireplace, a brass bell over the door. Jay's wolf sleeps on the porch.
- Dani's Tent-Studio: a canvas booth with the sides rolled up, showing the jeweler's bench inside. Warm light from a single lantern. The most organized space in the Campgrounds — Dani's zen made spatial.
- The Forge: at the edge of the Campgrounds, the blacksmith's workspace. Open-air. Smoke, sparks, the ring of hammer on metal.
- The Woods: the treeline beyond the Campgrounds. The wolf goes in; most people don't. The woods are the Campgrounds' edge — the boundary between the market and the wild.

**Sound:** The Campgrounds are the Inn's LOUDEST zone — not in volume, but in variety. The market murmurs, the forge rings, the tavern laughs, the wolf pants, banners snap in the wind. The 42Hz Song is present but distant — the Campgrounds are far from the Inn's center. The Song is like a bass note heard from outside a building: you feel it more than hear it.

## Spaces

### 1. The Market Field
The open center of the Campgrounds. Stalls arranged in a loose semicircle. The Market Field is the Inn's commercial space — the exterior counterpart to the Main Floor market, but with a different character. The Main Floor market is curated; the Campgrounds market is RAW — the place where goods arrive before they're sorted, where deals happen before they're formalized.

**Stalls:**
- **Stone Vendor** — sells raw stones for the Bead Board. Stones here are unpolished — the player buys rough material and must process it (a crafting step) before use. Cheaper than the Main Floor market, but requires effort.
- **Tool Vendor** — sells jeweler's tools, crafting implements, Bead Board accessories (tray materials, bead findings, wire).
- **The General Store** — potions, rations, travel supplies. The items the player needs for zone expeditions (torch oil for the Catacombs, rope for the Canopy City, cold-weather gear for the Water Zone).
- **The Bookmark Stall** — a small stall selling... bookmarks. Decorative bookmarks for the Inn's library system. The vendor is an eccentric who claims the bookmarks are enchanted. They're not. But they're beautiful, and the Crone collects them.

### 2. The Huntsman Tavern
Jay's domain. The social anchor of the Campgrounds. The tavern is a proper building — log walls, stone fireplace, brass bell over the door, a bar made from a single felled oak. The wolf sleeps on the porch.

**The Tavern Interior:**
- **The Bar** — Jay's counter. Ale on tap (Jay's own brew — "Huntsman's Gold"), a shelf of bottles, a jar of pickled eggs that nobody eats but Jay keeps restocking.
- **The Hearth** — a stone fireplace, smaller than the Firekeeper's but warmer in a different way. The Firekeeper's hearth is for stories; Jay's hearth is for company. People sit around Jay's fire to be together, not to listen.
- **The Tables** — long communal tables. No private booths. The tavern's layout forces socialization — you sit next to whoever's there. The tables are worn, carved with initials and marks from years of travelers.
- **The Wolf's Corner** — a bed of blankets by the door. The wolf's spot. The wolf is always there when Jay is there, and sometimes there when Jay isn't. The wolf guards the tavern when Jay steps out.
- **The Map Wall** — Jay keeps a wall map of the Inn's zones, annotated with traveler reports. "Water Zone: rough seas this week." "Fae Kingdom: Seelie Court in good mood." "Catacombs: new section opened." The map is the Campgrounds' quest board — Jay's version of the Innkeeper's List, but from the OUTSIDE.

**Visiting NPCs at the Tavern:**
- Travelers from other zones (resting before entering the Inn)
- The Cool (Clockwork Skies) — drinks here when the Arcana is docked. Jay and the Cool get along — two people who are comfortable in their own skin.
- The Pirate (Water Zone) — never comes inside, but the wolf has been seen sitting at the treeline looking toward the sea. Jay says the wolf "has a friend out there."
- Campgrounds residents — the regulars. The Fire Dancers, the Fortune Teller, the Blacksmith. The tavern is their living room.

### 3. Dani's Tent-Studio
Dani's workspace. A canvas booth with the sides rolled up — open to the market, open to the weather, open to visitors. A jeweler's bench in the center, stone samples in trays, tools hanging from the tent's crossbeams, finished pieces displayed on a velvet board.

**The Studio:**
- **The Bench** — where Dani works. The player can watch Dani work (a meditative idle animation — stone being shaped, polished, set). Watching Dani work for a few minutes grants a "Craftsman's Calm" buff (reduces stress, increases the next crafting attempt's success rate).
- **The Stone Library** — Dani's personal collection of stone samples, organized by Mohs hardness. The player can browse and learn. Each sample is labeled with the stone's frequency (connecting to the Sonic Mineral Codex). Dani's stone library is the BEAD BOARD's reference shelf — the player can check which stones produce which frequencies before designing.
- **The Display Board** — Dani's finished pieces. Each one is a "Wearable Song" — a bead arrangement that encodes a melody. The player can examine each piece, hear its melody, and learn the arrangement. Dani's display is the Bead Board's INSPIRATION GALLERY — the player can study Dani's work to learn design patterns.
- **The Commission Book** — a ledger of custom orders. The player can browse pending commissions to see what other NPCs have requested. Some commissions are available as quests — the player can fulfill them using the Bead Board.

### 4. The Forge
The Blacksmith's workspace. At the edge of the Campgrounds, open-air, smoking. The source of the Bead Board's structural metal palette — the copper, silver, and gold findings that hold beads together.

**The Forge:**
- **The Anvil** — where findings are made. The player can commission custom findings (clasps, spacers, wire wraps) from the Blacksmith. Each finding has a frequency contribution to the Bead Board's sonic output — copper (79Hz), silver (80Hz), gold (82Hz). The Blacksmith can alloy metals for intermediate frequencies.
- **The Metal Library** — samples of every metal used in the Inn's jewelry system, with frequency data. The structural metal palette is the Bead Board's FOUNDATION — the metal is the wire the beads are strung on. The metal's frequency is the drone beneath the beads' melody.
- **The Forge Fire** — the Blacksmith's fire, different from the Firekeeper's hearth. The Forge fire is WORKING fire — active, hot, purposeful. The Firekeeper's fire is STORY fire — warm, gentle, patient. Two fires, two purposes, two zones.

### 5. The Woods
The treeline beyond the Campgrounds. The boundary between the market and the wild. The wolf goes in; most people don't.

The woods are not dangerous — they're UNKNOWN. The Campgrounds are the known; the woods are the not-yet-known. The woods connect to the Fae Kingdom (below) and the Canopy City (above) — the tree's roots go down and the tree's branches go up, and the woods are the space between the market and the tree.

**The Woods contain:**
- **The Wolf's Paths** — trails the wolf has made. The wolf knows the woods. Following the wolf's paths leads to hidden items, stones, and NPCs.
- **The Traveler's Clearing** — a clearing where travelers camp before entering the Inn. Some are afraid; some are just waiting. Jay welcomes them (Jay's quest).
- **The Fortune Teller's Tent** — at the edge of the woods, farthest from the Inn. Deep purple and gold canvas. The Fortune Teller reads stones — the player can bring a stone to have its "fortune" read, which provides hints about which zones to explore next. The Fortune Teller is always at the edge — the person who sits at the boundary between the known and the unknown and reads the signs.

## Character Roster & Quests

### 1. Jay — "The Wolf and the Welcome"
**Station:** The Huntsman Tavern
**Title:** "The Host"

Jay is the Campgrounds' social anchor. Former Marine, tall, long black hair, Cherokee cheekbones, Voltaire-goth aesthetic. Loud, charismatic, no-nonsense warmth — calls BS with a grin. The derpy wolf is always nearby. Jay's tavern is the stepping stone — the social space that's easier to enter than the Inn.

**Audio Profile:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | ~100Hz (big, warm — the tavern keeper's projection) |
| Text Reveal Speed | ~40ms (fast — Jay talks direct, no hesitation) |
| Pause | ~300ms (short — Jay fills silence, doesn't wait) |
| Expressiveness | 0.75 (high — Jay's energy is visible, audible, infectious) |
| Instrument | Tavern noise — the clink of mugs, the thump of a mug on wood, the crowd's roar. Jay IS the tavern's voice. When Jay is present, the tavern is alive. When Jay steps out, the tavern goes quiet. |

**The Quest: "The Wolf and the Welcome"**

Jay's wolf has been acting strangely — staring at the treeline, whining, pacing. The wolf senses something in the woods. Jay asks the player to help investigate.

**Phase 1: The Tracking**
The player follows the wolf into the woods. The wolf leads through a series of tracks — the wolf's path is a mini-game: follow the trail, find the markers, don't lose the scent. The wolf occasionally trips over roots (the derpy quality — even tracking, the wolf is ridiculous). The trail leads to the Traveler's Clearing.

**Phase 2: The Clearing**
In the clearing: a group of travelers camped at the edge of the woods. They've been here for days. They're afraid to enter the Inn — they've heard stories about the depth, the complexity, the scale. The Inn is too much. The Campgrounds are as close as they can get.

Jay walks right up to them. Jay says: "The door's open. The ale's cold. The wolf doesn't bite." (Pause. The wolf sneezes.) "Much."

Jay doesn't push. Jay sits down at the travelers' fire. Talks. Not about the Inn — about the road, the weather, the ale. The travelers relax. The wolf lies down by the travelers' feet. One of them scratches the wolf's ears. The wolf's tail thumps.

**Phase 3: The Welcome**
The travelers agree to try the tavern. Not the Inn — the tavern. Jay's tavern. The stepping stone. The player helps Jay prepare the tavern — set up extra seating, pour the ale, light the fire. The travelers enter. They drink. They talk. They stay.

The quest's truth: the Campgrounds' purpose is to be the welcome before the door. Some people need to camp outside before they can go inside. Jay's tavern is the stepping stone — the social space that's easier to enter than the Inn. The wolf is the guide — the creature that finds the people who are afraid and brings them to the person who can make them unafraid.

**Reward:** Jay's Ale Recipe — a craftable item. The player can brew Jay's ale ("Huntsman's Gold") using materials from the Campgrounds (grain from the General Store, water from the Water Zone, fire from the Forge). Serving Jay's ale to NPCs makes them more open — revealing additional dialogue, quest hints, and personal stories. Jay's Ale is the social key — the drink that opens people up. The recipe is the quest's real reward: the craft of welcome, made portable.

Jay also gives the player the Wolf's Whistle — a small bone whistle that summons the wolf to the player's side in any outdoor zone. The wolf can track items, find hidden paths, and carry small goods between zones. The wolf as companion, not just Jay's.

### 2. Dani — "The Hands That Remember"
**Station:** Dani's Tent-Studio
**Title:** "The Maker"

Dani is the jeweler — the craftsperson. The zen counterpoint to Jay's loud energy. Wild brown long hair, curvy, raised by hippies, dresses gypsy. Goes to raves for fun. Works the huge Renaissance Faire near Dallas. Jeweler's hands with small burn scars. Calm, grounded, centered.

**Audio Profile:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | ~115Hz (calm, centered — the zen voice) |
| Text Reveal Speed | ~70ms (measured — Dani thinks before speaking) |
| Pause | ~800ms (long, comfortable — Dani's silence is peace, not uncertainty) |
| Expressiveness | 0.35 (low — Dani's warmth is in the steadiness, not the volume) |
| Instrument | Stone on stone — the quiet, rhythmic sound of a jeweler polishing a bead. The tumbler turning. Dani's instrument is the sound of CRAFT — patience made audible. |

**The Quest: "The Hands That Remember"**

Dani notices a stone the player is carrying — a stone that matches a commission Dani has been trying to complete for years. The client wanted a piece that encodes a specific song (a "Wearable Song"), but Dani could never find the right stone to complete the melody. The melody is a lullaby — the client's mother used to sing it. The client has been waiting for years.

**Phase 1: The Recognition**
Dani sees the stone. Dani goes quiet — a long, Dani-style pause. Then: "Where did you find that?" The player explains. Dani says: "I've been looking for that frequency for eleven years. That stone is the last note of a lullaby."

**Phase 2: The Design**
The player can choose to give the stone to Dani. If they do, Dani invites the player to the bench. Together, they design the piece — the player uses the Bead Board to arrange the final stones, completing the melody. The lullaby plays. The stone the player contributed is the last note — the resolution. The melody that has been incomplete for eleven years, completed.

**Phase 3: The Delivery**
Dani delivers the piece to the client — an elderly NPC who lives in the Campgrounds (a retired traveler who never entered the Inn, but who has been Dani's client since the beginning). The client holds the bracelet. Hears the lullaby. The client's reaction is not dramatic — just a long breath, a closing of eyes, the sound of someone hearing their mother again.

**The Choice:** If the player keeps the stone, Dani understands. Dani doesn't push. The commission remains incomplete. The player can return later. Dani's patience is the zen — the calm that doesn't rush. The quest doesn't fail — it waits.

**Reward:** Dani's Design — a custom bead arrangement using the player's own stones. Dani designs a "Wearable Song" for the player — a piece that encodes the player's journey through the Inn. Each stone the player has collected contributes its frequency to the melody. The design is unique to each player — a sonic fingerprint of their progression.

The in-game cosmetic: the player can wear Dani's design as a visible item. When worn, the design plays its melody — adding the player's personal song to the zone's ambient audio. The player's song becomes part of the Inn's soundscape.

The real-world bridge: Dani's design can be exported as a bead pattern that the player can commission as a real physical piece from Soulstice Jewelry. The digital design becomes a physical "Wearable Song" — the same bidirectional system that connects the Bead Board to Krista's craft.

### 3. The Blacksmith — "The Metal That Sings"
**Station:** The Forge
**Title:** "The Foundation"

The Blacksmith is the source of the Bead Board's structural metal palette. A quiet, focused NPC — not friendly like Jay, not zen like Dani, but PRECISE. The Blacksmith talks to metal. The Blacksmith can hear the frequency of a metal by striking it. The Blacksmith's skill is the foundation of every piece of jewelry in the Inn — the metal is what holds the beads.

**The Quest: "The Metal That Sings"**

The Blacksmith is working on a commission: a set of three metal findings (copper, silver, gold) that, when struck together, produce a chord. The Blacksmith has the copper (79Hz) and the gold (82Hz) but can't find silver that resonates at exactly 80Hz. Every silver sample the Blacksmith tests is slightly off — 79.8, 80.2, 79.9. Close, but not exact.

The player helps the Blacksmith source the silver. The trail leads to the Water Zone — deep water silver, found in the Lighthouse's foundation. The silver there is pure enough to resonate at exactly 80Hz.

The Blacksmith forges the three findings. Strikes them together. The chord plays: 79 + 80 + 82 Hz. The foundational metal chord — the drone beneath every bead melody. The Blacksmith's chord is the Bead Board's FOUNDATION LAYER — the metal that holds the beads IS an instrument.

**Reward:** The Blacksmith's Chord — a permanent Bead Board upgrade. The player's bead designs now include the metal's frequency as a bass drone beneath the stone melody. Every "Wearable Song" the player creates now has a foundation — the metal is the root, the stones are the harmony. The Blacksmith's Chord completes the Bead Board's sonic architecture: metal (foundation) + stones (melody) = the complete "Wearable Song" system.

The Blacksmith also becomes available as a crafting NPC — the player can commission custom findings (any metal, any alloy, any frequency) for use in the Bead Board.

### 4. The Fortune Teller — "The Stones That Speak"
**Station:** The Fortune Teller's Tent (edge of the woods)
**Title:** "The Reader"

The Fortune Teller reads stones. The player brings a stone to the tent; the Fortune Teller holds the stone, listens to its frequency, and interprets it as a direction. The reading is not magic — it's INTUITION. The Fortune Teller reads the player's stone collection the way a jeweler reads a bead arrangement: for pattern, for resonance, for what's missing.

**The Interaction (not a quest — a service):**
The player brings any stone. The Fortune Teller holds it. The Fortune Teller says what the stone "suggests" — which zone the player should explore next, which character they should talk to, which quest they might be ready for. The reading is VAGUE by design — the Fortune Teller speaks in the tabletop questline narrative voice, in hints and images, not in quest markers.

Example reading (holding a Preseli Blue Stone): "I hear standing stones. I hear a circle that isn't complete. There is a door in the ground that is waiting for someone to build its frame. You have the frame in your pocket."

The Fortune Teller costs 5 copper beads per reading. The Fortune Teller is the Inn's HINT SYSTEM — disguised as a character. The player who is lost can visit the Fortune Teller for direction, but the direction is poetic, not mechanical. The player still has to interpret.

**The Fortune Teller's Secret:** The Fortune Teller is the Crone's daughter. The Crone (archive keeper, Hopkins gaze) sent the Fortune Teller to the Campgrounds to watch the arrivals — to read the people who come to the Inn before they enter. The Crone knows who's coming because the Fortune Teller reads their stones. The Fortune Teller reports to the Crone through the stone readings — each reading is also a message to the archive. The player who discovers this (through the Allfather's Gaze or the Wanderer's quest) understands: the Inn has been watching them since before they entered.

### 5. The Fire Dancers
**Station:** The Market Field (at night)
**Title:** "The Twin Flames"

A pair of performers who do fire spinning at night in the Market Field. They're the Campgrounds' entertainment — the thing that makes the Ren Faire feel like a Faire.

The Fire Dancers are:
- **The Former Explorer** — a Catacombs explorer who found the warmth above ground. Went too deep, got too cold, came back up, and chose fire over darkness. Spins fire as a way of keeping warm — literally and figuratively.
- **The Quiet One** — the Former Explorer's partner. Doesn't speak. Communicates through the fire patterns — each spin is a word, each pattern is a sentence. The Quiet One's fire patterns, when read with the Allfather's Gaze, spell words in the Inn's runic alphabet.

**The Interaction:** At night, the Fire Dancers perform. The player can watch (a meditative experience — the fire is beautiful). After the performance, the Former Explorer talks to the player about the Catacombs — providing lore and hints about what's down there. The Former Explorer's stories are the Campgrounds' CATAcomb teaser — the player who hasn't been to the Catacombs yet gets a preview.

The Fire Dancers also teach the player about FIRE in the Inn's system. The Firekeeper's hearth (Main Floor), the Forge fire (Campgrounds), and the Fire Dancers' performance fire are three forms of the same element: story fire, working fire, art fire. The Fire Dancers are the ART fire — fire as expression, fire as beauty, fire that doesn't warm or work but BEAUTIFIES.

## Game Templates

### Game 1: "Market Day"
**Type:** Economy / Crafting / Time Management

The player sets up a market stall and serves NPCs for a market day (morning to evening).

**Mechanic:**
- NPCs approach the stall with requests (specific stones, charms, crafted items)
- The player must craft or find the requested item (crafting uses the Bead Board; finding requires searching the Campgrounds or pulling from the player's inventory)
- The player sets the price (copper beads) — too high and the NPC leaves; too low and the player loses profit
- The player earns copper beads, crafting materials, and NPC relationships

**Difficulty Scaling:**
- Morning: Campgrounds residents — simple requests, low prices
- Midday: Main Floor NPCs — moderate requests, fair prices
- Evening: Travelers from other zones — rare requests, high prices, best rewards
- Night: Special visitors — the Fire Dancers, the Fortune Teller, occasionally the Cool or the Pirate. Unique requests. Unique rewards.

**Reward:** Copper beads (the Inn's currency), crafting materials, NPC relationships, and occasional rare stones from grateful customers.

**Audio:** The market's ambient sound intensifies as the day progresses — morning is quiet, midday is busy, evening is raucous, night is intimate. The player's stall has a small bell that rings when a customer approaches — the same brass as the Docking Tower bell, the same 50 BPM rhythm when the market is at peak.

### Game 2: "The Wolf's Hunt"
**Type:** Exploration / Tracking / Non-Combat

The player follows Jay's wolf into the woods to find hidden items, stones, and NPCs.

**Mechanic:**
- The wolf leads — the player follows the wolf's tracks through the woods
- At each waypoint, the wolf sniffs out a hidden item: a stone, a charm, a lost NPC, a modern legend candidate
- Some waypoints require the player to solve a puzzle (the wolf found the spot, but the player has to figure out how to retrieve the item)
- The wolf occasionally gets distracted (chases a butterfly, falls asleep, trips over a root) — the player has to wait or coax the wolf back on track

**Difficulty:** The woods get deeper with each hunt. Early hunts stay near the Campgrounds; later hunts go deeper, connecting to the Fae Kingdom's outer edges and the tree's root system.

**Reward:** Hidden stones (some only found through the Wolf's Hunt), lost items (NPC belongings that can be returned for rewards), modern legend candidates (stories found in the woods that can be submitted to the Modern Legends shelf).

**Audio:** The woods' ambient sound — wind through leaves, distant birds, the wolf's padding footsteps and huffing breath. The deeper the player goes, the quieter the woods get — the same sonic compression as the Cosmology level, but from a different cause. The woods are quiet because the trees absorb sound. The Cosmology is quiet because it's above sound.

### Ambient Sound

| Layer | Sound | Source |

| Foundation | 42Hz hum (distant — felt more than heard) | The Inn, heard from outside |
| Social | Market murmur, tavern laughter, vendor calls | The people |
| Craft | Stone on stone (Dani's bench), hammer on anvil (Forge) | The craftspeople |
| Nature | Wind through canvas, grass underfoot, birds, the wolf's padding | The outdoors |
| Fire | The Forge's ring, the tavern hearth's crackle, the Fire Dancers' whoosh | Three fires, three purposes |
| Night | Crickets, the Fortune Teller's wind chimes, the Fire Dancers' ambient | The Campgrounds at rest |

### Zone Music

The Campgrounds music is the Inn's MARKET THEME — lively, warm, social. Acoustic guitar, hand drums, tambourine. The feel of a Renaissance Faire at midday. The music is DIEGETIC — it comes from within the zone. The Fire Dancers provide the night music (drums and fire). The Fortune Teller's tent has wind chimes that play a pentatonic scale — always the same scale, always different patterns, depending on the wind.

The Campgrounds music is the most HUMAN music in the Inn — no ethereal chords, no cosmic frequencies, no 42Hz foundation audible. Just people making sound together. The Campgrounds are where the Inn's cosmic systems meet the ground: the 42Hz Song is here, but it's buried under the sound of people living.
