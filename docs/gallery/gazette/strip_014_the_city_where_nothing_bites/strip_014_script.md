---

## PANEL 1 — The Setup

**Art:** The traveler enters through a wrought-iron gate into a New Orleans-style above-ground cemetery city. Rows of white plaster tombs with crumbling names, magnolia trees, late afternoon gold light. Weapon drawn, shield up, every potion slot filled. Charon leans against the gatepost, arms crossed, watching with the patience of someone who's seen this exact entrance a thousand times.

**Caption:** "The traveler came into the Cities of the Dead the way most of them do — armed for the Catacombs and confused by the sunshine."

---

## PANEL 2 — The Moment

**Art:** The traveler creeps between the family rows, weapon raised, spinning at every sound. A cat sits on a white tomb, watching with total disinterest. Lizards on warm stone. A magnolia leaf falls. The traveler flinches at the leaf.

**Caption:** "The Sanctuary law holds here. The only city in the Inn where nothing can hurt you. The traveler didn't know this. The cat did."

---

## PANEL 3 — The Twist

**Art:** A jazz funeral procession rounds the corner — brass band, parasols up, people dancing behind a casket. The traveler raises their sword. A woman in a yellow hat dances past them, beaming. The traveler stands frozen in combat stance while the second-line flows around them like water around a rock that hasn't figured out it's in a river.

**Caption:** "The Cities of the Dead play both directions at once. The dirge on the way in. The second-line on the way out. The traveler was the only one on the street who didn't know which face to make."

---

## PANEL 4 — The Button

**Art:** The traveler sits on the steps of Marie's tomb, weapon on the ground, sharing shade with the cat. In the background, oven tombs glint in sunset light. A workman changes a name plate on a vault — the year-and-a-day. Quiet, warm, golden hour.

**Caption:** "The vaults here change hands every year and a day. The dead are tenants, not inmates. The oven tombs are the slow kiln — clay's other miracle, the one that keeps the city breathing. The traveler put their sword away. The city didn't ask them to. That's the point of a sanctuary. It doesn't need to."

## Voice Check

- **Rothfuss precision:** "armed for the Catacombs and confused by the sunshine" — the compressed contrast, one phrase doing the work of a paragraph. "The dead are tenants, not inmates" — the exactness of the legal distinction that changes everything.
- **Pratchett warmth:** "The traveler didn't know this. The cat did." — the deadest, warmest deadpan. The woman in the yellow hat dancing past a raised sword — the human absurdity rendered without mockery, the traveler's dignity preserved even while they're the joke.
- **Gaiman mythic-made-personal:** "The dirge on the way in. The second-line on the way out." — the ritual made street-level, the myth walking in a parade. The year-and-a-day as a lease, not a sentence. The workman changing the name plate — eternity as paperwork.

---

## What the Strip Says About the Inn Today

The Cities of the Dead (ruled 2026-09-19) is the Inn's only true sanctuary — the one zone where the combat system goes quiet and the building becomes purely a place to walk through. The strip's joke is the subversion: the Catacombs are dangerous, everyone knows the Catacombs are dangerous, and the traveler arms accordingly. But the Cities of the Dead are not the Catacombs. They're the Catacombs' neighbor — the above-ground tomb city where the dead are housed, not hunted. The Sanctuary law is the Inn's theology in urban planning: some places exist to be safe, and safety doesn't need to announce itself. The sword on the ground is the button's image. Nobody asked for it. That's what makes it a sanctuary and not a truce.
