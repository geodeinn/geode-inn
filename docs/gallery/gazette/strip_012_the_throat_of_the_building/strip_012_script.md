# The Geode Inn Gazette — Strip #012: "The Throat of the Building"

**Date:** 2026-09-21 | **Zone:** The Staircase
**Characters:** The Watcher Gargoyle, A Traveler
**Art style:** Klimt-influenced dark fantasy, warm amber and gold tones
**Voice:** Tabletop storyteller — Rothfuss precision, Pratchett warmth, Gaiman mythic-made-personal
**Source canon:** The Gargoyle Seek (Arcade Roster #87 candidate, ruled 2026-09-19)

---

**Alt text:** The traveler learned that every terrifying face on the building was plumbing with a vocation, and that the things that seem to judge you are usually the ones taking care of you.

**Panel 1:** The traveler on the grand stone staircase, looking up at a carved gargoyle perched on a cornice. Fierce face, folded wings, open mouth. Gold-leaf and candlelight. CAPTION: "The traveler had passed the gargoyle seven times before they noticed it had moved."

**Panel 2:** The traveler stands where the gargoyle was. Empty cornice. Further down the curving staircase, a different gargoyle — same fierce expression — now watches them. CAPTION: "The Inn's gargoyles do not hide. They relocate. There is a difference, and the patient learn it."

**Panel 3:** Close-up of the gargoyle's fierce stone face at eye level on a landing. Its carved mouth is open. A single raindrop falls from it. The traveler beside it, surprised, then amused. The mouth is a downspout. CAPTION: "Gargouille. French. It means 'throat.' The scariest face on the building is a drainpipe with a vocation."

**Panel 4:** The gargoyle, stone and impassive, water trickling from its mouth into a rain chain. The traveler sits beside it on the landing, no longer afraid. Warm amber light. CAPTION: "The grotesque watches. The gargoyle drains. Both are load-bearing. Every face in the Inn that seems to judge you is also taking care of you, and neither job cancels the other."

---

**Storyteller's notes:** The Gargoyle Seek teaches the hide-to-greet law — found by patience, the seek chains watcher-to-watcher across every zone. The strip's joke is the honest label: the scariest faces on the world's cathedrals are drainage systems. The button connects to the Inn's whole theology — things that appear to judge are often the things that serve. The gargoyle doesn't perform intimidation; it does its job and happens to look terrifying while doing it. That's the Child-Actor Law in stone.
