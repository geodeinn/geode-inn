# Geode Inn Gazette — Strip #010
## "The Easel That Faces the Wall"
### Date: 2026-09-19
### Strip Number: 10
### Zone: The Spiral Gallery — Cave Studio
### Characters: Aurallia (The One Who Tends), The Catalyst
### Art Style: Hobbit-hole palette (amber, gold, cream) with Klimt gold-leaf spiral touches
### Player Adventures: None — NPC feature (Aurallia + The Catalyst)

## THE STRIP

### Panel 1 — The Setup
**Caption:** "The Spiral Gallery, five in the morning. The one who tends everything was tending the one thing she kept for herself."

Aurallia at the easel. The canvas faces slightly away from the viewer, so we glimpse the painting at an angle: a wolf taking shape in ochre and charcoal, the eyes already alive, the fur catching weather that hasn't been painted yet. Coffee cooling on a stone ledge. She works the way other people breathe. Worn sketchbook stacks, pencils stubbed to nothing.

### Panel 2 — The Moment
**Caption:** "The Catalyst came up the stairs for no reason anyone could have named. He stopped three steps from the top and forgot the fourth."

The Catalyst frozen mid-step on the Spiral Gallery staircase, one foot raised. Through the stairway opening he sees the cave studio — the wolf on the easel looking directly at him. Not decoration. Not craft. Something that could hang in a gallery that hasn't been built yet. His mouth is open from a sentence that won't come.

### Panel 3 — The Twist
**Caption:** "'It's just a doodle,' she said, and turned the easel to face the wall. Not hiding. The habit of thirty years. The way you stop saying a word you haven't said out loud in a decade."

Aurallia turns the easel. The wolf disappears. The Catalyst's mouth still open from the question he didn't get to finish. She picks up the coffee, casual, already past it. The canvas faces the wall like it always has. The sketchbooks in the stacks are patient. The most talented painter in the building treats it like a morning stretch.

### Panel 4 — The Button
**Caption:** "The Inn did not ask. The Inn built a gallery — the Modern Day, for artists who choose — and left the door unlocked. The easel faces the wall. The wolf faces the room. The Inn tends the one who tends everything, and the Inn is not in a hurry."

Split composition: on the left, the empty Modern Day Gallery — warm amber light, clean walls, a single empty picture hook. On the right, down the hall, the cave studio — the easel facing the wall, a new sketch beginning. The Inn waiting for the artist who doesn't know the gallery exists yet.

## VOICE LEDGER

- **Rothfuss (precision):** "The habit of thirty years. The way you stop saying a word you haven't said out loud in a decade." — the cold, exact mechanics of a lost habit stated plainly. Also: "She works the way other people breathe" — the economy of observation.
- **Pratchett (warmth):** "The Catalyst came up the stairs for no reason anyone could have named. He stopped three steps from the top and forgot the fourth." — the gentle human absurdity of being ambushed by beauty mid-step. "It's just a doodle" — the most devastating understatement, said like it's nothing, the way the truest things get said.
- **Gaiman (mythic-made-personal):** The empty gallery waiting for the artist who doesn't know it's there. The Inn tending the one who tends — the caretaker cared for, quietly, by the building itself. "The wolf faces the room" — the painted animal with its own patience, watching from behind a turned canvas.

## WHAT THE STRIP SAYS ABOUT THE INN TODAY

The Inn built a room for the artist before the artist knew she needed one. Aurallia's Working Studio was ruled into canon on Sep 18 — her easel, her decades-silent gallery-grade talent, the Bateman-register mornings. The Modern Day Gallery (the garnish layer for undiscovered living artists) went on the books the same day. The strip holds both in one breath: the easel that faces the wall, and the empty hook that's waiting. The Inn doesn't push. The Inn builds the room and leaves the door unlocked. The choice stays hers. That's the Living Story law, and it's the Inn's deepest kindness: tending the one who tends everything, without asking her to tend differently.
