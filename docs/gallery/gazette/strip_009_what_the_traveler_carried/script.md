## THE STRIP

### Panel 1 — The Setup
**Caption:** "The traveler came in the way most of them do — with nothing on their back and something in their mouth."

A dusty road-worn traveler stands in the open doorway of the Inn. The Bard sits by the stone fireplace, tuning a stringed instrument. The traveler carries no pack, no bags — nothing visible.

### Panel 2 — The Moment
**Caption:** "Nine verses. Tuam to Dublin to Liverpool to London. The whole leaving in a hum, carried the way other people carry letters they never sent."

The traveler hums — mouth slightly open, eyes distant. The Bard's hands freeze on the strings. A faint thread of golden light winds from the traveler toward the Archive shelf. The Bard has recognized something.

### Panel 3 — The Twist
**Caption:** "'Not for playing,' the traveler said. 'For keeping. I've carried it farther than I carried anything I own.'"

The Bard reaches for the melody. The traveler holds up a hand — gently, not harshly, the way you'd stop someone reaching for a cherished photograph. A deposition, not a gift. The Inn accepts it the way the Inn accepts everything: without asking why you kept it.

### Panel 4 — The Button
**Caption:** "The first road song entered the canon on a Tuesday. The song outlived every hand that played it. That is the point of a road song. You don't carry it. It carries you. And when you can't walk anymore, it walks on."

The Archive shelf. A small scroll labeled 1841. A quill beside it. The traveler gone, the door still open.

## VOICE LEDGER

- **Rothfuss (precision):** "Nine verses. Tuam to Dublin to Liverpool to London." — the melody mapped to its geography, stated flat. The mechanics of the diaspora arc as prose.
- **Pratchett (warmth):** "carried the way other people carry letters they never sent" — the human register of a mythic act. "For keeping. I've carried it farther than I carried anything I own." — the quietly devastating line delivered plainly.
- **Gaiman (mythic-made-personal):** the road song as diaspora archive, the Inn as keeper of what people carried when they couldn't carry much. "You don't carry it. It carries you." — folklore experienced as a single traveler humming in a doorway.

---

## WHAT THE STRIP SAYS ABOUT THE INN TODAY

Music is the luggage. The Inn's first EchoMelody — "The Rocky Road to Dublin" (D. K. Gavan, c. 1841) — entered the canon as a deposition, not a performance. The Bard understood the difference. The Archive keeps what people carried when they couldn't carry anything else. A song from 1841 walked through the door on a Thursday, and the Inn accepted it the way the Inn accepts everything.
