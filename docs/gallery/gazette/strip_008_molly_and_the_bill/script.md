### THE STRIP

**Panel 1 — The Setup**
The leprechaun called Molly leaned on the bar the way only a man who can be summoned by name can lean — already halfway to leaving, and not by choice.

**Panel 2 — The Moment**
From across the tavern, the one who grows things said his name warmly. "Molly, love — the kegs." A leash, said the way you'd say *come here*, and about as deniable.

**Panel 3 — The Twist**
He carried the kegs. He did not carry the coin. The coin carried itself — briefly, with the platypus, for one second — and was gone before anyone could say whether it was coming home or leaving again.

**Panel 4 — The Button**
"Evening, Bill," said the one who grows things, to the air where the platypus had been. Molly set down the kegs. The bill, like the Bill, like the coin, would settle itself. Briefly, and without comment.

### VOICE LEDGER
- **Rothfuss (precision):** the one-second law stated flat, the coin "carried itself" — mechanics as prose.
- **Pratchett (warmth):** the bickering pair's leash worn as affection; "about as deniable"; the triple pun in the title (the bar bill, the platypus Bill, the borrowed coin's reckoning).
- **Gaiman (mythic-made-personal):** the true-name summoning law, a piece of old folklore, experienced as a woman calling a six-foot fairy across a room for kegs.

### WHAT THE STRIP SAYS ABOUT THE INN TODAY
The Inn holds questions it never answers, and the people inside them keep each other company. The coin returns, or leaves — and the leash that could bind is used, instead, for kegs. The building's two honest criminals orbit a coin forever, and the one who grows things greets them both by name.
