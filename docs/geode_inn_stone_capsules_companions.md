# Geode Inn — Stone Capsules and Companions
## The Pokémon System, Reimagined as Geology

---

## The Core Mechanic

Two things Krista just connected:

1. **Stones are capsules.** Each stone the player collects is stored in inventory as a Pokémon-esque capsule — a small, hand-held stone that contains a frequency. The stone IS the capsule. The Inn's medium is stone. The capsule is not technology — it's geology.

2. **Harmonized enemies become companions.** When you win a resonance combat encounter (harmonize an enemy), the newly-harmonized creature joins your adventures as a fighting companion — stored in a stone capsule, deployed in future combats, carrying its own frequency and its own story.

Together: the player carries a pouch of stone capsules. Each capsule contains a frequency (the stone's natural vibration) and possibly a companion (a harmonized being stored inside). The player deploys frequencies as combat moves. The player deploys companions as combat allies. The stones are the Pokéballs. The harmonized enemies are the Pokémon. The geode cave (the baby dragon's reward home) is the PC box.

## The Stone Capsule

### What It Is

A stone capsule is a GEODE — a plain stone exterior, a beautiful interior. The Inn's core image is the geode: the ugly rock that opens to reveal crystals. The capsule is this image made into a game mechanic.

The player collects stones throughout the Inn — from arcade game rewards, from zone quests, from the Canopy City market, from the deep catacombs. Each stone has a frequency (the 164-stone database, each with Mind/Body properties and a vibration). The stone is stored in the player's inventory as a capsule.

The inventory is not a menu screen. The inventory is a POUCH — a physical, leather, heavy-thing-in-your-hand pouch of stones. The player feels the stones' vibrations through the pouch (haptic feedback — a subtle, constant hum, different for each stone). The player opens the pouch and the stones are visible — small, varied, each one a different color and texture. The stones vibrate at their frequencies. The pouch is a portable geode cave — the baby dragon's cave, carried on the belt.

### How It Works

**In combat (Pokémon-style resonance combat):** The player selects a stone from the pouch and deploys its frequency. The stone IS the projectile — the player throws or places the stone, and the stone vibrates, sending its frequency as a wave toward the enemy. The stone's frequency is the "move." Different stones have different frequencies, and different frequencies counter different dissonance types. The player's stone collection IS their move set.

- Ammolite (40Hz, tidal timing) counters tidal dissonance. The stone ripples when thrown — the wave radiates outward in concentric circles.
- Preseli Blue Stone (the Stonehenge stone, spotted dolomite, steady frequency) counters cardiac dissonance. The stone pulses when thrown — the wave travels in a straight, rhythmic beam.
- A bluestone from the Fae Kingdom counters suppressive dissonance. The stone shimmers when thrown — the wave travels as a curtain of vibration.

The stone is not consumed when thrown — it returns to the pouch after the frequency deploys. (The stone is the capsule, not the ammunition. The frequency is infinite — the stone vibrates as long as the stone exists.) The combat is about CHOOSING the right stone, not about running out of stones. The choice is the Pokémon "which move do I use" decision — strategic, not resource-based.

**Out of combat:** The stones in the pouch provide passive effects. Each stone's Mind/Body properties (from the 164-stone database) influence the player's state:
- A calming stone (e.g., amethyst, known for mental clarity) in the pouch slightly enhances the Nook Effect — the player recovers resonance faster in nooks.
- A protective stone (e.g., black tourmaline, known for shielding) in the pouch slightly reduces dissonance damage in combat.
- An attuning stone (e.g., clear quartz, known for amplification) in the pouch slightly enhances the Resonance Pulse — the "warmer/colder" signal is stronger.

The passive effects are subtle — not game-breaking, not min-max-able. The effects are the stones' Mind/Body properties made mechanical. The player who knows their stones (the Artisan Thief, the jeweler) knows which stones to carry for which situations. The player who doesn't know their stones carries what feels right and discovers the effects through experience.

## The Companion System

### What They Are

When the player harmonizes an enemy in resonance combat, the enemy's true form is revealed (the dissonance drops to zero, the distortion clears, the real creature emerges). The harmonized creature doesn't just join the Inn as an NPC — it joins the PLAYER as a COMPANION.

The companion is stored in a stone capsule — a geode. The geode forms around the harmonized creature at the moment of harmonization. The creature's frequency, now consonant, crystallizes into a stone — the creature's essence compressed into mineral form. The stone is the creature's capsule. The creature rests inside the stone, present but compact, the way a crystal rests inside a geode.

The companion stone is ADDED to the player's pouch. The pouch now contains a new capsule — a stone that holds a being. The being is the harmonized enemy. The being has:
- A frequency (the consonant version of the enemy's original dissonance — the frequency you harmonized it TO)
- A form (the true shape revealed when the distortion cleared)
- A story (why it was dissonant, what it was before the distortion, what its culture/origin/era is)
- A combat function (what it does when deployed)

### How Companions Work in Combat

The player can deploy a companion in combat — open the stone capsule, release the companion, and the companion fights alongside the player for the duration of the encounter.

The deployment is the geode opening — the stone splits along its natural fracture line, the interior glows, the companion emerges. The emergence is the Inn's core image: the ugly rock opening to reveal the beautiful thing inside. Every companion deployment is a geode opening. Every geode opening is the Inn's thesis: what's inside is more beautiful than what's outside.

In combat, the companion provides:
- **Frequency support:** The companion vibrates at its consonant frequency, adding to the player's deployed waves. If the player deploys a stone's frequency and the companion's frequency is consonant with it, the two frequencies AMPLIFY — the wave is stronger, the effect on the enemy is greater. (The Pokémon "same-type attack bonus" — if your companion's frequency matches your stone's frequency, the attack is more effective.)
- **Dissonance absorption:** The companion can absorb a dissonant burst from the enemy (the enemy's counter-attack), protecting the player from resonance loss. The companion takes the dissonance instead of the player, and the companion's own consonant frequency partially neutralizes the dissonance. (The Pokémon "tank" role — some companions are better at absorbing than others.)
- **Timing contribution:** The companion has its own timing — its own rhythm, learned from its origin culture/era/zone. The companion's timing can SYNC with the player's timing (making the player's deployments more effective) or COUNTER the enemy's timing (disrupting the enemy's dissonance pattern, making it easier to read). (The Pokémon "support" role — some companions enhance the player's abilities rather than directly attacking.)

After the combat, the companion returns to its stone capsule — the geode closes, the companion rests, the stone is back in the pouch. The companion is not "fainted" (Pokémon's knockout state) — the companion is RESTING. The companion needs time in the capsule to recover its resonance after a tough fight. The recovery happens in the nook — the Nook Effect restores companions as well as the player. The nook is the Pokémon Center. The nook is the Inn within the Inn.

### The Companion's Story

Each companion has a story — the story of why it was dissonant, what it was before the distortion, and what it becomes after harmonization. The story is the companion's CHARACTER — the thing that makes it more than a combat tool.

The companion's story unfolds over time. As the player adventures with the companion (deploys it in combat, carries it in the pouch, rests with it in nooks), the companion REVEALS more of itself — its memories, its origin, its connection to the zone it was found in. The revelations come through the Messenger Principle: the companion's frequency carries timing, and the timing carries the companion's story. The player who listens to the companion (in the nook, in the quiet moments) hears the story. The player who only deploys the companion in combat gets the combat function but not the story.

This is the Pokémon relationship mechanic — the friendship/affection system — reimagined as LISTENING. In Pokémon, you raise friendship by spending time with your Pokémon, feeding it, grooming it. In the Geode Inn, you raise the relationship by LISTENING to the companion's frequency in the nook. The listening IS the friendship. The Thrum's teaching applies: hear the timing, and the timing tells you who the companion is.

### Story Companions vs. Wild Companions

Two types of companions:

**Story Companions:** Earned by completing story quests — the main storyline, the zone quests, the character quests. These companions are NAMED characters with full backstories, connected to the Inn's narrative. When you complete a character's questline (e.g., the Pirate's "The After" quest, the Siren's "The Mirror and the Shore" quest, Kali's "The Garland" quest), the character (or a being connected to the character) joins you as a companion. Story companions are the most powerful and the most narratively rich — they carry the weight of the quest you completed to earn them.

Example story companions:
- After completing the Pirate's quest ("The After" — charting the uncharted zones), the Pirate's drum rhythm joins you as a companion. The drum is a stone capsule that contains the Pirate's beat — the bodhrán rhythm, the "good hands" that held a sword and now hold the rhythm. Deployed in combat, the drum provides TIMING support — the companion's rhythm synchronizes the player's deployments, making every frequency more effective. The drum's story: the rhythm that was a mantra ("Hello, my name is Inigo Montoya...") became a beat, and the beat became a companion, and the companion is the Pirate's gift to the player who helped him find the AFTER.
- After completing Kali's quest ("The Garland" — facing false selves, stopping the destruction), a severed head from Kali's garland joins you as a companion. The head is a stone capsule that contains a false self — a version of the player that Kali cut away. Deployed in combat, the false head provides DISSONANCE support — it vibrates at the frequency of a false self, and when deployed against an enemy, the false frequency RESONATES with the enemy's dissonance (because the enemy's dissonance is also a kind of falseness), creating a sympathetic vibration that makes the enemy's true frequency easier to identify. The false head's story: it was a version of you that wasn't real. Kali cut it away. Now it serves the real you. The false becoming useful — the Inn's alchemy in miniature.

**Wild Companions:** Earned by harmonizing enemies in random encounters — the Unseelie creatures, the water monsters, the deep-catacomb echoes. These companions are unnamed, procedurally generated from the zone's frequency palette. They have simpler stories (their origin is the zone, their dissonance is the zone's dissonance type). They are less powerful than story companions but more numerous — the player collects many wild companions as they explore. The wild companions are the Pokémon — the creatures you catch, train, and deploy. The story companions are the LEGENDARY Pokémon — the unique, named, quest-rewarded beings that define your playthrough.

### The Geode Cave — The PC Box

The player's pouch has limited space — the player can carry a set number of stone capsules at once (the Pokémon party limit — six, or whatever number feels right for the Inn). The companions not in the active pouch are stored in the **geode cave** — the baby dragon's reward home, the cave that fills with stones.

The geode cave is the PC box — the storage system for unused companions. The cave is physically present in the Inn (the main floor, near the baby dragon's home). The player visits the cave to deposit and withdraw companions. The cave's walls are lined with geodes — each geode a stored companion, each geode glowing with the companion's consonant frequency. The cave that started empty (a new player's cave) becomes full (an experienced player's cave) — the formation of the geode, the reward cave filling with gems, the original design made into a storage system.

The baby dragon tends the cave. The dragon is the PC box operator — the being who watches over the stored companions, who keeps the geodes warm, who lets the player know when a stored companion's frequency has changed (a companion that has been resting in the cave has been recovering, and its frequency is stronger when withdrawn). The dragon doesn't explain the system. The dragon just lives there, among the geodes, and the player learns the system by watching the dragon tend the stones.

The geode cave is also the VISUAL representation of the player's macro-progression. A player with a full geode cave has harmonized many enemies, collected many stones, completed many quests. The cave's fullness IS the XP bar — the visual proof that the player has done the work. The cave that is nearly empty is a new player. The cave that is nearly full is an experienced player. The cave that is overflowing — geodes on the walls, on the floor, on the ceiling, the baby dragon nestled among them — is a player approaching the endgame.

## The Companion and the Storyline

### The Unbreakable Bond

The storyline establishes: "An unbreakable bond between two existences — sometimes chosen, sometimes not a choice." The companion system is this bond made mechanical.

The bond between the player and their companions is unbreakable — once harmonized, a companion does not leave. The companion is not a temporary ally. The companion is a permanent addition to the player's geode cave. The bond was formed through harmonization — the player GAVE the enemy the right frequency, and the enemy's true form was revealed, and the revealed form chose to stay. The companion stays because the companion CHOSE to — the harmonization was not capture, it was RECOGNITION. The companion was always this creature. The player recognized it. The recognition is the bond.

Some bonds are chosen (the player chose to harmonize, chose the right frequency, chose to include). Some bonds are not a choice (story companions — the being joins you because the quest's narrative demands it, because the bond was always there, because the storyline's "unbreakable bond between two existences" is the companion system's deepest layer).

### The Reincarnation Connection

The storyline's reincarnation mechanic — reliving lives, accountable for all ripples — connects to the companion system through the geode cave. The companion stones persist across reincarnation cycles. When the player reincarnates (the Inn's respawn/restart mechanic), the geode cave's contents carry forward. The companions from previous lives are still there — still in their geodes, still vibrating, still bonded. The reincarnation is not a reset — it's a CONTINUATION. The companions are the thread that connects one life to the next.

The false-head companion (from Kali's quest) is especially resonant across reincarnation — each life produces false selves, and Kali cuts them away, and the false selves join the geode cave as companions. Over multiple lives, the geode cave accumulates a collection of false selves — a gallery of who the player WAS, each one a companion that serves the real player. The cave becomes a record of the player's growth across lives. The geode cave is the reincarnation journal — not written, but MINERAL. The companions are the entries.

### The Owl and the Companions

The Owl — the narrative spine's watcher, the bloodline representative, the lighthouse keeper — watches the geode cave. The Owl doesn't tend the cave (the dragon does that). The Owl WATCHES — because watching is the Owl's function, and the companions are the proof that the player has been doing the work the storyline demands. The Owl's presence near the geode cave is the narrative's way of saying: we see what you've gathered. We see who you've harmonized. We see the bonds. The bloodline knows.

## The Inventory Entity

The existing PlayerInventory entity (dateCollected, isPurchased, itemId, itemName, notes, playerId, playerName, purchaseDate) can track both stones and companions:

- **Stones:** itemId = the stone's identifier, itemName = the stone's name, notes = the stone's frequency/properties. These are the frequency capsules — the "moves."
- **Companions:** itemId = the companion's identifier, itemName = the companion's name, notes = the companion's frequency, story, and combat function. These are the beings — the stored allies. The isPurchased field distinguishes purchased stones (from the market) from earned stones (from quests and combat).

The geode cave is the visual interface for the PlayerInventory — the cave displays the stored items as geodes, each one a capsule containing a frequency or a companion. The cave is the inventory made beautiful. The inventory is the cave made functional.

## Summary

|---|---|
| Pokéball | Stone capsule (a geode — plain exterior, beautiful interior) |
| Pokémon | Harmonized enemy (a being whose true form was revealed by the right frequency) |
| Catching a Pokémon | Harmonizing an enemy (dissonance reaches zero, true form revealed, being chooses to join) |

| Friendship/affection | Listening (hearing the companion's timing in the nook — the relationship grows through attention) |
| Legendary Pokémon | Story companions (named, quest-earned, narratively rich, unique) |
| Wild Pokémon | Wild companions (procedurally generated from zone frequencies, simpler stories) |
| Fainted | Resting (the companion needs nook-time to recover — not knocked out, just tired) |
| Trainer | The player — but the player is a TUNER, not a trainer. You don't train companions. You LISTEN to them. |

The Geode Inn's companion system is Pokémon reimagined through geology: the balls are stones, the monsters are harmonized beings, the storage is a cave, the healing is a nook, and the relationship is built by listening. The system preserves everything that makes Pokémon satisfying (collection, team-building, type-matchups, progression) while serving the Inn's thesis: the world is not full of enemies to be defeated. The world is full of beings to be RECOGNIZED.
