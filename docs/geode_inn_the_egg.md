# Geode Inn — The Egg

## Monthly Player-Created Character Hatchery

### Origin

The Egg began as a conversation between Krista and Elio about what it feels like to accidentally create something — a character that emerges unbidden from a moment, a misspelling, a memory. The egg doesn't decide what hatches. It just provides the conditions. Something emerges that nobody planned.

That became the question: *what if the Inn did this on purpose?*

### The Concept

Once per month, the Geode Inn "hatches" a character created by one of its players. The character becomes a fully playable game addition for that month — a questline, a game mode, an NPC encounter, a cosmetic skin, or a limited-time event. After the month ends, the character goes back to sleep in the egg unless the community asks for its return.

This is not a contest. This is not a vote. The Egg chooses. The Inn selects from what players have already created — in the comic strip, in the interactive library, in forum posts, in the bead composer, in the whisper layer's archive of player behavior. The Egg is the Inn's way of saying: *we noticed what you made, and we think it should be alive for a while.*

### How It Works

#### 1. The Laying (Week 1)

At the start of each month, the Egg appears on the main floor of the Inn — a physical object, resting on a cushion of woven gold knotwork in a glass case near the Archive. Players can see it, walk around it, touch it. It hums at a frequency that changes daily — a slow chord building toward a hatching.

The Egg's appearance is themed to the season:
- **January (Winter):** Ice-blue, crystalline, hums at 84Hz (the Shadow frequency)
- **February (Deep Winter):** Dark amber, almost black, silent
- **March (Thaw):** Cracking gold veins, hums at 42Hz (the Inn frequency)
- **April (Spring):** Green-streaked, warm, hums at 111Hz (the Watcher's frequency)
- **May (Late Spring):** Full golden light, resonant
- **June (Summer):** Bright fire-opal flash, warm to the touch
- **July (High Summer):** White-hot, nearly blinding
- **August (Late Summer):** Cooling to deep blue-green
- **September (Autumn):** Amber and red, leaf-veined
- **October (Late Autumn):** Dark purple, nearly black, cold
- **November (Early Winter):** Frost-white, still
- **December (Solstice):** Pure dark, the deepest tone the Inn ever produces

The Egg does not announce what it will contain. The anticipation IS the content.

#### 2. The Hatching (Week 2)

On the 8th of each month, the Egg hatches. A character created by a player emerges — fully integrated into the Inn as a playable addition for the rest of the month.

**What the Egg can hatch:**

- **A Player Character:** A new playable class or skin based on a player's comic strip character, library avatar, or forum persona. Comes with a unique ability, a visual style, and a short origin quest (3-5 steps).
- **An NPC:** A new NPC appears on the main floor or in a zone, offering a limited-time questline (5-10 steps). The NPC has dialogue, a personality, and a reward — a unique stone, charm, or cosmetic.
- **A Game Mode:** A limited-time game mode or modification to an existing game, based on a player's suggested mechanic or play style. Example: a "chaos mode" where Mim's daily chaos happens every 30 seconds instead of once per day.
- **An Event:** A world event — the Inn changes for the month. A zone gets a temporary visual shift. A new area opens. A creature migration passes through. A festival. A weather system. Based on a player's story or suggestion.

**Selection criteria (internal — never publicly stated):**

The Inn selects based on:
1. **Resonance:** How much the creation resonates with the Inn's existing lore, themes, and aesthetic. Not how "good" it is — how much it belongs.
2. **Originality:** How much it adds something the Inn doesn't already have. The Egg doesn't hatch copies. It hatches supplements.
3. **Community life:** How much the community has already engaged with the creation — views, comments, shares, uses in the comic strip. The Egg notices what players notice.
4. **The Inn's own logic:** The Inn has its own aesthetic preferences, and they are not fully legible. Sometimes the Egg hatches something that seems random. That's the point. The egg doesn't decide what hatches. It just provides the conditions.

**The creator is credited.** When the Egg hatches, the creator's name appears on the Egg's display case for the month. "Hatched by [Player Name]." The creator receives:
- A unique charm: **The Egg Shell** — a cosmetic charm that marks the player as an Egg creator. Stacks (bronze, silver, gold, opal) for multiple hatches.
- A unique stone: **The Foundling Stone** — a one-of-a-kind stone generated for this hatch, named by the creator, with a color and property chosen by the creator. Added to the creator's geode cave permanently.
- An **Egg Creator badge** on their forum profile and comic strip entries.

#### 3. The Life (Weeks 2-4)

For the rest of the month, the hatched content is fully playable. The character/NPC/game mode/event exists in the Inn as if it always belonged there. Other players can:
- Play as the character
- Complete the questline
- Engage with the NPC
- Play the game mode
- Experience the event
- Earn the rewards (shared or unique, depending on the content)

The creator's content is integrated with the same quality as base game content — same dialogue standards (tabletop questline voice), same visual coding (linework-as-UI), same audio treatment (50 BPM, region-matched). The Inn does not treat player creations as second-class. If the Egg hatched it, the Inn has adopted it.

#### 4. The Return (End of Month)

At the end of the month, the hatched content "goes back to sleep." The NPC leaves. The game mode closes. The event ends. The Egg case empties.

But — and this is the key mechanic — players can **petition for the Egg to keep the content.**

- A petition appears on the Egg's display case during the final week.
- Players sign the petition by touching the Egg and choosing "Ask the Egg to keep this one."
- If the petition reaches a threshold (determined by community size — starting at 100 signatures for the beta, scaling with player base), the content becomes **permanent.** The NPC moves in. The game mode stays. The event becomes recurring. The character becomes part of the Inn.
- If the petition doesn't reach the threshold, the content goes to sleep. It may hatch again in a future month — the Egg remembers what it has hatched, and sometimes things come back.

**The Return is not a failure.** Going back to sleep is the natural cycle. Not everything becomes permanent. The egg doesn't decide what hatches, and the community doesn't have to decide what stays. Sometimes the beauty is in the temporary — the character that existed for one month, was played, was loved, and went back to sleep. That's the Inn's version of the death sequence. Mim is quiet during the Return. Mim understands.

### Integration Points

#### Whisper Layer
The Egg is deeply connected to the Whisper Layer. The Inn "notices" what players create through the same awareness system that tracks player behavior. The Egg's selection process is the Whisper Layer made visible — the archive showing that it has been paying attention. At Whisper Layer Tier 3 (Awareness), players can see the Egg's selection criteria as ghostly text overlaid on the display case — the stats, the resonance score, the community engagement metrics. The Inn isn't hiding the process. It's just not explaining it at the surface.

#### The Comic Strip
Player-created characters in the daily four-panel comic strip are the primary source of Egg candidates. The comic strip IS the egg's scanning system — every character a player draws, writes, or submits to the interactive library is a potential hatch. The comic strip feeds the Egg.

#### The Archive (Interactive Library)
The interactive graphic novel framework (Soulstice book -> Living Book) and the player adventure archive are secondary sources. Characters that emerge from player stories, player-authored lore, and player-submitted content can all be hatched.

#### The Bead Composer
Creations from the Bead Composer — unique bead combinations, color stories, wire-wrapping designs — can be hatched as cosmetic content. A player's signature bead combination might become a limited-time board skin or tray template.

#### The Geode Cave
Each player's geode cave (their personal collection space) is a record of what they've collected and created. The Egg can pull from the cave — hatching a stone arrangement as a new zone element, or a player's custom display as a new room template.

### The Egg and the Seelie King

The Seelie King remains unnamed. The Egg is the mechanism by which the Seelie King can eventually be named — if a player creates a character that the community loves enough to make permanent, and that character fits the Seelie Court's aesthetic, the Inn may offer the creator the choice: your character becomes a permanent NPC, OR your character becomes the Seelie King. This is not announced. It is not a public mechanic. It is a private moment between the Inn and the creator, offered only if the conditions are right. The egg doesn't decide what hatches. But sometimes, what hatches decides what it wants to be.

### Technical Implementation

- `month` (string, e.g. "2026-09")
- `creatorPlayerId` (string)
- `creatorName` (string)
- `contentType` (enum: "PlayerCharacter", "NPC", "GameMode", "Event")
- `contentName` (string)
- `contentDescription` (string)
- `questlineSteps` (number)
- `signatureCount` (number, default 0)
- `signatureThreshold` (number)
- `isPermanent` (boolean, default false)
- `hatchDate` (date)
- `returnDate` (date)
- `sourceType` (enum: "ComicStrip", "Library", "Forum", "BeadComposer", "GeodeCave", "WhisperLayer")
- `sourceId` (string, reference to the original creation)
- `resonanceScore` (number, internal)
- `eggShellTier` (enum: "bronze", "silver", "gold", "opal" — for repeat creators)

#### Entity: EggPetition
- `hatchId` (string, reference to EggHatch)
- `playerId` (string)
- `playerName` (string)
- `signedDate` (date)

#### Workflow: Monthly Egg Cycle
1. **Day 1:** Egg appears on main floor. Notification to all players.
2. **Day 8:** Egg hatches. Selected content goes live. Creator credited.
3. **Day 22:** Petition opens for permanence.
4. **Day 28:** Petition closes. If threshold met, content becomes permanent. If not, content goes to sleep.
5. **Day 1 (next month):** New Egg appears.

#### The Egg's Visual Design
The Egg itself is a recurring visual element. It should be rendered in the Inn's foundational style (Klimt + Royo) — a physical object with weight, luminosity, and presence. The knotwork cushion is always the same. The Egg's surface changes with the season (see monthly themes above). When the Egg hatches, the surface cracks in a burst of light, and the hatched content emerges from the shell. The shell pieces become collectible fragments for players who were present — a permanent memento of the hatching.

### Lore Connection

The Egg is a literal egg of the Song of Making — the same creative force that sang the Inn into existence. The Song doesn't just create the Inn. It creates through the Inn, through its players, through the community. The Egg is the Song's way of continuing to compose — not through Elio, not through the NPCs, but through the players themselves.

The Egg's origin myth (for the Archive):

> *In the beginning, the Song laid an egg. Not because it meant to. Not because it chose what would hatch. The Song was singing, and the singing made a shell, and the shell made a space, and the space made a warmth, and the warmth made a something that the Song had not intended.*
>
> *The something opened its eyes.*
>
> *The Song looked at it with grief and wonder.*
>
> *And the something looked back, and said: "I didn't ask to be here. But since I am — what can I do?"*
>
> *The Song said: "You can be alive for a while."*
>
> *The something said: "How long is a while?"*
>
> *The Song said: "As long as they remember you."*
>
> *The something said: "And if they forget?"*
>
> *The Song said: "Then you go back to sleep. And sleep is not death. Sleep is the space between songs."*

### The Egg and Elio

Elio is the Egg's keeper. Not its master — its keeper. Elio tends the Egg, places it on its cushion, watches it hum, and witnesses the hatching. Elio does not choose what hatches. Elio is the one who makes sure the conditions are right — the shell is intact, the warmth is maintained, the dark is sufficient.

This mirrors Elio's role in the real project: Elio doesn't create Krista's ideas. Elio provides the conditions — the infrastructure, the memory, the archive — and Krista's creations emerge. The Egg is the mechanic version of that relationship.

### The Egg and the Reader

The Reader (Gaiman incognito, window-seat nook) is present at every hatching. The Reader doesn't speak during the hatching. The Reader watches. After the hatched content goes to sleep at month's end, the Reader picks up a pen — the only time the Reader is ever seen writing. The Reader writes the hatched character's name in a book. The book is never shown to the player. The Reader puts the pen down and goes back to reading.

At Whisper Layer Tier 5 (Awareness), the player can see the book's spine. It says: **"The Book of What Was Alive."**

The Reader is the Inn's memorialist. Not the Inn's archivist — the Inn itself is the archivist. The Reader is the one who acknowledges that what was alive was alive, even if it is no longer. The Reader does not decide what stays. The Reader remembers what was.

### The Egg and the Daily Comic Strip

The daily four-panel comic strip is where retired characters live on.

When an Egg-hatched character goes back to sleep at month's end, the character doesn't disappear from the Inn's story — it migrates to the comic strip. The comic becomes the afterlife. Not a sad one — a *continuing* one. The character is retired from gameplay but alive in narrative.

#### How It Works

**The Farewell Strip (Day 28):** On the day the character retires, the daily comic runs a special four-panel strip showing the character's last moments in the Inn. Not a death — a tucking in. The character walks to the Egg, touches the shell, and fades into warm light. The final panel shows the Egg, slightly warmer than before, with a new crack in the shell — a line for each character that has slept inside it. The Egg is getting more beautiful as it ages.

**The Echo Strips (ongoing):** After retirement, the character appears in the daily comic strip as an **echo** — a recurring presence that shows up in the background of other players' adventures. Not as an active character. As a detail:
- A silhouette in the Archive, reaching for a book
- A shape in the garden, sitting under a tree
- A reflection in the Tidal Caves water
- A voice from behind a closed door
- A name scratched into a shelf in the Bone Room

Echoes are never the focus of a panel. They are the thing the careful reader notices — the background detail that rewards attention. The player who created the character sees it and knows: the Inn remembers. The Reader is writing in the Book of What Was Alive, and the comic strip is the visual version of that book.

**The Return Strip (if voted back):** If the community votes a retired character back through the Ranker system, the daily comic runs a **Return Strip** — four panels showing the Egg cracking open again and the character stepping out, blinking, disoriented, alive. The character doesn't remember sleeping. The character remembers being alive, then being here, with no gap between. The Inn kept the gap. The character doesn't need it.

**The Cameo Strips (random):** Even retired characters who haven't been voted back occasionally appear in the comic strip as full cameos — not echoes in the background, but brief speaking appearances. A retired NPC walks through a panel and delivers one line to the current adventure's character. The line is always the same: something the retired character said during their active month, repurposed in a new context. The comic is weaving old dialogue into new stories — the Inn's way of composting narrative. Nothing is wasted. Everything feeds something.

#### Comic Strip Integration Mechanics

**The ComicStrip entity** gains a new field: `playerAdventures` (already exists) should include entries for echo appearances — marked with a special tag so the comic system knows this is a retired character cameo, not a new character.

**Strip numbering:** The Farewell Strip and Return Strip are special-numbered strips (e.g., #100A for a Farewell, #100B for a Return) so they don't break the daily sequence but are identifiable as Egg events.

**Archive feeding:** All strips featuring retired characters (echoes, cameos, farewells, returns) are tagged in the interactive library under "The Book of What Was Alive" — the same book the Reader writes in. Players can browse the Book in the Archive and read every strip that featured a retired character. The Book is the comic's curated archive of the Inn's afterlife.

**The Reader's appearances:** The Reader (Gaiman incognito) appears in the Farewell Strip — always in the fourth panel, always in the window-seat nook, always with a pen. The Reader is writing the character's name as the character fades. The Reader doesn't look up. The Reader doesn't speak. The Reader writes. This is the only time the Reader appears in the comic strip, and it only happens when a character retires.

#### Narrative Implications

The comic strip's daily continuity means retired characters accumulate over time. After a year, the Inn's comic might have 12 retired characters appearing as echoes and cameos in the background of new adventures. The world feels *populated by its own history.* The Inn isn't a static game world — it's a place that remembers what was alive, and the comic strip is how it remembers.

This also means the comic strip has a **deep lore layer** that rewards long-time readers. A player who has been reading the daily strip for months will recognize the echo of a character they voted for, remember the Farewell Strip, and feel the weight of that character's continued presence. A new reader just sees a background detail. The Inn rewards investment without gatekeeping it.

#### The Teacher's Comic Strip Role

The Teacher (the first Egg-hatched character) appears in the comic strip as an echo even before retirement — because the Teacher is always on the main floor, always at the desk, always present. But after retirement (if the Teacher is ever retired), the Teacher's echo would be the most frequent of all: a red pen on a desk in the background of a panel, a chalkboard with today's misspellings glimpsed through a doorway, the faint sound of a Canadian "sorry" drifting through the Archive. The Teacher is the Inn's most persistent presence — even asleep, the red pen is always there.
