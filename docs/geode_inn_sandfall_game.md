# Geode Inn — Sandfall: The O'Keeffe Sinking Sand Game
## Egypt Zone — Desert Bone Arcade Game
## Mario Bros 2 Sand Mechanic × Georgia O'Keeffe Visual Language

---

## The Concept

Sandfall is a sinking sand platformer set in the Desert Bone zone. The player descends through layers of Egyptian history — each layer deeper, each layer more O'Keeffe. The sand is the hazard and the pathway. You sink to progress. You dig to survive. The deeper you go, the more the world strips away its detail, revealing the essential forms beneath — O'Keeffe's thesis made playable.

The game is the Desert Bone quest chain's mechanic distilled into an arcade format. The quest chain teaches Form Sight (seeing the form beneath the detail). Sandfall makes you LIVE it — you physically descend through layers of detail, and each layer is simpler, starker, more honest than the one above.

---

## Core Mechanic: Sinking Sand

Inspired by Mario Bros 2's sinking sand levels, but deeper (pun intended):

### The Sand
- The player sinks into sand at a steady rate when standing on it — slow at first, accelerating the longer you stand still
- Moving/digging slows the sinking — but doesn't stop it entirely on soft sand
- Hard sand (packed, ancient) doesn't sink — it's a platform
- Soft sand (loose, wind-blown) sinks fast — it's a hazard
- Living sand (resonance-active sand, humming at 42Hz) sinks UP — it rises instead of falls, carrying the player

### The Controls
- **Left/Right (or A/D):** Walk — slows sinking on soft sand, normal speed on hard sand
- **Down (or S):** Dig — actively sink faster, burrowing through sand to reach lower layers
- **Spacebar/Tap:** Jump — leap out of sand if not too deep (if you've sunk past chest height, you can't jump — you must dig sideways to reach hard sand or an edge)
- **Up (or W):** Pull — grab the edge of a hard sand platform and pull yourself up (slow, vulnerable)
- **Form Sight Toggle (F or button):** Switch to O'Keeffe vision — see the essential forms of each layer (this is the zone's earned ability, available here as a mechanic)

### The Sinking States
- **Ankle-deep:** Normal movement, slow sink. No danger.
- **Knee-deep:** Slower movement, faster sink. Warning — find solid ground soon.
- **Waist-deep:** Very slow movement, fast sink. Can still jump out if near an edge.
- **Chest-deep:** Can't jump. Can only dig down (to fall through to the layer below) or dig sideways (to try to reach an edge). This is the danger zone.
- **Neck-deep:** Only head and eyes above sand. Can't move. Can only dig straight down — a desperate plunge to the layer below. If there's no layer below, you're buried.
- **Buried:** Game over. The sand claim. The screen goes to O'Keeffe — the world simplifies to forms and colors as the view sinks below the surface.

### The Dig
When the player presses Down while in sand, they actively dig — burrowing through the sand. This is faster than passive sinking, and it's directional — the player can dig down, left, or right through sand. Digging creates tunnels that collapse after a few seconds (the sand refills). The player must move through the tunnel before it collapses.

This is the core tension: dig down to reach the next layer faster, but risk tunneling into a pocket with no solid ground. Dig sideways to find a platform, but risk the tunnel collapsing before you reach it.

### The Layers
The game is structured as a vertical descent through layers of Egyptian history. Each layer is a "floor" of the Desert Bone — a stratum of time. The player descends through:

1. **The Surface** (O'Keeffe's desert floor)
2. **The Sand Sea** (wind-sculpted dunes, the first test)
3. **The Bone Field** (O'Keeffe's bones — the layer where Form Sight starts mattering)
4. **The Tomb Colonnade** (simplified architecture — cylinders, triangles, the Sphinx's lesson)
5. **The Lost Library** (the Examiner's fragments — form-matching puzzle layer)
6. **The Mummy's Chamber** (the essential form — the deepest layer, the O'Keeffe culmination)
7. **The Bone** (the secret layer — only accessible with full Form Sight, the form beneath ALL forms)

## The Levels (Each Matching O'Keeffe's Style)

### Level 1: The Surface — "The Shape of Patience"
**O'Keeffe Reference:** O'Keeffe's desert floor paintings — stark, minimal, warm. "Sky Above Clouds" meets the Egyptian desert.

**Visual:** A vast, flat desert under a gradient sky (O'Keeffe's skies: bands of color — ochre to coral to dusty rose). The horizon is a single line. The sand is warm amber with a grain texture. In the distance: the Sphinx's head, half-buried — an O'Keeffe-simplified shape (a triangle emerging from a flat plane). The Vulture circles overhead (a tiny moving shape against the gradient sky).

**Gameplay:** Tutorial. The sand is soft — the player sinks slowly. Hard sand patches (packed, lighter colored) provide safe standing. The player learns: move to slow sinking, find hard sand to rest, dig down to progress. Bones half-buried in the sand are platforms — O'Keeffe's bone paintings as level geometry. The bones are white, simplified, smooth — femurs as ramps, skulls as platforms, ribcages as archways.

**O'Keeffe detail:** The shadows are NOT grey. They're lavender. Even on Level 1, the shadow color is the zone's signature. The player who notices this has started seeing the way O'Keeffe saw.

**Objective:** Sink to the bottom of the surface layer (dig down through soft sand) to reach Level 2.

**Reward stone:** Carnelian (warm orange — the desert's heart stone)

---

### Level 2: The Sand Sea — "The Gradient"
**O'Keeffe Reference:** O'Keeffe's paintings of White Place / Ghost Ranch — flowing, sculpted sand forms, monumental but simplified.

**Visual:** Dunes as O'Keeffe forms — sweeping curves, no detail, just form and shadow. The sand is deeper here — the player sinks faster. The dunes are enormous, rendered as simple curves with colored shadows (lavender on the north faces, coral on the south, dusty rose in the valleys). The sky is closer — the gradient presses down. No horizon line anymore; the dunes ARE the landscape.

**Gameplay:** The sand sea is all soft sand. There is no hard sand. The player MUST keep moving — standing still means sinking. Bones are the only solid platforms — scattered through the dunes at various depths. The player must navigate from bone to bone, sinking and pulling themselves up, timing their movement to reach the next platform before sinking too deep.

Living sand patches appear here — patches of sand that hum (42Hz, barely audible) and rise instead of sink. The player can ride them up to reach high bones or skip sections. But living sand is unpredictable — it rises and falls in waves, and if it falls while you're on it, you drop.

**O'Keeffe detail:** In Form Sight, the dunes simplify to pure curves — sine waves. The bones simplify to lines and points. The level's geometry becomes a mathematical drawing. The player who uses Form Sight sees the underlying structure — the dunes ARE waves, the bones ARE lines. The form IS the level.

**Objective:** Navigate the dunes to find the dig point — a dark patch of sand that leads to Level 3.

**Reward stone:** Desert Rose Selenite (the rose that blooms in bone)

---

### Level 3: The Bone Field — "The Form Beneath the Form"
**O'Keeffe Reference:** O'Keeffe's bone paintings — "Bone Black, Blue, and White," "Horse's Skull with Pink Rose," "Cow's Skull with Calico Roses." Bones as sculpture, bones as landscape.

**Visual:** A field of bones — not scattered, ARRANGED. The bones form the level's architecture: femurs as pillars, skulls as stepping stones, pelvises as archways, vertebrae as stairs. Everything is bone-white against warm sand. The bones are O'Keeffe-simplified — smooth, abstracted, no anatomical detail. You know they're bones, but they read as pure form. The sand here is grey-white — bone sand. It sinks slowly.

**Gameplay:** Platforming. The bones are the platforms — hard surfaces that don't sink. The sand between them is the hazard. The player jumps from bone to bone, pulling themselves up when they miss. The bone shapes create the level's rhythm: long femurs for running, round skulls for single hops, curved pelvises for wall-jumps.

The twist: some bones are buried — partially submerged in sand. The player must dig around them to expose the full bone, creating new platforms. The exposed bones are brighter white than the buried ones. The player is literally excavating the level's architecture.

**Form Sight mechanic:** In Form Sight, the bones lose all detail — they become pure shapes. Triangles, circles, curves, lines. The level becomes a geometry puzzle. Some platforms are ONLY visible in Form Sight — buried bones whose forms are perceptible but whose surfaces are below the sand. The player must toggle Form Sight to find hidden platforms, then toggle back to see the sand to dig toward them.

**O'Keeffe detail:** The flowers. O'Keeffe placed flowers in the eye sockets of skulls. In the Bone Field, certain skulls have a single flower growing from them — a tiny, bright green-leafed desert bloom. These flowers mark safe platforms (they don't sink). The player learns: the flower is the form's assertion of life. The bone is the form's record of death. Both are O'Keeffe. Both are the level.

**Objective:** Find the three skull-and-flower platforms. Each one reveals a fragment of a shape when touched (a curve, a line, a point). Match the three fragments to form a column capital — the key to the Tomb Colonnade below.

**Reward stone:** Turquoise (the desert's protection stone)

---

### Level 4: The Tomb Colonnade — "The Cylinder and the Triangle"
**O'Keeffe Reference:** O'Keeffe's architectural paintings — simplified structures, monumental forms, the essential geometry of human-made things.

**Visual:** A buried Egyptian temple colonnade — six columns, half-exposed in sand. The columns are O'Keeffe-simplified: pure cylinders with lotus capitals (simplified to a triangle flare at the top). The sand between the columns is deep amber. The ceiling is intact in places — flat stone slabs (rectangles) supported by the columns. The floor is hard sand — the first consistently safe surface. But the sand from above keeps falling — the ceiling is crumbling, and sand pours through the cracks.

**Gameplay:** Horizontal and vertical. The colonnade is a structure the player navigates — climbing columns, crossing ceiling slabs, avoiding sand falls. The sand falls are the hazard — streams of sand pouring from ceiling cracks that the player must avoid or dig through. If a sand fall catches the player, they sink rapidly in the column's base.

The columns can be climbed (they're hard surfaces). But some columns are broken — their lower halves are buried in deep sand. The player must dig to expose the base, or climb to the top and cross via the ceiling slabs.

The Sphinx's riddles appear here as level text — brief, O'Keeffe-toned riddles on wall fragments:
> "The column is not a column. The column is a cylinder. The pyramid is not a pyramid. The pyramid is a triangle. What is the shape of the temple?"
> Answer: a circle (the colonnade's floor plan, visible only in Form Sight from above — the player must climb to the highest ceiling slab and look down in Form Sight to see it)

**Form Sight mechanic:** In Form Sight, the colonnade's detail vanishes — the carvings, the lotus capitals, the sand grain. What remains: six cylinders, arranged in a curve. The ceiling slabs become rectangles. The sand falls become straight lines. The level becomes a blueprint. Hidden passages are visible in Form Sight — gaps in the cylinder arrangement where the player can dig through to reach Level 5.

**O'Keeffe detail:** The shadows in the colonnade are deep lavender — the deepest shadows in the game. The columns cast lavender bars across the sand floor. The player walks through stripes of light and lavender shadow. This IS an O'Keeffe painting.

**Objective:** Climb to the highest point, activate Form Sight, see the circle floor plan, find the hidden passage between two cylinders, dig down to Level 5.

**Reward stone:** Lapis Lazuli (the stone of Egyptian royalty — deep blue, the complement to the desert's warm palette)

---

### Level 5: The Lost Library — "The Fragment and the Form"
**O'Keeffe Reference:** O'Keeffe's later abstract work — fragments of form, the essential gesture.

**Visual:** A buried chamber — the Examiner's lost library. Papyrus fragments are everywhere, suspended in the sand like objects frozen in amber. The fragments are O'Keeffe-toned — warm cream, aged ochre, faded ink. The sand here is mixed with papyrus fiber — a textured, fibrous sand that sinks slowly but is harder to dig through (it catches, it tangles).

**Gameplay:** Puzzle-platforming. The fragments ARE the level. Each fragment is a small platform — a piece of papyrus suspended in sand. The player jumps from fragment to fragment. But the fragments ROTATE — they're suspended in sand that shifts, and the fragments tilt and turn. The player must time jumps to land on flat fragments.

The puzzle: the fragments have EDGE SHAPES — curves, lines, points, spirals. The player must find three fragments whose edges match — three fragments that fit together to form a complete shape (a scarab). In Form Sight, the fragments lose their text (which is illegible anyway) and become pure edge shapes. The player must match the FORM, not the content.

The fibrous sand makes digging harder — the player can't dig fast through it. The strategy is to stay on fragments (solid surfaces) and only dig when necessary. Falling into the fibrous sand means a slow, desperate struggle to reach the nearest fragment.

**Form Sight mechanic:** Essential here. The fragments are unreadable without Form Sight — they're just damaged papyrus. In Form Sight, they become shapes — edges, curves, lines. The player must use Form Sight to identify which fragments match. Some fragments are buried — only their edge shapes are visible in Form Sight, sticking up from the sand like the tips of icebergs.

**O'Keeffe detail:** The completed scarab — when the three fragments are matched — glows with a warm amber light. The scarab is O'Keeffe's beetle — not anatomically detailed, but FORM-true. A simplified, essential scarab. The kind of form O'Keeffe would have painted.

**Objective:** Match three fragments by form to create the scarab. The scarab is the key to the Mummy's Chamber below.

**Reward stone:** Peridot (the soul of the volcano goddess — fire that learned to be still)

---

### Level 6: The Mummy's Chamber — "The Essential Form"
**O'Keeffe Reference:** O'Keeffe's most reduced paintings — "Blue and Green Music," the late abstractions where everything becomes color, form, and space.

**Visual:** A burial chamber. A single mummy lies in the center, wrapped in linen — an O'Keeffe form: smooth, tapered, curved, no anatomical detail. The linen is bone-white. The chamber is dark — the only light comes from the mummy itself, a faint warm glow. The sand here is almost invisible — a fine dust on the floor, not deep enough to sink in. This is the safest layer. The quietest. The simplest.

The chamber has four walls. Each wall is a single color — O'Keeffe's four desert colors:
- North wall: Ochre (warm earth)
- South wall: Coral (warm shadow)
- East wall: Lavender (cool shadow)
- West wall: Dusty Rose (the valley color)

The walls are blank. No hieroglyphics. No carvings. No detail. Just color. This IS O'Keeffe's thesis: the essential form IS the color IS the form. The chamber is a painting you stand inside.

**Gameplay:** No sinking. No sand. No hazard. The player walks. The player explores. The mummy holds a stone — Peridot, if they didn't collect it on Level 5 (or a second Peridot if they did). Touching the mummy triggers the revelation: the player's Form Sight activates involuntarily. The mummy's linen wrappings fall away in Form Sight, revealing the essential form beneath: a SHAPE. Not a body. A shape — a curved, tapered, smooth form. The form of a human reduced to its essential geometry. The O'Keeffe thesis, completed.

The player then looks at the four walls in Form Sight. Each wall's color reveals a hidden shape — the ochre wall has a triangle, the coral wall has a curve, the lavender wall has a circle, the dusty rose wall has a spiral. The four shapes, combined, form the floor plan of the entire Desert Bone zone — the architecture the player has been sinking through, revealed as a single, simple form.

**The choice:** The mummy's chamber offers a choice:
- **Take the stone:** The player takes Peridot from the mummy. The chamber stays. The player ascends (the game provides a ladder — a simple, O'Keeffe-styled line from floor to ceiling). The game ends. The player keeps Peridot.
- **Leave the stone:** The player leaves Peridot with the mummy. The mummy's form settles — the linen smooths, the glow steadies. The player has honored the form, not the treasure. The chamber opens a new passage — down, to Level 7.

**O'Keeffe detail:** This is the most O'Keeffe level in the game. Four colors. One form. No detail. The player who has been sinking through increasingly simplified levels arrives here — at the bottom, at the essence — and finds... almost nothing. A shape. Four colors. A choice. The O'Keeffe thesis: the form is enough. The adequate is enough.

**Objective:** Reach the mummy. Make the choice.

---

### Level 7: The Bone — "The Form Beneath All Forms" (Secret Level)
**O'Keeffe Reference:** O'Keeffe's very late work — when even the forms began to dissolve into pure color and space. "Sky Above White Clouds" — the ultimate simplification.

**Access:** Only if the player leaves the stone with the mummy.

**Visual:** Below the Mummy's Chamber, below the tomb, below the colonnade, below the bones — the Bone. Not a bone. THE Bone. The essential form of the entire Desert Bone zone, stripped of everything — sand, stone, color, shadow. Just white. White form. White space. A single white shape in a white void. The shape is the Inn itself — the tree, the form the player will see at the end of the Form Sight quest chain, rendered here as pure geometry.

**Gameplay:** No sand. No sinking. No platforms. The player floats — a gentle, weightless drift through white space. The white shape (the Inn-as-tree) is the only thing in the space. The player drifts toward it. Touching it triggers the zone's ultimate reward: the Bone Keeper's charm — "O'Keeffe's Rose" (a tiny bone-white flower cast in silver with a single green enamel leaf).

**O'Keeffe detail:** This level has no color except white. No warm amber. No lavender shadow. No coral. No dusty rose. Just white — the absence of detail, the presence of pure form. The player who has been learning to see forms through six layers of descending detail arrives at the bottom and finds: the form IS the white. The form was always white. Everything else was decoration.

**The silence:** No 42Hz hum. No wind. No sand sound. Complete silence. The quietest moment in the Geode Inn. The player floats in white silence toward the white form. The only sound: a single bell tone when the player touches the form — the Form Sight activation sound, the sound of seeing.

**Objective:** Drift to the form. Touch it. Receive O'Keeffe's Rose.

**Ascension:** After touching the form, the player ascends — not through the levels (no retracing), but straight up through a white shaft. The seven levels are visible as colored bands: amber, ochre, bone-white, ochre, cream, bone-white, white. The player rises through the colors. The surface is warm amber. The player emerges from the sand in the Desert Bone zone, standing beside the Sphinx, holding O'Keeffe's Rose.

## Visual Design (O'Keeffe System)

### Art Direction
Every visual element follows O'Keeffe's principles:
- **Simplify to the essential form:** A column is a cylinder. A pyramid is a triangle. A bone is a curve. A mummy is a tapered oval.
- **Colored shadows:** Shadows are NEVER grey. They are lavender (cool), coral (warm), or dusty rose (valley). Each level has its dominant shadow color.
- **Stark horizons:** Where horizons exist, they are single lines. No gradient transitions.
- **Warm palette:** The base palette is O'Keeffe's desert — ochre, rust, coral, dusty rose, bone-white, amber.
- **Minimal detail:** The deeper the level, the LESS detail. Level 1 has grain texture. Level 7 has none. The descent IS the simplification.
- **Form as architecture:** The level geometry IS O'Keeffe forms — bones, cylinders, triangles, curves. The platforms are paintings you stand on.

### Color Progression (by level)
| Level | Sand Color | Shadow Color | Sky/Wall | Detail Level |
|-------|-----------|-------------|----------|-------------|
| 1. Surface | Warm amber | Lavender | Ochre-to-rose gradient | Full (grain, wind, Vulture) |
| 2. Sand Sea | Ochre | Coral + Lavender | No sky (dunes) | Medium (dune curves, grain) |
| 3. Bone Field | Bone-white | Deep lavender | No sky (underground) | Medium (bone forms, flowers) |
| 4. Colonnade | Deep amber | Deep lavender | Stone ceiling | Medium (column carvings, riddle text) |
| 5. Library | Fibrous cream | Warm ochre | No sky (chamber) | Low (fragment edges only) |
| 6. Mummy | Dust (barely visible) | Four wall colors | Four colored walls | Minimal (one form, four colors) |
| 7. The Bone | White | None | White void | None (pure form) |

### Form Sight (In-Game Mechanic)
- Toggle with F key or button
- In Form Sight, all level elements lose detail and become pure shapes:
  - Bones → lines and curves
  - Columns → cylinders (rectangles with rounded ends)
  - Dunes → sine waves
  - Fragments → edge shapes
  - Sand → flat color fills (the grain disappears)
  - Hidden platforms become visible (buried forms whose edges show through sand)
- Form Sight uses a slight visual filter: reduced saturation, increased contrast, a thin white outline around essential shapes
- The world in Form Sight looks like an O'Keeffe drawing — sparse, essential, beautiful

## Sound Design

### Audio Philosophy
Sandfall is the second-quietest game in the Geode Inn (after Tumble Run). The sound is the desert — wind, sand, the 42Hz hum, and the occasional bone-sound.

### Per-Level Sound
| Level | Ambient | SFX | Music |
|-------|---------|-----|-------|
| 1. Surface | Desert wind, distant Vulture cry | Sand crunch, bone clink on landing | Low-dub classical (50 BPM, Egyptian ney flute) |
| 2. Sand Sea | Wind through dunes (hollow, singing) | Sand hiss on sinking, muffled thud on landing | Low-dub (ney + hand drum, 50 BPM) |
| 3. Bone Field | Quiet (underground) | Bone clack, sand whisper, flower chime | Low-dub (ney + sistrum, 50 BPM) |
| 4. Colonnade | Stone echo, sand fall hiss | Column ring on touch, sand pour | Low-dub (ney + deep strings, 50 BPM) |
| 5. Library | Papyrus rustle | Fragment crinkle, fibrous sand tear | Low-dub (ney + quiet harp, 50 BPM) |
| 6. Mummy | Near-silence | Single bell on Form Sight trigger, linen whisper | Near-silence (a single sustained note) |
| 7. The Bone | Complete silence | Single bell on touching the form | Complete silence |

### The Descent Sound
As the player sinks through layers, the ambient sound gets quieter, the music gets simpler (instruments drop out one by one), and the 42Hz hum gets louder relative to everything else. By Level 6, only the hum remains. By Level 7, even the hum stops. The silence IS the O'Keeffe thesis: the form exists in the quiet, not the noise.

## Technical Implementation

```
Game Type: Sinking sand platformer (vertical descent)
Engine: CSS/JS or Godot (cross-platform)
Resolution: Responsive, scales to container
Input: Keyboard (arrows + space + F) + touch (swipe + tap + Form Sight button)
Save: Level progress + stones collected saved to PlayerProgress entity
Leaderboard: gameMode "sandfall" in Leaderboard entity (score = deepest layer reached + time)
```

### Physics
- Sinking: passive downward velocity in soft sand (accelerates with time stationary)
- Digging: active downward/sideward velocity through sand (faster than passive, directional)
- Jump: upward velocity (only if not past chest-deep)
- Pull: slow upward velocity at edge of hard sand (triggered when adjacent to solid surface)
- Sand collapse: dug tunnels refill after 3 seconds (gradual, visible)

### Level Data
Each level is a tile-based grid:
- Hard sand (solid platform)
- Soft sand (sinking hazard)
- Living sand (rising platform)
- Bone (solid platform, O'Keeffe form)
- Fragment (rotating platform)
- Wall (impassable, colored)
- Empty (air — falling)
- Dig point (transition to next level)

## Connection to the Geode Inn

### Zone Integration
- **Location:** Desert Bone zone → arcade area → "Sandfall" cabinet
- **Unlock:** Complete Quest 3 (The Weight of Meaning) in the Desert Bone quest chain
- **Form Sight:** The in-game Form Sight toggle requires the player to have earned Form Sight in the main quest chain (Quest 7). Players who haven't earned it yet can play the game but can't toggle Form Sight — the hidden platforms are invisible. This is a soft incentive to progress the main quest.
- **Stones earned:** Each level's reward stone is added to the player's Stone entity collection

### Narrative Connection
- The game IS the Desert Bone quest chain's thesis, playable. The quest chain teaches Form Sight through story; Sandfall teaches it through gameplay.
- The Vulture appears in Level 1 as a background element — the same Vulture from the quest chain, circling the player. The player who remembers the quest chain knows the Vulture is watching.
- The Bone Keeper's charm (O'Keeffe's Rose) is earnable both through the quest chain AND through the game's secret Level 7. Two paths to the same reward.
- The Mummy's Chamber choice (take the stone or leave it) mirrors the quest chain's stone sacrifice — the player's attachment is being tested through gameplay, not just story.

### Standalone Potential
Like Tumble Run, Sandfall could work as a standalone sellable game:
- **Free in-game:** Base game (Levels 1-6), no Form Sight (requires quest chain)
- **Standalone ($2.99):** All 7 levels, Form Sight built-in (taught through the game itself), additional challenge modes (time trial, no-Form-Sight hard mode, endless sand descent)
- **DLC ($0.99):** "The Blue Mesa" — a Petrified Forest-themed expansion with 3 new levels using petrified wood as platforms and the 174Hz frequency (deep time) instead of 42Hz

## Design Rules

1. **The descent IS the thesis.** Every level strips more detail. The player physically descends through O'Keeffe's process — from full detail (Level 1) to pure form (Level 7). The game doesn't TELL you this. The game makes you live it.
2. **The sand is the teacher.** The sand is the hazard, but it's also the pathway. You can't progress without sinking. You can't survive without digging. The sand is the lesson: to find the form, you must go through the detail, not around it.
3. **Form Sight is not required but changes everything.** Players who don't use Form Sight can complete Levels 1-6 (harder, missing hidden platforms). Players who do use it see the game as O'Keeffe saw the desert — as essential geometry. The choice to see is the player's.
4. **The Mummy's choice is the game's question.** Take the stone (treasure) or leave the stone (form). The game doesn't judge. But only one choice opens Level 7.
5. **Level 7 is the reward for seeing.** The player who leaves the stone — who chooses form over treasure — finds the Bone. The white void. The pure form. The quietest moment in the Inn. The O'Keeffe thesis completed: the form IS enough.
6. **No combat. No enemies. No time pressure (except sinking).** Sandfall is a meditation on form. The only enemy is the sand, and the sand isn't evil — it's just doing what sand does. The player fights geometry, not monsters.
7. **The O'Keeffe aesthetic is the level design.** Every platform is a form. Every shadow is a color. Every level is a painting the player descends through. The game IS the art.
