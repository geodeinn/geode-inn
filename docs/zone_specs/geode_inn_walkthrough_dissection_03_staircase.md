## THE JUNCTION

Pale wood — not the dark oiled bark of the main floor but the living interior of the trunk, still growing, not yet carved by the brownies' hands. The spiral ascends into light and descends into dark. Each step is a note. The player stands at the junction and feels the pull of both.

**THE HOST IS AT THE BASE.** Standing on the first step, looking up, the way someone looks at a road they've traveled before and will travel again.

"You're ready."

The Host's speech (novel canon, the game's voice): the main floor is where everyone lives. The Inn is also where you learn. The learning is up and the learning is down, and the choosing matters, and the choosing doesn't have a wrong answer, and both directions have a right answer, and the right answers are different.

**THE ANCHOR MECHANIC — THE STONES ARE THE KNOTS:** The Host tells the player to take their stones. "The dark changes frequencies. Your stones will keep your note steady. The Song is the rope, and the stones are the knots, and the knots are what you hold when the rope goes slack." This is the game's descent-protection system, delivered as gift, not tutorial: in dark zones, the 42Hz drifts. Stones anchor the player's frequency. The more stones, the steadier the note. The collector's path and the descent path feed each other — the economy's spine.

**If you go up, go to ASCENT. If you go down, go to DESCENT.** Both are live forever. Neither expires.

## ASCENT — THE ALCHEMICAL STAIRCASE

**AUDIO LAW, ACTIVE:** the music rises in key with every landing. This is not ambience — it is literal. The player is hearing their own elevation. (Climb far enough and the game will modulate mid-stride; the first time the key changes under the player's feet is a moment none of them forget.)

**THE FREQUENCY:** In-Between — oscillating 42↔84Hz at 50 BPM. The staircase IS the In-Between, architecturally: neither growing nor still, always becoming. (Camera note: third-person default; the In-Between camera lock does NOT apply on the staircase — the staircase is the journey TO the In-Between, not the place itself.)

**THE GARGOYLES WATCH EVERY LANDING.** Six resonant sentinels at 111Hz — Watcher, Listener, Sage, Gatekeeper, Caller, Keeper. They do not speak. They have never spoken. They note. (Storyline tie: the frequency-match game — the staircase's unclaimed gap game — lives in THEM. They're also the Whisper Layer's earliest witnesses: the player is being seen on every landing, and the seeing is the safety.)

### LANDING 1 — THE PHILOSOPHERS (Nigredo → first questions)
Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, in argument that has been running for centuries and is not close to resolving. The player can:
- **Listen** (the argument is the ambience; the three voices never agree, and the never-agreeing is the point — the trivium as background radiation)
- **Ask** (one question per visit; each philosopher answers the same question differently — the Inn's first dialogue puzzle: the answer isn't in any one reply, it's in the triangle)
- **Sit** (the philosophers acknowledge a sitter with a half-second pause in the argument — the Inn's respect for stillness, even here)

Nigredo's black work happens on this landing — the stage of dissolution, the raw material before refining. The landing's light is low; the philosophers' argument is the fire. Seed: the Ragana's path (questioners learn by asking here).

### LANDING 2 — THE RETORT (Albedo's first white work)
Tesla, reincarnated, electrically unstable, brilliant. The Retort's lab hums at a pitch no other room has. The player can:
- **Watch the experiments** (arcs of electricity, conducted risk; the Retort never demonstrates twice the same way)
- **Play The Retort's circuit puzzle** (energy redirection — the staircase's own arcade moment; the Retort built the machine and pretends not to be proud of it)
- **Get shocked** (touching the lab's wrong surface gives a comic 30ms haptic jolt — the Inn's gentlest consequence; the Retort's single line: "Now you know which one it is.")

Albedo: the washing, the whitening. The landing is lit blue-white. The Retort, renamed from The Alchemist, is the staircase's transformation made resident.

### LANDING 3 — THE COOK (transmutation through food)
The Cook's alchemy runs through the stomach. The player can:
- **Eat** (one dish per visit; each dish grants a small, real, temporary effect — warmth in the catacombs, steadier hands for the Artisan's craft, clearer hearing for the Library. Food as buffs, but the Cook would never use the word buff. The Cook says: "It will help.")
- **Learn a recipe** (the building system's kitchen tier; recipes recorded in the player's book)
- **Watch the oven** (the Cook's game — the apothecary's cousin, timing-based; Dr. Mario's pill-matching lives one landing down from the kitchen, and the Cook pretends not to be jealous)

Storyline tie: everything the Cook serves once grew somewhere in the Inn's zones. The player who eats attentively can taste where. (Coppola: the epic anchored in one small dish.)

### LANDING 4 — THE FIVE SMITHS (Iron, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Copper)
The forge tier. The player can:
- **Commission** (the building system's forge tier — bring material and design; the Smiths make. Wait times are real: the game world keeps working while the player is away, per the always-on Inn)
- **Watch the work** (each Smith's hammer has its own cadence — five rhythms, all at 50 BPM, all different; the landing's music IS the hammers)
- **Apprentice** (the Smiths' favor system: bring ore, earn standing, unlock the Undercity forge doors)

### LANDING 5 — GESSO (the upside-down writer)
Hanging upside down, writing in mirror script. The player can:
- **Read the mirror writing** (upside down, reversed; the puzzle is the reading; what Gesso writes is the Inn's marginalia — corrections to the building itself, notes on what the tree grew wrong and what it grew on purpose)
- **Recieve a page** (Gesso occasionally hands a page down, reversed. The pages are seeds: half of them become quests later, when the player can read them)
- **Watch Gesso work** (stillness again; Gesso is the staircase's quietest resident and its most observant — the whisper layer's scribe)

### LANDING 6 — DA VINCI, BETWEEN THE LAB AND THE KITCHEN
The landing that connects everything: lab on one side, kitchen on the other, and Da Vinci in between with the flying machines that exist through the window. The player can:
- **Look through the window** (Clockwork Skies visible — gears, flight, the Arcana's distant contrails. The window is the staircase's promise: there is a door to that sky, and the player has just seen through it)
- **Handle the designs** (the flying machine sketches turn; the Arcana's lineage is on the table — Da Vinci designed the time ship; the Inn-Tree grew it; the Captain claimed it. The player learns the Arcana's lineage before they meet its Captain)
- **Repair a design** (light crafting: fitting Da Vinci's gears; the mechanic that later matters in Clockwork Skies)

### THE PINBALL TABLE (the staircase's own arcade)
On a mid landing: the Geode Inn pinball table, whose playfield IS the staircase — the ball climbs from Nigredo (bottom) to Rubedo (top), each bumper an alchemical stage, the win condition the Philosopher's Stone at the top. The staircase, playable. The Inn's most literal self-portrait in the Arcade's roster. (Storyline tie: the table's completion chimes in the key the staircase's music is currently in — the pinball machine literally tunes itself to the player's elevation.)

### THE TOP LANDINGS — what waits
- **The Spiral Gallery arch** (off the staircase: six chambers, six artists, the history of art in a spiral — dissected in its own dissection; enterable day one, no gate)
- **The Canopy door** (Tumble Run territory begins here; the trade winds' sound leaks through the seams)
- **The Escher Bridge** — visible above the top landing, impossibly angled, NOT crossable yet (Form Sight is the key, and the Cartographer's quest owns it). The player sees the bridge in hour one and learns the Inn has doors that wait. (The bomb under the table, second placement.)
- **The Void Door** — where the staircase stops being stairs and becomes sky. The stillness door. The player can stand here; the door notices. The Rest lives behind it, someday. No marker. The player who eventually opens it will remember the first time they stood here and heard — nothing. The nothing was the door, listening back.

## DESCENT — THE FIRST DARK

**AUDIO LAW, ACTIVE:** the waltz-macabre. 3/4 time, the rhythm of descent through the levels. The key drops with every landing. The player who spent an hour in the Inn's warmth is now walking into the first music that doesn't comfort — and doesn't lie, either. (Elfman's waltz, fully deployed.)

**THE ANCHOR MECHANIC ENGAGES:** below the first landing, the 42Hz begins to drift — subtly, then unmistakably. The stones in the player's cave answer: the further from the fire, the more the collected stones hum to hold the note steady. The player FEELS their collection for the first time as protection. (Game feel: the Pulse Line at the bottom of the screen tightens — its trace simplifies, then steadies, as each stone's anchor engages. The player's progress bar, made visible and made CARE.)

### DESCENT LANDING 1 — THE FIRST COLD
No rooms yet. The landing exists to teach one thing: down is real. The wood darkens. The light goes from living-pale to hearth-orange remembered — the warmth is now memory, not presence. One gargoyle (the Watcher) is mounted here, and its eyes track the player's descent. It has always tracked every descent. (Del Toro: the cold landing has a wear map — the banister polished by ten thousand nervous hands, exactly here, exactly this high.)

### DESCENT LANDING 2 — THE RAGANA'S ARCHIVE
The Librarian Ragana's domain — shelves that go down, not up; the archive's dark half. The player can:
- **Play Archive Maze** (Pac-Man-class; the maze is the shelving, the pellets are marginalia, and the ghosts are the Ragana's four spectres — catch-able, never lethal, gently humiliating)
- **Ask the Ragana a question** (the Borrower's Ledger's keeper; the Ragana reads every text and answers from context — the deepest dialogue system in the Inn, and it does not suffer small talk. The Ragana's blink: every 8 seconds. The Owl blinks every 8 seconds. The coincidence is: the adequate.)
- **Read in the dark** (the Archive's texts continue; the Deep Read works here too; the reading light is the books themselves)

Storyline tie: the Ragana and the Owl share a blink, and no one in the Inn will say why. The player doesn't need to know yet. The player who someday reads the ledger's oldest entries finds the answer in a margin note Gesso wrote upside down.

### DESCENT LANDING 3 — THE PIPE
The moss-lipped root-pipe, breathing faint cold air. The Pipe Descent game lives here (SMB-class) — the player's first view of the Catacombs' weird retro underlayer. The Pipe Stone is the key (portal registry). The player can play now or pass; the pipe waits. (Anti-FOMO law: unfound doors don't go away. Neither do unprompted ones.)

### DESCENT LANDING 4 — THE RIVER
Memory's Illusion — the underground river's sound arrives before its light. The ferryman is audible. The player who descends this far on the FIRST descent has outpaced the Host's expectations, and the Host knows it, and says nothing, and the knowing is the respect.

This is the natural base of Dissection 03 — the Catacombs proper, the Underworld Queens, Charon's crossing are the next dissection's depth, and the thread mapping (15 loose threads) ties them.

## THE CONVERGENCE — THE CHOICE'S CONSEQUENCE

The novel's traveler chose down — the amethyst had chosen first, and the traveler was following. The game honors the same pull: the player's first collected stone (after the shell) whispers its direction. Amethyst-family says down. Sun-family says up. The player who notices this discovers the Inn has been listening to HOW THEY PLAY, and answering, and the answer was always in their own cave. (The class key's cousin: the stone compass. The collection is a moral compass and the game never labels it.)

Whichever direction was chosen first, the other remains — forever live, forever unchanged, always a little curious why the player went the other way first. The Inn's two answers, both right, never the same.
