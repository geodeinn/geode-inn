## THE ONE GRAMMAR

**PATTERN = FREQUENCY = FUNCTION.** Every craft in the Inn is arranging matter so it holds a frequency. Marie's veve, Luna's bead circle, the Blacksmith's finding, the Meadery's fermentation, the builder's join — the same act at different scales: a pattern drawn in material, tuned until it does one small true thing.

The dialects live in their home zones and teach each other: THE QUARTER'S CRAFT (Marie — the assembly), THE BENCH (Luna — the stone), THE GARDENS (the Van Gogh garden, the Poison Garden, the homestead plots — the herb), THE CORRIDORS (the woods), THE MEADERY (the brew — fermentation as slow chemistry). No craft is complete without the others; every craft is whole on its own terms (the anti-gatekeeping law: the player who only wants Luna's bench is never punished for it; the player who learns all of it makes things nothing else can make).

## PILLAR TWO — HERB: THE CHEMISTRY DIALECT
**What the components actually do, and how they tie together. THE EFFECT IS REAL (the anti-superstition law): the herbs do what herbs actually do — the science is the lesson, the story is the delivery. The Inn never asks the player to believe a fiction about a real plant; it asks them to learn the real one.**

**The honest chemistry, played straight (the working set):**
- WILLOW — the aching-head tree: the bark carries the precursor of the modern painkiller; the tree that grows by water, leaning over it, eases what water can't. (The willow is the bridge between pillars — wood in ritual, bark in remedy.)
- THYME — the field antiseptic: the herb of the wound and the kitchen, planted by the rose to guard it.
- ROSE — the heart-opener: the petal for grief tea, the hip for winter medicine (the Vitamin C the winter lacks).
- NIGHTSHADE — the Poison Garden's own lesson: DOSE IS THE DIFFERENCE — the poison and the pupil-dilator are the same plant at different amounts; the Poison Garden teaches respect, not fear (the Little Alchemy overlay stays the game layer; the real plant is the curriculum).
- GINSENG — the mountain's root: the adaptogen the frost MAKES (the cold cycle is the potency — the Appalachian winter is the ingredient the root needs; greenhouse ginseng is a different, lesser thing).

**The pairing layer (the cross-herb ties): HERBS THAT GROW TOGETHER WORK TOGETHER** — the companion law: thyme planted beneath the rose (the guard), the sisters of the field (the bean, the corn, the squash — three roots, one soil, three helpers), the meadow's self-sorting (yarrow and the grasses). The game's combos inherit this: the pairing taught by the Van Gogh garden's rows, the homestead plot's companion-planting tutorial, the Poison Garden's warning pairs (what never to plant beside what — the garden's one gentle dread).

**The climate layer: THE WORLD MAP IS A PHARMACOPEIA.** Where an herb grows is set by climate cycles, not by design convenience — the biome IS the prescription: THE BAYOU'S HUMIDITY (the water-loving herbs, the humid-air potency), THE ANDES' ALTITUDE (the thin-air plants, the altitude as ingredient), THE DESERT'S XERIC PULSE (the bloom-after-rain ephemerals — the plants whose whole year happens in six weeks), THE APPALACHIANS' FROST CYCLE (the ginseng's cold), THE POISON GARDEN'S SHADE (the alkaloids the sun would burn away — the poison plants are the shade's plants). THE GATHERING CALENDAR: the Beat Line extends to the beds — seasons, tides, and moon phases open and close harvest windows (the same clock Zeke keeps; the herb beds run on the tide's calendar).

## PILLAR THREE — WOOD: THE PROPERTY DIALECT
**The types of woods used in different rituals, and WHY. THE FOLKLORE IS THE FIELD NOTE:** every wood's ritual role was earned by an actual property — the legend is the label someone wrote on a true observation. The Inn teaches the property, and the player understands the ritual for free.

**The working set (each wood, its property, its ritual, its home):**
| Wood | The actual property | Why the ritual chose it | Home corridor |
|------|--------------------|--------------------------|--------------|
| CYPRESS | Does not rot in water; endures the wet | THE CROSSING WOOD — planted in graveyards, carved for what must last through both worlds; the Bayou's keeper-tree | Bayou Bridge |
| OAK | Dense, hard, the lightning tree | THE THRESHOLD WOOD — doors and door posts; the god-touched (the strike) made it the oath wood | Garden Path + the building tiers |
| WILLOW | Bends without breaking; grows by water | THE GRIEF WOOD — the moon's tree; wreaths and mourning work; the bend that survives the flood | Garden Path |

| CEDAR | Repels insects; endures decades | THE KEEPING WOOD — chests and coffins and closets; purification by preservation | The homestead's storage builds |
| ASH | Straight-grained, the world-tree of the north | THE CONNECTION WOOD — handles and hafts (what joins the hand to the tool); Yggdrasil's own | The Norse reaches |

**The synthesis law (where all three pillars meet): A GRIS-GRI IS THE FULL CURRICULUM IN ONE BAG** — one stone (set in a pattern — Pillar One), one herb (chosen for its honest chemistry — Pillar Two), bound with a wood token or fiber (chosen for its property — Pillar Three), plus one intent: the charm that does one small true thing. Marie teaches the assembly; Luna teaches the stone; the gardens teach the herb; the corridors teach the wood. **The player who learns all four dialects makes what nothing else can make — and the player who masters any one dialect alone is still a whole craftsperson.** (The anti-gatekeeping law holds; the synthesis is the reward, not the requirement.)
