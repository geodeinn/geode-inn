## WHAT THE QUARTER IS

The Bayou's urban crown: the swamp's carved jewel, the city at the water's edge where all the swamp's threads come ashore. The Bayou is the murmur at rest; THE QUARTER IS THE MURMUR MADE A CITY — the murmur with iron lace on it, the murmur at a dinner party with good candles.

**Art Style:** ANNE RICE'S NEW ORLEANS — the 1994-film-lineage gothic, not the remake: gaslight and candlelight (never fluorescents), iron-lace balconies, gilt parlors gone green at the edges, oak shadows on plaster, the beautiful decay of old money in wet weather. THE BEAUTIFUL DECAY is the Quarter's law: the city is not ruined, it is AGED IN PLACE, and the aging is the beauty. Del Toro's worn asymmetry at full polish. (Tone teacher law: Rice is craft/tone reference only. The Inn's residents are originals — the gothic is the architecture, not the cast.)
**Vibration:** 42Hz carried in DRUM PATTERNS — the voodoo rhythms are the murmur made audible; the second-line beat runs at 50 BPM (the heartbeat's own parade rhythm). The two-song law: Marie's Catholic mass in the morning and the voodoo songs at night — TWO MUSICS PRAYING IN THE SAME ROOM, and the Quarter is the room.
**Access:** Bayou Zone → the river road into the city (the Bayou's Option 6), or the World Map's New Orleans pin. The St. Louis Cemetery tomb city is the Catacombs family's American kin (the pin already stands on the map).

## THE VOOODO CRAFT SYSTEM (the hoodoo/voodoo craft layer)

**GRIS-GRIS — the charm-bag crafting:** swamp materials + one stone + one intent = a charm that does ONE SMALL TRUE THING. (Wired to the charm systems: a gris-gris IS an ElementCharm or QuestCharm in a bag.) The one-thing law: the charm that does one thing works; the charm that promises everything is a fraud, and the Quarter has frauds, and Marie's consultation queue exists to sort which is which.
**THE VEVE — frequency diagrams:** the drawn patterns, each one a SIGNATURE — the Inn's law: a veve is drawn to tune, not to summon. Each pattern is literally a frequency diagram (the player who traces a veve correctly hears the tone; the ones who trace it carelessly hear nothing, and that is the whole lesson in one mechanic). THE DRAWING IS THE LISTENING.
**THE CONSULTATION (Marie's mini-game):** the player brings one problem. Marie names one material, one stone, one intent. The player makes it themselves — Marie's craft is taught, never sold. "You'll make it yourself. It works better when you do."

## THE CITIES OF THE DEAD (the deep layer)

St. Louis Cemetery No. 1 — the tomb city, the Catacombs' American kin. **THE SCIENCE IS THE LESSON:** the above-ground burial is not gothic affectation — the water table REFUSES. The ground pushes the coffins back (1789 mandate, Spanish colonial engineering answering the flood). The city that buries its dead above ground because the ground says no.
**MARIE'S TOMB — the X-mark legend:** visitors draw X's on the tomb asking favors, and the tomb grants ONE SMALL TRUE THING per player, once, never explained. (The legend layer honored straight — the real-world custom, played with the Inn's one-wish-no-explanation law. The player turns away to mark it; when they turn back, the chalk is gone.)
**THE TWO-DIRECTIONS LAW (the funeral bands):** the music to the tomb is a slow dirge; the music from the tomb is a joyful parade — death gets BOTH TEMPOS, and the second-line beat at 50 BPM is the heartbeat's own answer to grief. The player who follows a funeral both ways learns the whole of it: the walk out and the walk home are one ceremony.

## THE COFFEE GIRLS (the all-night café; added 2026-09-10, Krista's Bitter Grounds reference)

**The reference:** Neil Gaiman's "Bitter Grounds" (Fragile Things, 2006) — coffee, zombis, New Orleans, the man who drifts into someone else's life. The story's cultural tilt is exactly the Quarter's: the dead girls who pour coffee, the ones who never sleep, the lore underneath the diner's ordinary morning. Tone teacher law applies (all works under copyright — reference for craft, tilt, and lore only; the Inn builds its own coffee girls, owes the story nothing but the nod).

**The café:** The all-night coffee house at the Quarter's edge — the one that never closes. CHICORY COFFEE is the house pour, and the history is the lesson: coffee ran scarce in the wartime blockades, and the city stretched it with chicory root — the scarcity that became the taste, the improvisation that became the tradition (the science-is-the-lesson law: the history is the curriculum, the coffee is the delivery). Beignets at all hours. The café is THE QUARTER'S GATHERING SPACE (the Gathering Law: one social space per level) — and the TWO-DIRECTIONS LAW's last stop: the parade home from the tomb ends here, because the joyful walk deserves somewhere warm to end.

**The girls:** The dead who stayed. Played with the Inn's honest-core law and the Shield of Lore's restraint (no morbid mechanics, no spooky costume, the heavy history honored in silence, prose quiet): the coffee girls are the Quarter's answer to the Bayou's keeper thesis — the Bog Fairy Golem stays as the keeper placed in the kept; the coffee girls stayed because SOMEONE HAS TO POUR FOR THE TRAVELERS WHO ARRIVE AT 3 A.M. They are warm. They are unhurried. They are always exactly tired. They remember EVERYONE'S ORDER FOREVER — the dead keep the small memories, not the big ones, and every order anyone ever loved is safe with them. The player gradually notices: no girl ever leaves, no girl is ever new, and the café has no closing time because the girls have nowhere else to be. NEVER EXPLAINED (the one-small-true-thing law). Played straight, per the Child-Actor Law's spirit: no cute-ification, no jump-scare inversion — just the gentlest uncanny in the Inn, served with sugar on the side.

**The nod — RULED (Krista, 2026-09-10):** The café's name is THE BITTER GROUNDS — the title as homage, the pun already true: the coffee IS bitter grounds. Gaiman's story is credited in this spec as the tilt reference; the coffee girls are the Inn's own.
