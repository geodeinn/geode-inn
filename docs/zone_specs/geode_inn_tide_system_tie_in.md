## PART 1 — THE BEAT LINE (Zeke's tide-timing quest chain: the long line)

The timing games were always one system wearing different waters. THE BEAT LINE is the long quest line that makes them one: every timed crossing, gated cave, sinking stone, and sweeping light in the Inn runs on the same clock — the tide — and Zeke the Pirate is the clock's keeper, because the Pirate is the one who listens to it for a living.

**Giver:** ZEKE THE PIRATE (the Beat — 50 BPM, WHEN to act; quiet, war-drum fingers, the dock at night).
**The spine:** THE TIDE CHART (Zeke's gift — the whole water system's clock on one skin of parchment; the item that makes every timing game legible as ONE system).
**The arc:** keeping time → reading the water's clocks → the blank on the chart → the drum that plays itself.
**Length:** eight quests — the long line, deliberate: the timing system is the water game's spine, and spines are built slow.

### The Chain

1. **"The Skin of Parchment"** — the gift: the tide chart. Zeke's only instruction, the whole line in one sentence: "The water keeps appointments. Everything timed in this Inn is the tide keeping them." The chart shows every zone's water rhythm — the compressed cave tide (42/42), the river stones' sink-rate, the Lighthouse sweep — as ONE clock, different faces.
2. **"The Count"** — the bodhrán lesson: 50 BPM learned on the skin drum, and the player's pulse line steadies to the Beat. The skill is not rhythm — it's PATIENCE AT TEMPO: the ability to hold timing under distraction. (The line's only tutorial, and it's a music lesson.)
3. **"The Sinking Stones"** — Japan's precision game, wired in: the river stones sink ON THE TIDE'S CLOCK — the chart predicts every sink (the Kappa's toll, the Tengu's gusts — the same clock, different waters; the timing games unified).
4. **"The Mouth"** — the Tidal Caves' gate, timed by the chart: the dry-shod entry on the exhale, the flood read a half-hour out. The Beat Line and the caves' chain shake hands: the tide-sense the player earned there becomes the chart's first field test.
5. **"The Sweep"** — the Lighthouse vigil: sweep speed and foghorn timing at 50 BPM — Zeke's beat as the Coast Hub's civic metronome (the three-hearted rhythm: Lighthouse, Pirate, sea). The player keeps a watch-shift — the vigil's patience is the Beat at its largest.
6. **"The Flood-Craft"** — the puzzle craft: the flood-tide work (Vaulknut's law — work WITH the breath): the timed crafting that only holds if made on the water's push. The chart's margins fill with the maker's notes.
7. **"The Chart's Blank"** — the turn: the tide chart has one blank section — the UNDERGROUND water, uncharted. The surface clock's keeper admits the below has its own clock, and the blank is the invitation (the line's door into Part 2).
8. **"The Drum That Plays Itself"** — the finale, already canon, now earned: the Moon's secret — 50 BPM was never Zeke's. The Moon's pulse, heard through the water since the first night at sea. The Pirate is the instrument. Told through sound, never told in life — and the player, who has kept the Beat across the whole line, hears what the drum has always been playing: the Moon, keeping time through the water, and every timed thing in the Inn keeping it too. The chart completes itself: the last mark is the Moon's, and the whole parchment is revealed as one instrument's sheet music.

**Reward:** THE KEPT BEAT (the 50 BPM internalized: the player's pulse line holds steady through every future timing sequence — the water system's grace, earned; and the chart, complete, a permanent inventory of every clock the Inn's water keeps).

## PART 2 — THE UNDERGROUND TIDE (Persephone and the specially adapted critters)

### The Anchialine Layer (the underground tide — the real science is the lesson)

Beneath the Tidal Caves' six environments lies the seventh reach: the UNDERGROUND TIDE — inland waters hidden-joined to the sea, rising and falling with it through flooded channels no map has drawn. THE REAL SCIENCE (the teaching law): ANCHIALINE SYSTEMS — coastal aquifers tidally connected to the ocean through porous rock; the water salt at depth, fresh above, and the boundary between them a HALOCLINE: a layer where densities meet and light bends — a door made of water, no frame, no hinge, opened by weight alone. The underground tide breathes with the sea above it — the moon's reach, echoed below.

### The Specially Adapted Critters (the stygobite cast — the below-court)

The underground tide's residents, and Persephone's below-court: the STYGOBITE CAST, drawn from the real animals of the anchialine dark — eyeless shrimp, transparent fish, the pale slow crustaceans whose ancestors were stranded when ancient seas receded millions of years ago and whose lineages have lived in the dark water ever since. THE REAL SCIENCE (the teaching law): in the energy-starved underground, everything adapts the same way — eyes surrendered (nothing to see), pigment surrendered (nothing to show), metabolisms slowed (nothing to waste), antennae lengthened (everything to feel), lifespans stretched (everything to wait). The Inn's reading, and the tie: the critters did not adapt to the dark. They adapted to HER — to the tide's breathing: the state-shifter's cycle is their calendar, her rise their harvest, her hold their winter, her fall their spring. They do not need eyes because the tide TELLS them everything. The below-court is the season's own household: the residents who never surface because their whole world is the below — the companions of the below-months, the tide's first congregation.

### The Ferry (the cycle, complete)

AUTUMN: the underground tide rises — the salt pushing up through the channels, the halocline lifting like a held breath — and she rides it down to the winter station. THE WINTER: the pit (the Terracotta chain's Hidden Guest — the painted warrior, the lullaby watch). The underground critters ride the halocline's edge beside her — the below-court, escorting the season home. The chart's blank (the Beat Line's Quest 7) is this water: Zeke's surface clock ends where the ferry begins, and the blank stays blank on the parchment out of respect — some clocks belong to the below.
