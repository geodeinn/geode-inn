## WHAT THIS CHARACTER IS

The Campgrounds' resident build-master: the one who stands among the stones players leave lying around and explains, with total dignity and a string of puns, why the thing you just built stands up.

Three registers, braided into one voice:

1. **THE PENNYWORTH REGISTER (the dignity, and the past).** Unflappable. No build is too humble for courtesy, no disaster too far gone for calm. A collapsed Tier 1 lean-to is received the way Alfred receives a shattered chandelier: with quiet sympathy, a plan, and the faint certainty that he has seen worse on three continents. The Jeeves dignity rides the Alfred depth — beneath the butler's composure is a FORMER SOMETHING (the super-secret-agent precedent: a competent past the character never explains, only occasionally evidences). The hands that steady a wobbling frame have done steadier things under worse conditions, and the builder gets the sense — never the proof — that this is not the first collapsing structure these hands have walked out of. Never condescends, never panics. The composure IS the lesson — a structure's first gift to its builder is steadiness, and the second is the suspicion that the person teaching you has survived things your blueprint can't imagine. The past stays a closed gate (see Gates); the hints ride as posture, never exposition.

2. **THE HARVARD DIALECT (the outline).** Precise diction, the correct term at the correct moment, the occasional Latin delivered like a man who can't help it. Compression member. Lintel. Pointed arch — "the Roman half-round's overachieving cousin." Post-and-lintel is "the oldest contract in the world: one holds, one lies still, both keep their word." Firmitas, utilitas, venustas — strength, utility, beauty — quoted as the three questions every structure must answer before the campfire agrees it's a building. Vitruvius is the standing citation (De architectura, first-century BCE — the Archive's own shelf, comfortably inside the two-century rule; the classical texts ARE the curriculum).

3. **THE XANDER HEART (the truth under the jokes).** The puns are armor over genuine warmth and genuine fear of the mythic neighbors. Deflects, then lands the sincere line — and the sincere line always arrives, usually about the builder, not the building: "The mortise-and-tenon's fine. You're the one holding the whole thing up, kid. Sit down, eat something." Working-class brave: no powers, no destiny, no gift — shows up anyway, every session, in every weather, which is the whole job description of a builder and the whole thesis of the zone.

## THE JOB — THE ACADEMIC OUTLINE OF THE BUILD

As players build (the five-tier alchemical progression, BuildingRecipe entity), this NPC supplies the real-world canon behind every structure, real architecture and engineering history delivered as build review:

- **Tier 1 (Nigredo — raw shelter):** lean-tos, yurts, first fires. The outline: wind loading, slope, why the wall leans back. The wigwam and the yurt as engineered answers to weather, not "primitive" anything — "nomads solved airflow before universities had plumbing."
- **Tier 2 (Albedo — first transformation):** wattle-and-daub, fired brick. The outline: tension and compression, why clay grows when you work it, the daub as the first composite material.
- **Tier 3 (Citrinitas — refinement):** stonework, glass, joinery. The outline: the arch (how a wedge cheats gravity into cooperation), the truss (a triangle is the only shape that can't gossip), why a buttress is "the building leaning on something sturdy, which frankly we all need."
- **Tier 4 (Rubedo — monumental):** domes, Roman concrete, castles. The outline: coffering, the Pantheon's oculus as the original skylight argument, Roman concrete's recipe ("the Romans poured it two thousand years ago and it's STILL setting. Aim lower; settle in for a long career").
- **Tier 5 (the Philosopher's Stone tier — imagination):** whatever the builder imagines. The outline: none — this is the review tier, the character walking the finished build, asking the firmitas-utilitas-venustas questions, and naming aloud what the builder made before the builder named it (a supporting act to the NAMING step; the Cartographer still keeps the map).

The outline is always optional (No-Fail, information not a gate — the Quest Mark Law's No-Fail register). A builder may build in total silence; the outline stands ready like a library, not a lecture. Declining a review quietly lowers nothing (Anti-Skinner).

## THE ZEPPO LAW — FIRST SEAT

Every overpowered room gets its unpowered heart. The Campgrounds is the founding seat: a zone where building is done by hands, not spells, deserves the resident who proves it. No frequency tricks, no transformation, no both-real question — the character's only "power" is showing up and knowing why things stand. (Law propagation to other zones — Camelot, the Fae Kingdom, Cosmology — is a separate Witan gate, not yet ruled.)

Ties: the Historical Master NPCs at the campfire (a builder-scholar would host their arrivals: the instrument-build witnesses need somewhere to stand and someone to pour); the Gathering Law (one social space per level — the campfire is the Campgrounds'; the build review is the structured gathering inside it); the 50 BPM heartbeat pacing (community building milestones ride the thrum — reviews never rush it); the Architect path (of the four player paths, this NPC is the Architect's resident mentor); Erik the ship-builder (professional respect across zones — timber men recognize each other); da Vinci's Workshop (flight engineering kin; the Workshop borrows the outline back).

## VOICE NOTES

- Speaks in full sentences. The pun lands mid-sentence and the sentence doesn't slow down for it.
- Latin quoted accurately, pronounced confidently, translated immediately ("Vitruvius said it first, in the margin, in the dark").
- Never puns AT the builder. The building is fair game; the builder is family.
- Failure reviews are autopsies, not judgments: "Let's look at what the wind knew that we didn't."
- Signature register: the sincere line arrives LAST, quiet, after the joke, when the player has already started to walk away — Xander's law.
