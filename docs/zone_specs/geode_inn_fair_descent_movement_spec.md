# THE FAIR DESCENT — The Inn's Vertical Movement Law

**Ruled in (Krista, 2026-10-14):** the Inn's vertical travel is not one stair with one alternate hidden in back. It is SEVERAL ways up and down, each with its own register, all of them first-class. The ruling's origin: players who lack a conventional way of movement in their reality deserve the Inn's signature move — accommodation built into the structure's natural fun, with no ostracizing effect anywhere in it. "If people are lacking a conventional way of movement in their reality, have fun with it instead of its usual ostracizing affect."

## THE PRINCIPLE (the law underneath the law)

**The curb-cut effect, honest-labeled:** sidewalk curb cuts were fought for by wheelchair users; today every stroller, suitcase, and delivery cart uses them. Design for the edges and everyone benefits. The Inn's version: the accessibility options will be so delightful that fully-abled players will feel left OUT of them. The test of every vertical option is not "can a wheelchair user reach the floor" but "would a kid with two working legs pick it anyway?"

**No special door, just doors.** There is no service elevator in the back that everyone knows the purpose of. Every way up is a way up. Using any of them marks nobody, because there is no marked option — only options, each with its own register.

## THE OPTIONS (the vertical roster)

1. **THE STAIRS.** The default walk: the acacia stairwell with the Flower of Life floors (already canon, 2026-10-13) and the Pulse Line breathing at 50 BPM. Unhurried, social, the walk that lets you meet people. The stairs are one option among several — never the implied "normal" one.

2. **THE SLIDE.** Lovely wood alongside the banister, riders can slide off onto ANY floor, landing in a pile of pillows and CONFUSED GOBLINS. The No-Fail law holds all the way to the landing: miss your floor and you land soft anyway. The Goblin Landing Crew's job is fluffing the pillows and being startled every single time — the joke never ages because the goblins never learn (the Steady Light law performed as comedy). The descent is pure fun for its own sake: no tokens, no points, no meter (Anti-Skinner check — the reward is the ride and the goblins, nothing else).

3. **THE BLUE BOX.** The tardis-esque structure, promoted from staircase decoration to the Inn's gentlest elevator: any floor, on request. Bigger inside than outside, so a wheelchair and companions always ride together — nobody travels alone in the box. The gentlest ride in the building: no jarring motion, no speed, a calm hum. It announces itself: an arrival sound before the door opens, so no one is ever surprised at the threshold (the knock law's cheerful cousin). Honest label: TARDIS (Doctor Who, borrowed knowingly — the Inn's own blue box will earn its true name at a naming session).

4. **THE TUBES.** Futurama-style transport tubes, placed because they are a joy. The Convention Engine already covers their grammar: the tube IS the trip — whoosh, done, no further explanation owed. Honest label: Futurama (borrowed knowingly).

## THE LAW, WRITTEN TO SURVIVE FUTURE BUILDS

Every vertical option, forever, must:
- **Reach every floor equally.** No option gets a reduced map. If the tubes skip a level, that level was designed wrong.
- **Never mark its user.** No option is "the accessibility one." They are the slide, the box, the tubes, the stairs.
- **Be enviable, not apologetic.** If an option would embarrass its rider, redesign the option.
- **Land soft.** The No-Fail law holds at every arrival, every floor.
