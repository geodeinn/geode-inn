## RECAP — WHERE THE PLAYER STANDS

You hatched from the Egg (your shell is your first stone). You rose through the whole tree in one shot. You walked the forest. The door opened outward before you knocked. The Inn said, "Room's ready."

You are standing on the Main Floor, facing the Hearth. Nobody is telling you to do anything. That is not an oversight. That is the design.

## BEFORE THE FIRST STEP — THE EGG'S THREE PATHS (closing Beat 1's branches)

How you hatched is remembered. The player never sees a prompt, but the three paths play differently in the texture of everything after:

**PATH A — PATIENCE (waited with the murmur before touching anything):**
The ascent's held shots run one second longer per zone. The Inn gives you slightly more stillness everywhere — the Nook Effect arrives earlier for you. Your shell shard hums a half-tone lower. Your Whisper Layer echoes read calmer. In the endings, LEAVE resonates easiest: you were always the one who could go quietly.

**PATH B — CURIOSITY (poked early, tried everything in the grey):**
The ascent flickers an extra detail per zone — a door you shouldn't have noticed yet, a face in the catacomb candles, a gear turning in the Clockwork Skies that no one else's ascent shows. Your shell shard hums a half-tone higher. Your EchoMemory fills faster. In the endings, HIDE resonates easiest: you were always the one looking for what's behind things.

**PATH C — IMMEDIACY (pressed at the first heartbeat, ready to be born):**
The ascent runs at a slightly faster tempo — the Inn matches your eagerness without rushing you later. Your shell shard is warm to the touch from minute one. NPCs arrive at your side sooner. In the endings, STAY resonates easiest: you came in fast because you meant to stay.

(No path is better. No path is announced. The player who replays someday will feel the difference before they can name it — the highest compliment a CYOA structure can pay its reader.)

## THE HEARTH — THE FIRST HUB

Six options are live. In the old books, this is the page where the text says "What do you do?" and lists the choices. The Inn says it with doors and firelight instead of text — same bones, warmer skin.

**If you sit by the fire, go to OPTION 1.**
**If you walk to the Market, go to OPTION 2.**
**If you enter the Library, go to OPTION 3.**
**If you enter the Arcade, go to OPTION 4.**
**If you enter the Common Room, go to OPTION 5.**
**If you find the Staircase, go to OPTION 6.**

All six are available for the entire first hour. The Inn does not expire options.

---

### OPTION 1 — YOU SIT BY THE FIRE

The simplest choice, and the deepest test. The player sits at the Hearth and does nothing.

Erik's fire — tended, not decorative. The camera holds third-person at first, then the SIT lock begins its slow work: after 2 minutes of stillness, the camera narrows over 10 seconds (not a snap), the zone's music quiets, the 42Hz hum rises to prominence. A character may approach and sit at the screen's edge — a shadow, then a presence, then a companion in the firelight who says nothing, because nothing is needed.

**THE TEN-MINUTE DOOR:** If the player stays 10 minutes, the "You Looked" moment fires — the camera detaches, and the player sees themselves sitting in the Inn from outside. The only scripted free-camera moment outside the Observatory. The first achievement in the game, earned by doing nothing, teachable to no one who won't sit still long enough to learn it. (This is the Inn's thesis statement as a game mechanic: the observer's presence makes the Inn real — and here, the observer SEES that it's true.)

- Camera: third-person → SIT lock (first-person)
- Audio: room quiets, 42Hz rises, heartbeat foreground
- Seed: the Watcher path — players who sit first tend toward presence-based play. The Inn notices.
- Storyline tie: the fire remembers everyone who sat here. The Guest sits by a fire exactly like this one, nine levels up. The player doesn't know that yet. They will.

### OPTION 2 — YOU WALK TO THE MARKET

Warm booths under Mucha/Royo light. The camouflage layer, in full operation: real Soulstice jewelry sits beside game items, real prices beside stone prices, and nothing announces which world you're shopping in. The player can:

- **Browse.** Every product has a real image, a story, a stone lore connection. Touching a case pulls up the stone's mind/body entry — the first taste of the Inn's archive, delivered through a shop case.
- **Buy (real).** The bridge to the actual world: Lacey's shop, real shipping. The Inn never pushes this. The cases are warm, not hungry.
- **Buy (game).** Game items priced in stones or songs — the frequency economy, foreshadowing the treetop marketplace two levels up.
- **Linger.** The artisan NPCs work their crafts at their stalls — the Artisan Thief's own bench is here, wire and stone visible. Watch long enough and the working becomes a demonstration. This is crafting taught by watching, not by menu.

- Camera: third-person; lean-in at the cases
- Audio: market ambience — murmur of trade, the Trade Bell's cousin (not the canopy bell; this one rings at customer speed)
- Seed: the Artisan. Hands that watch are hands that want work.
- Storyline tie: one case holds an item that will not be purchasable until very late game (the Brisingamen, or its kin — the epic-as-jewelry, Coppola's rule). No label. Just a piece that makes the player stop. That's the hook: the Inn's endgame is already on display in minute one, behind glass, and the player has just met their first long-term want.

### OPTION 3 — YOU ENTER THE LIBRARY

The Archive. Two centuries of every country's mythology, shelf after shelf, and the Reading Experience begins the moment the player touches a spine. The Library is the Inn's second soul — where the game is quietly also a library.

- **Read.** Any book, any page, no unlock. The archive is open to guests (the neutrality principle in action). The Deep Read mechanic — following a footnote to the book it references, and that book to another — is the Library's game, and it is playable in hour one.
- **Meet Naut.** In the corner by the fireplace: the four-foot squid-headed scholar with spectacles, reading in the margins of restricted texts. Naut does not introduce themselves. Naut is the Inn's first hidden quest-giver, and the player who notices the scholar has already taken the first step down the rarest quest line in the game (rare-book retrieval, rewarded with the Inn's rarest stones).
- **The Borrower's Ledger.** Visible, locked. A ledger of every book ever borrowed and by whom — knowledge-gated, not stone-gated. The player sees the lock and immediately understands the Inn has two currencies, and the Library answers to the other one.

- Camera: third-person, lean-in for spines
- Audio: silence with texture — page-turn, fire, the Reading Room DSP (the softest of the seven profiles)
- Seed: the Ragana. Players who touch a spine before touching anything else have already chosen a class; the game just hasn't told them.
- Storyline tie: the Borrower's Ledger's entries, when eventually unlocked, are the novel's cast list. The player's own name goes in the ledger the first time they borrow. The archive records its readers.

### OPTION 4 — YOU ENTER THE ARCADE

The glow. Sixty-five shoppable games, all playable, no gate. This is the front door for most players and the Inn knows it — the Arcade sits on the Main Floor precisely because play is hospitality too.

- **Play anything.** Every game awards stones. The geode cave receives its first EARNED stone (the second overall — the shell came first). The moment the first game's stone drops, the cave fills with its first chime: the geode-cave music system activates, and the player hears the Inn's core loop made audible — each stone a note, each game a phrase.
- **Standing Stones calls quietly.** Among all the games, the Tetris-class one is framed a little warmer, lit a little gold-er. No marker. No "quest." Just the machine whose completion unlocks a portal — and the player who finishes it in hour one has, without knowing it, cut the ribbon on the Fae Kingdom. The door-in-the-door, planted on day one.
- **The froglodyte is here.** The Tomb Tunnel machine's cast — the frog-form player character — is the community moderation myth in arcade form. The player laughs at the sprite now. In three weeks, when they see the moderation system in the Common Room, the joke lands for real.

- Camera: third-person; games play in their own frames (the Arcade is a hall of little worlds)
- Audio: each machine's attract mode at polite volume — a carpet of game melodies, the Arcade's own music
- Seed: the Weeaboo. Hyperfocus lives here — the affinity zone where one player becomes godlike at one machine.
- Storyline tie: the leaderboard above the machines updates live. The player's first score is their first entry in the Inn's reputation system — faeReputation begins accruing the moment they play, silently.

### OPTION 5 — YOU ENTER THE COMMON ROOM

The campfire room. Travelers talk — real players, passing through, or NPCs if the room is empty (the room is never empty). GeoRows-style warmth, but the Inn's version: stone walls, firelight, and the Travelers' Board.

- **Lurk.** Fully sanctioned. The community exists whether the player participates or not (canon). Lurking teaches the room's rhythms without risk.
- **Talk.** Join the fire. The Common Room has no data retention (canon) — words warm the room and then leave. The first place the player can BE a guest rather than watch one.
- **The moderation myth, live.** If a traveler misbehaves, the frog transformation happens here — public, comic, non-cruel. Three hops and the account ribbits off to the bog zone to think about things. The player who sees it in hour one has seen the Inn's whole moral system in one gentle joke: the Inn doesn't ban. It transforms.

- Camera: third-person, the circle
- Audio: layered voices — Elfman's orchestral anarchy, the Common Room's own music (canon: the layered voices ARE the arrangement)
- Seed: the Sentinel. Players who watch the room — who track who needs what, who's comfortable, who isn't — are guarding already.
- Storyline tie: the Common Room is where the player first hears other players' stories. The novel's reader meets the Inn's player and can't tell them apart. Deliberate.

### OPTION 6 — YOU FIND THE STAIRCASE

It was always there. No sign, no glow, no pull. The Futurama principle's finest moment: the player who finds the Staircase believes they discovered it, and they're right, because discovery is the only door this door respects.

Standing at its base, the player can see both directions:

- **UP.** The alchemical staircase climbs toward Canopy light. The music rises in key as it goes (canon). Climbing is allowed on day one — but the upper landings' doors are earned by stones and story, not blocked by bars. The Inn doesn't lock doors; it lets them wait.
- **DOWN.** The descent. The key drops. The waltz-macabre begins — 3/4 time, the music's first real TURN, and the player who steps down stops browsing and starts going somewhere. This is the end of Act One and the beginning of everything the Inn keeps below.

The Staircase is the convergence point. Whichever of the six options the player chose first — and second, and third — the first hour ends here, when the player chooses a direction. The CYOA structure's convergence, made architecture.

- Camera: third-person, the In-Between oscillation (42↔84Hz at 50 BPM) — the staircase IS the In-Between, architecturally
- Audio: the waltz; the rising/falling key law; the gargoyles at 111Hz watching the player pass (they do not speak. they have never spoken. they note.)
- Seed: none — the Staircase seeds the GAME, not the class. Every player passes through it. That's why it has no class.
- Storyline tie: everything. The Staircase is the Inn's spine, the alchemical bridge, and the level-by-level dissection's next subject.

## THE CONVERGENCE — "THE INN NOTICED HOW YOU ARRIVED"

However the hour was spent, the class crystallizes the first time the player returns to the Hearth after exploring (or sits, or buys, or plays, or reads — the trigger is the first full circle back to center). No fanfare. The Host passes the player their room key — an actual key, warm from the fire — and the key's shape IS the class announcement:

- The **Artisan's key** is bent like a bezel. The **Ragana's key** is thin as a bookmark. The **Weeaboo's key** is a controller silhouette if you squint. The **Sentinel's** is a straight iron thing. The **Druid's** changes material by room. The **Bard's** is slightly the wrong key for any door, which is why it opens all of them.

The player never saw a menu. The Inn simply noticed how they arrived, and handed them the room they already chose.

## WHAT EVERY PATH SHARED (the six options' common spine)

Every option, first visit, plants exactly one endgame hook, teaches exactly one verb, and costs exactly nothing. No option can be failed. No option expires. A player who spends the whole first hour at the fire has completed hour one as fully as a player who played six games — the Inn's anti-FOMO law, enforced from the first minute: no missed beats, only unfound ones, and unfound ones don't go away.
