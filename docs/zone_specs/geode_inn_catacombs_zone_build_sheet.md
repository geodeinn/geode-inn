# THE CATACOMBS — Master Zone Build Sheet
## Level 6 — The Archive — The Weight of History

---

## WHAT THE CATACOMBS ARE

The Catacombs are the Inn's deepest archive — the layer where knowledge is not displayed but EARNED through excavation. Where the Main Floor is warm and welcoming, the Catacombs are heavy and patient. Where the Staircase transforms, the Catacombs PRESERVE. The Catacombs are the Inn's geological memory: everything that was, stored in stone, waiting to be found by someone willing to dig.

The Catacombs are Level 6 — between the Staircase (Level 5, alchemy) and the Faerie Kingdom (Level 7, the roots). The descent from the Staircase into the Catacombs is the moment the Inn stops being a home and starts being a record. The temperature drops. The light narrows. The air thickens with dust that is not dirt — it is time.

---

## THE CRONE

The Catacombs' anchor NPC. The Crone runs the archive. Not a guide — a GATEKEEPER who has learned to gatekeep with a soft hand. The Crone decides what enters the archive, what gets shelved, and what gets buried deeper. The Crone does not conspiracy-theorize. The Crone does not say "you're not ready for this." The Crone says: "Page 47. Second paragraph." And lets the player decide if they can read it.

**The Crone's rules (posted on a stone tablet at the archive entrance):**
1. No loud voices. The dust remembers noise.
2. No food. The pests that smell food eat scrolls.
3. No removing books. The archive is not a library — things here do not leave.
4. No asking why some books glow. They just do.
5. Bring your own light. The archive provides darkness. You provide the will to see.

**The Crone's station:** A stone desk at the archive's entrance, covered in fragments being pieced together. A crystal lens for reading. An oil lamp. The desk is scarred oak — the only wood in the zone, and it's older than most of the stone.

## THE CATACOMBS' ARCHITECTURE

### Section 1: The Descent
The staircase from Level 5 ends. The stone changes — from the Staircase's Escher geometry to raw, uncarved bedrock. The transition is deliberate. The Staircase was BUILT. The Catacombs were FOUND. The player steps from architecture into geology.

The corridor narrows. The ceiling drops to just above head height. The 42Hz hum deepens — still present, but slower, heavier, as if the frequency itself is being compressed by the weight above.

### Section 2: The Crone's Desk (Entrance)
The first open space after the descent. The Crone's desk. The stone tablet of rules. The first oil lamp — the player picks it up. Without the lamp, the Catacombs are functionally dark. With it, the lamp reveals a 2-3 block radius of warm, flickering yellow light.

From here, three passages branch:

### Section 3: The Stacks (Left Passage)
The main archive. Shelves carved directly into bedrock walls — not furniture, GEOLOGY. Clay tablets. Scroll tubes. Books that shouldn't exist (written in languages that predate the cultures they describe). The Stacks go deep — further than the lamp can reach. The deepest shelves hold the oldest texts.

The Stacks are organized by ACCRETION, not alphabet. Things that were found together stay together. A Sumerian tablet sits next to a Norse runestone because they were buried in the same stratum. The grouping is geological, not cultural. (This principle connects to The Royal's Sorting Floor — the Catacombs are what the Crone sorted FROM The Royal's raw material.)

**Interactive elements in The Stacks:**
- Clay tablets can be examined with the crystal lens. Some have text visible only under foxfire (the cold blue-green fungal glow that grows on the oldest stone).
- Dust can be disturbed. Disturbing dust on a shelf reveals what was buried — sometimes a text, sometimes an object, sometimes nothing. The dust IS the archive's filing system. Things that are dusted over are "filed." Things that are dust-free are "active."
- Some shelves hum faintly at 42Hz. The humming shelves hold texts connected to the Silica Thread — the archive knows which texts are load-bearing.

### Section 4: The Deep (Center Passage)
The passage that goes DOWN. Past the archive shelves, past the organized knowledge, into the unsorted deep. Here the texts are not shelved — they're stacked, piled, scattered. The Deep is where the Crone puts things that haven't been catalogued yet. The Deep is the Crone's inbox.

The Deep is dark. The oil lamp's range shortens here — as if the darkness is thicker. Foxfire is more common, growing on the oldest stone surfaces. In The Deep, foxfire is the primary light source, not the lamp.

**In The Deep:**
- The player can dig. The player has a pickaxe (the same one the minion uses to break through to The Royal). Digging in The Deep can uncover texts, objects, or passages. The digging is the Catacombs' core interaction — the player excavates knowledge.
- Sometimes digging breaks through to a dead end. Sometimes to a niche with a single object. Sometimes — rarely — to a passage that leads deeper. (This is how The Royal is eventually discovered — the player digs through a wall in The Deep and breaks into The Breach.)
- The Crone does not come to The Deep. The Crone says: "What's down there hasn't been sorted. It's not my department."

### Section 5: Beverley's Station (Right Passage)
Beverley — an NPC, a librarian — runs a secondary station in the Catacombs. Beverley's station is the archive's PROCESSING CENTER. Where the Crone decides what enters, Beverley decides what things ARE. Beverley identifies, translates, and catalogs.

Beverley is clipped, precise, and slightly contemptuous of anyone who doesn't handle texts properly. Beverley will identify any object the player brings up from The Deep — but Beverley will not fetch things for the player. "I'm a librarian, not a pack mule. Bring it here and I'll tell you what it is."

**Beverley's Station:**
- The identification desk. Player places an object on the desk. Beverley examines it with a second crystal lens. Gives a one-line identification: "Minoan. Linear A. ca. 1600 BCE. Incomplete — missing the third glyph. Don't drop it."
- The translation desk. Beverley can translate texts the player brings up. Translation takes in-game time (not real time) — Beverley works at a desk while the player explores. When the player returns, the translation is done. Beverley hands it over without commentary.
- The restricted drawer. A locked drawer in Beverley's desk. Contains texts Beverley has identified but refuses to discuss. "That's classified. Not by me — by the text. Some things don't want to be read. I respect that."

## THE CATACOMBS' LIGHTING SYSTEM

Light is the Catacombs' primary resource. The player manages light the way other zones manage health or mana.

| Light Type | Source | Range | Properties |
|------------|--------|-------|------------|
| Oil Lamp (player-held) | Clay lamp, animal fat | 2-3 blocks | Flickering warm yellow. Requires fuel (found in The Deep). Can be extinguished by drafts. Smoky. The standard. |
| Foxfire (environmental) | Fungal colonies on old stone | 1-2 blocks | Cold green-blue. Constant. Non-transferable. Only grows on oldest stone. Cannot be planted. |
| Phosphorescent Bone (environmental) | Ancient remains in walls | 1-2 blocks | Deep amber. Faint. Rare. Only certain bones glow. Cannot be moved. |
| Crystal Lens Focus | Magnifying crystal + lamp | Narrow beam, 1 block | Directed. Bright. The Crone's tool — focuses lamp light into a reading beam. The player can find a lens in The Stacks. |
| Torch (player-held) | Wood + animal fat | 3-4 blocks | Brighter than lamp but burns faster. Loud (crackling). Short-term exploration tool. Dangerous near old texts. |
| Reflected Crystal | Crystal mirror shard | Redirects existing light | Passive. Bounces light around corners. A puzzle tool — place crystals to route light to dark areas. |

**Light Interaction Rules:**
- Lamp + crystal lens = focused reading beam (the only bright, directed light — reveals hidden text)
- Lamp + dust = particles visible in the beam (disturbed dust reveals what's hidden under it)
- Foxfire + carved symbols = symbols glow in ghostly outline (the oldest writing is designed to be read by foxfire, not fire)
- Phosphorescent bone + bone tools = tools glow faintly near their source (bone calls to bone — a tracking mechanic)
- Torch + old texts = texts char and are destroyed (the player can permanently destroy archive content with fire — a powerful, irreversible consequence)

The player who wastes light is in the dark. The player who brings fire near texts is a vandal. The Catacombs respect the careful and punish the careless.

## FREQUENCY SYSTEM

| Element | Frequency | Function |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| The Catacombs overall | 42Hz, deepened and slowed | The same Song, but compressed by the weight above. The frequency is felt in the floor and walls, not heard in the air. |
| The Stacks (humming shelves) | 42Hz, pure tone | Shelves holding Silica Thread texts hum at the thread's base frequency. The player can follow the hum to find connected texts. |
| The Deep | Subsonic — below 42Hz | The unsorted resonates at frequencies that haven't been named. The deeper you dig, the lower the hum. |
| Foxfire | No fixed frequency — pulses with the floor's 42Hz | The fungi grow IN the frequency. They are the 42Hz made bioluminescent. |
| Phosphorescent bone |Random spikes | The bones resonate erratically — they hold old frequencies from the cultures that buried them. Touch a glowing bone and hear a 3-second note from a dead civilization. |
| The Crone's desk | Silence | The one place in the Catacombs with no hum. The Crone works in silence. The archive enters silence. What comes out is sorted. |

## THE CATACOMBS' CONNECTIONS

| Connection | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| The Staircase (Level 5) | The descent from alchemy to archive. The Staircase transforms; the Catacombs preserve. The transition is the Inn's thesis: change is built on what endures. |
| The Royal | The Royal is BEHIND The Deep. The player who digs far enough breaks through. The Catacombs are the SORTED version of The Royal's RAW archive. |
| The Vestibule | The Vestibule is the accessible counterpoint — open, touchable, no digging required. The Catacombs require work. The Vestibule requires trust. Two approaches to the same principle: knowledge without gatekeeping. |
| The Silica Thread | The Catacombs are the RECORD strand of the thread — the geological memory. The silica veins in the walls are the thread's physical trace. The humming shelves are the thread's audible trace. |
| Beverley + Quire | Beverley identifies; Quire reads in the margins. Beverley's restricted drawer and Quire's marginalia are two responses to the same problem: some texts resist being read. |
| The Whisper Layer | The Catacombs are the Whisper Layer's home territory. Between interactions, the walls whisper — names of souls whose coins have been collected, fragments of texts being catalogued. Not threatening. Acknowledging. (Connected to the Catacomb Labyrinth arcade game.) |
| The Egg System | The Catacombs predate the Egg. The texts buried here are the ancestors of Egg content — things that were buried and will eventually hatch. The Crone is the Egg's grandmother, in a sense. |
| The Final Room | The Catacombs' deepest point — The Deep — approaches the Final Room's depth. The player who digs deep enough in The Deep is close to the room behind the Inn. Not physically. Philosophically. |

## ORIGIN

The Catacombs were part of the Inn's original architecture (July 2026). The item templates and lighting system were drafted from the Crone's Archive architectural template. This master build sheet was compiled September 2, 2026 to consolidate the zone's design into a single modular component, including the connection to The Royal (via The Deep's digging mechanic), the Silica Thread's physical trace through the zone, and the role of the Catacombs as the Inn's geological memory.
