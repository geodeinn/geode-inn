## WHAT THIS CHAIN IS

The Andes are the Inn's proof that depth makes height. The real science is the curriculum (the teaching law): the Andes exist because of SUBDUCTION — the Nazca plate, the ocean floor itself, diving beneath the South American continent, and the dive building the height. The deepest dive makes the tallest peaks. The chain teaches plate tectonics as the Inn teaches everything: the science is the lesson, the story is the delivery — and the Andean story the Inca already told happens to be TRUE: Pachamama takes what is given DOWN and returns it UP. The offerings (pagos) the people bury become the mountains' height. The folklore knew the subduction before the seismographs did.

The zone runs on THE GIVING LAW (the cenote's law, the Andes' echo): nothing in the Andes is taken; everything is exchanged. The player who arrives as a taker finds the mountains closed. The player who arrives with something in their hands finds the whole range listening.

## THE CHAIN

### Quest 1 — "The Pagos"
The trailhead, and the zone's first law: the pago — the offering given TO the earth, buried, not displayed. Pachamama's voice is the ground itself (low, felt in the boots before the ears): "What you give down, I return up. This is not a bargain. This is how stone breathes." The player's first act is not a fetch or a fight — it is choosing what to bury (the choice is the adequate; the zone remembers what the player gave, and the giving shapes the chain's later echoes — the stone that received it appears higher up the trail, grown).
**Reward:** The buried seed (the player's pago — a permanent something in the mountain's keeping; the ground knows the player now).

### Quest 2 — "The Watchers"
The Apus — the sacred mountain spirits, each peak a presence, each presence a pair of eyes the weather lives behind. The quest is the learning to be watched WELL: the Apus do not test strength; they test whether the traveler notices they are being watched. The HUD-free mechanic: the weather. Every Apu expresses its attention through the clouds on its crown — the player learns to read the sky as the mountains' face (Bierstadt's job was always this: the peak lit as a presence, the cloud as the mood). The real folklore is the mechanism: the Apus watch over Andean communities — the zone scales that to the player.
**Reward:** The read (the player can now tell which Apu is watching — the weather-reading skill, active in every mountain zone of the Inn).

### Quest 3 — "The Tell in the Familiar"
The cloud forest, and Chullachaqui — the jungle dwarf with one animal foot, who mimics loved ones to lead travelers astray. The zone's trickster, played straight (the Child-Actor law: no cute-ification). The quest: someone the player trusts appears on the trail with an urgent reason to leave the path. The tell is ALWAYS there — the one wrong detail in the familiar (folklore's law: the animal foot; the Inn's law: the tell is findable). The player who spots the tell passes; the player who follows is led, gently, back to the trailhead, and the trailhead is further up than they remember — the forest's toll is a lesson, not a death. The science echo: mimicry in nature is never perfect — every mimicry system has its tells, and the predators who survive are the ones who read them.
**Reward:** The eye (the mimic-tell skill — permanent: the player can now spot the wrong detail in the familiar, and the Inn's future tricksters inherit a fairer game).

### Quest 4 — "The Whistle You Don't Follow"
El Tunche — the forest phantom whose whistle is beautiful, hypnotic, and wrong. The zone's audio centerpiece: the whistle is genuinely lovely (the Inn never cheats the player with ugly warnings — the danger is the beauty, Bradbury's law). The discipline is the INVERSE of the Pied Piper: the player must walk AGAINST the pull of the sound, and the game teaches it without a tutorial — following the whistle shortens the path in exactly the way that makes it wrong. The counter-skill from the Whisper Layer: the player's own hum (the 42Hz Song carried by breath) is the one sound the whistle cannot cross. The Inn's answer to every siren: you carry your own note.
**Reward:** The counter-hum (the whistle-resistance — the player's carried Song strengthens against all hypnosis-mechanics in future zones).

### Quest 5 — "The Miners' Goblin"
El Muki — the tiny cave goblin the Andean miners know, keeper of the veins. The underground layer, and the zone's exchange with the deep: the miners' law (real folklore: miners leave offerings for El Muki; the goblin guards the vein and takes the shine from the greedy). The player descends a working vein (the Tomb Tunnel's sibling mine, the Inn's mining canon) and meets the goblin at the richest seam. The Muki's test is the giving law one floor down: the player who reaches for ALL the vein finds it dull (the shine taken); the player who takes a little and leaves a pago finds the same seam bright tomorrow. The greed-mechanic is the Inn's quiet moderation teacher — and the Muki, like the best Inn cryptids, is a worker, not a monster: small, dutiful, fond of coca-leaf offerings and clean tunnels.
**Reward:** The vein-right (a lifetime share of ONE small seam — the player's own modest, honest vein, yielding slowly forever, never running dry because it was never over-taken).

### Quest 6 — "The Deepest Dive"
The finale at Lake Titicaca — the highest navigable lake on earth, and the creator's forge: Viracocha shaped the sun and the moon FROM the lake. The quest is the zone's thesis walked in one descent: the player dives the lake (the water zone's diving skills carry over) and finds, below, the truth the whole range is built on — the subducting plate, the ocean floor diving under the continent, the deepest dive building the tallest peaks. The science revelation is the mythological one: what goes down comes up as height; Pachamama's law is plate tectonics with a face. Viracocha's presence is the lake's light at the surface — the creator seen only as reflection (Full Presence, never embodied: the sun and moon are the made things; the maker stays in the water). The player surfaces, and the Apus — every peak at once — have their crowns lit: the whole range watching the player who now knows what the mountains remember.
**Reward:** THE HEIGHT (the player's own Apu — a small new peak, unnamed, grown from the mountain's memory of the player's pagos: the buried offerings return as elevation. The player names the peak. The naming law: the naming wakes it. The Apu becomes the player's watcher-kin in every future mountain zone).
