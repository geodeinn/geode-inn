## 1. THE LAYER LAW (the naming discrepancy, resolved)

The two master water documents name the seven layers differently — and BOTH are right, because they name two different truths:

**EVERY LAYER HAS A PLACE-NAME AND A KEEPER-NAME.** The place-name is what the map calls it (Spring, Shallows, Tidal Caves, Atlantis, the Deep, Underworld Boundary, the Source). The keeper-name is what the water calls it (Rain, Surface, Tidal, Memory's Illusion, the Siren's Layer, Hades' Harbor, the Roots). Players learn the places first; the keepers' names surface with depth — the way a town's name gives way to its people's names the longer you live there.

## 2. POE'S COAST (Layer 2 — the Coast Hub / Granite Port)

Edgar Allan Poe is the Coast Hub's tone teacher: New England Gothic, granite, cold coastal fog, STONE PERSISTENCE, and the 50 BPM heartbeat. And the Lighthouse is Poe's law made architecture:

**THE HEARTBEAT, MADE VISIBLE AND AUDIBLE.** The beacon sweeps at 50 BPM — the heartbeat made visible. The foghorn sounds at 16-20Hz — the heartbeat made audible. The Tell-Tale Heart, but instead of guilt under the floorboards, it is care on the coast: the same insistence, the same refusal to stop, the opposite moral. (The Inn's whole move with its darkness, in one tower: Poe's dread, inverted into vigilance. The heartbeat under the floor was accusation. The heartbeat across the water is company.)

**THE ABANDONED FISHING TOWN.** New England granite, cold fog, empty streets — and the town is not dead. Ocean-dwelling entities have moved in and WEAR THE LOCALS' DISCARDED CLOTHING AS HOMAGE. Not mockery. Memory. The town's ghosts are honored by its new tenants.

**THE BEACON ABILITY (Poe's haunted house, made kind).** The Lighthouse's deepest mechanic: the beacon can ILLUMINATE MEMORIES IN EMPTY HOMES — and the ocean tenants only move into a house AFTER a witness acknowledges the previous occupants. The haunted-house story inverted: nothing is exorcised, nothing is erased. The old memory is lit, witnessed, thanked — and then the house may be lived in again. (Poe's houses never let go; the Inn's houses hand over gently. The Coast Hub is what Fall of the House of Usher would have been if the house had been loved properly.)

- **Play the Beacon** (memory-lit house scenes, the witness mechanic, the foghorn as confirm)
- **Meet the Murlocs' daily conversation** (the recurring comedic element: the Coast Hub's Murlocs hold a daily "conversation" with the Owl, the keeper — mostly weather, mostly complaints, always at exactly 50 BPM gesticulation)
- **Read the tide chart** (the Pirate's chart, the whole system's clock)

## 3. THE CAVE SYSTEM (Layer 3 — the Tidal Caves)

**THE TIDE-GATED LOOP (the Pirate's law: the waiting is the beat).** Cave exploration is gated by the tides: check the Pirate's tide chart, wait for the window, sail out, explore 20-40 minutes, return before the flood. You cannot cave-dive constantly — THE OCEAN GATES THE ACCESS, and the waiting is the beat. (The Inn's anti-grind law, made tidal: the game that respects patience gets the deepest caves.)

- **The Selkie's workshop** (Vaulknut's in-game character — the three-act crafting quest, cabochons and wire and the grinding wheel; the physical jewelry pipeline's in-world anchor)
- **THE BONE CAVES** (where the water system meets the catacomb system — the seam between Dissection 04 and Dissection 06; Charon's exit route, the bone mail crew's coastal dock)
- **THE GARGOYLE CAVES (THE ORIGIN POINT OF THE INN'S GARGOYLES).** The six resonant sentinels — Watcher (111Hz), Listener (64Hz), Sage (528Hz), Gatekeeper, Caller (432Hz), Keeper (adaptive) — were carved here, in the wet stone, by the water itself: the tide's patient sculpture. The gargoyle frequencies are the caves' resonant notes, found and carved, not invented. (This ties the water system to the Golden Contract: the gargoyles' signatures on the Contract are the Gargoyle Caves' notes. The deepest legal document in the Inn is signed by the water's own carvings. The caves are the Contract's birthplace in every sense.)

## 4. ATLANTIS (Layer 4's upper face — Royo's drowned city)

Dark romanticism meets biomechanical architecture: spires of dark iridescent metal entwined with pale coral and luminous anemones, drowned kings' statues tilted in cerulean shadow.

**PERSEPHONE'S TIDE (the Sacred Clockmaker).** Atlantis runs on Persephone's presence: HIGH TIDE (Persephone present) — the city awakens, conduits hum 42Hz, zero-G underwater vault diving; LOW TIDE (Persephone absent) — the Silt Sleep, copper-dim, drained chambers, flooded archive basements opening as the water recedes. Atlantis is the only city in the Inn with a SEASON inside it, and the season is a queen.

**OCEANIANOIR'S DOMAIN.** The scientist-linguist — Mensa goth, periodic-table tattoos — holds the layer's science with the Murloc companion Squeak at their side. The periodic-table puzzles (mineral catalysts open Atlantean tech-vaults) are the Inn's chemistry curriculum wearing a drowned city. (The ElementCharm entity's home questline: every element charm the player collects upstairs is REAL down here — the vaults are the periodic table's museum, and Oceanianoir is its docent.)

**THE ATLANTIS MONSTER ROSTER** (frequenters of the layer): Mami Wata (serpent-wielding water queen, trades rare alchemy for secret lore), Bakunawa (moon-eating serpent, swims the Atlantean skies during eclipses), Grootslang (elephant-snake guarding flooded gem vaults, takes mineral offerings), Cipactli (crocodilian living gatekeeper of the aqueducts), Jengu (gentle water spirits guiding high-tide labyrinths).

**BENEATH: CHARON'S LOZEN** — the dead's highway: memory-water (water that has passed through so much stone it carries the mineral record of everything it touched), the smuggler's route between coast and catacombs. Charon talks here — the only character who talks more than the Pirate, and the ferryman's river-talk is the water system's oral history.

**THE LOVECRAFT SEAT (canon law: the mythos as literary source material stays).** The Inn's Lovecraft is not the dread — it is the SCHOLARSHIP: the abyssal deep is what Lovecraft's protagonists wanted to see and survived. The Mythos is the Deep's literary source; the Inn's own Lovecraftian characters live UPSTAIRS, and the pairing is the design: **Naut by the fire, Quire in the cold.** Naut (formerly Cthulhu — the squid-headed scholar, "Fascinating," hidden quest-giver for rare book retrieval, rewards the Inn's rarest stones) and Quire (hushed, reading the margins of the restricted Archive texts, always looking over one shoulder).
