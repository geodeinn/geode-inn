## ENTERING THE BAYOU
**FREQUENCY:** 42Hz — but the MURMUR expression: the Song's quietest voice, filtered through humid air and biological time. Every zone has sung the Song at its own volume; the Bayou is where the player learns the Song is present even when it whispers. The heartbeat is far away here — this is not the Inn's pulse but the Inn's BREATH AT REST. The swamp hums under everything, and the hum has been going for 1,500 years without needing anyone to hear it.
**AESTHETIC:** Don't Starve / Tim Burton hand-drawn gothic — 2D, papercraft-adjacent, dark under the canopy. The Bayou is the only zone where the Inn's dark is DRAWN, not lit: every shadow is an ink line, every tree a pen stroke. Rackham's trees if Rackham had been raised on swamp gas and humidity. The palette: grey-green water, cypress black, moss silver, and one unnatural color — the GREEN FIRE. Del Toro's law at its most biological: the worn asymmetry of a landscape that eats straight lines.
**AUDIO:** The swamp's own orchestra: water moving slowly through reeds, the cypress knees' patient silence, cicadas in the triple-digit humidity, and under it all the murmur. The Root Reader's step. The Loup-garou's howl, once a night, always far, always exactly far enough. And the Moss-folk: tiny voices in the Spanish moss, the zone's whisper layer — the fairy lights that are actually fairies.

The Bayou presents five options. All live, all forever.
**If you find the colonial church and its keeper, go to OPTION 1.**
**If you play the Reclamation, go to OPTION 2.**
**If you open the basement door and descend to the buried cities, go to OPTION 3.**
**If you seek the swamp's people, go to OPTION 4.**
**If you gather — the Bayou Bridge corridor, go to OPTION 5.**
(If you leave, the World Map pin is where you entered — and the swamp does not wave goodbye. The swamp is not rude; the swamp is patient. It was here before you and it will be here after, and somehow that is the friendliest thing about it.)

### OPTION 1 — YOU FIND THE COLONIAL CHURCH AND ITS KEEPER
The colonial structure, 300 years old, being consumed by swamp — the steeple tilting, the cypress growing through the pews, the altar a root-heap. THE KEEPER: THE BOG FAIRY GOLEM — the being the swamp MADE (not a person who came to the swamp; a creature of earth, clay, and animate frequency, grown from the swamp's own materials over 1,500 years). Four feet tall — the height of the cypress knees that break the waterline. Grey-green bog clay, always slightly damp. Spanish moss growing from head and shoulders — the moss IS the hair, and the moss is the golem's frequency catcher. The eyes are BOG WATER: dark, reflective, showing the world, not the golem — the traveler who looks in sees the swamp looking back, and themselves inside it.
THE GOLEM IS FAERIE — the ley line network's swamp expression, the Between (not Seelie, not Unseelie, the third thing). The voice: water moving slowly through reeds — partly vocal, partly tactile, the moss vibrating the words the clay cannot shape. Slow. Biological time.
THE MAKER PLACED IN THE MAKING (the canon truth): the Bog Fairy Golem is Krista's avatar — the maker made of the same materials the maker works with, living in the in-between (not land, not water), the keeper placed in the kept. The player never learns this. The player doesn't need to. The golem is the zone's proof that a place can make its own guardian, and the guardian's whole job is STAYING.
THE BASEMENT DOOR: the hums-at-42Hz-when-touched door (Quest 1: "The Door in the Basement"). The door is the dry threshold; below it, the water returns.
MAMA COUMINE is in the roster at the church's edges (the zone's other resident, per the master audit) — the full block is a pending draft; the dissection flags her rather than inventing her.
- Camera: hand-drawn stillness — the Bayou is the Inn's most painted zone, and the camera knows it (held frames, ink-line compositions, almost no cut); the golem's first appearance in one held wide: a cypress knee that blinks
- Audio: the golem's voice arrives before the golem (moss-vibration under the reeds); the door's hum is the 42Hz made touchable
- Seed: all classes — the golem is everyone's keeper; the Artisan Thief feels the uncanny kinship (a maker made of materials); the Librarian wants to ask the golem 1,500 years of questions and the golem answers three of them, slowly, one per visit

### OPTION 2 — YOU PLAY THE RECLAMATION
The Don't Starve-type survival crafter, the zone's arcade layer: gather (cypress wood, moss, water plants, clay from the bayou), craft, survive the swamp's nights. THE SANITY MECHANIC IS THE LESSON: the swamp disorients — flat terrain, no landmarks, humidity, darkness under the canopy, sounds that arrive without visible sources. Sanity is kept three ways: LIGHT (lanterns of cypress wood and swamp gas — bioluminescent bacteria, the water's own glow), FIRE (the swamp's fires are GREEN — methane from decomposition; the green fire is the swamp's native light source, the only fire in the Inn that burns a color the Inn did not give it), and THE KEEPER'S KEEPING (passive buff, earned in Quest 4: sanity drains 20% slower in ALL zones — the golem's gift travels with the player forever; the swamp's patience is a portable blessing).
COMPLETION: reaching the colonial structure completes the Reclamation — the reward is ANTHRACITE (the highest-grade coal, the transformation stone: biological pressure made geological value — the swamp's whole thesis in one black stone).
- Camera: the survival loop in Del Toro's practical darkness; the green fire in held shots (the only light the player ever fully trusts here, and it is a color the swamp chose)
- Audio: the zone's loudest layer only during nights; the green fire's low hiss carries the murmur
- Seed: the Tinkering Weeaboo is the Reclamation's best player (hyperfocus is preternatural survival sense here); the Bard Blowhard's debuff lessons arrive as swamp-mishaps (the swamp is the best teacher of humility in the Inn, and it does not charge)

### OPTION 3 — YOU OPEN THE BASEMENT DOOR AND DESCEND
"GOING DOWN SLOW" (Quest 2, the golem's instruction — the zone's signature law): "Go down. But go slow. The cities are patient. They can wait for you to take your time." The descent's pacing IS the content: the player who runs falls; the player who walks arrives; the player who stops and looks discovers. THE BAYOU IS THE ANTI-URGENCY ZONE (the ruling gate).
THE BURIED CITIES (Quest 3) — three intact cities preserved beneath the jungle canopy, stacked deeper than the church is tall: THE MOUND CITY (oldest — the Mississippian earthwork mound, the oldest architecture in the Inn, surviving because it is the most adequate structure ever built; the Mound is a 42Hz resonance chamber — the HOLLOW EARTH'S SURFACE EXPRESSION, the same frequency surfacing through the ground; the Mound-keeper: dry earth and dust, older than the golem, hums 42Hz, never speaks); THE RIVER CITY (middle — built along a river that no longer exists; buildings of PETRIFIED WOOD, biological becoming geological through time and water; the petrified boat is the transformation made permanent; the Ferryman — older than Charon, charges a story instead of an obol); THE CIRCLE CITY (deepest — built around a North American stone circle; the Inn's architecture — tree, circle, center, radiating zones — was being built here 1,500 years before the Inn existed; the Stone-singer sings 42Hz into the stone — the bayou's Mona, the mother creation principle in stone instead of wood; the circle connects via ley lines to the Fae Kingdom and the Silk Road's deepest fast-travel node).
THE DESCENT'S COMPANY: the Root Reader — the buried cities' guide, who reads root systems the way the Examiner reads potsherds (the buried city's living library); the sleeping giants — the buried cities sit in the mountains that the jungle grows on, and the mountains are sleeping giants, and the cities are dreams the giants are having (never wake them; the game never lets you try, and the not-letting is the mercy).
TOUCHING each city's central element awakens the 42Hz resonance — the cities wake up, one per touch, the order the player chooses.
QUEST 4 — "WHAT WAS THERE BEFORE": the return to the golem, and the zone's thesis spoken in the swamp's voice: the colonial structure is not the truth — it's the DOOR. The cities are the truth. The circle is the truth. "The Song isn't European. The Song isn't African. The Song isn't Indigenous. The Song isn't anyone's. The Song is: the Song. Different voices. Same frequency. 42Hz. The Adequate."
REWARDS: THE BAYOU STONE (a piece of the Circle City's largest stone, humming the murmur — the quietest 42Hz in the Inn: proof the Song doesn't have to be loud to be present) + the golem's Keeping + the ley-line fast-travel node.
- Camera: the descent in Hitchcock's held suspense (each stair is a beat; the water rises with the player's patience); the cities in Kubrick's one-axis frames (the mound, the circle, the center — the Inn's architecture discovered, not explained)
- Audio: the murmur amplifying by layer — water, earth, stone; the Stone-singer's 42Hz is the zone's only actual SONG, one note, unaccompanied, and the player will remember where they were standing when it started
- Seed: the Librarian completes the cities' readings (the Ferryman's story-fare is this class's natural currency); the Sentinel stands watch at the sleeping giants' ridge and understands the assignment without being told

### OPTION 4 — YOU SEEK THE SWAMP'S PEOPLE
The Bayou's living layer, between the church and the water: THE MOSS-FOLK — the Fae of the Spanish moss, tiny grey-green plant-beings living IN the moss the way the Little People live in the Appalachians; the swamp's whisper layer, the zone's fairy lights (some of the lights ARE lights; some are the Moss-folk carrying lanterns of swamp gas; the trick is never finding out which, and the Moss-folk know the player chooses not to look — and that is the friendship). THE LOUP-GAROU — the Cajun werewolf, the French Louisiana shapeshifter, the swamp's gatekeeper on its outer edge, testing the traveler's sanity with a game of kept-distance (always far, always exactly far enough; the howl is the question and the silence after is the answer, and the player passes by keeping their own counsel). MAMA COUMINE at the roster's edge — the pending block.
- Camera: the Moss-folk never in focus (the zone's softest mystery); the Loup-garou in single held shots across the water (never approached, never closed)
- Audio: tiny voices in the moss at 12dB under the murmur; the howl, once nightly, timed by the swamp's own clock
- Seed: the Chameleon Druid blends with the Spanish moss and the Moss-folk accept the newcomer instantly (the shapeshifter is the zone's most natural resident); the Bard Blowhard tells the Loup-garou a story across the water and earns one extra beat of silence — the finest review of a career

### OPTION 5 — YOU GATHER (THE BAYOU BRIDGE CORRIDOR)
The building system's wetland feed — the corridor the Campgrounds' homesteads draw from (tonight's ruling, wired): CYPRESS (the structural wood — the water's answer to oak), BOG MOSS (the sealant and the whisper-layer), BOG OAK (the dark wood — trees that fell and were kept by the water, time-turning the grain the way the River City turned its buildings), SPANISH MOSS (the thatching and the frequency catcher — gather it respectfully; the Moss-folk move out, and they wave, and the wave is the payment).
THE CORRIDOR'S LAW: gathering in the Bayou is done at the murmur's pace (the building system's 50 BPM heartbeat slows here to match biological time — the swamp teaches the hands as the descent teaches the feet). The homestead's Tier 1 Lean-to (Oak + Reed + Moss) has its reeds here; the Tier 5 Ancestral Hall wants one beam of bog oak, and the bog oak is earned, not taken.
- Camera: the gatherer's hands in macro (the zone's craft shots are its warmest frames — work, done slowly, in a place that approves)
- Audio: the seven gathers' work-song at the murmur's tempo — the slowest crafting music in the Inn
- Seed: the Artisan Thief's gathering is indistinguishable from prayer; the Tinkering Weeaboo's gathering baskets get extremely elaborate extremely fast (the zone's gentlest comedy)

## THE CONVERGENCE — THE MURMUR
However the Bayou is walked, the player leaves with the same additions: a keeper's patience carried as a permanent blessing, a stone that hums the Song's quietest proof, the knowledge that the oldest architecture is a pile of earth that survived by being adequate, and the zone's thesis in the ears: different voices, same frequency. The Song isn't anyone's. The Song is the Song.
The next doors: the ley-line node to the FAE KINGDOM (the Circle City's connection) · the Mound down to the HOLLOW EARTH (the Resonance Engine's surface expression) · the World Map pin back to the MAIN FLOOR — or stay on the church porch with the golem. The moss grows an inch while you sit, and the inch is the swamp's way of saying it noticed.
