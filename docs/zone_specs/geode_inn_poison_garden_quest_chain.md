## WHAT THIS CHAIN IS

The Poison Garden has two live games (#50, the goose-stealth of NOT touching; #57, the Mirror's Seven) and an empty PoisonHerb entity — the mechanics were built first and the meaning has been waiting. This chain is the meaning: the zone where the Inn teaches its most practical truth — THE DOSE MAKES THE POISON — through real toxicology wearing a mythic coat. The garden is not evil. The garden is honest. Nothing here hides what it is; every beautiful lethal thing is labeled, and the whole zone is the Inn's lesson in respect: presence without contact, knowledge without fear, and the discovery that most of the Inn's medicine cabinet grows out of the deadliest beds.

Like the parks chains, the science is the lesson and the story is the delivery: digitalis comes from foxglove (the deadly plant that steadies failing hearts), atropine comes from belladonna (the beautiful killer that is carried in every soldier's antidote kit), aspirin began as willow bark. The line between poison and cure is not the plant. The line is the reader of the dose.

**Giver:** The Apothecary (the garden's keeper — practical, unshocked, measures everything to the drop; speaks the way a scale reads: precise, dry, kind by accuracy. The zone's quest-giver and its still-room master.)
**Access:** Main Floor → World Map → the Poison Garden pin (z10)
**Prerequisite:** None. The garden takes guests with empty hands and empty pockets — both for their own safety.
**Reward spine:** restraint → measurement → the mirror → the harvest → the orchid → the dose.

## THE CHAIN

### Quest 1 — "Do Not Touch"
The gate. The most beautiful garden the player has entered — and the one rule, carved at the threshold: DO NOT TOUCH. Every plant lethal, every plant labeled, none of it hidden. The garden is the only zone honest enough to put its danger in its name. The first quest is the whole zone's mechanic taught as a law: walk the garden's paths without touching anything, and learn that presence without contact is not deprivation — it is the beginning of respect. The beautiful thing you do not touch is not withheld from you. It is simply not for hands.
**Reward:** The restraint (the stealth-restraint pass that gates all the zone's harvest quests — the not-touching IS the skill).

### Quest 2 — "The Foxglove's Heart"
The Apothecary's still room. The lesson is real: foxglove will stop a heart at one dose and steady it at another — digitalis, the failure-medicine grown from a killer's flowerbed. The player learns the drop-scale: an eyedropper, a balance, a book of conversions. The mechanic is honest measurement — the player makes a tincture, drop by drop, and the Apothecary checks the arithmetic with a jeweler's patience. The teaching layer: the plant did not choose to be medicine or murder. The dose chose. The reader chose.
**Reward:** The measure (a permanent precision ability — dose mechanics unlocked for the Inn's alchemy; the staircase's texts become practical here).

### Quest 3 — "The Mirror's Errand"
The garden's center holds the mirror — game #57's mirror, Snow White's honest descendant. It shows the player their own hands WANTING, before the hands move. The quest is seven errands through the garden's beds, each past something more beautiful than the last, and the mirror is not a judge — it is a witness. The player who finishes the seven has watched their own wanting seven times and touched none of it. The eighth look is the reward: the mirror shows the same wanting, and the same hands, resting.
**Reward:** The Mirror's Seven completion counts + Snowflake Obsidian (beauty from rapid cooling — the stone of things that formed too fast to be safe, worn now as proof you slowed down).

### Quest 4 — "Belladonna's Sister"
The harvest quest — the chain's most careful hour. Belladonna, the beautiful killer: the berries that took empires' enemies, the plant that gives atropine — carried in every field soldier's kit as the antidote to nerve agents. The paradox played straight: to harvest the most dangerous plant in the garden, the player performs the full respect ritual — the gloves, the timing, the two-pan balance, the breath. The Apothecary works beside the player the whole time and speaks exactly twice. Once to say "gloves." Once to say "well done."
**Reward:** Belladonna harvest rights + the first PoisonHerb records loaded into the garden's beds (the entity fills with real toxic botany, dose notes on every card — the garden becomes what its mechanics always wanted: a labeled, honest pharmacopoeia).

### Quest 5 — "The Antidote Orchid"
The zone's own stone-plant, game #50's reward: the cure that grows beside the poison. The orchid refuses the safe beds — it grows only within reach of the deadliest plant in the garden, close enough that harvesting the cure means standing nearer the poison than comfort allows. The mechanic is the thesis: you cannot have the antidote while keeping your distance from the toxin. Every cure in the garden has spent its whole life next to what it cures.
**Reward:** The Antidote Orchid (the zone's stone — the gem of proximity: the cure carried in the shape of the thing it survived).

### Quest 6 — "The Last Drop"
The finale plays it straight (the Child-Actor Law's spirit — no wink, no trick). A poisoned critter is carried to the still room — a small thing that ate the wrong berry, accidental, ordinary, dying. On the table: everything the chain has taught — the foxglove's arithmetic, the mirror's honest watching, the harvest's steady hands, and one vial the player has measured themselves. The vial would kill at a careless dose. It cures at a careful one. The Apothecary does not take the vial. The Apothecary says: "You know the arithmetic." The player reads the weight, reads the dose, gives the drop. The critter sleeps. In the morning it is gone — back through the hedge, carrying its second life.
The garden's thesis, said once, plainly: THE DOSE MAKES THE POISON. THE DOSE MAKES THE CURE. THE READER MAKES THE DIFFERENCE.
**Reward:** The Apothecary's signature (a permanent still-room key — the dose mechanics unlock across the Inn; the staircase's alchemy, the meadery's craft, and every future medicine in the world's systems all pass through the arithmetic the player now owns).
