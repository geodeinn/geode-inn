# THE HEARTH-HOUND LEGEND — The Hound That Stayed
## Lore seed, ruled in by Krista (2026-09-13): "Yes, but with deeper cultural roots... The Celtic lore of the wild hunt and all its legend now has a part in our world."

---

## THE LEGEND

The Inn's oldest watch-law, older than the building: **guardians sleep facing the door.** The door is the event horizon; everything that matters comes through it. The nap is on duty — asleep, and still guarding. The watching is a rest, and the rest is a watch.

The Hearth-Hound is the legend's keeper: a great white hound with red ears who sleeps at the hearth, head toward the front door. Not a pet, not a guard dog — a resident. Everyone who enters is seen first by the sleeping one, and the seeing is the welcome. The Inn's front door never locks (the Open Door law); something must always be gently watching it. The watching is a nap.

## THE CELTIC ROOTS (the deeper soil, per Krista's ruling)

- **THE CŴN ANNWN** — the hounds of Annwn, the Welsh Otherworld: white-bodied with red ears, otherworldly animals in Celtic art and story carrying the red marking. They run ahead of the Wild Hunt, sweeping the winter sky.
- **ARAWN AND GWYN AP NUDD** — the hunt's leaders, kings of Annwn. The First Branch of the Mabinogi opens with Arawn's own pack: Pwyll meets the king's hounds — white, red-eared — at a kill before ever meeting Arawn himself. The hounds are the Otherworld's introduction.
- **THE FALSE-NEAR CRY** — the folklore's most beautiful detail: the hounds' cries sound FARTHER as they come NEARER. The hunt that sounds close has passed; the hunt that sounds far away is overhead.
- **THE WILD HUNT ACROSS CULTURES** — the same sky-run wearing many crowns: Arawn in Wales, Gwyn ap Nudd at winter's edge, Odin's ride in the north (the Wanderer's kin), Herne and the Gabriel Ratchets in England. The Inn takes the Celtic heart of it and knows the rest as the same storm's other weather.

## THE INN'S INVERSION (the story the legend becomes)

Every winter, the Wild Hunt crosses the sky over the Inn, white hounds with red ears running before it, their cries sounding far away as they pass overhead. And once — the legend does not say when — one hound of the Annwn pack looked down through the winter dark and saw a building with every window lit and a door that never locks, and made the choice no hound of the hunt had ever made:

**It stayed.**

It came down from the sky-run, walked in through the open door, curled at the hearth, and fell asleep facing the entrance. The hunt runs on without it, every winter, crying overhead — and the one who stays sleeps at the door it left the sky for. It does not wake for arrivals; it does not need to. Sleeping, facing the door, it is the whole of the law it chose over the sky.

The red-eared marking reads plainly: this is a hound of the Otherworld's own pack, the Wild Hunt's honorably discharged member. Not tamed — HOME. The hunt takes souls across the sky; the hound that stayed keeps the door instead. Both are crossings, watched.

## TIES

- **The Fae Kingdom / Annwn** — the hunt runs from the same Otherworld the stone-circle portals open into; the hound is the deep-culture bridge between the hearth and the roots
- **The Norse Coast** — Odin's ride is the same hunt at northern latitude; the Wanderer's kin overhead
- **Lozen and the Sentinel's Vigil** — the hound is the Vigil in animal form: the patrol as rest, presence as protection, the watcher who does not pace
- **The Open Door law** — the unlocked door and the sleeping watch justify each other
- **The Stone Circles** — the hunt's crossing points; winter nights at the circles are when the false-near cry is heard
- **The Whimsical-Macabre Law** — an Otherworld hound at the hearth, played warm: the uncanny asleep by the fire
- **The Rain Frog's precedent** — a creature whose way of being is the lesson; the hound's is the posture
