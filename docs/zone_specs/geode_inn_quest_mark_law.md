# THE QUEST MARK LAW — The Approach Mark and the Glow Register
## Canon (Krista's ruling, 2026-09-13): every quest-giver and game NPC in the Inn wears the approach mark; the GLOW itself tells the player how difficult the game waiting is.

---

## THE LAW

Every game and quest-giver in the Inn wears the approach mark — the WoW-descended, instantly-readable sign that says "there is story here." Approach, talk, and the resident character gives the story hook in their own voice, and then the game carries the player out to the zone where the storyline runs. The quest mark IS the catalog. Talk to the face, not the menu.

**The Glow Register (Krista's addition, same ruling): the type of glow marks how difficult the game waiting is — from a simple candle flicker (gentle) to a spiral of rainbow hues (the deepest asks).**

---

## THE REGISTER

| Tier | The Glow | What It Means |
|------|----------|---------------|
| 1 — THE CANDLE | Plain amber candle flicker — the hearth's own light | The gentle ask. Pick-up-and-play, no wrong way through |
| 2 — THE BREATH | One slow additional hue breathing through the amber (candle-class shift, unhurried) | A focused challenge — one mechanic, one idea, held carefully |
| 3 — THE WEAVE | Two and three hues braided in a slow shift, still sine-clamped, still warm | The committed questline leg — multi-step thinking, real patience |
| 4 — THE PRISM SPIRAL | The full spiral of rainbow hues, turning slowly, the Prism's whole spectrum in miniature | The deepest asks — the legend-tier quests, the zone endgames |

**The scale runs from the hearth to the Prism.** The closer to the candle, the gentler the game; the closer to the full spectrum, the deeper the quest. The Prism (the Inn's sound-to-light transformer, whose colors ARE the Solfeggio spectrum) is the register's far end — every Tier 4 mark is the Prism in miniature, worn on a character's head. The building's deepest mechanic becomes its most readable sign.

---

## THE INVERSION (the law's soul)

The Inn inverts the usual game grammar. Conventional design says red = danger, warning colors = hard content. The Inn says: **the harder the ask, the lovelier the light.** No red, no threat colors, no urgency strobes. Difficulty is not a warning — it is an invitation, honestly labeled. A Tier 4 spiral is beautiful ON PURPOSE: the player should WANT the hard thing, not fear it.

---

## THE LAWS THAT RIDE ALONG

- **Steady Light** — every tier is candle-class: sine-clamped, amber-anchored, no strobe, no blink-rate urgency. The spiral TURNS slowly; it never flashes. Glow, never strobe.
- **No-Fail** — the mark is information, not a gate. A player may attempt any game at any tier regardless of the glow. Difficulty tells you what you're walking into, the way "it hissed at water" tells you what you mixed — honest observation, not a lock.
- **Anti-Skinner** — the mark never pesters. Decline a quest and the NPC quietly takes the mark down; it returns only when there is genuinely something new. The glow never escalates to pressure.
- **The Room Kit Law** — the mark is part of each resident's character kit (a glow state, not a separate UI layer): the Inn's grammar lives on its people, not on its menus.
