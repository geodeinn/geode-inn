## ORIGIN

Born twice in one night (2026-09-12/13):

1. **The Forge-to-Fab Ladder** (the chip conversation): semiconductor fabrication understood as the smith's trade at nanometer scale — a Surface Pro holds ~30 billion transistors; the crystal drawn from the furnace, the wafer sliced like lapidary, the doping like the bronze-age pinch. "Modern day technology is its own alchemy."
2. **The Glass Kingdoms Dream** (Krista's sleep, same night): kingdoms created from impossibly thin layers of glass forming impossibly complex pyramid schematics, duplicating the pattern of the human nervous system, then showing the mirror image of a tree's system — the identical pattern system. The dream is the chapter's eyes. The chip conversation is its hands. This spec is the questline that ties them to the Inn.

## THE THESIS

**Silica (SiO2) is the Inn's most-traveled material — the only stone in the building that finished as a crystal, got interrupted as glass, slept as opal, was struck into a root by lightning, and was taught to think as a chip.**

Five lives of one formula. The Glass Kingdoms are not a new invention — they are what the Inn has been standing on all along, wearing five different costumes already in canon. This questline is where the costumes meet and introduce themselves.

## THE LAWS THAT HOLD

- **Steady Light** — the interference colors of thin sections bloom as slow sine-wave washes, never strobes. The Prism's pulse stays 50 BPM.
- **No-Fail** — wrong answers at any station are observations ("it hissed at water" family).
- **Anti-Skinner** — no streaks, no timers, no decay. The chapter is paced like geology: five visits, unhurried.
- **The Neutrality Law** — the modern chip station is taught as craft history, not commerce; the alchemy framing (the Forge-to-Fab Ladder) keeps it inside the pre-industrial continuum: the same trade, a smaller forge.
- **The Both-Real Law family** — no transformation rendered, no abstraction asserted. The pattern mirror (tree/nerve/chip) is SHOWN, never lectured.
