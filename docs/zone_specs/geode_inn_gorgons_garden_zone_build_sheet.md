# THE GORGON'S GARDEN — Master Zone Build Sheet
## The 84Hz Embassy — Where the Shadow Inn Grows in the Living Inn

---

## WHAT THE GORGON'S GARDEN IS

The Gorgon's Garden is the one zone in the Geode Inn that exists natively at 84Hz — the Shadow frequency. While every other zone must be shifted to 84Hz using the Octave Mirror, the Garden is ALWAYS shadow. It was BUILT at 84Hz. It is the Shadow Inn's embassy in the Living Inn.

Medusa's domain. A poison garden where every plant is lethal and every flower is beautiful. The Garden is the Inn's thesis made botanic: the power of what MAY BE. A poison is not evil. A poison is potential. The same plant that kills can cure — the dose makes the medicine. The Garden doesn't choose. The Garden GROWS.

The Garden is accessed through the Catacombs (Level 6) — a passage in The Deep leads to a stone doorway carved with entwined snakes. The door opens for any player who has earned Medusa's respect (completed any quest in the Catacombs).

---

## FREQUENCY: 84HZ (PERMANENT)

The Garden is the Inn's only native 84Hz zone. This means:

- Everything in the Garden is in its SHADOW state. Plants are crystallized — not frozen, but naturally mineral. The vines are quartz formations. The flowers are geode slices. The trees are petrified wood. The Garden looks like the Shadow Inn, but it was always like this. It didn't shift. It grew this way.
- The 84Hz hum is constant. There is no 42Hz version of the Garden. The Garden has always been the Shadow frequency's home.
- The Gorgon's Garden is a Threshold (as documented in the Shadow Inn spec) — the player can physically cross from the Garden into the Shadow Inn through any stillwater surface in the Garden's central pool.
- The Garden's combat is the Shadow Inn's combat: matching frozen frequencies. Enemies in the Garden are petrified creatures that the player harmonizes at 84Hz.

---

## MEDUSA

The Garden's anchor NPC. Not a villain. Not a monster. A gardener.

Medusa's mythology in the Inn: the original story is one of violation and punishment — Medusa was cursed for being a victim. The Inn reclaims the narrative. Medusa was not cursed. Medusa was GIVEN a gift — the power of preservation. The power to make things still. The power to say "stop" to anything that moves.

Medusa uses this power to maintain the Garden. Every plant in the Garden was once alive — growing, spreading, threatening to overrun the space. Medusa petrified each one at its most beautiful moment. The Garden is a gallery of preserved moments. Each flower is frozen at peak bloom. Each vine is arrested at full extension. Each tree is caught mid-growth.

**Medusa's voice:** Slow. Measured. Neither warm nor cold. Speaks in present tense — everything in the Garden IS, nothing was or will be. Medusa does not use past or future tense because in the Garden, time is still.

**Medusa's thesis:** "The Garden is not death. The Garden is the moment before death — held. The flower at peak bloom lasts one day. I give it forever. Whether forever is a gift or a cage depends on whether you asked for it."

## THE GARDEN'S ARCHITECTURE

### Section 1: The Snake Gate
The entrance. A stone doorway carved with entwined snakes — not threatening, decorative. The snakes form a Celtic knot pattern (connecting to the Inn's ambient texture). The door is warm to the touch — 84Hz hum starts here. Two stone snakesEN guard the gate. They don't attack. They watch. Their eyes follow the player. If the player has earned access, the snakes part. If not, the snakes are a wall.

### Section 2: The Petrified Orchard
The first area. Rows of petrified trees — each one a different species, each one frozen at a different stage of growth. Some are flowering. Some are fruiting. Some are bare. The orchard is organized not by species but by SEASON — each row is a different moment of the year, all held simultaneously. A spring tree next to a winter tree. The orchard is calendar made arboreal.

**Interactive elements:**
- The player can attempt to touch a tree. The tree is stone. Touching it gives the controller a single haptic pulse — the tree's last living frequency, captured at the moment of petrification. Different trees vibrate at different frequencies (connects to the 164-stone database: each tree's wood type correlates to a stone frequency).
- The player can attempt to use the Octave Mirror here. Nothing happens — the Garden is already at 84Hz. The mirror has no effect. Medusa says: "You can't shadow what was never in the light."

### Section 3: The Poison Borders
The garden beds. Rows of petrified plants — each one a real poisonous species, accurately depicted:

- **Monkshood (Wolfsbane)** — the most toxic plant in the garden. Blue flowers, frozen mid-bloom. A single touch in life could stop a heart. In the Garden, it's stone. The threat is preserved, not active.
- **Foxglove** — the source of digitalis. Beautiful bell-shaped flowers. The line between medicine and poison is the dose. The Foxglove in the Garden has a small plaque (carved, not printed — Doré engraving style): "A medicine. A poison. You decide."
- **Nightshade (Belladonna)** — the namesake of Wednesday (the In-Between NPC). Belladonna grows in the Garden and in the In-Between. It's the only plant that exists in two frequencies.
- **Hemlock** — the plant that killed Socrates. The Hemlock in the Garden leans slightly toward the Snake Gate, as if trying to leave. Medusa lets it lean. "Everything here wants to grow. That's the problem with preservation — the thing preserved still has its original intention."
- **Yew** — the longest-lived tree in Europe. Every part toxic except the berry. The Yew in the Garden is the oldest petrified tree — it was already ancient when Medusa preserved it. Its wood is dark red. Its frequency is the lowest in the Garden (subsonic).

**The Poison Borders' interactive mechanic:**
The player can examine each plant with the crystal lens (same as in the Catacombs). The lens reveals: the plant's real-world properties (toxic compounds, historical uses, cultural significance) AND the plant's frequency at the moment of petrification. Some plants have a frequency that matches a stone in the player's pouch — these plants resonate when the matching stone is nearby. The resonance is a puzzle mechanic: certain paths in the Garden only open when the right stone is near the right plant.

### Section 4: The Lithophone
The Garden's musical instrument. A lithophone is a stone xylophone — bars of resonant stone that produce musical tones when struck. Medusa's Lithophone is made of petrified wood bars, each one from a different tree species, each one producing a unique tone.

**The Lithophone's function:**
- The player can play the Lithophone. Each bar produces a tone based on the tree's petrified wood density (harder wood = higher pitch). The tones span the Inn's frequency range: 42Hz (softest wood) to 84Hz (hardest stone).
- Playing specific melodies unlocks paths in the Garden. The melodies are not written anywhere — the player must EXPERIMENT. The Garden rewards exploration, not instruction.
- The Lithophone connects to the Inn's audio architecture: each bar's tone is processed through the IEM system. The Lithophone is the only instrument in the Inn made of PRESERVED material — the sound it produces is the sound of a living tree transformed into a musical stone.

### Section 5: The Mirror Pool (Central)
The Garden's center. A still pool of water — the only liquid in the Garden. The water is perfectly still, perfectly clear, perfectly cold. The surface is a mirror.

**The Mirror Pool's function:**
- The pool is a Threshold to the Shadow Inn (as documented in the Shadow Inn spec). The player who steps into the pool crosses into the Shadow Inn's version of the Garden — which is IDENTICAL, because the Garden is already 84Hz. The pool is the one Threshold where the Living and Shadow versions are the same room.
- The pool shows reflections of rooms the player has already visited — but the reflections are from 84Hz. The player sees the Shadow Inn from the Garden's perspective. This is the Garden's gift: a safe vantage point to observe the Shadow Inn without entering it.
- The pool contains the Antidote Orchid (the Poison Garden arcade game's reward). The Orchid grows in the center of the pool, on a small stone island. The Orchid is the Garden's paradox: the cure that grows in the place of poison. The player can take the Orchid — but the Orchid is the only LIVING thing in the Garden, and taking it means the Garden has no cure anymore.

### Section 6: The Gorgon's Gaze
The Garden's deepest point. Medusa's station. A stone bench beside the Mirror Pool. Medusa sits here. Around the bench: statues. Not plants — PEOPLE. Previous visitors who looked at Medusa directly. They were not attacked. They made a choice: to look. Looking at Medusa preserves you. The statues are the Garden's archive — a record of every visitor who chose to see.

**The Gorgon's Gaze mechanic:**
- The player can CHOOSE to look at Medusa. If the player does, the screen goes white — then returns. The player's avatar is stone. The player is now a statue in the Garden.
- This is not death. This is PRESERVATION. The player's progress, inventory, and stones are all preserved. The player can observe the Garden from the statue's perspective — a fixed position, unable to move, watching the Garden's stillness.
- The player can release themselves at any time by using the Octave Mirror (which doesn't shift the Garden's frequency but does reverse the Gaze's effect on the player specifically). The Stone Circle Chamber's Prism shard can also reverse it.
- WHY would a player do this? Because being a statue in the Garden is a perspective. The Garden looks different from a fixed position — the player sees patterns in the petrified orchard, alignments in the Poison Borders, reflections in the Mirror Pool that aren't visible from a moving perspective. The Gaze is a puzzle tool: some secrets in the Garden are only visible to someone standing still.
- The statues of previous visitors each have a small plaque (carved, Doré style) with their names and a one-line epitaph. The epitaphs are the players' own — the game records what the player was thinking when they chose to look. If the player typed nothing, the epitaph reads: "They looked."

## ORIGIN

The Gorgon's Garden was first seeded as an arcade game concept (tier2_poison_garden.md — "Untitled Goose Game reversed, you are the garden"). The zone design was developed September 2, 2026 as an independent component, drawing from the Shadow Inn's 84Hz system (the Garden is the native 84Hz zone), Medusa's mythology (reclaimed as preservation, not punishment), and the Inn's thesis about potential and choice. The Garden's question is: is forever a gift or a cage? The Garden doesn't answer. The Garden grows. Even at 84Hz, the Garden grows.
