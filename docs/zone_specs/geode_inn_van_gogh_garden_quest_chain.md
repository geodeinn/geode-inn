## WHAT THIS CHAIN IS

The Garden's aesthetic bible already exists in the recovered library (library/zones/Geode_Inn_—_Garden_Aesthetic_(Van_Gogh).txt — the principle, the two gardens, the singing vines, the Story-Born, the sunflowers). What was missing is the chain: the questline a player walks through both gardens. This is that chain — the zone where the growing is VISIBLE, where attention is water, where stories planted in soil become beings, and where the Inn's most beloved art history is held the way the garden holds everything: gently, at the exact level that heals.

The science is the lesson (the parks law, continued): young sunflowers track the sun on a real circadian rhythm, companion planting is real horticulture, olive trees really do take decades to fruit, and a real time-lapse of any vine proves the swirl isn't metaphor — plants MOVE. Van Gogh saw it a century before cameras could show it to everyone else. The garden is his vindication, grown green.

**Giver:** The Catalyst (red — fire, will, performance; the wild garden's grower, the bar's botanist). The Gardener (Sorrel, green — patience, knowledge, hands) takes over at the tended field.
**Access:** The Catalyst's garden: behind the bar, the impossible door (the garden that shouldn't fit in the Inn's architecture and does, because the Catalyst grew it there). The Gardener's garden: Vanaheim.
**Prerequisite:** None. The garden takes guests the way gardens take sun — everyone, evenly.
**Reward spine:** the attention → the sunflowers' why → the seeds → the story → the plot of one's own.

## THE CHAIN

### Quest 1 — "The Garden Behind the Bar"
The impossible door behind the bar opens into the wild garden — Saint-Rémy in living matter: impasto leaves thick enough to feel, complementary colors vibrating at the edge of too much and never past it, every vine spiraling in the Starry Night swirl. The first lesson is a noticing, not a task: the vibration in the eyes IS the Song — the 42Hz heard by the ear everywhere else in the Inn is SEEN here. The player stands in the most visually alive space in the world and understands the garden doesn't look like a Van Gogh. The garden IS one. Rendered in sap instead of oil.
**Reward:** The seeing (the garden's color pairs now read as frequency — the player's eyes are tuned).

### Quest 2 — "Attention Is Water"
The singing vines. The Catalyst's law, taught as a mechanic: talking is attention, attention is water, water is growth. The player stands before a dormant vine and pays attention — no timer, no prompt, no minigame. The act of watching IS the play. And the vine answers: it swells, it spirals, it SINGS — the tendrils curling in the Van Gogh swirl, the sound made visible in motion. A vine talked to daily for three visits blooms; a vine ignored stays dormant. Nothing withers. Nothing is punished. The garden just waits, the way gardens wait.
**Reward:** The attention (a permanent mechanic: the player's standing-presence now grows things — the dormant beds across the Inn respond to a guest who lingers).

### Quest 3 — "The Flower of Hoping Someone Stays"
The sunflowers. The science first, played straight: the young blooms track the sun across the sky on a real circadian clock, turning east again overnight to be waiting at dawn — heliotropism, the botany lesson delivered as wonder. Then the why, delivered once and never repeated: the Catalyst grows them because they follow the sun, because they are dramatic, and because they are the flower painted for a friend's bedroom, to welcome someone hoped would stay. The Catalyst grows them for the Inn. The Inn is the friend the Catalyst is hoping will stay. The player carries that sentence out of the garden and the game never says it out loud again. The knowing is: the player's now.
**Reward:** A sunflower head, heavy with seed (the first entry in the seed pouch — see Quest 4).

### Quest 4 — "The Seed-Saver"
The Gardener's garden in Vanaheim — Auvers in rows: the wildness held in structure, alive differently, the energy pressing outward and the structure strong enough to hold it. Sorrel teaches the companion rows (roses and herbs, pomegranate and sage — the real horticulture of plants that protect each other), the olive trees that take decades to fruit ("the waiting is the growing"), and the season's law: the garden does not avoid the ending. The garden INCLUDES the ending — the wheat harvested, the crows arriving, the season closing. And the seeds saved. The player walks the harvest, meets the crows (who are not villains — they are the season's punctual guests), and saves seed from what ended. The saving is the carrying. The carrying is the kindness.
**Reward:** The seed pouch (persists across reincarnations, the pit-house precedent gone green — the player's carried seeds plant themselves wherever the player settles, and the ruins of old lives bloom).

### Quest 5 — "The Story-Born"
The garden's deepest magic, introduced by its children: the four Story-Born Beings — the Wayfarer moving in the swirl, the Hearthkeeper sitting in the bedroom garden, the Mourner standing at the wheat field's edge, the Laughing One dancing among the sunflowers. Each one is a Van Gogh painting of an emotion, grown thick with the story that birthed it. Then the Catalyst gives the player the plot: tell the garden one true story, and something grows from it. The player chooses a moment of their own playthrough (the game pulls from EchoMemory — the real choices, the real kindnesses, the real weather of the player's days) and tells it aloud in the green. The earth listens the way the vines listen. Something small and vivid and thick with impasto grows — the fifth Story-Born, the only one in the Inn grown from a guest's own story, different for every player who tells one.
**Reward:** The fifth Story-Born (a unique companion grown from the player's own true story — it follows no script, because it IS the script the player wrote).

### Quest 6 — "The Golden Hour"
The finale plays the Saint-Rémy law gently and straight: golden hour in the Catalyst's garden, yellow against purple, the whole garden swirling, and the player's own energy met — matched, held, calibrated at exactly the level that heals. This is the garden's inheritance from the painter who saw the growing: the garden that holds a mind the world could not hold, not by fixing it, not by quieting it, but by matching it — the vibration attuning to the visitor's own weather, the therapy being the attunement, the garden being the friend that stays. The Catalyst's sunflowers all face the player at once — not the sun, the player — one rotation, one held breath, then back to their tracking. The golden hour passes. The seeds are saved. The season includes its ending. And the plot waits.
**Reward:** THE PLOT OF ONE'S OWN — a permanent garden bed beside the two great gardens: the wild and the tended and now the player's, whatever it becomes. The player plants the pouch. What grows across visits is the player's own growing, made visible.

## TONE LAW
The garden holds its history the way it holds everything: gently. The Saint-Rémy inheritance — the garden as the place a mind was held by growing things — is played with Gaiman's warmth and none of the world's hungry grief for the painter's ending. The botany carries Weir's precision (heliotropism, companion planting, the decades-long olive); the quiet magic carries Rothfuss; the golden hour is Bradbury's, always. The sunflowers' why is said once and then trusted to the player. The most alive space in the Inn is also its kindest — and that is not a coincidence. That is the design.
