# THE SKY-STORY RUN: ANANSI'S FOUR IMPOSSIBLE THINGS
## Drafted 2026-09-16, following Krista's ruling in session — awaiting the Witan's gates
## Home: the Arcade, by way of Nyame's sky court (the tales are Ashanti; the spider is canon cast — original sixty, chibi trickster class)

---

## THE CORE IDEA (Krista's, gathered across the session)

A classic-arcade Anansi platformer where the player never fights anything — they out-clever it. The mechanic DNA named by Krista: catching, dodging, jumping, and the Star World part of Mario World. The design thesis: the sky god's price IS the level structure. Nyame said: bring me the hornets, the python, the leopard, and the fairy. Four levels. Four impossible things. Four different arcade engines, one spider.

## WHY IT'S TRUE (the honest-label foundation)

This is the real tale, cited and dated: the Ashanti story of how stories were bought. Anansi wanted Nyame's sky-stories for the world; Nyame named a price of four impossible things — the hornets, the python, the leopard, the fairy — and Anansi paid it with nothing but cleverness, catching every one. The prize at the end of the purchase was the stories themselves. The game does not decorate this tale; it IS this tale, played. (The Gaiman connection, kept honest: he cast the spider; he did not write the spider. The character amused audiences for centuries before the casting — the game credits the Ashanti canon, not the adaptation.)

## THE FOUR IMPOSSIBLE THINGS (the levels — one arcade engine each)

**1. THE HORNETS' RAIN (catching — Kaboom DNA).** Anansi holds up a gourd and tells the hornets it is raining: would they like shelter? The level is a stream of hornets pouring across the sky; the player carries the gourd beneath them, mouth open, catching the swarm on the pretense. Every hornet saved by the "rain" climbs in on its own — the trick is kinder than it looks. Full gourd: plug the hole. The catching is the con.

**2. THE PYTHON'S POLE (timing puzzle — platformer tempo).** Nothing in this level attacks. The python is merely long, and Anansi is merely flattering: "surely you are longer than this pole — stretch and be measured." The player baits the stretch, times the pole placement, and makes the tie. It is a rhythm-and-position puzzle — the enemy is talked into the trap, never beaten into it. The best runs end with the python still mid-boast.

**3. THE LEOPARD'S PIT (digging — Lode Runner DNA).** The fastest thing in the forest, dropped into a hole it did not believe in. The player digs and covers: the level's floor is a puzzle of plausible paths, each one a pit wearing a carpet of leaves. The leopard never stops being magnificent — the pit just disagrees with it. Honest failure holds: a missed cover is the leopard strolling past, amused, unimpressed by your pit. Try again. Better pit this time.

**4. THE FAIRY AND THE GUM-BABY (the inversion — Arkanoid, run in reverse).** THE FAIRY IS THE BALL. She ricochets around the level like she owns it — faster and angrier with every wall, because she is a fairy and ricochet is her natural state. The player's paddle is the gum-baby: the one surface that holds whatever touches it. Each deflection is another touch of the sticky doll — first hit costs her a hand (she keeps bouncing with the other), then the other hand, then the feet. Four clean deflections and she hangs mid-tantrum, stuck to the doll by her own curiosity. The level's bricks are the cover between player and fairy — branches and gourds she rattles through, opening the board as she goes. Catchables drift down for the sticky paddle to collect (gum, palm oil, patience). The boss is never killed; she is annoyed into custody. And a missed deflection is not a fail state — the fairy mocks you on the way past, laughing. She is a fairy. Being missed is her favorite thing. The heckler is built into the honest failure.

## THE WEB BETWEEN (Star World connective tissue)

The levels do not touch each other — they are strung together by web-lines the player bounces along, Mappy-trampoline style, through a floating sky-court architecture that never once asks for gravity's permission. This is the Star World register Krista named: bright, weightless, secret-riddled. SECRET EXITS ARE A CORE MECHANIC, NOT A BONUS: every stretch of web holds the clever route — the hidden door that isn't a door until you trick it. The player who looks finds the better path; the player who doesn't still arrives. The trickster's road is always optional, always worth it.

## THE COLLECTIBLES (stories, not coins)

Nothing in this game is money. The collectibles are STORIES — small warm motes gathered along the webs, each one a tale-snippet from the wider Ashanti canon. The counter is not a score; it is a told-list. And the Anti-Skinner law holds absolutely: the four tales are finite, told warm, and the streak is never measured. The game cannot be ground; it can only be finished, then retold.

## THE ENDING (the gift is the win)

Clear all four impossible things and Nyame hands down the box of stories — the actual ending of the actual tale, which is the whole point: Anansi bought the stories for the world, and the player just walked the purchase themselves. The stories collected become theirs, kept on the spider's shelf in the Arcade. Completion rewards nothing but the knowing and the keeping — which is, as always at this Inn, the entire economy.

## THE LAWS THAT HOLD

- NO-FAIL: no death, no lives, no game over. A failed trick is a funny story, told by the level, immediately.
- HONEST FAILURE: every miss is information. The hornets scatter, the python laughs, the leopard strolls, the fairy heckles — none of it hurts, all of it teaches the trick.
- STEADY LIGHT: amber sine glow throughout; the sky court breathes at candle-class. No strobes, no flash on capture — the gourd plug and the fairy's final stick are gentle amber blooms.
- ANTI-SKINNER: no timers, no score decay, no streaks, no daily anything. Four tales, forever kept.
- THE REC LAW: one Anansi class master (already canon cast — the chibi trickster of the original sixty); level props and critters follow the one-class-master route. No new paint line for recolors.
- THE QUEST MARK LAW: Anansi's spider mark carries players out to the game — talk to the face, not the menu. Glow tier: 2 (one-hue breath) — the ask is a four-level romp, gentle and sure.
- THE BACKGROUND LAW: the game's background is its home ENV master, one paint, many games — the sky-village ENV row is a new kit gate (below).

## THE SOUND (per the Arcade Music Profile master spec)

Base track: the drum circle — West African percussion registers, the Inn's own drum-circle canon brought to the arcade. Signature sound: the web-bounce (a plucked-string "twang-bloom" pitched to Anansi's own tone). The four levels each take a different lead instrument over the same base track (the water-monster law, borrowed): hornets take the high flute, python takes the talking drum, leopard takes the low woodblock, the fairy takes a bright kalimba run that gets faster every deflection. Heartbeat holds at 50 BPM throughout — the sky court is calm even when the fairy is furious.

## THE FRAME — NYAME'S DIALOGUE (Krista's ruling, 2026-09-16: the sky god appears as the NPC frame-storyteller)

Nyame bookends all four levels from the sky court — not the aloof god of the myths-as-taught, but the register the tale actually supports: a sky father who set a high price because he wanted the stories FREE, and was quietly hoping all along that somebody would pay it. Vast, warm, unhurried. The voice is the drum circle's deep head carried into speech.

**THE OPENING (first approach, the price):**
"Little weaver. You climb to my court asking for the box.
Hear me, then, and hear me once: the stories were never hidden because they are cheap. They were kept because the keeping was easy and the giving was not.
I will not give them away. But I will sell them — and the price is four things that cannot be caught.
Bring me the hornets, who sting the sky. Bring me the python, who is longer than patience. Bring me the leopard, who is faster than fear. And bring me the fairy, who is angrier than all three together.
Pay it, weaver, and the stories belong to the world. Fail, and the webs stay strung — come back when you're cleverer. The court keeps no ledgers."

**AFTER THE HORNETS' RAIN:**
"The hornets sleep in your gourd, thanking you for rain that never fell. That is the trick, little weaver — cleverness worn as a kindness. Three remain."

**AFTER THE PYTHON'S POLE:**
"You measured a lie against a snake, and the snake chose to believe you. He is still boasting, you know. Mid-air. Two remain."

**AFTER THE LEOPARD'S PIT:**
"The fastest thing in the forest sits in a hole, wondering where the ground went. You dig well, weaver. One remains — and she bites."

**AFTER THE FAIRY AND THE GUM-BABY:**
"She hangs there, mid-tantrum, and she will be furious for a hundred years. It is the happiest I have ever seen her.
Come up, then. The court is open. You have paid."

**THE HANDOVER (the ending):**
"This box was never mine to keep — only mine to price. I set the price high so the world would know what these stories cost.
Take them down, weaver. Tell them warm. Tell them crooked if you must, but tell them.
From tonight, whoever tells a story owes a little of the telling to a spider — and the spider owes it to the world. That is the whole law of the box. Go on, now. It's yours."

**THE RETURN (replay, Anti-Skinner-compliant):**
"Back again? The webs missed your weight. Rain, pole, pit, doll — whichever you like, whenever you like. The tales don't wear out. That's how you know they're true."

**THE DECLINE (if the player walks away mid-frame):**
"Grand." — no, that is not Nyame's line; that is the leprechaun's, and he is not invited into the frame. Nyame simply: "The webs stay strung. The court keeps no ledgers. Come back when the sky amuses you."

**PRODUCTION NOTE:** Nyame is a Full Presence-class frame NPC (no roaming); one class master, the sky-court throne pose plus the handover gesture (the box lowered on an amber sine bloom — Steady Light law holds; the god never flashes). The dialogue is written to be read warm over the drum-circle base track, 50 BPM throughout — the price is spoken like a lullaby, because it was always meant to be paid by someone small.
