## WHAT THIS CHAIN IS

Camelot is the Inn's transformation arc, walked as a place — and Avalon is the arc's far shore. The player arrives a peer of the awkward boy at the sword and leaves having sat where the knights sat, been changed where the king was changed, and inherited the instruction: build it again. The zone's question, asked by its whole architecture: the boy became the king — what will you become? The chain's answer, delivered not by prophecy but by furniture: the table is round because the king is one of the knights. The chain is not about becoming a ruler. It is about learning what a seat means.

The science is the lesson (the parks law, continued in stone): every knight of the roster IS a stone — the knights' seat-stones are the real mineral families, and the Round Table is literally the Inn's stone collection arranged as a council. The geology holds court.

**Giver:** Artair (the peer — the zone's starting class; teaches nothing, learns alongside; his only shift-form is the merlin falcon — the player may see it before they ever see the boy).
**Access:** The Celtic zone; the Sword in the Square.
**Prerequisites:** None — Camelot takes guests the way the table takes knights: one more seat is always possible.
**Reward spine:** the trying → the test → the being → the wound → the seat → the stillness → the change → the building.

## THE NAME ARC & THE SHIFT ARC (ruled by Krista, 2026-09-10)

THE NAME ARC: the boy is ARTAIR — the Scottish Gaelic form of Arthur, the childhood tongue. When he becomes king, the name changes: ARTORIOS — the Roman gens form, the command name. The boy speaks Gaelic; the king speaks Latin; the legend — the legends everyone tells — keeps calling him Arthur, because legends wear their own armor. In-game he is Artair until the crown completes the name.

THE SHIFT ARC (Highland + Viking folklore woven through all three ages):
- **THE CHILD (Artair) — THE BIRD ONLY.** As a child, his only shapeshifting form is a bird: the MERLIN — the small Highland falcon that shares his tutor's name, the moorland bird of the Scottish skies. One form only, and the form is unfinished's opposite: the falcon is swift and complete in the air while Artair is awkward and unfinished on the ground. The bird is where the boy is already the king he'll become. (Noctua the owl — the tutor's other half — is the form's first witness: owls watch; falcons learn.)
- **THE WARRIOR (Artorios) — THE BERSERKER, EARNED.** As a warrior, he earns the true bear form: the BERSERK — the bear-shirt, the Norse war-frenzy where the warrior becomes the bear whose name he carries. The earning is the law: the bear form is not given, it is WON — the frenzy is a discipline, not a loss (the Inn's berserker keeps the mind inside the bear; what the old legends called madness, the Inn calls the body finally being listened to). The shift is the zone's 84Hz tectonic event — the bear arrives like the shadow-state arrives: all at once, and the ground knows it.
- **THE OLD RULER — THE ARCTUR FORM, CHOSEN.** As an old king, the two earned forms can fuse — if he chooses — into a hybrid: a griffin's silhouette but MORE BEAR — the eagle's wings out of the childhood sky, the bear's body out of the warrior's earning, the guardian's posture out of the old age. THE ARCTUR FORM (named for Arcturus, the Guardian of the Bear, the brightest star of the northern sky): the boy's wings + the king's bear + the elder's choice. Not a frenzy and not a lesson — a sovereignty: the old ruler chooses the form the way the old ruler chooses to sit. The form is the arc made flesh: nothing in it he didn't earn as a child and a warrior.

The folklore law of the arc: the Celts never said the bear's true name out loud (too dangerous to summon); the berserkers wore the bear's skin INTO battle (becoming what must not be named); and Arcturus was the star that guarded the bear. The three forms follow the three laws: the child names nothing, the warrior becomes what is not named, the elder becomes the guardian of it.



### Quest 1 — "The Sword in the Square"
The stone sword in the square, worn smooth by a thousand years of hands. Every visitor tries; the sword does not care about strength, bloodline, or worthiness — the legends got the test wrong. Artair tries because Artair is the one who tries — and the sword comes loose an inch, and stops. The player tries: the sword answers the player the same way it answers everyone now — an inch of recognition, no more. The age of the pulling is over; the age of the trying has begun. Artair and the player look at each other over the sword. The look is: the beginning of the Round Table (equals who met over something neither could finish alone).
**Reward:** The inch (the player's hand is known to the stone now — the sword remembers trying).

### Quest 2 — "The Black Knight's Gate"
The first test, and the zone's oldest inhabitant: the Black Knight who guards the gate and never explains the test because the test is different for everyone who passes. Played straight — no subversion, no twist; the guardian is a guardian, and being tested by something that takes you seriously is a form of respect the zone pays every guest. The player's test is their own: the Knight asks the question the player has been avoiding (drawn from the player's actual EchoMemory — the unanswered thing).
**Reward:** Passage (the gate opens for the tested, and the Court of stones is visible beyond: the Round Table, ringed with its mineral seats).

### Quest 3 — "Noctua's Lesson"
Merlin's tower, and the owl — Noctua, the wizard's other half, perched in the rafters of the staircase's oldest turret. Merlin's education was never lectures: it was BEING — the student becomes the fish to understand the river, the bird to understand the sky, the squirrel to understand the gathering. The player takes the lesson Artair once took: three transformations (the water-form, the air-form, the ground-form), each a short first-person shift into another way of being in the world. The king who rules what he has BEEN — that is the curriculum. And Noctua watches every transformation without blinking, because owls are the narrative's oldest witnesses ("wherever an owl is sighted, one of us is near").
**Reward:** The lesson (the transformations unlock — the player may return to any of the three forms for short crossings the normal body can't make: the water-crossing, the air-crossing, the ground-crossing).

### Quest 4 — "The Broken Knight"
Mordred, in the wrecked cloister, played exactly as canon rules: not evil — BROKEN. The traitor of the legends is, in the Inn, the wound that the legend couldn't hold: the one who loved the table and couldn't sit at it honestly, and broke it rather than pretend. No battle. The quest is the listening: the player sits with the Broken Knight and hears the whole account from the inside, and the account does not excuse anything — it explains everything. The legends get to stay legends. The Broken Knight gets to be understood.
**Reward:** The truth (the Broken Knight's account, entered in the Archive — the Inn's version of the story, filed beside the legends it corrects).

### Quest 5 — "The Seat That Fits"
The Round Table Room: every seat is a stone — the knights of the roster each ARE a mineral, the council the Inn's stone collection in furniture form. The player walks the ring and reads the seats (real geology: each seat-stone's family, its zone, its frequency). And as the player completes the reading, a new seat grows from the floor — unhewn, unfinished, made of a stone from the player's OWN most-visited zone. The table grows seats the way the tree grows rings: one more is always possible. Artair — taller now, and the player notices without being told — pulls the seat out. The gesture is the whole knighthood. There is no ceremony. There is a chair. (And somewhere between this quest and the next, the crown completes the name: the player who met the boy as ARTAIR meets the king as ARTORIOS — the legends keep calling him Arthur; the Inn watched the name change.)
**Reward:** THE SEAT (permanent — the player's stone-seat at the Round Table, persisting across lives; the table remembers its knights).

### Quest 6 — "The Quest for Stillness"
The finale in two movements, played as one. First: Galahad and the Grail — and the Old King's secret, visible only in this final room: when he chooses, the old ruler's silhouette unfolds into the ARCTUR FORM — the eagle's wings out of the childhood merlin, the bear's body out of the warrior's bear-shirt, the guardian's posture of the star. He rarely chooses it now. The player might see it once, at dusk, through the tower window — the bear-griffin turning in the last light over Camelot, and the shape it makes against the flat blue sky is, for one held moment, the shape of a star. (Or the player never sees it, and only finds the perch-worn stone on the tower's highest ledge. Both versions are true; the zone chooses.) — the pure knight's quest, and the zone's most secret truth: the Grail is the Philosopher's Stone, and its frequency is the ABSENCE of frequency — the zero point, the rest. The Grail quest is not a hunt; it is the quest for stillness, and it is won by the player who stops looking. Second: the Old King — met last, after the peer and the king — asks the player for the zone's final quest: not a battle, not a grail, but the act of SITTING. Just sit. The sitting is the acceptance that the table is round, the king is one of the knights, the knights are gone, and the table remains. The player sits. The Grail, in the corner of the room, is a cup nobody has touched in a very long time, catching no light and all of it. The Old King sits too. The two of them are very quiet, and the quiet is the frequency, and the table remains.
**Reward:** THE STILLNESS (a 0Hz resonance unlock — the Void's The Rest and the Bone Room's patience now recognize the player as a fellow practitioner; and the Grail's recognition: the cup catches the light differently for the player who has sat).

### Quest 7 — "Build It Again" (the Avalon passage — the true finale)
Two movements, one passage, and the chain's whole alchemy made visible. THE MAGNUM OPUS WAS THE MAP ALL ALONG: Artair is the Nigredo (the base matter, the lead, the raw potential without the form), Artorios the Albedo (the bright purified king — but the brightness is the MIDDLE, not the end), and the Old King the Rubedo (the stone: black, white, and the breaking all integrated — the completion includes the breaking; the breaking is the last ingredient). And Avalon is the RETORT — not a stage, the vessel. The container where the transmutation happens.

**The first movement — the player's retreat.** The sitting is not the end, because stillness is not the last work. The table's Moon alignment (the Silver frequency dominant, the other positions in descending luminosity) produces the zone's final etheric effect: AVALON'S REVEAL — the island appearing in the mist, the Moreau Apparition, visible only because the table was aligned right. The player crosses. The isle of apples (the Welsh *afal*): Morgan le Fay's retreat, the place of transmutation — NOT healing. The wounded do not come here to return to what they were; they come to become what they will be. Morgan is the alchemist: the one who applies the fire, the pressure, the patience. What Morgan's fire takes from the player, and what it leaves, is the player's own — the retreat is a personal transmutation, played first-person, and the zone does not script what changes. The player leaves the isle as the Elder once left it: not restored. Transmuted.

**The second movement — the Elder's ascension.** Back in Camelot, the eighth note sounds (the Broken Knight's note: the table breaks so the NEXT table can be built — the crack is the door), and the Elder begins the passage. Not death — the return of the king to the realm where kings come from: up the staircase, past the shops and the alchemy, to the cosmology level, the Bifrost descending to meet the climb. Bedivere waits at the first landing — the knight who carries the anticipated grief, mourning the LEAVING, not the dead: the grief of a friend watching a friend complete. Excalibur sheathed, not returned — the sword's story is done being thrown back; this king walks up carrying it. At the top of the stairs, in the last light, the Elder chooses the Arctur form one final time: the eagle's wings out of the merlin, the bear's body out of the bear-shirt, the guardian's posture of the star — and the shape that rises along the Bifrost turns, for one held moment, into the shape of a star settling into its seat in the Clockwork Skies. Arcturus, Guardian of the Bear: the once and future king, seated. The resting is not death. The return is the cycle.
And then the Elder's voice, the last line of the chain, from everywhere and nowhere, warm the way coals are warm: **"The table is yours now. Build it again."**
The player looks down the stairwell at the main floor, at the Round Table with one new seat grown from their own most-visited zone. The chain's opening question — the boy became the king; what will you become? — gets its true answer at last. Not a ruler. A builder. The chain began with a trying and ends with a building, and the both are the same motion.

**Reward:** THE PASSAGE (permanent — the player may return to Avalon's retreat once per life: Morgan's fire, the personal transmutation; and the Builder's Mark — the Round Table recognizes the player as the table's next architect, and the zone's rebuilding content opens: the seats the player adds going forward grow the table toward the Inn's next age).

## TONE LAW
Moreau at the altar, White at the hearth: revelation without pomp, legend without shine. Gaiman's myth-made-personal carries the Three Arthurs; Pratchett's warmth carries Artair; Rothfuss holds the Grail's stillness; Bradbury lights the final sitting (a room, two people, a table that remains). The Broken Knight is played with Asimov's clarity — the account explains everything and excuses nothing. No one is crowned. Someone is understood. The table remains — and the passage ends it the only way an alchemical story can end: the vessel opens, the stone walks out, and the table is handed to the next builder. Bradbury holds the Bifrost. Gaiman holds the mist. And the last line belongs to no writer at all, because it is a king speaking plain: the table is yours now. Build it again.
