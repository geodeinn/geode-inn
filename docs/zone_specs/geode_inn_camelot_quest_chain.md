## WHAT THIS CHAIN IS

Camelot is the Inn's transformation arc, walked as a place. The player arrives a peer of the awkward boy at the sword and leaves having sat where the knights sat. The zone's question, asked by its whole architecture: the boy became the king — what will you become? The chain's answer, delivered not by prophecy but by furniture: the table is round because the king is one of the knights. The chain is not about becoming a ruler. It is about learning what a seat means.

The science is the lesson (the parks law, continued in stone): every knight of the roster IS a stone — the knights' seat-stones are the real mineral families, and the Round Table is literally the Inn's stone collection arranged as a council. The geology holds court.

**Giver:** The Wart (the peer — the zone's starting class; teaches nothing, learns alongside).
**Access:** The Celtic zone; the Sword in the Square.
**Prerequisites:** None — Camelot takes guests the way the table takes knights: one more seat is always possible.
**Reward spine:** the trying → the test → the being → the wound → the seat → the stillness.

## THE CHAIN

### Quest 1 — "The Sword in the Square"
The stone sword in the square, worn smooth by a thousand years of hands. Every visitor tries; the sword does not care about strength, bloodline, or worthiness — the legends got the test wrong. The Wart tries because the Wart is the one who tries — and the sword comes loose an inch, and stops. The player tries: the sword answers the player the same way it answers everyone now — an inch of recognition, no more. The age of the pulling is over; the age of the trying has begun. The Wart and the player look at each other over the sword. The look is: the beginning of the Round Table (equals who met over something neither could finish alone).
**Reward:** The inch (the player's hand is known to the stone now — the sword remembers trying).

### Quest 2 — "The Black Knight's Gate"
The first test, and the zone's oldest inhabitant: the Black Knight who guards the gate and never explains the test because the test is different for everyone who passes. Played straight — no subversion, no twist; the guardian is a guardian, and being tested by something that takes you seriously is a form of respect the zone pays every guest. The player's test is their own: the Knight asks the question the player has been avoiding (drawn from the player's actual EchoMemory — the unanswered thing).
**Reward:** Passage (the gate opens for the tested, and the Court of stones is visible beyond: the Round Table, ringed with its mineral seats).

### Quest 3 — "Noctua's Lesson"
Merlin's tower, and the owl — Noctua, the wizard's other half, perched in the rafters of the staircase's oldest turret. Merlin's education was never lectures: it was BEING — the student becomes the fish to understand the river, the bird to understand the sky, the squirrel to understand the gathering. The player takes the lesson the Wart once took: three transformations (the water-form, the air-form, the ground-form), each a short first-person shift into another way of being in the world. The king who rules what he has BEEN — that is the curriculum. And Noctua watches every transformation without blinking, because owls are the narrative's oldest witnesses ("wherever an owl is sighted, one of us is near").
**Reward:** The lesson (the transformations unlock — the player may return to any of the three forms for short crossings the normal body can't make: the water-crossing, the air-crossing, the ground-crossing).

### Quest 4 — "The Broken Knight"
Mordred, in the wrecked cloister, played exactly as canon rules: not evil — BROKEN. The traitor of the legends is, in the Inn, the wound that the legend couldn't hold: the one who loved the table and couldn't sit at it honestly, and broke it rather than pretend. No battle. The quest is the listening: the player sits with the Broken Knight and hears the whole account from the inside, and the account does not excuse anything — it explains everything. The legends get to stay legends. The Broken Knight gets to be understood.
**Reward:** The truth (the Broken Knight's account, entered in the Archive — the Inn's version of the story, filed beside the legends it corrects).

### Quest 5 — "The Seat That Fits"
The Round Table Room: every seat is a stone — the knights of the roster each ARE a mineral, the council the Inn's stone collection in furniture form. The player walks the ring and reads the seats (real geology: each seat-stone's family, its zone, its frequency). And as the player completes the reading, a new seat grows from the floor — unhewn, unfinished, made of a stone from the player's OWN most-visited zone. The table grows seats the way the tree grows rings: one more is always possible. The Wart — taller now, and the player notices without being told — pulls the seat out. The gesture is the whole knighthood. There is no ceremony. There is a chair.
**Reward:** THE SEAT (permanent — the player's stone-seat at the Round Table, persisting across lives; the table remembers its knights).

### Quest 6 — "The Quest for Stillness"
The finale in two movements, played as one. First: Galahad and the Grail — the pure knight's quest, and the zone's most secret truth: the Grail is the Philosopher's Stone, and its frequency is the ABSENCE of frequency — the zero point, the rest. The Grail quest is not a hunt; it is the quest for stillness, and it is won by the player who stops looking. Second: the Old King — Stellan's Arthur, met last, after the peer and the king — asks the player for the zone's final quest: not a battle, not a grail, but the act of SITTING. Just sit. The sitting is the acceptance that the table is round, the king is one of the knights, the knights are gone, and the table remains. The player sits. The Grail, in the corner of the room, is a cup nobody has touched in a very long time, catching no light and all of it. The Old King sits too. The two of them are very quiet, and the quiet is the frequency, and the table remains.
**Reward:** THE STILLNESS (a 0Hz resonance unlock — the Void's The Rest and the Bone Room's patience now recognize the player as a fellow practitioner; and the Grail's recognition: the cup catches the light differently for the player who has sat).
