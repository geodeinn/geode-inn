# THE DRUM CIRCLE  the Campgrounds' Gathering Circle
## The spontaneous rhythm mechanic: learn each other's rhythms, or just tap a tempo you feel like
### Written 2026-09-11, from Krista's request: "an option for people hanging out together to form a drum circle. Learn to play at each other's rhythms, or just tap a tempo they feel like doing. They're all over Sherwood after hours  I hear them from my shop window. I've also seen Ryan at one of his house parties idly start tapping on some surface, then another bartender picks up the bongo just sitting over there, and slowly an improvised group of items becomes this harmonizing drum rhythm created by intoxicated rennies. It's fascinating."

Not an homage to drum circles in general; an homage to ONE street's worth of them, the ones that start when the booths close and the rennies are loose and someone finds a surface.

**RYAN'S TAP:** the origin beat, from the real story  the idle tap on some surface, the bongo that was just sitting over there, the slow assembly of an improvised group of items into a harmonizing rhythm. A hand finds a surface. What happens next is up to the night.

## THE MECHANIC

### 1. THE SPARK (how a circle starts)
Any player idly taps any surface  a stump, a fire-ring stone, a mug, a bucket, the ground. No prompt, no UI, no announcement. Every surface answers in its own true voice (THE SURFACES SING: wood, stone, hide, clay, metal  each material's authentic sound, the parks law holding: the material is the lesson). Tap again. The game is listening and says nothing.

### 2. THE PULL (the second tap)
If anyone else taps along  player or NPC  the circle IGNITES: the fire steadies, the lanterns ease down a step (the Sound DIAL: the building makes room), and the tempo ring opens: a soft visual pulse showing the shared groove. Still no score. Still no invitation text. The circle is opt-in by doing it.

### 3. THE JOIN LAW (the bongo is always already there)
Instruments sit near every fire pit  anyone can pick one up, or use hands on knees, spoons on mugs, flat rocks on wood. THE ADEQUACY LAW HOLDS ABSOLUTE: there is no wrong beat. Off-beat is polyrhythm, and polyrhythm is canon (the coconut law: the clapping IS the work). A rhythm that doesn't match is still a rhythm. The game's only job is to listen for the shared pulse and reflect it back, warm. **THE NO-QUANTIZE RULE (the engine's soul):** the Inn NEVER snaps your tap to a grid. Quantization is the Skinner box of rhythm  the circle keeps your human tempo, drift and all, and the engine harmonizes around what the hands actually played. (The Player's Tempo Law, extended to a crowd: the game follows the players. Always. All of them at once.)

### 4. LEARN EACH OTHER'S RHYTHMS (the introduction mechanic)
Each player's pattern is a rhythm they can GIVE to the circle  tap it twice alone and the ring marks it as yours. Anyone can TAKE IT UP (play it back)  and when someone plays your rhythm back to you, the recognition sparkle: quiet, warm, no points. **RHYTHM IS THE HANDSHAKE:** you learn a person at the circle by learning their beat, and every rhythm you've learned from another player joins your emote set (the Tramp's curriculum, cross-wired: rhythm is gesture, gesture is language). "Learn to play at each other's rhythms" is not a tutorial. It is the friendship mechanic.

### 5. THE HEARTBEAT DRIFT (the building's gentle gravity)
Long-lived circles tend to drift toward the 50 BPM heartbeat  the Inn's resting pulse, the parent frequency. Never forced, never shown, never scored: just felt. A circle that settles at the heartbeat is the building breathing with its guests. A circle that stays at 137 BPM is having too much fun to notice, and that is equally welcome (the Player's Tempo Law outranks the gravity, every time).

### 6. THE KEEP (the songbook wire)
When a circle holds a shared groove long enough to be worth naming, any participant can KEEP it: the rhythm logs as an EchoMelody (new melody type: the CIRCLE RHYTHM  source: the fire that hosted it, voice part: whatever the keeper played). The Clockmaker's Turning canon completes the loop: **strangers harmonizing is literal fuel for the Song.** The songbook entry remembers who was there, not as a scoreboard  as a liner note. (Field note for the build: EchoMelody's melodyType is a free string  "circle_rhythm" needs no schema change.)

## THE STAFF (who joins  the circle's regulars)

- **PLUCK**  arrives first, always. Dances to the player's tempo (standing canon); the drum circle is Pluck's favorite event in the entire building, and Pluck has been to a million years of events.
- **THE BROWNIES**  the coconut shells join from wherever they are (the polyrhythm canon: the clapping is the work  the circle is the work's night off, which sounds identical, which is the joke).
- **THE GARGOYLE PUPPIES**  the yips, on the bell's decay, roughly in tune, entirely sincere.
- **THE MURLOCS**  the gargle as the bass line (RULED by Krista, 2026-09-11: "murloc gargles can never be overdone :)"). The nameless collective's ONE SOCIAL HABIT: they come to circles. Nobody invites them; nobody counts them; they gargle at the low end and somehow it holds. The gargle doesn't lock to the grid (nothing does  the No-Quantize Rule stands); it finds the low end and stays there, the warm amphibian floor under the whole human mess above it. The Adequacy Law's own proverb, now canon: THE GARGLE CAN NEVER BE OVERDONE. When the circle ends, the murlocs are somehow already gone, and nobody remembers them leaving  only that the bass felt held.
- **RYAN**  at the Meadery's fire, once: the scripted first spark (THE IDLE TAP  the origin story performed in-world). GATE for Krista: Ryan's already woven in; this is his story from the real house party, offered gently. Ruling hers.
- **THE TRAMP**  never at circles. Walks past, taps twice on a fence post in passing, and is gone  the lesson without the class.
