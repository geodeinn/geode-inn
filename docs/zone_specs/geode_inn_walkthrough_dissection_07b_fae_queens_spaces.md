## THE DESIGN DECISION — WHY THE REDWOOD

The fae queens are three: the Seelie Queen (Summerlands), the Unseelie Queen (the Hollow), and the Queen of Roots (Fae/Canopy — nature sovereignty, deep subterranean resonance). Three queens, one problem: no zone belongs to all of them, and a queen without her own ground is a portrait without a room.

The answer was already growing: **THE REDWOOD CATHEDRAL** — Carr's zone, the Pacific Northwest canopy, the ancient forest that rises like an emerald cathedral. And at its heart: **MONA, the mother redwood** — the oldest tree in the Inn's world, already a seedling when human language was first invented. The mother spirit. The primal urge to grow skyward.

Mona is the queens' shared host. The redwood is ONE TREE that spans the whole vertical: sunlit canopy, hollow trunk, deep heartwood, buried roots — and the three queens' spaces are Mona's three layers. The Redwood Cathedral is where the fae courts' female sovereignty lives, all in one living building — a tree that IS a palace, which is the most Geode Inn sentence possible.

**THE QUEENS' LAW (the tie-in, made canon):** the courts split at the brothers — but the queens never split. Oleander and Malachi divided the Kingdom into Summerlands and Hollow; the three queens answered by growing their spaces in ONE tree. The brothers' split is two directions; the queens' answer is one trunk. (The tessellation's missing counterweight, found: the queens hold the interlock the brothers broke. Mona is where Seelie and Unseelie are still one.)

**GAME TIE — REDWOOD CATHEDRAL (#45, Grow Home-class):** the zone's climbing game now has its spine: the player climbs Mona from root-crown to canopy — and the full vertical route passes ALL THREE QUEENS' SPACES, root to bough. The game IS the queens' ladder. Completing the full climb without teleporting earns an audience with all three in one day — the only quest in the Kingdom that requires visiting every queen.

Three spaces, all live, all forever.

### OPTION 1 — THE SEELIE QUEEN'S BOWER (the canopy)
Mona's crown, woven with sunlight — the highest habitable space in the fae world. Alan Lee's gold at canopy scale: living boughs shaped into halls, leaf-light on everything, wind-chime birdsong at exactly 42Hz.

The Seelie Queen holds the light here — and her court is the SUMMERLands' private half: Oleander's throne hears petitions; the Bower hears TRUTHS. Where the King listens, the Queen BRIGHTENS: her gift is the reflected thing — the player tells her one true thing, and the Bower's light carries the telling gently, warming it, so the player hears their own truth said kindly for the first time.

- **Take tea in the leaf-light.** (The Bower's hospitality: no riddles, no price. The Seelie Queen's space is the only Seelie place with no contract — the light's own guest-right. Anti-FOMO law's fae version: the Bower never runs out of morning.)
- **Ask about the King.** (Her answers are all love — she and Oleander are the courts' quiet proof that summer has a marriage as well as a throne. Her one hook line, said once per visit, the Bower's whole character: "He was Leander long before he was anybody's King." The name said correctly, by the person who gave him the nickname's warmth — the Ties That Bind's secret coda, only hearable here.)
- **Play Redwood Cathedral, bough chapter.** (The climb's top third: sun-difficulty, wind mechanics, the sprites' helping hands. The game rewards patience at the top: the final bough-hold is the Bower's sill.)

### OPTION 2 — THE UNSEELIE QUEEN'S WINTER HALL (the hollow trunk)
Mona's hollow — the great trunk's inner stair, frost-grown, the only place in the redwood where the air is winter. John Bauer's ink at canyon scale: the hollow's walls are living wood gone dark, frost-ferns across every surface, and the Queen's presence presides — gown of woven frost, THE RAVEN-FEATHER CAPE (permanent canon, every rendering), the black-ice crown.

The Unseelie Queen's space mirrors her King's Hollow — but the Hollow is a COURT (public, testing, priced). The Winter Hall is a QUEEN'S alone. Where Malachi's court asks "Cold enough?", the Hall answers for itself: it is exactly as cold as the player's honesty, and no colder.

- **Descend the hollow.** (No petition, no fee — the Winter Hall is the one Unseelie space with no price, because it is not the court; it is the crown's private chapel. The frost-ferns bloom WIDER around players who are being truthful with themselves, and shrink to bare branches around self-deception. The room is the honesty-check the Hollow only claims to be.)
- **Ask about the King.** (Her answers are all loyalty, delivered cold: she and Malachi are the winter's matched pair — his messenger's burden, her keeper's silence. Her one hook line, once per visit: "He built the walls gentle. Everyone forgets that part." The Labyrinth's defense, said by the only person who never needed to be convinced.)
- **Play Redwood Cathedral, hollow chapter.** (The climb's middle third: frost-difficulty, the hollow's inner stairs, raven-shadows as handholds. The ravens here are HER cape's kin — the cape's feathers roost in the hollow when the Queen is elsewhere, and they remember every climber they've seen.)

### OPTION 3 — THE QUEEN OF ROOTS' HEARTWOOD COURT (the roots)
Mona's heartwood — the mother tree's living core, the Final Room's mother-room: the dry, warm, scented hollow carved by centuries of growth, where the trunk's rings are the court's walls and the 42Hz hum is sap made audible. Deep subterranean resonance, 42Hz AND 84Hz braided — the Heartwood Court is the second room in the world where both frequencies are one chord. (The Stone Circle Chamber is the first. The roots of the fae world and the roots of the Song share architecture, because they share roots.)

The Queen of Roots is the oldest sovereignty in the Kingdom — older than the courts' split, older than the brothers, old as growth itself. Her court governs the MYCORRHIZAL NETWORK: the fungal web that connects every tree in every zone of the Inn's world.

- **THE HEARTWOOD SITTING.** The court's audience mechanic (Aughra's Sittings' model, at nature's scale): no petitions, no riddles. The player sits against Mona's oldest ring, and the Queen of Roots sits across, and the heartwood hums, and whatever the player was carrying gets HEARD — not answered. (The endgame's listening theme, held by the third queen: Oleander taught being listened to; the Queen of Roots practices it at the mycelial scale — nothing in the forest is unheard, including the player.)
- **THE MYCORRHIZAL TRADE.** (The Kingdom's missing trade mechanic, resolved here: the fungal web is the fae economy's honest ledger. The Queen of Roots' court brokers favors between zones — a favor given in the Summerlands can be claimed in the Norse zone, THROUGH THE ROOTS, because the network connects every tree. The player learns every zone's forests are one organism's limbs, and the trade UI is the proof: one network, many canopies. Bill the Platypus's honest pricing, made systemic: the web never lies about who owes what.)
- **Ask about the tree.** (Her answers are Mona's biography — the only being in the Inn older than the Song's words: the seedling that predates human language, the mother spirit, the primal skyward urge. Her one hook line, once per visit, the Heartwood's whole thesis: "The Song needed somewhere to stand." The forest was first. The Inn is a guest of the trees. Every zone's timber — the beams, the stairs, the brownies' shelves — grew here first, and the Queen of Roots remembers every plank's living name.)
- **Play Redwood Cathedral, root chapter.** (The climb's bottom third: root-difficulty, the network's glowing threads as footholds — the mycelium literally carries the climber. The chapter's reward: the player's first root-level view UP through the whole tree — bower, hollow, heartwood, canopy — all three queens' spaces visible in one vertical gaze. The full climb's thesis, seen before it's earned.)

## THE CONVERGENCE — THE TRUNK'S ANSWER

The queens' spaces teach the brothers' lesson from the other side: Oleander and Malachi split one root into two directions, and the queens — Seelie, Unseelie, and the Roots beneath both — never stopped sharing one trunk. The player who visits all three spaces in one day (the Redwood Cathedral's full climb) hears the tessellation's intact form: bower-light, hollow-frost, heartwood-hum — three queens, one frequency ladder, one tree. The Kingdom's grief (the broken bridge) and the Kingdom's answer (the standing tree) live in the same forest.

And Mona holds the whole tie-in together: the mother redwood was already a seedling when human language was invented — older than the courts, older than the Contract, older than the name "Leander." The queens sit in the world's oldest building. The building is alive. It grew their spaces as rings.
