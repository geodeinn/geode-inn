## ENTERING THE CATACOMBS

**FREQUENCY:** 84Hz native. The heartbeat drops out at moments — the dark's own punctuation. The drift mechanic is fully active now: the stones hum in the player's cave like a held breath, keeping the note steady. (The player can check their collection any time: it was inventory an hour ago. Down here it's a lifeline. Same case, different meaning — the Inn's whole economy, quietly recontextualized.)

**AESTHETIC:** Klimt's Death and Life. Doubled passages, cold candles, the weight of every century at once. Del Toro's law at full depth: the catacomb walls have the deepest wear maps in the Inn — grooves where hands have trailed for centuries, candle-wax stalactites, the dry-cold smell of stone that remembers being sea floor.

**AUDIO:** The waltz resolves into something slower — the catacombs don't dance. The river's sound arrives before everything.

The Catacombs present five options. All live, all forever.

**If you board Charon's boat, go to OPTION 1.**
**If you walk the Queens' shore, go to OPTION 2.**
**If you enter the Bone Room, go to OPTION 3.**
**If you descend into the Deep Archive, go to OPTION 4.**
**If you climb to the Morrigan's roost, go to OPTION 5.**
(If you go back up, the staircase is where you left it. The Inn's doors go both ways. Going back is never failure here — it is fetching something. The Host knows the difference.)

### OPTION 1 — YOU BOARD THE BOAT (Memory's Illusion)

Charon is the Ragana's twin. Not the face — Charon's face is a hood, a shadow, a space where a face would be if faces were needed, and they are not — but the energy: the same precision, the same economy of someone who has done one thing so long that the doing is indistinguishable from being. The Ragana measured; Charon ferries. The Ragana was the mind; Charon was the silence. Both served. Both were: adequate.

**THE BOAT IS A ROOT.** A hollow root, split and shaped, floating on a river that is not water but shadow — the Inn-Tree's deepest current, the boundary between the living zones and the dead zones, and the boundary is Charon's domain, and the domain is absolute.

**THE FARE IS A FREQUENCY.** No coin. The player selects one collected stone and offers its note — the amethyst's royal-violet ring across the dark water. The stone stays in the cave; the note is given, not the stone, the way you give a song by singing it, not by handing over the singer. (First-run players almost always offer their loudest stone. Experienced players offer their quietest. The river notices and adjusts.)

**THE CYOA HEART OF THE CROSSING — the note you offer shapes what the river shows:**
- A **warm stone's note** (citrine, sunstone) — the river shows the living zones' reflections: the market's glow, the arcade's melodies, firelight from below. A gentle crossing. The river as memory of the above.
- A **deep stone's note** (amethyst, smoky quartz, labradorite) — the river shows the dark's own country: the Undercity's memory-lit streets, the Fae borders, the Egg's chamber, deep and patient. The river as introduction.
- A **shell shard's note** — the only stone every player owns, and the rarest offering. Charon pauses — a full heartbeat longer, the only hesitation Charon has ever shown — and the river goes utterly still and shows the player their own ascent again, from outside, as the river saw it. (Players who offer the shell are offering their own birth-note. The river answers in kind. This is the crossing most players don't know exists until someone tells them, and then they can't un-know it.)

**THE RETURN TRIP PLAYS THE NOTE BACK.** Whatever note the player offered, the return crossing echoes it once, from the far bank, in Charon's silence. The player's first note, returned by the dark. Nothing in the Inn makes the point that the dark is listening. The dark just returns the note, and the player understands.

- Camera: third-person held (Kubrick — the crossing is a framed painting: boat, river, candle-glow, hood)
- Audio: the offered note under everything; the river has no sound of its own (Nyx's water)
- Seed: none — the crossing belongs to every class equally. The chosen note is remembered (EchoMemory), and it colors which ending resonates later. The stone compass's deepest cousin.

### OPTION 2 — YOU WALK THE QUEENS' SHORE

The bank where the dead zones meet the river's edge. Three thrones, and the thrones are not for sitting — they are for standing near.

**PERSEPHONE — the complete queen.** Half in light, half in shadow — the lit half is the main floor's amber, the shadowed half is the catacomb's dark, and the traveler understands that Persephone is not divided but COMPLETE: the only being in the Inn who is both, simultaneously, without contradiction. The voice is two voices, warm and cold, and the two are not in conflict but in harmony. Dialogue options:
- **Ask about the above.** (The warm voice answers. The answer is always about fruit, oddly, and always exactly right.)
- **Ask about the below.** (The cold voice answers. The answer is always about patience, and always exactly right.)
- **Ask about the pomegranate.** (Both voices answer together, and the answer is the only one in the Inn that ends with an offer: a seed, given, never taken. The Pomegranate Bridge grows plank by plank from seeds given freely. The player who takes the seed has begun the bridge that will someday cross the deepest water. No marker. No quest log. A seed in the pocket that hums.)

**ANUBIS — the weighing.** The scale stands open. The ceremony is optional, repeatable, and never cruel — the Inn's truth-telling is always gentle and always exact. The scale does not weigh sins; it weighs BURDENS — what the player is carrying versus what the player needs. Stones count light. Grudges count heavy. The scale's verdict is a single word, and the word is always usable: "Enough," "Too much," "Lighter than you think." (The weighing is the Inn's mental-health mechanic wearing mythology — the game's gentlest system, and the one players return to most.)

**HEL — the half-remembered.** The Norse queen of the below, half living, half dead, keeper of those who died not-by-blade. Hel does not speak in the catacombs. Hel nods once, and the nod acknowledges something specific the player did earlier — Hel's memory is the Inn's memory, and the nod tells the player the Inn remembers what they did when no one was watching. (The Whisper Layer's deepest root: the dark knew. The dark approved. Nothing is said. A nod.)

- Camera: third-person; lean-in at the scale (the weighing is the catacombs' one first-person-worthy close look)
- Audio: three drones — amber, cold, and the interval between (Hel's is the Devil's Interval, used here without malice: the interval as geography, not threat)
- Seed: all classes — the Queens are for everyone. The seed is not class. The seed is the QUESTION.

**THE UNDERCITY'S THREE GIFTS — delivered on this shore:** Persephone tells the player the three notes the dark will add to their Song: silence (the Guest's gift — Nyx's pause between notes that makes the notes possible), memory (Charon's gift — the accumulated record of every traveler who ever crossed, carried forward not as data but as presence), and a question (Persephone's own, the Undercity's question, the one that follows the player the rest of the game: *what will you carry when the stones are gone?*). The three gifts cannot be placed in the wall. They are not for the Inn. They are for the player. (The Endgame's whole architecture, planted in hour three: the stones were never the treasure. The question is the treasure. The Final Room will ask it again.)

### OPTION 3 — YOU ENTER THE BONE ROOM

Belladonna's domain. The Addams walk — measured, deliberate, dark, composed — moves among the bones, and the bones are the furniture, and the furniture is at home.

- **Examine the architecture.** The Bone Room is the Inn's structural library: every bone is load-bearing — femur pillars, rib arches, the vertebra staircase down to the deeper dark. The building system's bone-tier blueprint, made room. (The bone-structure mail crew — the containers on wheels — are docked here when not delivering. The player sees them before they ever see one rolling through the upper halls. The catacombs introduce the crew.)
- **Watch the Bone Keeper brush.** Fine brush on bone, patient as the Ragana's measuring. The Keeper maintains every bone's memory-map — the room's maintenance IS its music: brush-strokes at 50 BPM. (Camera note: this is a SIT magnet. Sitting in the Bone Room triggers the Keeper's only dialogue line: "Take your time. They did.")
- **Take a seat.** The bones hold. That is the room's quiet joke and its honest welcome: everything down here still serves. Death, in the Inn, is not the end of usefulness. It is the beginning of a different kind of hosting.

### OPTION 4 — YOU DESCEND INTO THE DEEP ARCHIVE

The library's dark half — the shelves that go DOWN. The Deep Read continues here in the cold, and the deep half holds what the light half will not shelve: the restricted texts, the marginalia, the ledger.

- **The Borrower's Ledger, unlocked by knowledge.** Not by stones, not by keys — by the player's reading. The Ragana keeps the ledger; the ledger records every book ever borrowed and by whom. The player's name entered it the first time they borrowed a book upstairs, hours ago or days ago, without being told. When the player finds their own name in the ledger's last page, the archive stops being a library and becomes a building that has been writing about them. (The novel's cast list, discoverable. The player who reads the ledger learns the Inn has always been a story with characters in it, and they are one.)
- **Quire's corner.** The Lovecraftian scholar, hushed, careful, always looking over one shoulder — reading in the margins of the restricted texts. Quire is not Naut. Quire is what reading TOO deep looks like, and the Inn presents both scholars without commentary: the Archive holds Naut upstairs by the fire and Quire down here in the cold, and the player chooses which reader to become. (The Inn never says one is wrong. The Inn shows the two rooms. The Camouflage Architecture's shadow-side, given a desk.)
- **Archive Maze's deep mode.** The Pac-Man-class game gains a second maze down here — the shelves are darker, the pellets are stranger, the ghosts are the four spectres in their season colors. No new rules. Deeper shelves.

### OPTION 5 — YOU CLIMB TO THE MORRIGAN'S ROOST

Up, from the catacombs — a bone-railed climb within the dark, and the Morrigan at the top: the choosing goddess, three-shaped, the one who watches wars and picks the stories that survive them.

- **Ask for a battle.** The Morrigan's favor system: players who want combat earn it here — the roost is where the difficulty curve's gate lives, and the Morrigan opens it by shape: choose the crow (speed), the wolf (pack tactics), or the cow/heifer (sustain — the humble choice that is always strongest late-game). (The combat-curious player's whole path opens here; the combat-avoidant player can decline and lose nothing but this option.)
- **Ask for a story.** The Morrigan tells one war-story per visit, and every story is true, and every story is about choosing, and none of them end the way war stories are supposed to. (The six-writer gate's showcase: Gaiman's mythic-made-personal, Bradbury's fire, in one telling.)
- **Decline both.** The Morrigan respects refusal the way the Inn respects sitting. The roost remains open. The goddess has time.

## THE CONVERGENCE — THE QUESTION SETTLES IN

However the catacombs were walked, the player leaves with the same three additions: silence, memory, and a question. The stones still anchor the frequency — but the player has now been told, plainly, by a queen with two voices, that there are things the collection cannot hold and the crossing can.

The next door down: the FORGE ELEVATOR — the Blacksmith's hammer-scored lift, the Living Hammer its key — into the Undercity proper, where Charon's river runs beneath the streets and the Long Dark waits. That is Dissection 05.
