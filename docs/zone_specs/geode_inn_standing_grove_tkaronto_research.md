## WHAT THE ZONE IS
The Inn's archive ends before the last two centuries. The Standing Grove is the ORIGINAL Toronto—what the name meant before it named a modern city. Long before glass towers and municipal boundaries, *tkaronto* described a living architectural and ecological landmark: wooden stakes and living trees standing in the shallow waters of Atherley Narrows between Lake Simcoe (*Ouentironk*) and Lake Couchiching, holding fish weirs and anchoring the ancient overland portage between Lake Ontario and the upper Great Lakes.

The zone combines three interwoven elements:
1. **The Grove in Water:** A wetland sanctuary of flood-tolerant trees (tamarack, black ash, silver maple, white cedar, black willow) growing directly in still, shallow waters.
2. **The Weirs:** The ancient Mnjikaning fish fence architecture—submerged stakes, woven brush funnels, and arched heart-traps used for over 5,000 years.
3. **The Carrying Place:** The 80 km Humber and Rouge portage trails (*Passage de Taronto*) connecting water systems.

## 1. THE NAME & ETYMOLOGY
Following the Inn's **Steady Light Law** (keep uncertainty visible; present variants honestly without smoothing):

* **Mohawk (*tkaronto*):** Meaning *"where there are trees standing in the water"* (or *"place where trees grow over the water"*). Refers specifically to the wooden stakes driven into the muddy lacustrine riverbed at Atherley Narrows to support fish weirs, as well as shoreline trees overhanging the channel.
* **Wendat / Huron (*Toronto / Toronte*):** Recorded in Gabriel Sagard's 1632 Huron-French dictionary (*Dictionaire de la langue huronne*) with the entry *Toronto* meaning *"plenty"* or *"abundance"* (*abondance*), referring to the extraordinary yield of fish harvested at the narrows. (Lake Simcoe itself was called *Ouentironk* or *Ouentaronk* in Wendat).
* **The Cartographic Southward Drift:**
  * **1615:** Samuel de Champlain visits Atherley Narrows with Huron guides and documents the weirs.
  * **1675:** French cartographer Pierre Raffeix maps Lake Simcoe as *Lac Taronto*.
  * **1678 & 1680:** Jean-Baptiste-Louis Franquelin (*Tarontos Lac*) and Abbé Claude Bernou (*Lac de Taronto*) solidify the lake name.
  * **1686:** The portage route from Lake Ontario up the Humber River to Lake Simcoe becomes known as *Passage de Taronto* and the river as *Rivière Taronto*.
  * **1695:** Italian cartographer Vincenzo Coronelli introduces the modern spelling *Toronto*.
  * **1750:** French construct *Fort Toronto* (Fort Rouillé) at the mouth of the Humber River on Lake Ontario—transferring the name 100 km south from the narrows to Lake Ontario.
  * **1793:** British Lieutenant Governor John Graves Simcoe renames the settlement *York* (favoring English names).
  * **1834:** The city incorporates and restores its original name *Toronto*, cited during council debates as "the old, original name of the place, and the sound is in every respect much better."
* **Settler Folk Etymology (Misattribution):** Later 19th/20th-century sources frequently claimed Toronto meant "place of meeting." Modern linguistic research (Percy Robinson, Natural Resources Canada) confirms the primary Mohawk *tkaronto* weir-stake origin, while acknowledging the Wendat abundance root.

## 2. THE WEIRS (MNJIKANING FISH WEIRS)
* **Location & Age:** Situated at Atherley Narrows between Lake Simcoe and Lake Couchiching. Radiocarbon dating of preserved wooden stakes dates the site to ~4,500 BP (~2,500 BCE, Archaic Period). The site remained in continuous operation for over 5,000 years until the early 1700s. Designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 1982, stewarded by the Chippewas of Rama First Nation (*Mnjikaning Anishinaabek*).
* **Construction Technique:**
  * **Stakes:** Thousands of sharpened wooden stakes driven deep into the soft, lacustrine clay and silt riverbed.
  * **Verified Wood Species:** Eastern White Cedar (*Thuja occidentalis*), Sugar Maple (*Acer saccharum*), and White Birch (*Betula papyrifera*). White cedar was prized for its natural rot resistance underwater.
  * **Fence & Arched Heart:** Woven wattling, brush, branches, and aquatic reeds were interwoven between stakes to form long V-shaped funnels guiding migrating fish into an arched central trap ("heart of the weir").
* **Stewardship & Multi-Nation Care:** Kept and maintained sequentially and collaboratively across millennia by Archaic/Woodland ancestors, the Wendat (Huron), the Haudenosaunee (Mohawk/Iroquois), and the Anishinaabe (Ojibwe/Mississauga). The weirs were treated as a shared communal trust and sacred fishing ground rather than exclusive property.
* **Seasonal Runs:**
  * **Spring Run:** White Sucker (*Catostomus commersonii*), Longnose Sucker, Yellow Perch.
  * **Autumn Spawning Run:** Lake Whitefish (*Coregonus clupeaformis*), Lake Trout (*Salvelinus namaycush*).
  * **Year-Round & Secondary Catch:** Walleye/Pickerel, Northern Pike, Smallmouth Bass, Muskellunge.

## 3. THE CARRYING PLACE (TORONTO CARRYING-PLACE TRAIL)
* **Route:** An 80 km (50 mi) ancient overland portage connecting Lake Ontario to Lake Simcoe:
  * **Humber Branch (West):** Mouth of the Humber River on Lake Ontario, tracking the eastern bank north, forking near Woodbridge, crossing the Oak Ridges Moraine to the West Holland River, discharging into Lake Simcoe.
  * **Rouge Branch (East):** Mouth of the Rouge River on Lake Ontario (site of Seneca village *Ganatsekwyagon*), north over Oak Ridges Moraine to the East Holland River near Aurora.
  * From Lake Simcoe, water craft passed through Atherley Narrows into Lake Couchiching, then via the Severn River directly into Georgian Bay / Lake Huron.
* **Age & Pre-Industrial Importance:** In use for over 11,000 years (since glacier retreat, ~9000 BCE). It provided a vital trade shortcut between the lower and upper Great Lakes, avoiding the treacherous voyage around the Niagara Peninsula and Niagara Falls.
* **Nations & Fur Trade:** Maintained by Wendat, Haudenosaunee (Seneca villages of *Teiaiagon* on the Humber and *Ganatsekwyagon* on the Rouge), and Anishinaabe (Mississaugas). Later used heavily by French coureurs des bois (Champlain, Brûlé, La Salle) and British traders.

## 4. THE PEOPLES & CANON LAWS
Following the Inn's **Shield of Lore Law**:

* **Honoring Indigenous Sources:** Pre-industrial nations (Wendat/Huron, Haudenosaunee/Mohawk, Anishinaabe/Ojibwe/Mississauga) are represented as master engineers, cartographers, and stewards of the waterways. Modern political conflicts and historic trauma are not touched; only the old, honored ways are showcased.
* **No Invented Mythology:** Zero fictionalization or synthesis of First Nations sacred lore. Game mechanics draw strictly on physical engineering (weirs, portage, wood craft, dendrochronology, acoustics) and verified ecological facts.
* **Handling Source Conflicts:** Historical disagreements (e.g., Wendat vs. Mohawk etymology, precise tribal occupation timelines) are presented side-by-side in zone lore texts rather than forced into a single harmonized narrative.
* **NPC Rule:** Real First Nations sacred figures or mythological entities must NOT be assigned to game NPCs without explicit approval. All cultural representations are flagged for Krista's ruling.

## 5. ECOLOGY OF THE GROVE
* **Trees Standing in/over Water (Ontario Wetland / Swamp Species):**
  * **Tamarack / Eastern Larch (*Larix laricina*):** Deciduous conifer that thrives in submerged bog peat; needles turn brilliant gold before dropping in autumn.
  * **Black Ash (*Fraxinus nigra*):** Deep swamp tree that grows in standing water; pliable wood splits cleanly along annual growth rings (historically harvested for basketry).
  * **Eastern White Cedar (*Thuja occidentalis*):** Shoreline and bog species with extreme water/rot tolerance; primary material for weir stakes.
  * **Silver Maple (*Acer saccharinum*):** Floodplain giant with widespread buttressed roots capable of enduring spring flood inundation.
  * **Black Willow (*Salix nigra*):** Overhanging riverbank species whose extensive root systems stabilize wet shorelines over water channels.
* **Water & Still-Water Ecosystem:** Shallow lacustrine narrows, submerged macrophyte beds, yellow pond-lily (*Nuphar lutea*), fragrant water lily (*Nymphaea odorata*), wild rice (*Zizania aquatica*), cattails (*Typha*). Anchialine and freshwater critter systems tie to Persephone's tide.
* **Fish Species:** Lake Whitefish, Lake Trout, White Sucker, Walleye/Pickerel, Muskellunge, Smallmouth Bass, Yellow Perch.
* **Bird Species:** Bird life includes Great Blue Heron, Osprey, Bald Eagle, Common Loon, Belted Kingfisher, Wood Duck, Red-winged Blackbird.

## 6. GAME SEEDS (SEEDS, NOT RULINGS)
* **Portage Mechanic ("What You Carry Between Waters You Keep"):**
  * *Inn Post Tie:* Connects directly to the Inn Post philosophy (*"what cannot be said directly gets carried"*). Items or cargo carried on foot along portage paths between water bodies retain spectral/morphic resonance and gain durability or purity buffs.
* **Weirs as Carrying-Place Architecture:**
  * Uses the **Campgrounds building system**. Players can construct water-sieves, fish traps, and current-directing wooden stake fences using white cedar, birch, and sugar maple stakes with woven brush wattling.
* **The Grove as Gathering Space (The Gathering Law):**
  * The standing grove acts as a neutral sanctuary zone suspended over still water. Under The Gathering Law, weapons are sheathed; players and NPCs gather on wooden platforms or living boughs to trade lore, cargo, and acoustic notes.
* **Lithophone & Water-Tone System:**
  * The Inn's tone system maps material to pitch. Submerged cedar stakes and water-striking wooden beams create a unique low-frequency acoustic register (wood over water). Resonates with Persephone's tide clock and D16's parent frequency.
