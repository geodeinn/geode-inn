# THE BLUE BOX — TARDIS Easter Egg Specification
## The Staircase's In-Between Joke

---

## WHAT THE BLUE BOX IS

A police box. London, 1963. Dark blue with white windows. The lamp on top blinks. Slowly. Once every 1.2 seconds — which is exactly 50 BPM. The Inn's heartbeat. The blink is warm. The blue is deeper than it should be. The wood grain is real.

The box does not open. There is no quest. There is no dialogue. No NPC mentions it. No gargoyle watches it. It is simply THERE — in an alcove off the Staircase, between the third and fourth landing, where the alchemical texts transition from elemental to celestial.

It is the Inn's inside joke with itself. A building that is bigger on the inside than it appears from the outside, acknowledging the original. The Geode Inn's nickname is "The TARDIS" — earned organically because the Inn is a door that looks like one thing (a jewelry website, a book about rocks) and opens into an entire world. The Blue Box is the Inn wearing its nickname on its sleeve.

---

## PLACEMENT

**Level:** The Staircase (Level 5)
**Location:** An alcove between the third and fourth landing
**Context:** The alcove is where the alchemical texts transition from elemental (Nigredo, Albedo) to celestial (Citrinitas, Rubedo). The transition zone. The in-between section of the in-between level. The Blue Box sits in the space between stages — which is exactly where a TARDIS belongs.

The alcove is not marked. No sign. No arrow. No quest marker. The player walks past it on the way down or up. The alcove is slightly recessed — the player must turn to look into it. Most players will walk past. Some will notice. Fewer will stop. Fewer still will examine.

## VISUAL SPECIFICATION

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| **Box material** | Wood — dark navy blue, weathered. The paint is old but not peeling. The wood grain is visible through the paint. Real wood texture, not painted metal. |
| **Windows** | Frosted white. 12 panes per side (6 over 6). The glass is real — the player can see light through it, but nothing inside. The light is warm — the box's interior is lit, but the door doesn't open. |
| **Lamp** | Brass. Dome-shaped. The bulb is warm amber — not white. The lamp blinks once every 1.2 seconds (50 BPM). The blink is slow, deliberate, warm. Not a warning. A heartbeat. |
| **Sign** | White painted text on the box's front: "POLICE TELEPHONE FREE FOR USE OF PUBLIC ADVICE AND ASSISTANCE AND INN." The last word is added. Three letters. The player must be pressed against the glass at maximum zoom to read it. |
| **Size** | Exact proportions of a 1963 Met Box — approximately 10 feet tall. The alcove is sized to fit. The ceiling of the alcove is slightly higher than the Staircase ceiling — the box is taller than it looks from the stairs. |
| **Base** | The box sits on a stone floor — the Staircase's bedrock. No platform. No pedestal. The box sits on the ground the way a real police box would sit on a London street. |
| **Shadow** | The box casts a shadow that doesn't match the Staircase's light source. The Staircase is lit by torches and alchemical glow. The box's shadow falls as if lit from above — by a sky that isn't there. The shadow is the first clue that something is wrong. |

## THE BLINK

The lamp blinks at 50 BPM. This is the Inn's heartbeat. The Staircase hums at 50 BPM. The Inn breathes at 50 BPM. The box breathes with the building.

The blink cycle:
- **ON:** 0.6 seconds (warm amber glow, slightly brighter than the alcove's ambient light)
- **OFF:** 0.6 seconds (the lamp is dark, but the glass windows still show warm interior light)
- **Total cycle:** 1.2 seconds = 50 beats per minute

The blink is synced to the Inn's global 50 BPM clock. When the Inn's heartbeat is audible (haptic or audio), the lamp blinks on the beat. The box is IN RHYTHM with the building. It is not part of the Inn. It is not separate from the Inn. It is in-between.

## THE CRONE'S MEASURING TAPE

The one interaction. The only acknowledgment that the box exists within the Inn's fiction.

If the player approaches the box and uses the crystal lens (the Crone's tool from the Catacombs), a hidden detail becomes visible: a measuring tape extending from the keyhole. The tape is the Crone's — the same measuring tape visible on the Crone's desk in the Catacombs. The tape extends from the keyhole into the box. The measurement on the tape reads: "bigger."

The implication: the Crone once measured the box's interior through the keyhole. The interior is larger than the exterior. The Crone removed the tape, rolled it up, and never spoke of it again.

If the player asks the Crone about the box (in the Catacombs, at the Crone's desk), the Crone says:
"I don't discuss the Staircase. That's not my level. But if I had measured something that shouldn't be possible, I would put the measuring tape away and focus on my work. Which is what I did. Page 47. Second paragraph."

If the player asks Naut about the box, Naut says (tentacles still):
"I've been by the fire for a very long time. I've never been up the stairs. But I've heard the blink. 50 beats per minute. The same heartbeat I hear down here. The box is in rhythm with the building. Whether that means the box is part of the building or the building is part of the box — I don't know. I'm a scholar, not a physicist. Have some tea."

If the player asks Beverley about the box, Beverley says:
"Not in the catalog. Not my problem. If it's not on a shelf, it doesn't exist. I'm very consistent about this."

## THE BLUE BOX IN THE SHADOW INN (84HZ)

In the Shadow Inn, the Blue Box is frozen mid-blink. The lamp is either ON or OFF, depending on when the Song completed. The frozen state reveals one detail invisible in the Living Inn: the box's shadow is correct. In the Shadow Inn, the light source is fixed, and the shadow falls as if lit by a sky — a sky that is visible in the Shadow Inn's version of the alcove. The alcove has a ceiling that wasn't there in the Living Inn. The ceiling shows stars. The box's shadow is cast by starlight.

The starlight ceiling in the Shadow Inn's alcove is the only place in the Staircase where stars are visible. The Staircase is interior — no sky. But the Blue Box's alcove in the Shadow Inn has stars. The box brought its own sky.

---

## THE BLUE BOX IN THE IN-BETWEEN

In the In-Between (the grey space between frequencies), the Blue Box is neither blinking nor frozen. The lamp is on. Steady. Not blinking. The warm amber light is constant. The box is the most colorful object in the In-Between — everything else is grey, but the box retains its navy blue. The box has its own color in a space that has no color.

The box's door in the In-Between is slightly ajar. Not open — ajar. A crack of warm light. The player cannot fit through the crack. The player cannot open it further. The crack is the width of a hand. If the player puts their hand through the crack, the controller haptics pulse at 50 BPM — the heartbeat, felt from inside the box. The interior is warm.

The In-Between version of the box is the closest the game comes to saying "yes, it's bigger on the inside." It never says it outright. The haptic is the confirmation. The warmth is the confirmation. The player who reaches into the crack feels the box's heartbeat and knows.

## WHAT THE BLUE BOX IS NOT

- **Not a quest.** No quest log entry. No reward. No progression. The box gives nothing the game can measure.
- **Not a secret.** Secrets are hidden and can be found by looking. The box is VISIBLE. It's in an alcove on the main path. Anyone can see it. Most people walk past it.
- **Not a crossover.** The box is not a Doctor Who crossover. It is not the TARDIS. It is a police box — a real object that existed in London. The resemblance to the TARDIS is intentional but the box is not time Lord technology. The box is the Inn's self-awareness made architectural.
- **Not functional.** The door doesn't open (in the Living Inn). The box doesn't move. The box doesn't make sounds (beyond the lamp's light, which is silent). The box is a PRESENCE, not a mechanic.
- **Not explained.** No document in the Inn explains the box. No NPC explains the box. The Crone's measuring tape is the only in-fiction acknowledgment, and the Crone refuses to discuss it. The box is the Inn's unsolved mystery — not a puzzle to be solved, but a fact to be accepted. The box is there. The box blinks. That's all anyone knows.

## ARTIST STYLE

The Blue Box is the ONLY object in the Inn that is NOT rendered in any artist's style. The box is rendered as PHOTO-REALISTIC. In a building of 42 artistic visions, the box is the one thing that looks like a photograph. The wood grain is real wood. The paint is real paint. The brass is real brass. The glass is real glass.

The photo-realism is the joke. In a building where every surface is painted — Klimt gold, Goya dark, Escher geometry — the Blue Box is the one thing that WASN'T painted. The box is real. Everything else is art.

The shadow (the one that doesn't match the Staircase's light source) is rendered in Escher's style — the shadow is the only part of the box that belongs to the Inn's artistic world. The box is real. The shadow is art. The box casts an artistic shadow. That's the visual pun.

The box is the Inn's only unsolved mystery — not because the answer is hidden, but because there is no answer. The box is there. The box blinks. The box is bigger on the inside. The Crone measured it and went back to work. That's the whole story. The adequate is: enough.
