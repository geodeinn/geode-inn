## THE PREMISE

Color in the Inn is not decoration. IT IS CHEMISTRY WITH A RECIPE CARD. Every color in the wheel is a blend of real materials doing real things — and the wheel teaches itself: primaries from single sources, secondaries from honest overdyes, tertiaries from precise dips. The player who learns the wheel learns the chemistry; the player who learns the chemistry can glaze.

**The One Grammar holds here too:** color = frequency you can see. The dye wheel and the glaze line are the Stone pillar's light-ward branch — the pattern is the spectrum.

## PART ONE — THE DYE WHEEL (the twelve)

**The primaries (three anchors, now live in the Material entity):**
| Color | Source | The honest chemistry |
|-------|--------|----------------------|
| RED | MADDER (the root) | The alizarin red — the ancient world's cloth; three years in the ground before the root is red enough; washes to pink without the mordant |
| YELLOW | WELD (the tops) | Luteolin — the yellowest yellow the old world knew; the tapestry yellow that holds for centuries |
| BLUE | INDIGO (the leaf) / WOAD (the north's branch) | The blue that breathes — the vat is living chemistry, a tended fermentation; woad is the same molecule from a cold climate |

**THE MORDANT (the law under the wheel): THE COLOR IS ONLY AS PERMANENT AS ITS HOLD.** Oak Gall (the tannin) and the iron salts fix the dye to the cloth — the anti-superstition law made visible: unmordanted color is a promise; mordanted color is a fact. The mordant bench is the wheel's first lesson.

**The secondaries (the overdyes — cloth dipped twice):**
| Color | The blend | The note |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| ORANGE | Madder + Weld | The warm blend — the sunset of the dye garden |
| GREEN | Weld overdyed in Indigo | THE GREEN IS ALWAYS AN OVERDYE (nature's green and the vat's green are two different chemistry families — the wheel's own secret) |
| PURPLE | Madder overdyed in Indigo | The north's Tyrian — no murex needed; the purple of the patched cloak |

**The tertiaries (the precise dips — the master's range):** red-orange (madder-heavy), yellow-orange (weld-heavy), yellow-green (the light green dip), blue-green (the deep green dip), blue-violet (the indigo-heavy purple), red-violet (the madder-heavy purple). Six more recipes, each a ratio, each a lesson in how a color LEANS. The tertiary tier is where the player stops following recipes and starts making them (the graduation: the wheel is learned when the player can hit yellow-green blind).

## PART TWO — THE GLAZE LINE (color becomes mineral)

**The bridge law: GLAZE IS THE DYE WHEEL AT KILN TEMPERATURE.** The same chemistry, the same wheel — but the cloth's plants are replaced by the earth's metals, and the fire decides what the color becomes.

**The mineral colorants (the stone-pillar primaries):**
| Glaze color | The mineral | The chemistry |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| BLUE | Cobalt | The blue that survives every fire — unchanged by heat, the potter's constant |
| GREEN | Copper | Copper goes green in the OXIDATION fire — patient, predictable |
| RED/METALLIC | Copper (again) | The same copper pulls RED and metallic in the REDUCTION fire — the same element, two answers, depending on how you starve the flame (the raku secret) |
| CELADON/RUST | Iron | Iron green in reduction, rust in oxidation — the glaze that depends on what the kiln breathes |
| PURPLE-BLACK | Manganese | The deep glaze — the purple's mineral ghost |
| WHITE | Tin | The historic white — the ground the other colors stand on |

**The firing methods (the game modes — each a different ask of the fire):**
- **OXIDATION (the patient fire):** the kiln breathes freely; copper is green, iron is rust; the predictable path — the teaching kiln
- **REDUCTION (the starved fire):** the kiln is starved of oxygen; the flame pulls it from the clay itself, and the colors invert — celadon is born here
- **RAKU (the drama — the amazing-but-simple process):** the pot is pulled GLOWING from the kiln and plunged into sawdust and air; the copper flashes metallic, the glaze crackles with the shock, and no two pots answer the same. THE SIMPLE PROCESS THAT CAN'T BE INDUSTRIALIZED — the perfect Inn craft: fast, dramatic, honest, slightly dangerous, and absolutely original every time. (The raku moment is the game's best single-button payoff: one pull, one plunge, one held breath, and the reveal.)

**BONE CHINA (the historical end of the line):** kaolin + stone + BONE ASH — the Bone Room's own contribution (the Ossuary recipes' Bone material, ground fine and fired white; the structure that outlasted the body, made luminous). Bone china is the Charnel Cathedral's tea service: the game's quiet way of saying even the bones keep working.

**THE BLEND LINE (the wheel crosses into glaze):** the glaze room uses the SAME WHEEL — cobalt-green from copper+cobalt overlap, the blue-black of cobalt+manganese, the iron-copper teadust... the player who learned the dye ratios in Part One brings them to the kiln and they half-hold (mineral blends are wilder than plant blends — the wheel's lessons transfer, then the fire edits). THE CHEMISTRY TIES THE TWO CRAFTS: one grammar, two temperatures.

## PART THREE — THE GLASS HOUSE (the Glassblower; added 2026-09-10)

**The craft:** glassblowing — the gather, the marver, the blow. GLASS IS BREATH MADE PERMANENT: the pipe's breath shapes the gather, and the shape holds the breath forever. The craft's whole discipline is breath control — the Inn's oldest mechanic (the breath phase) becomes a craft here (the player's exhale length and steadiness shape the piece; the tell is true — the wobble in the breath is the wobble in the glass).

**THE COLOR EQUATIONS ARE SACRED — the Equation Law:** the glassblower's color formulas are EARNED ONE AT A TIME, never sold, never told. Historically true (the Murano guild kept color formulas as state secrets — masters who left the island were hunted; the color was the state's treasure), and the Inn's version strips it to the honest core: the equations aren't hoarded from greed, they're EARNED BY OBSERVATION — THE DISASSEMBLY METHOD APPLIED TO COLOR (the architecture law's sibling: disassemble the building to learn what to gather; hold the known glass to the unknown and read the difference). The player recovers each equation by comparison — a red that's ruby-like beside one that's rust-like teaches the gold. The glassblower never tells; the glassblower shows.

**The equation set (the honest chemistry, the sacred ladder):**
| Equation | The chemistry | The status |
|----------|--------------|-----------|
| Cobalt blue | The deep blue — the constant, the first equation earned | The apprentice's first |
| Manganese clear | THE GLASSMAKER'S SOAP — the decolorizer: the secret isn't coloring the glass, it's making the sand's own rust disappear | The counterintuitive lesson |
| Copper aventurine | The sparkle caught in the body — copper crystals grown slow in the cool | The patience equation |
| Iron green | The sand's own color, owned | The local equation |
| SANG DE BOEUF (the ox-blood red) | Copper red in the REDUCTION fire — THE SAME SECRET AS THE RAKU LINE: the same copper, the same starved flame, two crafts, one truth. The Kiln Keeper and the Glassblower share one secret and teach it twice | The shared equation |
| GOLD RUBY | Colloidal gold — the color that costs a fortune per ounce and was guarded like a throne; the master's equation, the most guarded color in the history of the craft | THE SACRED ONE — the colorist's master certificate |

**The raw glass (the gathering ties):** SEA GLASS (the Tidal Caves' tumbled cullet — the recycled glass of the sea, the studio's beloved remelt) and DESERT GLASS (the Egyptian gift — the lightning-made raw). The glassblower is the Inn's great recycler: the broken piece is never waste, it's the next gather (the staying theme again).

**THE TWO-FIRE SECRET (the law):** copper red in reduction belongs to both the kiln and the furnace — the raku pot and the sang de boeuf vase are the same chemistry at two temperatures, and the zone teaches them as one lesson learned twice. THE CHEMISTRY TIES THE CRAFTS (the One Grammar, fire branch: forge, kiln, furnace — the fire family's three rooms, one flame).

**The Glassblower (the resident — RULED 2026-09-10, Krista): THE CHANGING VENUE + THE GRUMPY ARTIST.**
- **THE CHANGING VENUE:** the Glassblower ROTATES between two homes. (1) FAMILY DAYS — with the family, DEBATING THE SECRETS OF THE CRAFT WORLD (the guild made kin: the Murano echo, the color equations argued at a dinner table instead of a council chamber; the player overhears fragments of the debates — the family's arguments are the deepest lore the Glassblower never teaches directly. The family is the Equation Law's other face: the secrets aren't hoarded, they're ARGUED OVER — kept alive by disagreement). (2) SHOP DAYS — at THE BEAUTIFUL STEAMPUNK-STYLE SHOP IN THE CAMPGROUNDS: brass and glass, the furnace's pipes made architecture, the blowpipe rack like an organ; LESSONS AND PRESENTATIONS (the public face of the craft — the Equation Law softened for company: the demonstration where the grump shows rather than tells; the presentation nights are social events, the Gathering Law honored — glassblowing is a SPECTATOR craft and the shop is built for watching). The rotation is a schedule the player learns (the wander-AI set: family days and shop days on the movement system's calendar — finding the Glassblower at the right place is itself a small game).
- **THE GRUMPY ARTIST (the temperament, ruled):** grumpy — played straight (no cute-ification, no softening: the grump is real, the perfectionism of someone whose material punishes sloppiness with a week of wasted gathers). Speaks in partial recipes ("A little more. Not that much."), criticizes every beginner's first bubble — and then quietly keeps the beginner's best piece on the shop's high shelf, never explaining why it's there. The warmth is in the WORK, never in the words. The one craftsperson in the Inn completely at peace with secrets — the equations are sacred because they're earned, and the earning is the gift.
- **The family:** the Glassblower's kin, the debating table — background voices first (the debate heard through the shop's back wall on shop days), full NPC designs only if Krista calls for them (names and members are Krista's rulings, never assumed). Name of the Glassblower: Krista's ruling.
