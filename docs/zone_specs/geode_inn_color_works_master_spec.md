## THE PREMISE

Color in the Inn is not decoration. IT IS CHEMISTRY WITH A RECIPE CARD. Every color in the wheel is a blend of real materials doing real things — and the wheel teaches itself: primaries from single sources, secondaries from honest overdyes, tertiaries from precise dips. The player who learns the wheel learns the chemistry; the player who learns the chemistry can glaze.

**The One Grammar holds here too:** color = frequency you can see. The dye wheel and the glaze line are the Stone pillar's light-ward branch — the pattern is the spectrum.

## PART ONE — THE DYE WHEEL (the twelve)

**The primaries (three anchors, now live in the Material entity):**
| Color | Source | The honest chemistry |
|-------|--------|----------------------|
| RED | MADDER (the root) | The alizarin red — the ancient world's cloth; three years in the ground before the root is red enough; washes to pink without the mordant |
| YELLOW | WELD (the tops) | Luteolin — the yellowest yellow the old world knew; the tapestry yellow that holds for centuries |
| BLUE | INDIGO (the leaf) / WOAD (the north's branch) | The blue that breathes — the vat is living chemistry, a tended fermentation; woad is the same molecule from a cold climate |

**THE MORDANT (the law under the wheel): THE COLOR IS ONLY AS PERMANENT AS ITS HOLD.** Oak Gall (the tannin) and the iron salts fix the dye to the cloth — the anti-superstition law made visible: unmordanted color is a promise; mordanted color is a fact. The mordant bench is the wheel's first lesson.

**The secondaries (the overdyes — cloth dipped twice):**
| Color | The blend | The note |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| ORANGE | Madder + Weld | The warm blend — the sunset of the dye garden |
| GREEN | Weld overdyed in Indigo | THE GREEN IS ALWAYS AN OVERDYE (nature's green and the vat's green are two different chemistry families — the wheel's own secret) |
| PURPLE | Madder overdyed in Indigo | The north's Tyrian — no murex needed; the purple of the patched cloak |

**The tertiaries (the precise dips — the master's range):** red-orange (madder-heavy), yellow-orange (weld-heavy), yellow-green (the light green dip), blue-green (the deep green dip), blue-violet (the indigo-heavy purple), red-violet (the madder-heavy purple). Six more recipes, each a ratio, each a lesson in how a color LEANS. The tertiary tier is where the player stops following recipes and starts making them (the graduation: the wheel is learned when the player can hit yellow-green blind).

## PART TWO — THE GLAZE LINE (color becomes mineral)

**The bridge law: GLAZE IS THE DYE WHEEL AT KILN TEMPERATURE.** The same chemistry, the same wheel — but the cloth's plants are replaced by the earth's metals, and the fire decides what the color becomes.

**The mineral colorants (the stone-pillar primaries):**
| Glaze color | The mineral | The chemistry |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| BLUE | Cobalt | The blue that survives every fire — unchanged by heat, the potter's constant |
| GREEN | Copper | Copper goes green in the OXIDATION fire — patient, predictable |
| RED/METALLIC | Copper (again) | The same copper pulls RED and metallic in the REDUCTION fire — the same element, two answers, depending on how you starve the flame (the raku secret) |
| CELADON/RUST | Iron | Iron green in reduction, rust in oxidation — the glaze that depends on what the kiln breathes |
| PURPLE-BLACK | Manganese | The deep glaze — the purple's mineral ghost |
| WHITE | Tin | The historic white — the ground the other colors stand on |

**The firing methods (the game modes — each a different ask of the fire):**
- **OXIDATION (the patient fire):** the kiln breathes freely; copper is green, iron is rust; the predictable path — the teaching kiln
- **REDUCTION (the starved fire):** the kiln is starved of oxygen; the flame pulls it from the clay itself, and the colors invert — celadon is born here
- **RAKU (the drama — the amazing-but-simple process):** the pot is pulled GLOWING from the kiln and plunged into sawdust and air; the copper flashes metallic, the glaze crackles with the shock, and no two pots answer the same. THE SIMPLE PROCESS THAT CAN'T BE INDUSTRIALIZED — the perfect Inn craft: fast, dramatic, honest, slightly dangerous, and absolutely original every time. (The raku moment is the game's best single-button payoff: one pull, one plunge, one held breath, and the reveal.)

**BONE CHINA (the historical end of the line):** kaolin + stone + BONE ASH — the Bone Room's own contribution (the Ossuary recipes' Bone material, ground fine and fired white; the structure that outlasted the body, made luminous). Bone china is the Charnel Cathedral's tea service: the game's quiet way of saying even the bones keep working.

**THE BLEND LINE (the wheel crosses into glaze):** the glaze room uses the SAME WHEEL — cobalt-green from copper+cobalt overlap, the blue-black of cobalt+manganese, the iron-copper teadust... the player who learned the dye ratios in Part One brings them to the kiln and they half-hold (mineral blends are wilder than plant blends — the wheel's lessons transfer, then the fire edits). THE CHEMISTRY TIES THE TWO CRAFTS: one grammar, two temperatures.
