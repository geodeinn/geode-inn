## WHAT THIS CHAIN IS

The Spiral Gallery is the Inn's museum: a Guggenheim-style ramp, chronological, from the ochre hands of the caves to the illuminated gold of the pre-industrial — one species' single continuous sentence, spoken in pigment and stone and bronze and ink. The chain's thesis: ART IS THE LONG HANDPRINT — the record of everyone who ever said WE WERE HERE, and the gallery is where the Inn keeps the whole conversation.

The teaching layer is the Inn's own spine: making is the core verb. The player who walks the spiral walks the whole archive's thesis — from the first makers to the last — and finds, at the top, the standing question the neutrality law built: the gallery ends BEFORE the industrial age on purpose, and the last room is unfinished on purpose. Every generation adds its own hand. The future will look back and add the player's.

**Giver:** THE MASON (the builder NPC, at the ramp's lower terminus beside the Bone Room — the gallery's keeper: the one who BUILT the shelf for the record).
**Access:** The lower terminus (start with the oldest) or the upper (start with the newest) — the ramp runs both ways, and the gallery reads differently in each direction.
**Prerequisites:** None — the museum is the Inn's most open zone. The record belongs to everyone.
**Reward spine:** the walking → the hands → the doors → the turning → the gold → the unfinished.

## THE CHAIN

### Quest 1 — "The Lower Terminus"
The Mason's introduction, and the gallery's law: the ramp only spirals — every step is both a climb and a circle, and the chronology ASCENDS from the deep (the catacombs' law: the oldest at the bottom — the Bone Room's neighbor holds the first marks ever made). The first quest is one full revolution, walked slow: the Mason's instruction is the museum's whole ethic: "Don't read the wall. Meet it." The player learns the gallery's pace — the only zone in the Inn with a suggested walking speed, and the speed is slow.
**Reward:** The pace (the gallery's walking rhythm — the contemplative gate that the Nook Effect's museum sibling: the player can enter the slow state in any future gallery chamber).

### Quest 2 — "The Ochre Hands"
The prehistoric chamber, and the Inn's oldest artifact class: the handprints. Real science (the teaching law): the cave stencils of Chauvet and Lascaux — pigment blown around an outstretched hand, thirty thousand years and more, the NEGATIVE SPACE preserved (the hand is gone; the hand's AROUND remains). The chain's revelation lands here: this is where the Inn's whole permanence economy was born — the walls keep shape (Bosch), the forest keeps sound, the keel keeps words, and the ochre hands are the FIRST of them: the original imprint, the first we-were-here. And the player's ceremony: at the chamber's blank stone, the player makes their own — ochre, breath, outstretched hand, the oldest spell in the house. The Mason watches the way one watches a christening.
**Reward:** THE HANDPRINT (the player's ochre hand on the prehistoric chamber's wall — permanent, per the Anchor Key precedent; the youngest handprint next to the oldest; the Inn's imprint economy closed where it began).

### Quest 3 — "The Nine Doors"
The Muses' chambers: nine acoustic portals behind the Muses' nooks on the cosmology level, each opening onto a Spiral Gallery chamber in its assigned artist's style. The quest is the restoration: some chambers have gone quiet (the portals' frequencies drifted — the acoustic architecture needs retuning), and the player — with the tuned pours' training and the counter-hum — retunes each door. Each restored chamber plays its Muse's domain: the hall of epic, the hall of dance, the hall of astronomy-in-art, the whole patronage system made walkable. The Muses' law, learned by walking: art was never one thing. It was nine ways of listening to the same world.
**Reward:** The nine restored (the portals permanent-open — the Muses' patronage features unlock gallery-side).

### Quest 4 — "The Turning"
The middle of the ramp, and the Escher chamber: the stretch where the spiral doubles back on itself visually and the chronology seems to run both directions at once — the same artifact readable as a beginning or an ending depending on the walker's direction (the Escher stairs' museum sibling; the Between's geometry, indoors). And past it, the Rothko room: the chamber where the DATES fall away — color fields with no chronology at all, presence without era, the paintings that stop being history and become weather. The quest is the turning: the player walks the middle stretch in both directions and experiences the gallery's deepest lesson — the record is not a line. The record is a spiral: every ending walks back past every beginning, and the meeting is the meaning.
**Reward:** The turn (the double-sight: the player can read any artifact in the Inn as both a beginning and an ending — the Spiral sight, the gallery's own perception layer).

### Quest 5 — "The Gold"
The illuminated manuscripts' chamber, and the family beat: the Bard's country — Bill's craft, the Book of Kells lineage, the hand's slowest work (one page a season, the gold laid by breath, the lampwork the tradesmen's guild still keeps). The chamber holds the Inn's manuscript treasures under Carr's light, and the quest is a making-lesson, not a reading one: the Bard (or the guild's master illuminator) teaches the player ONE drop of the craft — a single illuminated capital, the player's initial, worked slow, with the gold breath applied. The lesson the chamber keeps: the archive's treasures were made by hands that thought slowly in a world that moved slowly — and the slow thinking is IN the gold. You can't rush the gold. The gold is the rush.
**Reward:** THE ILLUMINATED CAPITAL (the player's initial, hand-worked in the guild's style — the player's mark for the manuscript record; joins the handprint, the tone, the toast, the imprint: the Inn's five personal permanences, one per craft).

### Quest 6 — "The Unfinished Room"
The top of the spiral, the pre-industrial terminus — and the chain's ending that is a question: the last chamber is EMPTY. Unfinished walls, no exhibits, the light ready and nothing hanging. The Mason's finale, delivered with the builder's plainness: the gallery stops before the industrial age BY DESIGN (the neutrality law — the archive keeps what predates the machine age, and the last two centuries stay off the walls), and the last room is unfinished ON PURPOSE: "Every generation adds its own hand. Yours isn't ready yet. Neither was mine." The room is the Inn's humility and its Window echo — the mystery that stays open on purpose. The player stands in the empty room at the top of the spiral, next to the whole species' record, and the zone's only mechanic is the oldest one: leave a mark anyway. The player's choice — the blank wall receives whatever the player's five permanences have made them: a handprint, a line, a note, a toast, a tone. Any. The future visitors will walk past it and wonder who.
**Reward:** THE UNFINISHED (the last room's founding mark: the player's mark — first on the wall of the room every future generation will fill; and the standing invitation: the gallery grows when the era's story is ready to be looked back on).
