## WHAT THIS ZONE IS — THE CRITTER LAW

The Annex has outgrown the reading nook it was drawn as. It is not a room with shelves anymore. It is an ORGANISM — a knowledge-jungle that grows, migrates, and rearranges itself around what it collects. The shelves move between visits. The study is never where you left it. The door out is never far, but it is never the same door. The Annex does this the way a jungle does: not dramatically, not with warnings — one day the path is simply somewhere else, and the new path was always obviously the right one, and you cannot remember the old one.

**THE CRITTER LAW (proposed canon, from Krista's own words — "its own shape-shifting critter"):** The Annex is a living archive. It reorganizes in response to three things: new knowledge (a book retrieved is a book the jungle grows around), the reader (the shelves rearrange to place the book you need next at eye level — sometimes before you know you need it), and Naut (the only fixed point — the study finds Naut, not the other way around; the armchair is the anchor, because the deep likes its librarian where the librarian is).

**Why Rousseau makes the shifting bearable:** the horror of a shifting space is usually the dark — you can't see what moved. The Annex refuses that. Every rearrangement happens in impossible clarity: bright, flat, sharp, every spine readable. The player SEES the jungle grow. The seeing is the wonder, not the fear. A shifting zone in any other aesthetic would be a labyrinth; in Rousseau it is a garden that pays attention to you.

## THE WORLD IT HOUSES

### The Study — "The Dream"
Naut's armchair, the fireplace, the spectacles, the window. The domestic and the cosmic in the same frame, equally sharp, refusing to choose. The study is the Annex's only fixed room — the anchor around which the rest grows. THE WINDOW is the study's one unsettling inheritance: through it is not any zone of the Inn — a flat blue sky, shapes that might be mountains, a world without perspective. The gap in the Inn's architecture where something else looks in. Or where Naut looks out. The window is never explained. **RULED (Krista, 2026-09-10): the window stays unexplained — the Sleeping Gypsy's moon law.**

### The Three Deep Archives — the retrieval worlds of the Borrower's Ledger
The Annex houses three archives that are not rooms but WORLDS — the deepest libraries of the Inn's other systems, each reachable through the Annex's back shelves, each its own mini-zone:

**1. THE DROWNED LIBRARY** (the water archive) — the library where books are preserved IN water, not against it. A flooded reading cathedral: shelves descending in terraces below the waterline, the texts kept legible by the clear mineral water (real preservation science: bogs and cenotes preserve organics where air rots them — the science is the lesson). The reader dives to read; the pages part like kelp. The deepest terrace holds the books the water will only show to readers who have earned the water system's trust. Tie: the Water Zone's completion layer — the Drowned Library is Charon's river's other shore, the library the drowned city kept.

**2. THE NIFLHEIM ARCHIVE** (the mist archive) — the Norse cold-shelf: runestones and ice-core texts kept in the deep freeze that is the only honest preservation for the oldest writing. The archive's law: what is carved in stone and frozen in mist cannot be edited — the archive that keeps original words. Real science echo: ice cores literally preserve atmospheric history, layer by layer, year by year — the archive that remembers the weather of the world. Tie: the Norse Realms, the rune-script lineage (the Cave City's root language is a cousin script).

**3. THE SACRED CENOTE** (the well archive) — the Maya archive: a sunken limestone well, the books of the surface world delivered to the water below by ritual and care, preserved by the chemistry of limestone and stillness. The cenote's law: some knowledge is given to the well ON PURPOSE — the archive as offering, not storage. Real archaeology echo: the actual sacred cenotes held what was given, not lost — the deposits were deliberate, the library was always a library. Tie: the Peruvian/Maya zone lineage, the Sacrifice-keeper's jurisdiction.

### The Restricted Shelf — QUIRE'S MARGIN (RULED 2026-09-10)
The Annex's deepest growing edge: the shelf of texts the jungle grows AROUND, never over — the restricted Archive texts Quire reads in the margins of, hushed, always looking over his shoulder. This shelf is Quire's territory inside the Annex, distinct from Naut's study — two scholars, one jungle, different shelves, never sharing a fire. **RULED (Krista, 2026-09-10): the scholars never acknowledge each other.** The two pass like the books pass — in silence, in margins, each certain the other is the more dangerous reader.

### The Borrowers
The Ledger's names: the books checked out and never returned, by borrowers whose names are shapes, not words. The Borrowers are the Annex's cryptid population — glimpsed between shelves, never twice the same shape, each carrying a book somewhere into the green. Not villains. Patrons. The Annex lets them borrow because the Annex knows where every book is, always, the way the jungle knows where every leaf is. The final ledger quest is about ONE book that even the Annex cannot find.

## THE QUEST CHAIN — "THE BORROWER'S LEDGER"
(The Hungry Lion, renamed in the sweep — the retrieval is the hunt, the texts are the antelope, the deep water is the jungle at night. The danger is VISIBLE — Rousseau's law: no lurking, only clarity.)

**Quest 1 — "The Sleeping Gypsy"** — The first encounter, played exactly as the aesthetic doc wrote it: the player turns a corner, and there is Naut — four feet tall, spectacles, armchair, fire, reading. The moment holds the way the painting holds. Then Naut looks up, adjusts the spectacles, and the lion becomes a librarian. First line: "Fascinating." (Naut's canon hook.)
**Reward:** The library card (the player is now a patron of the Annex — the shelves begin rearranging FOR them).

**Quest 2 — "The Ledger"** — Naut opens the Borrower's Ledger: the map of every book the Annex houses, including the three deep archives. The player learns the Critter Law by watching it work — the Ledger's map redraws itself as the shelves move, and the player learns to read the shifting, not fight it. The library card is the only map; the map is alive.
**Reward:** The reading of the shift (the player can now track the Annex — a shifting-zone navigation skill unique to this zone).

**Quest 3 — "The Drowned Shelf"** — The first retrieval: a text in the Drowned Library. The dive, the terraces, the clear water, the book that only opens below the waterline. The water system's trust carries over: the player who has earned the deep's trust reads freely; the player who hasn't is watched by something patient and large that does not mind waiting.
**Reward:** The drowned text (a BookTranslation unlock for the Archive — the retrieved text joins the reading room's public shelf).

**Quest 4 — "The Mist Shelf"** — The second retrieval: a runestone text in the Niflheim Archive. The cold-shelf, the original words, the reading of what was carved and frozen. The archive's guardian: an Einherjar hold-guard (BATCH HOLD: the Einherjar voice blocks are held for the Valhalla layer pass — this quest wires to that batch when it lifts).
**Reward:** The frozen text (plus a rune-script fragment that echoes the Cave City's root language — the languages tie across the deep archives).

**Quest 5 — "The Given Shelf"** — The third retrieval: the cenote. The player learns the cenote's law — the books were not lost, they were GIVEN — and must make the exchange the well requires: something of the player's own, given on purpose, to receive the text on purpose. The first true trade in a knowledge economy that has no currency. (The Treetop Marketplace's no-currency law, echoed in water.)
**Reward:** The given text (and the cenote's mark on the player — the well remembers givers).

**Quest 6 — "The Book the Annex Cannot Find"** — The finale. The Ledger's one red entry: a book checked out by a borrower whose name is a shape the Ledger cannot draw twice — the one book even the jungle has lost track of. The player follows the shape-name's trail through all three deep archives, through the shifting green, to the study of the window — and finds the book sitting on the sill of the window to nowhere, as if someone outside set it down. The player takes the book to Naut. Naut opens it, reads one line, and closes it slowly. Naut does not say what the line says. Naut says: "Fascinating." The book joins the Restricted Shelf. The window is never explained. The quest is complete.
**Reward:** THE ANCHOR KEY (the player may now fix one shelf — one shelf, one visit, never moved while the player is away: the player's own permanent shelf in the living library, whatever it holds. The shapeshifting zone trusts the player with one unmoving thing).

## TONE LAW
