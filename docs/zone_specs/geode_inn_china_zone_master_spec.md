## "The Mulan Aesthetic" — The Terracotta Army

---

## WHAT THE CHINA ZONE IS

The Chinese zone's visual design is inspired by Disney's animated Mulan (1998) — not the live-action remake. The animated original that used ink-wash painting as its visual foundation, that painted mountains as living ink, that treated cherry blossoms as emotional punctuation, and that understood the difference between beauty and prettiness.

The art direction is not "Asian-inspired" — it is Chinese painting in MOTION. The ink-wash mountains are the mountains of shanshui. The cherry blossoms are the blossoms of Tang dynasty poetry. The zone respects the aesthetic tradition it borrows from, and the respect is what makes the borrowing beautiful.

The zone IS a Chinese painting, and the player is inside it.

## VISUAL DESIGN

| Element | Aesthetic |
|---------|----------|
| The Great Wall (approach) | Ink-wash. The Wall as a dragon's spine across the mountains. The boundary between known and unknown. |
| The Mountains (shanshui) | Layered ink: closest = dark/sharp, middle = lighter/softer, far = mist/ghost-brushstrokes. Cherry blossoms as narrative seasonal markers. |
| The Army Pit | 8,000 warriors, each face unique. Ink-wash rendering — clay texture, brushwork faces, ink-dot eyes, brushstroke mouths. Amber light = aged silk. The pit is a painting you can walk into. |
| The Mercury Ocean | The "water" of shanshui. Silver, mirror-smooth. Below the surface: liquid calligraphy, living brushstrokes. The warrior's living faces swim past as ink portraits. |

## THE ONE WHO IS AWAKE

Among 8,000 sleeping warriors, one is awake. The Mulan figure — not the Disney Mulan specifically, but the QUALITY of Mulan. The person who goes to war not because they are called but because someone they love is going and they cannot let them go alone.

The warrior who stayed. The one who has been awake among the sleeping army for two thousand years. The wakefulness is not a curse — it is a CHOICE. "I will be the one who stays. I will be the one who is here. I will be the one who remembers when everyone else is sleeping."

Casting: Ming-Na Wen. The voice of the animated Mulan. Not the young Mulan of 1998 — the woman that voice became. Determination without flash. The engine who makes the scene work without demanding you notice. The elder Mulan who carries the quality: "Maybe I didn't go for my father. Maybe what I really wanted was to prove I could do things right."

The voice: warm, slightly husky, unhurried. The Ming-Na Wen voice that asked "When will my reflection show who I am inside?" and then spent the rest of the answer answering it: the reflection already showed who she was. She just had to stop looking for someone else's reflection and see her own.
