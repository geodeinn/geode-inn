## ENTERING THE CAMPGROUNDS
**FREQUENCY:** The heartbeat is heard differently out here. Indoors the 50 BPM saturates everything; outdoors it becomes CARRIED — the lighthouse beam sweeps to it, the tavern hearth keeps it, the forge rings it in triplets, and the tide breathes it at the shore. The Pulse Line on the horizon's light. The player who stops moving can feel the Inn behind them like a hand at their back. The first open sky: the same sky the Astronomer reads upstairs, seen from the ground, from grass, with a forge nearby — the Campgrounds are where the sky and the Inn meet at eye level.
**AESTHETIC:** A Renaissance Faire — canvas tan, grass green, ale amber, forge orange, banner red, market-stripe purple. The tradesman's linework (heavy cel-shaded): the Campgrounds are where the craftspeople live, and the craft IS the costume. Rackham's trees at the treeline; Bierstadt's light in the open field at dusk; Del Toro's worn asymmetry in every stall (each one repaired by hand, each repair a story). The Huntsman Tavern is a proper building — log walls, stone fireplace, a brass bell over the door.
**AUDIO:** The Inn's LOUDEST zone in variety, not volume: the market's murmur, the forge's ring, the tavern's laugh, the wolf's occasional single note, the loch's lap at the shore — the faire's full crowd layered as one fused sound (the Tool law again: variety, not noise).

The Campgrounds present six options. All live, all forever.
**If you cross the Market Field, go to OPTION 1.**
**If you enter the Huntsman Tavern, go to OPTION 2.**
**If you watch Luna work, go to OPTION 3.**
**If you approach the Forge, go to OPTION 4.**
**If you follow the wolf into the Woods, go to OPTION 5.**
**If you walk down to the shore — the loch, the dock, the lighthouse light — go to OPTION 6.**
**If you claim a plot of your own — the homestead — go to OPTION 7.**
(If you go back inside, the front door is where you left it — and the Campgrounds' whole point is that the door is easier to approach from out here. The Inn's doors go both ways.)

### OPTION 1 — YOU CROSS THE MARKET FIELD
The open center, stalls in a loose semicircle around the Inn's front entrance. THE INN'S EXTERIOR ECONOMY: the copper bead is earned and spent here — the craft economy's working face (the Main Floor market is the museum; the Market Field is the workshop's storefront).
THE STALLS (canon): the Stone Vendor (raw, unpolished stones for the Bead Board — the player buys rough material and must process it; the craft chain begins here), the Tool Vendor (jeweler's tools, tray materials, findings), the General Store (torches, rope, rations — the expedition supplies the deeper zones will ask for), and the Bookmark Stall (an eccentric selling bookmarks to a library the vendor claims is infinite; the eccentric is correct).
THE STALL-ROTATION LAW (the ruling gate): new stalls appear as the player unlocks zones — the Fae stall only appears after the portal opens; the parks' vendors arrive with the chains. The field grows with the player. The market is never finished because the world isn't.
- Camera: Del Toro's crowd drift; each stall gets one held, framed portrait-shot (every vendor is a character, not a shopkeeper)
- Audio: each stall has one signature sound (the stone bin's tumble, the forge bellows from afar, the bookmark pages' riffle); the faire's murmur rides 6dB under the forge
- Seed: the Artisan Thief is home here (the selkie ashore among the trade); the Librarian browses the General Store's bookshelf corner; the Sentinel notes the field's single entrance-gate sightline and approves

### OPTION 2 — YOU ENTER THE HUNTSMAN TAVERN
Jay's domain — the Campgrounds' social anchor: former Marine, long black hair, Cherokee cheekbones, Voltaire-goth aesthetic, loud, warm, no-nonsense. THE TAVERN IS THE STEPPING STONE (the ruling gate): the welcome before the door — some players camp outside before they can go inside, and Jay's tavern is the middle step between the world and the Inn. The brass bell rings when anyone enters; the wolf's blanket-corner is by the door (the wolf is always there when Jay is, and sometimes there when Jay isn't).
THE COMMUNAL TABLES: no private booths — the layout forces sociability; you sit next to whoever's there. Jay's own brew on tap ("Huntsman's Gold"); the pickled eggs nobody eats but Jay keeps anyway (a running gag with no explanation, forever).
THE MAP WALL: Jay's annotated zone map, updated with traveler reports — "Water Zone: rough seas this week." "Fae Kingdom: Seelie court in session." The Map Wall is the Campgrounds' news organ; the Inn's biggest gossip post lives OUTSIDE the Rockies' jurisdiction, and the Rockies resent it.
THE QUEST — "The Wolf and the Welcome" (canon): the wolf acting strangely at the treeline → the tracking mini-game → the Traveler's Clearing → Jay's quiet welcome ("The door's open. The ale's cold. The wolf doesn't bite." [the wolf sneezes] "Much.") → the travelers try the tavern, not the Inn. The quest's truth: the Campgrounds' purpose is the welcome before the door. Rewards: Jay's Ale Recipe + THE WOLF'S WHISTLE (a bone whistle summoning the wolf to the player's side in any outdoor zone — the wolf can track anything).
THE PARENTAL GATE (standing law): TJ and Gabby are Jay's and Bethany's kids — they do not appear in or around the tavern until BOTH parents approve. The tavern stays adult until the gate opens.
- Camera: the tavern in warm Kubrick symmetry when Jay stands at the bar's center; Del Toro handheld when the wolf decides things
- Audio: Jay IS the tavern's voice (when Jay is present, the room's noise organizes around that ~100Hz projection); the brass bell's ring is the room's door-word
- Seed: the Bard Blowhard's war stories play best here (the loud room rewards the loud teller); the Chameleon Druid takes the corner seat and is welcome in it

### OPTION 3 — YOU WATCH LUNA WORK
Luna's Tent-Studio: canvas with the sides rolled up — open to the market, open to the weather, open to visitors. THE MAKER: the zen counterpoint to Jay's loud; wild brown hair, raised by hippies, the jeweler's calm. THE BENCH is the room's altar: stone shaped, polished, set. WATCHING LUNA WORK IS A MEDITIVE IDLE (canon): the animation is a rest-state for the player — the campgrounds' answer to the Library Nook's nook-state. Luna's silence is peace, not uncertainty.
THE STONE LIBRARY: Luna's personal collection, organized by Mohs hardness — the Inn's mineralogy classroom (the science is the lesson; the shelf is the curriculum). THE DISPLAY BOARD: finished pieces, each a "Wearable Song" — a bead arrangement encoding a melody (the Bead Composer's soul made visible). THE COMMISSION BOOK: pending orders browsable — other NPCs' requests, some of them years old.
THE QUEST — "The Hands That Remember" (canon): Luna recognizes a stone the player carries — the one matching a commission she could never complete. The long Luna-pause. "Where did you find that?" The player may give the stone (the giving is the quest; the Maker's unfinished work finished by a stranger's find).
- Camera: held, Kubrick-still at the bench (the workshop is the Inn's most patient frame); the stone in macro at the moment of the reveal
- Audio: stone-on-stone, the tumbler's slow rotation — Luna's instrument is the sound of CRAFT itself, and it is tuned to the room
- Seed: the Artisan Thief and Luna never compete — the selkie found the field's one bench that needs no protecting; the Tinkering Weeaboo hyperfocuses on the Stone Library and Luna lets the hyperfocus be the whole visit

### OPTION 4 — YOU APPROACH THE FORGE
The Blacksmith's open-air forge at the Campgrounds' edge: smoke, sparks, the ring of hammer on metal. THE WORKING FIRE (canon distinction): the Firekeeper's hearth is for stories; the Forge fire is ACTIVE — hot, purposeful, purpose-driven. Two fires, two truths, one heartbeat under both.
THE ANVIL: custom findings commissioned here — clasps, spacers, wire wraps, each with its own frequency data (the Bead Board's structural metal palette is born at this anvil). THE METAL LIBRARY: every metal in the Inn's jewelry system, with frequency tables — the copper that carries the market, the steel that carries the wolf's whistle, the silver that carries the songs.
THE QUEST — "The Metal That Sings" (canon): the Blacksmith teaches the player to hear metal's resonance — tuning findings to stones (the metal sings when it matches). The Inn's craft chain: rough stone (Market Field) → shape (Luna's bench) → structure (the Forge) → song (the Display Board).
- Camera: the forge in Del Toro's ember-light; the hammer strikes on the beat — the forge is the only Campgrounds sound that plays 50 BPM explicitly (the blacksmith has never counted; the blacksmith has never needed to)
- Audio: the ring of the anvil tuned to the Inn's key; each strike a bell-tone; the metal library literally playable
- Seed: the Bard Blowhard gets the fire-dancer's drum here; the Sentinel commissions a clasp that has never once failed

### OPTION 5 — YOU FOLLOW THE WOLF INTO THE WOODS
The treeline: the boundary between the known and the not-yet-known. The woods are not dangerous — they are UNKNOWN, which is different, and the game teaches the difference. The wolf goes in; most people don't; the player with the Whistle goes wherever the wolf goes.
THE WOLF'S PATHS: trails the wolf has made — hidden items, stones, and NPCs along the way. THE TRAVELER'S CLEARING: the campfire where the afraid and the waiting sit before entering; Jay's quest lives here. THE FORTUNE TELLER'S TENT: deep purple and gold, at the woods' edge, farthest from the Inn — THE STONES THAT SPEAK (the ruling gate): the Fortune Teller reads the player's collected stones, and the stones answer from the player's own EchoMemory — every reading true because it is the player's own history told back. The reading is never a prediction; it is a RECOGNITION. (The tent is farthest from the Inn on purpose: some truths need the door out of sight.)
THE WOODS CONNECT: the Fae Kingdom's border (the thin places begin here), and the wolf's paths are the map's margin notes. The Fortune Teller's tent placement = the Campgrounds' deepest interior.
- Camera: Hitchcock's held suspense at the treeline (the wolf's stare is the game's quietest jump-scare that never scares); Spielberg's awe at the first thin-place shimmer
- Audio: the faire fades behind; the woods have the Inn's quietest outdoor layer — wind in Rackham trees, the wolf's single note, and under it all, still, the heartbeat
- Seed: the Chameleon Druid is at home in the woods (the shapeshifter's garden); the Librarian argues gently with the Fortune Teller about whether recognition is prediction and loses beautifully

### OPTION 6 — YOU WALK DOWN TO THE SHORE
The loch, the dock, the lighthouse light — the Campgrounds' water-face. THE FIRST SKY meets the FIRST TIDE here (the ruling gate): ZEKE'S TIDE CHART is the player's first clock — the Beat Line's teaching shore. All the Inn's timing games (the sinking stones, the cave gates, the Lighthouse sweep) are ONE system, and the shore is where the player first hears it: the tide is not ambience, the tide is a METRONOME. The dock is where the water-system quests begin.
THE PIRATE AT THE DOCK: Zeke — the bodhrán, the tide chart, the sixty-year friendship with the Bard, married to the Siren. Zeke keeps the beat for the whole water system ("The Drum That Plays Itself" — 50 BPM is the Moon's pulse, and the Pirate is the instrument). THE SIREN IN THE WATER: Katya — dark red curls on land, tentacles beneath, guardian of the 7-Layer Deep; the sister who stayed in the water, the voice that lures people to the truth they've been avoiding. The wolf has been seen at the treeline looking toward the sea (Jay says the wolf "has a friend out there"). The loch itself: Monster of the Deep's home water — the loch's deep residents school beneath the dock pilings, visible on clear-still days.
PERSEPHONE IS THE TIDE (canon, the tie-in): the surface caves breathe with the season — autumn carries her down, spring carries her home — and the shore is where the player can watch the tide and feel that it is breathing. The lighthouse beam sweeps at 50 BPM: the shore's own pulse, the fixed point (the Owl keeps the light; the Beacon is earned here).
- Camera: the widest shot in the Inn (Spielberg's horizon); the lighthouse sweep in Kubrick's one-axis frame; Katya's surface-approach in Del Toro's wet light
- Audio: the loch's lap, the dock's hollow knock underfoot, the bodhrán somewhere out on the water keeping 50 BPM, and the tide breathing beneath everything — the shore is the water system's overture
- Seed: all classes — the shore belongs to everyone; the Artisan Thief (the selkie) feels the water call and answers differently than the others (the selkie's dive and return); the Sentinel watches the horizon the way the wolf watches the sea

### OPTION 7 — YOU CLAIM A PLOT OF YOUR OWN (THE HOMESTEAD)
The Campgrounds' outermost ring and deepest offer: the player stops being a guest and becomes a BUILDER. THE SIM DYNAMIC (the building system master spec, the village & community system): the Inn is the seed of a player-built world — the Inn is the permanent center, and everything around it is the players'. Plots are organized in concentric rings: closer to the Inn = more expensive, more prestigious. The faire's edge is the first ring a new player can afford — and that is deliberate (the welcome before the door extends to the welcome before the building).
**THE DON'T STARVE GATHERING LOOP** (7 base materials — wood, stone, clay, sand, ore, hemp, thatching — plus 17 derived composites): the player gathers at the Inn's own pace. GATHERING TEMPO IS 50 BPM — the player works at the heartbeat's speed; the pace is adequate. The gathering zones are the corridors themselves (the Bayou's Don't Starve aesthetic is the style's home: cypress, bog moss, bog oak, Spanish moss).
**THE FIVE BUILDING TIERS** (the alchemical progression): Lean-to (basic shelter) → Cottage (a real home) → Workshop Home (home + crafting station) → Estate (a grand home) → Ancestral Hall (a legacy home that persists across sessions). The Ragana's verdicts escalate with the tiers ("Adequate" is the ceiling, and the ceiling is the point).
**TOWN FORMATION — the Inn grows because the players grew it:** Homestead (1 — the Cartographer notes it) → Hamlet (3-5 — ERIK BRINGS THE COMMUNAL FIRE, the gathering point) → Village (6-10 — appears on the Silk Road map as a trade stop; market square forms; the mead begins) → Town (11-20 — gets a name chosen by the builders; the Host visits: "Room's ready.") → City (21-50 — gets a CHARTER FROM THE HOST; the Ragana comes to measure; a gargoyle takes residence, 111Hz protection) → WONDER (50+ — mapped in translucent crystal; a landmark on the scale of the five hubs; THE INN HAS GROWN).
**THE NOOK LAW:** sitting in a finished building triggers the nook effect — the building IS a nook the player made, and it hums at 42Hz (the Song, audible only when the player sits still inside something they built).
- Camera: the homestead in time-lapse orbit shots (Kubrick's symmetries assembling at the Inn's pace); Erik's communal fire in one held Spielberg wide (the first shared hearth is always the widest shot)
- Audio: the building hum at 42Hz in finished structures; the gathering sounds as the zone's work-song (felling, quarrying, digging, panning, smelting, spinning, thatching — the seven gathers, one rhythm)
- Seed: all classes — the homestead is everyone's endgame and no one's requirement (anti-FOMO: the plot never expires, the rings never close); the Artisan Thief builds the Estate the way the selkie builds anything — slowly, ashore, by hand; the Tinkering Weeaboo's Workshop Home is the nook the whole class was named for

## THE CONVERGENCE — THE HANDS
However the Campgrounds are walked, the player leaves with the same additions: an economy that grows with them, a welcome that meets them before the door, a craft chain from rough stone to wearable song, a companion who knows the woods, and a clock — the first clock, the tide, kept by a pirate who is also the drum. The Campgrounds are the Inn's HANDS: the part that reaches outward, touches the world, makes things and welcomes the people the door hasn't met yet.
The next doors: inside, the MAIN FLOOR (the hearth) · outward and forever, THE HOMESTEAD (the plot never expires; the rings never close) · up the coast path, the LIGHTHOUSE and the surface caves · out on the water, the DOCK quests and the 7-LAYER DEEP · down, the ANNEX ALTAR — or stay at the fire pit. The fire dancers come out at night, and the faire's best hour is the one with the sky in it.
