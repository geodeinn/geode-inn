## THE CORE IDEA (Krista's, verbatim in spirit)

A learning-by-trial-and-error game of mixing elements to find the proper shield for each.

## WHY THIS IS TRUE CHEMISTRY (the pedagogy underneath)

The "shield" is real. Most elements do not exist free in nature — they are too reactive, too eager, too dangerous alone. What exists is the SHIELDED form: the compound that holds them safely. Sodium explodes in water; chlorine is a poison gas; bound together they are table salt, the safest thing in the kitchen. Each one shields the other. THE GAME'S ONE LAW OF TRUTH: every mix in the game is real chemistry — the trial-and-error teaches the actual periodic table. Everything branches from it (the Spine Law, made playable).

The flagship first puzzle: Na + Cl. The reveal writes itself: the two most alarming elements in the cabinet make the thing on the dinner table. THE HIDDEN MATERIALS tie-in: the salt dossier (Khewra, Via Salaria, Paracelsus's Body of matter) is the shield found — the first completed shield unlocks the salt reading in the periodic system (Gap 2 of the sync spec, now wired as the game's tutorial victory).

THE GOLD EXCEPTION: gold is the only common metal found free in the ground — it needs no shield because it tarnishes at nothing. The gold puzzle inverts the game: the element that was never in danger. The player arrives expecting to shield it and finds it already complete. (The Lycurgus cup reading rides along: the one time gold took a disguise, it was to tell stories in two colors.)

## THE LOOP (No-Fail, Anti-Skinner, Steady Light throughout)

1. THE CABINET: the player picks two element charms from the shelf (the ElementCharm records the player has already collected — the game is the charm collection made playable).
2. THE MIX: pour them into the nautilus apparatus. Amber-violet glow. The reaction plays out honestly.
3. THE HONEST FAILURE: a wrong mix is never a red X — it is an observation. The mix hisses, or puffs a smoke ring, or turns a new color, or ripples the water. Each failure yields a FIELD NOTE: "it hissed at water" is a clue (that element is eager). The player learns the element's PERSONALITY through failure — the same way real chemistry was learned for four thousand years. Trial and error IS the curriculum; the game never mocks a wrong mix, it just tells the truth about what happened.
4. THE SHIELD: the correct compound crystallizes gently (never a strobe flash — amber sine bloom, the Steady Light law) and the charm earns its SHIELDED STATE: a small shield glyph etched on the ElementCharm record, plus the compound's true name and story (salt, quartz, feldspar, rust, bronze...). The shielded charm can now be displayed in the vaults stable-side-up.
5. NO STREAKS, NO TIMERS, NO SCORE DECAY: a shield found is kept forever. The lab waits. The Anti-Skinner law holds absolutely — the reward is the knowing, and the knowing compounds (the Inn's whole economy, again).

## THE STAFF (the lab crew, canon as of tonight)

The six sprites run the game's face: the BEAKER SPRITE fetches the chosen charms (carefully; occasionally the charm gets fetched twice), the CHART SPRITE records each observation in looping script (the game's field notes are literally the chart sprite's pages), and the DISTRACTED SPRITE provides the reaction comedy — every reaction is motion, so the distracted one is permanently, helplessly entertained. Squeak supervises from a perch: squeaks once for "try again" (the gentlest no in the building) and does the call-and-response when a shield crystallizes. OCEANIANOIR presides, obviously — this is her bench.

## THE SOUND (per the Arcade Music Profile master spec)

Base track: soft nautilus hum with water-glass taps. THE 5-SECOND SIGNATURE: the tiny crystalline "chime-crack" of a shield forming (pitched to the compound's Mohs, per the stone-tone law). Wrong mixes get honest sounds too — fizz, plunk, the little smoke-ring "poom." Heartbeat holds at 50 BPM (the Spiral Heartbeat; the game is a calm bench, not a twitch table). THE SHADOW STATE: none needed — this room has no shadow; the 84Hz register is not invited to the bench. This is a daylight game in a drowned city.
