# THE BONE ROOM — Master Specification
## O'Keeffe Meets the Subtractive Family — The Architecture of What Remains

---

## WHAT THE BONE ROOM IS

A quiet room below and behind the Viking ship mead hall. The meadery owner's wife — a rock hound and bone artist in the style of Georgia O'Keeffe — preserves the bones of things that have passed, not as relics or trophies, but as expressions of the cycle. The bone is the last structure the body builds, and it outlasts the body. The most adequate structure.

The mead hall is the warm public heart. The Bone Room is the cool private one. The passage between them is short and narrow — the gap between a heartbeat and its echo.

**The Connection to Krista's Life:** The meadery owner and his wife are family to Krista in the Inn. His fire and mead above, her quiet room below. The pattern mirrors Krista and Charles — the public warmth and the private craft, the fire and the bone.

## THE ROOM

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Walls | Stone with Viking-era wood paneling. Fossils visible in the flagstone. The rock hound's wife married the stone-knower's daughter. The floor remembers. |
| Window | One large window, soft golden afternoon light. The room is designed for this light — bones arranged so the afternoon sun reveals their architecture. O'Keeffe painted bones against sky. The Bone Keeper arranges bones against light. |
| Workbench | Heavy dark oak (same wood as the mead hall's ship ribs). Fine tools: brushes, chisels, copper wire, glass jars of whitening solution. The mead hall's table, smaller and turned inward. |
| Shelves | Finished pieces in carved stone niches. Each lit by a tiny amber oil lamp. Each bone given its own space, its own light, its own moment. |
| Hearth | Small, no fire — just warm coals glowing low. The fire that was welcome above becomes patient warmth below. A fire for patience. |
| Ceiling | Low, intimate. Dried flowers and pressed herbs hang from the beams — yarrow, sage, lavender. The plants that survived the body. The botanical bones. |
| Chair | One comfortable chair by the window with a fur throw. The O'Keeffe chair — where you sit and look at the sky through the skull in your hands. |

**The O'Keeffe Details:**
- Bones against window light — a deer skull silhouetted against golden glass, antlers framing the sky
- A single dried flower growing from a bleached skull — life emerging from the structure death left behind. The Inn's thesis in one image.
- Vertebrae strung on cord like prayer beads — the spine as a rosary. Each bead a station. Each station a moment the body was still becoming.
- Palette: bone-white, warm amber, dark oak, stone grey, dried-flower muted gold. The desert O'Keeffe painted, translated into a Viking room.

## THE QUESTLINE: "THE ARCHITECTURE OF WHAT REMAINS"

A five-stage questline. No combat. No timer. The player works alongside the Bone Keeper.

| Quest | Name | What Happens | The Lesson |
|------|------|---------------|-----------|
| 1 | The Finding | The player finds a naturally exposed bone — a deer skeleton in the forest, a bird's remains in the garden. Not hunting. Finding. | Death has already done its work. You are not taking life. You are witnessing what life left behind. |
| 2 | The Cleaning | The player sits at the workbench and learns the patient work of removing flesh, soil, moss. Fine brushes, gentle chisels. The Bone Keeper speaks about what the bone was — the leg that ran, the rib that breathed. | The cleaning is not sanitizing. It is MEETING. You are meeting the structure that was always underneath the living thing. |
| 3 | The Whitening | The bone is placed in whitening solution. The player returns over several in-game days. The bone slowly becomes pure — the last traces of organic color leaving. | The bone is calcium. Calcium is a mineral. The bone was always a stone that the body grew around itself. A crystal and a femur are the same act at different speeds. The giants chose to sleep and become stone. The deer ran and died and the bone became mineral. Same destination. The giant just took longer. |
| 4 | The Arranging | The player and the Bone Keeper arrange the bone — mounting on a dark wooden plaque with copper wire, angling the oil lamp to reveal architecture. The O'Keeffe stage. The player NAMES what they see in the bone (bridge? canyon? wing?). The arrangement is adjusted to reveal it. | This is the Inn's saving mechanism — the act of NAMING. The player names what they see, and the bone becomes that. |
| 5 | The Cycle | The Bone Keeper gives the player a seed. The player plants it in the skull they found, cleaned, whitened, and arranged. Over the next in-game week, a green stem emerges from the bone and grows toward the window light. | The cycle: the body built the bone. The bone outlasted the body. The seed grows from the bone. The plant will build its own structure. The structure will become the seed. The seed will become the bone. The both are: the same. The same is: the cycle. The cycle is: the adequate. |

**Final Reward:** The Bone Stone — a small, smooth, calcium-rich stone that the Bone Keeper found in the flagstone floor. "The floor has fossils because the floor is patient. The stone is the bone that the earth already arranged." The stone hums at the frequency of the bone — one clear, resonant tone, the tone of the last thing the bone heard.

**The resident:** The Bone Keeper's only companion before Saker started making deliveries. It DANCES (ruled  not bops): it syncs to the player's tempo, whatever that tempo is. Walk fast, it dances fast. Sit by the fire and it slows to the 50 BPM and just sways. The player's own rhythm, rendered in the smallest body in the building  the tempo system's tiniest instrument and the Beat Line's smallest keeper.

The chicken dance elbows. The two step. Never ironic, never explained (the Adequacy Law holds: no dialogue, no lore note, no apology). That's what makes it a rick roll: it doesn't ask.
