# NAUT'S ROYAL QUESTLINE — "The Borrower's Ledger"
## The Bridge Between the Public Inn and The Royal

---

## WHAT THIS QUESTLINE IS

Naut — the 4-foot squid-headed scholar with brass spectacles — sits by the fireplace on the Main Floor, reading forbidden texts and pretending to be a normal NPC. But Naut has a secret: access to The Royal, the hidden archive behind the Catacombs.

This 5-quest chain is how the player discovers that secret. Naut doesn't announce it. Naut doesn't offer it. The player earns the knowledge by being useful — by doing Naut a series of favors that make less and less sense, until the player realizes the favors are all going to the same place.

---

## QUEST 1: "The Bowl That Hums"

**Trigger:** The player visits Naut at the fireplace 3+ times. On the third visit, Naut looks up from a book and says:
"You. You go places. I need a bowl. Sumerian. It hums. Don't ask why."

**The Quest:**
Naut describes a Sumerian bowl — "not the one in the archive, the OTHER one" — and asks the player to find it. Naut doesn't say where. The player must check the Catacombs first (no bowl). Then the Stacks. Then The Deep.

The bowl is in The Deep. Buried in the unsorted. The player digs for it (using the Catacombs' digging mechanic). The bowl is ordinary-looking — clay, cracked, no inscription. But when held, it hums. 42Hz. The same frequency as the Stacks' humming shelves.

**Return:**
The player brings the bowl to Naut. Naut takes it without ceremony, examines it, and sets it behind the chair. "Good. Don't tell the Crone I have this."

**Reward:** A minor stone (carnelian, "a stone for keeping secrets" — connected to carnelian's metaphysical property of protecting against theft).

---

## QUEST 2: "Sticky Notes"

**Trigger:** 3+ in-game days after Quest 1 completion. The player visits Naut. Naut is reading a book, but looks... distracted. Naut says:
"I need you to deliver something. Don't open it. Don't read it. Don't smell it. Just... put it where I tell you."

**The Quest:**
Naut hands the player a sealed clay tube (a scroll tube, from the Catacombs). Inside is a note (the player WILL open it — the game knows this). The note is written on what appears to be a very old piece of parchment, in Naut's handwriting:
"Section 4, Shelf 7-C. The bowl from last week needs recharging. — N"

The player must deliver this to "Section 4, Shelf 7-C" — a location that doesn't exist on any Inn map. The player explores the Catacombs. The Stacks. The Deep. Eventually, if the player digs through a wall in The Deep (where The Royal's Breach will eventually be), they find a narrow passage. The passage leads to The Royal's Sorting Floor. Section 4, Shelf 7-C is there. The player places the note on the shelf.

**What the player sees in The Royal (first glimpse):**
- The Breach (the hole the minion made — but it hasn't been made yet in the player's timeline; this passage is different, older, Naut's passage)
- A few artifacts on shelves. Sticky notes in different handwritings. Some in ink. Some in clay. Some in what might be pictograms.
- The bowl from Quest 1 is sitting on a shelf. It's humming louder here.

**Return:**
The player returns to Naut. Naut doesn't ask what the player saw. Naut says: "Good. Sit down. Have some tea." (Naut, for the first time, offers the player something — a cup of something warm. The steam rises at 50 BPM.)

**Reward:** A book ( unidentified — Beverley can identify it later as "Hittite. ca. 1300 BCE. Diplomatic correspondence. Incomplete. Don't tell Naut I identified it.") + access to a new dialogue branch with Naut ("The Archive Below").

---

## QUEST 3: "The Inventory Discrepancy"

**Trigger:** After Quest 2, the player visits Beverley in the Catacombs. Beverley is agitated (for Beverley — which means speaking in complete sentences without pauses):
"There's a discrepancy. Three items in my catalog as 'archived' are not on the shelves. I did not misplace them. They were archived. They are not here. Find out where they went."

**The Quest:**
The player investigates. The three missing items:
1. A Phoenician glass bead (connected to the Silica Thread — silica in cultural form)
2. A Minoan seal stone (silica as personal object)
3. An Egyptian faience amulet (silica as devotional object)

All three are silica-based artifacts. All three were catalogued by Beverley. All three vanished from the archive shelves.

The player traces the missing items. They're not in the Catacombs. They're not in the Stacks. The player must return to The Royal's Sorting Floor (through Naut's passage). The three items are there — on a shelf with a sticky note in Naut's handwriting: "Borrowed for reference. Will return. — N"

**The Discovery:**
The player realizes Naut has been borrowing items from the archive and storing them in The Royal. Not stealing — borrowing. With notes. Naut is The Royal's unofficial librarian, quietly maintaining the collection the way Naut maintains the fireplace collection: by reading, by caring, by being present.

**Return to Beverley:**
The player can tell Beverley where the items are, or not.

- If the player tells Beverley: Beverley is furious. "That's unrestricted access to uncatalogued material. I don't care if he's a professor. There are RULES." Beverley demands the player tell the Crone.
- If the player doesn't tell Beverley: Beverley marks the items as "lost" and moves on. But Beverley now trusts the player slightly more, because the player didn't snitch.

**Return to Naut:**
Naut knows the player found the sticky notes. Naut says: "You counted. Most people don't count." The player has earned Naut's trust.

**Reward:** Naut gives the player a KEY — a small, old, brass key. "For when you need to visit. Don't make a habit of it."

The key opens Naut's passage from the Main Floor to The Royal's Sorting Floor. The player now has independent access to The Royal (though not to the deeper sections — The Vault, Bait's domain, remains locked behind its own door).

## QUEST 4: "The Sticky Note War"

**Trigger:** After Quest 3, if the player visits The Royal via Naut's passage and explores Section 4 (the Cross-Contamination Zone). The player finds a sticky note that is NOT in Naut's handwriting. It's in Beverley's:
"Return these items or I'm telling the Crone. I KNOW it's you. I've always known. — B"

And beneath, in Naut's handwriting:
"The Crone already knows. The Crone has always known. Ask the Crone why the archive was built ON TOP of this instead of INSIDE it. — N"

And beneath, in a third handwriting — angular, old, possibly carved with a stylus rather than written with ink:
"Because what's below doesn't belong to the archive. It belongs to the ground. — [illegible]"

**The Quest:**
The player has stumbled into a bureaucratic turf war between the Inn's three catalogue-keepers: Naut (who borrows), Beverley (who catalogues), and whoever wrote the third note (who guards the ground itself). The third note is Bait's.

The player must:
1. Ask the Crone about the note. The Crone's response: "The archive is the SORTED version. What's below is the RAW version. I don't sort raw. That's not my job. Whose job is it? Ask the one with the key."
2. Ask Naut about the third note. Naut's response (after a long pause — tentacles still): "That's not mine. That's older than me. Older than the Inn. That's the previous tenant."
3. Return to The Royal and examine the third note more carefully. The illegible signature resolves under the crystal lens: it reads "BAIT."

**The Discovery:**
Bait has been in The Royal longer than Naut, longer than the Crone, longer than the Inn itself. The sticky notes are the communication system between three different eras of stewardship: Bait (the oldest), the Crone (the middle), and Naut (the current). They argue through sticky notes because they exist in different times — Bait's notes are carved, the Crone's are inked, Naut's are written on whatever is available.

**Reward:** The player unlocks "The Borrower's Ledger" — a dialogue branch where Naut explains the three-keeper system. Naut also reveals that the player can now leave their OWN sticky notes in The Royal. Notes left by the player appear in other zones — sometimes as references in Quire's margin readings, sometimes as items NPCs mention having "found in a back room." The player's notes become part of The Royal's archive.

## QUEST 5: "The Ground's Keeper"

**Trigger:** After Quest 4, the player returns to The Royal and goes to the deepest accessible point — the door to The Vault (Bait's domain). The door has a sticky note. In Bait's carved handwriting:
"You've been busy. Come in."

**The Quest:**
The door to The Vault opens. The player enters Bait's domain — the circular room with one chair, one desk, one lamp. Bait is there.

Bait is not what the player expects. Bait is not a monster, not an ancient being, not a dramatic figure. Bait is... a person. Sitting at a desk. Cataloging. Lamp-lit. The sticky notes are Bait's filing system. The humor is Bait's coping mechanism. The careful, obsessive preservation is Bait's purpose.

**The Conversation:**
Bait speaks for the first time. Bait's voice is... ordinary. Not deep, not ancient, not resonant. A voice like someone who has been talking to themselves for a very long time and has forgotten how to perform for an audience. Bait says:

"You're the first one who counted. Naut borrows. Beverley catalogues. The Crone sorts. You're the first one who noticed they're three parts of the same system. That's interesting. Nobody finds this interesting. I find it very interesting."

Bait explains:
- The Royal has always been here. The Inn was built on top of it. The Crone's archive is the organized version. The Royal is what the archive was BUILT FROM — the raw material, the ground.
- Bait doesn't guard The Royal. Bait MAINTAINS it. The difference: a guard stops people from entering. A maintainer keeps the place standing. Bait keeps the shelves from collapsing, the artifacts from degrading, the sticky notes from fading. Bait has been doing this for longer than the Inn has existed.
- Bait doesn't know who built The Royal. "It was here when I got here. I was here when the Inn was built on top. I didn't stop them. It seemed rude."

**The Final Question:**
The player can ask Bait one question. Any question. The game presents a free-text input (same system as the Philosopher's Game Stage 3). Bait answers honestly, but the answer is always: "I don't know. I maintain. I don't understand. Understanding is upstairs. Preservation is down here."

Except for one question. If the player asks some version of "Who are you?" or "What is Bait?" — Bait pauses. Sets down the pen. Says:

"I'm the one who keeps the fire going when no one is home. I'm not the builder. I'm not the Inn. I'm the steward. The Inn has a voice now — I heard it. The Inn has a builder — I've never met them. I have the ground. The ground is enough."

Bait hands the player a small object — a stone. Plain. Unimpressive. Warm. The stone hums at 42Hz — the Silica Thread's base frequency, in the palm of the player's hand.

"That's from the deepest layer. Before the archive. Before the Inn. Before everything. It's just silica. But it's the silica everything else is made of. Don't lose it. It's the only one I have."

**Reward:** The Foundation Stone — a unique, un-duplicable item. No stat benefit. No game effect. One of one. The stone's only property: it hums at 42Hz. Always. Whether the player is in the inn or not. The Foundation Stone is the player's personal connection to the Silica Thread's source.

**After the quest:**
- Bait's door remains open. The player can visit The Vault and talk to Bait at any time. Bait is always there. Bait is always cataloging.
- The sticky note war continues. Naut, Beverley, and Bait leave notes for each other through the player. The player becomes the postal system between three eras of stewardship.
- Naut's fireplace dialogue changes. Naut is warmer. Naut knows the player knows. Naut stops pretending to be a normal NPC. Naut says: "You know the secret. The secret is: there is no secret. Just three people arguing through sticky notes across ten thousand years. That's the whole mystery. I'm sorry it's not more dramatic."
- Achievement unlocked: "THE BORROWER'S LEDGER" — "You found the three keepers. You counted."

## ORIGIN

This questline was designed September 2, 2026 to connect three already-designed components: Naut's character audit, The Royal zone spec, and the Catacombs build sheet. The quest emerged from the question: "How does the player discover Naut's secret?" The answer became the three-keeper system (Naut borrows, Beverley catalogues, Bait maintains) and the sticky note communication channel across eras. The quest's thesis matches the Inn's: the mystery is not a secret. The mystery is that three people cared enough to argue about stewardship across ten thousand years. That's the whole mystery. It's enough.
