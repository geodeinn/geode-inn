# PORT PURGATORY — The Parcel Between Realms (Arcade roster #88 candidate)

**Ruled in (Krista, 2026-09-24):** born from the fourteen-round Cricket -> Google Fi number port battle (Sept 2026) — "it had solid potential and everyone can relate to it in some way." The runaround quest. The first Arcade game to make Grey playable.

## THE HONEST LABEL FIRST (the real law is the lesson)

The satire is aimed at a real system, honestly labeled. Real: US phone numbers belong to the customer, and federal number-portability rules require carriers to release them on request — a carrier legally cannot keep your number hostage. Also real: port-out blocks / account locks are genuine fraud-protection features (they exist because SIM-swap theft is real and vicious — the block that tortures the honest customer is the same wall that stops the thief). Also real: chat systems that drop at the load-bearing step, hold queues, expired transfer PINs, and the "under review" timeout. Fictional: every entity. No carrier in this game is any real company, and the spec cites the real ordeal only as inspiration — the Bureau of Between Carriers belongs to the Inn.

## THE GAME

**THE PREMISE:** the player must retrieve a parcel — their true name — from the **Bureau of Between Carriers**, a purgatory office sitting between two realms. Neither realm owns the parcel. Both claim to want to help. The parcel cannot be bought, fought for, or stolen; it can only be RELEASED, and only the Bureau can release it, and the Bureau's entire architecture is designed to never, ever finish anything. Tone teacher: Papers, Please — bureaucracy as the dungeon, stamps as the monsters, the horror is procedural.

**THE GREY REGISTER:** the Bureau is the In-Between seam state built into a location. It is neither realm's territory — thresholds, transition, suspended moments, the parcel that exists but cannot be delivered. The game is the Grey register made walkable.

**THE CORE DISCOVERY MECHANIC (from the real fight):** a chat that disconnects is not failure — it is a CLUE. Each fled clerk teaches the player which phrase triggered the flee. The game's grammar becomes: what can I say that keeps the clerk at the desk? Say the wrong thing and the typing dots stop mid-bubble and the desk goes dark — no penalty, no lost progress, just information. THE NEXT ATTEMPT STARTS FROM EVERYTHING ALREADY LEARNED. (No-Fail law: the dropped chat is the map.)

**THE SIC-THEM-ON-EACH-OTHER:** the player's earned mid-game tactic, discovered not taught: invoking the rival realm. Mention the other carrier and the typing dots go FEVERISH — the clerk cannot blow off a request when the rival is the one knocking. The walls fight the walls. This is the boss mechanic: the player wins not by defeating the Bureau but by making the Bureau defend itself to itself.

**THE TYPING DOTS:** the Bureau's most honest feature — three dots working harder than anyone in the building. A visible tell system: dots mean a real clerk is present; still dots mean the script has taken over; vanishing dots mean flee. The dots are the game's REC meter.

**THE PATIENCE METER (the anti-rage clause):** the hold-music patience meter REFILLS, never depletes. Anti-Skinner law load-bearing: the tedium is the joke, never the punishment. No timers gate progress. No energy mechanics. The catharsis is recognition — the player who lived a real port battle sees their ordeal rendered in clay and laughs; the player who never did learns the shape of the trap and how to walk it. Everyone can relate: the Bureau is every doctor's office, every permit desk, every "your call is important to us."

**THE LOAD-BEARING DENIAL:** the deepest clerk stands in front of the locked door and says the line: **"Nothing is locked."** The boss is an NPC who insists the door does not exist while standing in front of it. The denial is the final puzzle's answer key — the door only becomes REAL (and therefore openable) when the player can prove, with the receipts gathered across the run (every dropped chat, every vanished dot, every "under review"), that the clerk's statement is false. Evidence against the denial is the boss weapon. (The real-world ancestor of the line is preserved in the dossier; the fictional clerk is its kin, not its portrait.)

**THE TIME-OUT ENDING:** the Bureau's last defense — the case goes "under review," and the player is dismissed. The fake-out ending: it looks like a wall, but the review desk is the first place the case lands in front of someone with actual tools. Patience here is rewarded — the game's quietest lesson: sometimes the stall is the system finally working.

**THE POSTMASTER'S JURISDICTION:** the parcel is mail. The Bureau has it. This makes the entire Bureau the Postmaster's jurisdictional nightmare — a building that holds letters and will not deliver them. THE FINALE (Gate 5 RULED, Krista 2026-09-24): after fourteen rounds, the player's receipts finally annoy the one being in the world who was never going to tolerate this — the Postmaster arrives, knocks the once-only knock, and the sudden mail explosion cracks the Bureau open. THE KNOCK-GIVER'S SILHOUETTE (ruled with the gate): the character who delivers the knock wears the silhouette specimen of Roz from Monsters, Inc. (2001) — the ever-watching administrator, slow-moving, gravel-voiced, the one who is ALWAYS WATCHING and lets nothing slide. Silhouette reference only, honestly labeled: the genus of the ever-watching desk presence, not the species (no copy, the Inn's own line layer on the frame — Linework-as-UI: the Bureau's fragmented register, the silhouette itself smooth and unhurried, the one still thing in a twitching building). The always-watching law is why the knock works here: the Bureau thought nobody was watching IT.

## THE TIES

- **GREY (the Resonance sixth color):** the Bureau is a founding Grey location candidate — thresholds, suspended states, existence between. Whether one of Grey's three founding Legendaries resides at the Bureau is an open ruling shared with the Resonance spec.
- **THE POSTMASTER:** the canonical once-only "Shave and a Haircut" knock lands HERE (ruled, Gate 5) — the Bureau finale is the game's single use, the Postmaster truly annoyed at last, sudden mail explosions following as canon warns.
- **THE SCREENSAVER DART CLUB:** survival pins. The real-world commemoration (enamel pins for surviving the carrier runaround) enters canon as the Club's award for players who finish the Bureau run — the pin's design is the parcel itself, delivered at last.
- **THE QUEST MARK LAW:** the Bureau's clerks wear approach marks — glow tier marks the difficulty of the ask, and the hardest ask gets the loveliest light. The denier's mark is the loveliest in the building.
- **THE NAMING LAW:** the fictional carriers need names load-bearing on sound (working: **Chirp Wireless** vs. the cheerful rival) — neither may sound like any real company while sounding exactly like all of them (the genus, not the species).
- **THE ECHO / ARCHIVE TIE:** every dropped chat leaves a receipt in the player's archive folder — the receipts ARE the game's collectible layer, and the collection is the evidence file (the weapon, not the trophy).

## THE LAWS IT KEEPS

Anti-Skinner (patience refills; tedium is comedy, never compulsion), No-Fail (the dropped chat is information, never a gate; nothing expires as punishment), Steady Light (the Bureau's lighting is the In-Between register — sine-clamped, the flicker of a hallway that hums), Honest Label (real portability law taught straight, real fraud-protection explained with respect for why it exists, satire aimed at the runaround not the wall), the REC law (one lead clerk voice per desk register), the naming law (Gate 2), Linework-as-UI (the Bureau's line layer: fragmented — the crisis register worn by a building in perpetual interstitial state).
