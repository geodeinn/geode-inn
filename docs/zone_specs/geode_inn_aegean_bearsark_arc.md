## WHAT THE ARC IS
The Bearsark is the Norse-in-Greece wanderer who never went home  the border between the Norse Coast and the Aegean Light has a guard, and the guard is both a man and a bear, and both are real. THE ZONE'S OWN THESIS, EMBODIED: in a zone where solidity is an illusion and every edge dissolves, the Bearsark is the law made flesh  and fur. The light changes; the thing doesn't. There is no transformation to show, because there is no change: BOTH, ALWAYS, AT ONCE. (The game never renders a shift. The player sees a man on the coast road and a bear in the olive grove and never once sees one become the other. The absence IS the truth.)

**THE WANDERING DISCIPLINE (the character's heart):** the patrol is not aimless. IT IS THE MEDICINE. The berserk's rage is real (played straight  the Child-Actor Law: no softening, the danger is true); the wandering is how he keeps it spent and managed. STANDING STILL IS HOW THE BEAR WINS. The card keyword is the diagnosis: wandering is not his weather, it is his PRACTICE  kin to Lozen's patrol pauses, the discipline worn as a life. The Inn's gentlest hard truth, worn as a border: some guards guard a place. This one guards a truce.

**FREQUENCY:** the border's own  the Aegean hum at the olive line, the coast wind. On patrol, the heartbeat is carried (the Campgrounds law: outdoors, the pulse is CARRIED  here it walks with him).
**AESTHETIC:** Monet's dissolving edge meets the Norse line: the Bearsark is drawn in the GUARDIAN hand at the man (sharp, angular) and dissolves at the bear into the zone's soft edge  the two lineworks on one body (the Linework-as-UI system's two modes, one character: the visual proof of both-real).
