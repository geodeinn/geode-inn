# THE ARTISAN THIEF — Master Specification
## The Selkie Who Chose the Bench

---

## WHAT THE ARTISAN THIEF IS

The Artisan Thief is Krista's player character and one of the five starting classes. Originally defined as: nimble fingers, low charisma — the jeweler who makes beautiful things and can't sell them. Now deepened: she is a selkie whose human form resembles a feminine depiction of the Sandman (Netflix series).

A selkie. A seal who sheds her skin to walk on land in human form. In Celtic and Norse folklore, selkies are gentle, beautiful, and fundamentally wild. They come ashore to dance, to love, to breathe — but the sea is always calling. Selkie stories are always about the tension between two worlds: the land (where things are made, where love is felt, where beauty is crafted) and the sea (where the selkie is free, weightless, formless, home).

The Artisan Thief is a selkie who CHOSE the land. Not trapped — not the selkie whose skin was stolen, not the selkie who longs for the sea and weeps on the shore. She chose. She came ashore, shed her skin, picked up tools, and made something. And the making was so good she stayed.

But the sea doesn't stop calling because you stop listening. The sea is patient. The sea is older than the land. And the selkie who chose the bench still hears it — in the sound of water running over stones, in the clink of tools that sounds like waves on a rocky shore, in the deep hum of the Song of Making that sounds, when you're very quiet, like the ocean heard from very far away.

---

## THE SKIN

The selkie's skin is her most precious possession. In folklore, if the skin is stolen, the selkie is trapped. If she finds it, she returns to the sea and never comes back.

The Artisan Thief's skin is her JEWELER'S APRON. The leather she wears at the bench. The thing she puts on when she works and takes off when she's done. The apron IS the skin — the thing that lets her transform from human-shaped jeweler into something older and wilder. When she ties the apron, the seal slides off and the maker slides on. When she unties it, the maker slides off and the seal slides on.

Nobody touches the apron. Nobody. The Crone once reached for it — to straighten it, motherly instinct — and the selkie's eyes went black and cold and ancient for one second, and the Crone, who is afraid of nothing, took a step back. The Crone never mentioned it. The selkie never apologized. They understand each other. The Crone's glasses are her skin. The apron is the selkie's. Some things are not shared.

## THE DUAL WORLD

| World | What It Is | What She Does There |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| The Land | The market, the bench, the tools, the stones, the making. The world where things are solid and shaped and sold. | Makes beautiful things. Can't sell them. The Artisan Thief — the jeweler who excels at craft and fails at commerce. |
| The Sea | The underwater traversal layer, the catacombs, Charon's river, the water zones. The world where the selkie is free and weightless and wild. | Doesn't need charisma because the sea doesn't require performance. The sea requires BEING. |

The Artisan Thief is the only character who moves between both worlds as naturally as breathing. She walks the market in human form. She dives into the catacombs and the underwater zones and the seal slides on and the water remembers her.

## FREQUENCY SYSTEM

| Element | Frequency | Function |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| The selkie's human voice | Soft, precise, quiet — the jeweler's voice | The voice of someone who works with small things. Low volume, high clarity. |
| The selkie's sea voice | Deep, resonant, ancient — the ocean's voice | The voice the selkie hears in the water. Not spoken — felt. The 40Hz ocean memory. |
| The apron tie/untie | A soft click (the clasp sound — bone on leather) | The transformation sound. The one sound that means the selkie is changing. |
| The workbench sounds | Metal on stone, tool on gem, the tap of a jeweler's hammer | These sounds sound like waves on a rocky shore. The sea calling while the selkie works. |
