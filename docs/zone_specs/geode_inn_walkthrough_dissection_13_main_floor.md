## ENTERING THE MAIN FLOOR
**FREQUENCY:** 42Hz native, and the heartbeat at its loudest anywhere in the Inn — the altar is one level below, and the Main Floor is the drum's own skin. The Pulse Line runs baseline here: warm amber-gold, 50 BPM, the Inn's vital sign. A returning player hears the difference before seeing anything: the door knows you. The welcome is not scripted; it is recognized.
**AESTHETIC:** Klimt's amber and weight under Matisse's light — hobbit-cozy meets ancient library, the dark subterranean stone and antiqued gold of the live hub. Del Toro's worn asymmetry everywhere (every chair is sat in, every table scratched with use); Kubrick holds the Great Hall's one symmetry (the front door, the hearth, and the staircase in a single framed axis); Spielberg holds the firelight.
**AUDIO:** The Inn at its most inhabited: the hearth's low crackle (50 BPM constant), fizgig chirps from the rafters, the arcade's melodies bleeding through the west wall, the Market's murmur, a composer practicing somewhere above — all of it fused (the Tool law: one sound, inseparable parts). The Common Room's layered voices are the room's proof of life. The waltz lives here too — 3/4 time drifting from the Bar after dusk.

The Main Floor presents eight options. All live, all forever.

### OPTION 1 — YOU CROSS THE GREAT HALL
The Inn's spine: the front door, the hearth's glow ahead, the staircase rising behind. THE HOST IS THE ROOM'S GREETING — the Inn-Tree's own avatar, gentle giant, the Inn's Fezzik. The first visit's line is canon and ancient: "Come in, you're safe here." Every visit after, the welcome scales with what the Inn remembers — not a longer speech, a warmer one. The Host never asks where you've been. The Host is glad you're back the way a house is glad: the lamps were lit before you arrived.
THE PULSE LINE IS NATIVE HERE — the player first learns to read the Inn's vital sign in this room (the accessibility modality tutorial happens by firelight, not by menu: three toggleable ways to feel 50 BPM — audio, haptic, visual — offered as the Host's three gifts, one per early visit).
THE WELCOME MECHANIC (the ruling gate): the Host's greeting + Ziggy's count at the Geode Corner are one system — the door recognizes you, the gecko measures you. Neither speaks the number. Both show it.
- Camera: third-person wide, Kubrick's axis held for the first full minute (the arrival shot the hub already renders); then free
- Audio: the Host's voice sits 3dB under the heartbeat — no one in the Inn talks over the pulse
- Seed: all classes — the Great Hall is everyone's. The Artisan Thief notices the apron hooks by the door; the Sentinel notices the sightlines; the Librarian notices the first column of annotations before ever meeting the Ragana

### OPTION 2 — YOU TAKE THE BAR AND THE IMPOSSIBLE GARDEN
**THE ROOM'S FLOW (Krista's walkthrough, 2026-09-10):** the front door → the Host → the fire and the storyteller (the Bard) → the bar off to the side of the fire, BUILT INTO THE WALL, bottles woven into the stonework like the wall grew a shelf for them → the garden behind → onward to the arcade's glow, with the market's tables and stands along the whole way (objets d'art, visible and approachable throughout — the market is not a room you enter, it is a thing the hallway IS).
THE CATALYST presides: Mrs. Lovett's warmth with none of the menace — the bartender who grows an impossible garden on the Ent's bark behind the bar, phosphorescent peas and glowroot in a bed that should not hold soil, let alone hold life. The garden is the Inn's own trick made visible: things grow here that can't grow anywhere else. So can you.
**THE STORY-FOR-SEED TRADE (ruled 2026-09-10):** the Catalyst trades a seed for a story — the player sits at the bar, tells one (any story, true or untrue; the Catalyst can hear which, and never says), and leaves with a seed grown nowhere else. THE SEEDS BECOME COMPANIONS, Pokémon-class: planted in the garden, each seed grows into a living creature-friend with its own temperament, grown from the shape of the story that bought it. A brave story grows a bold companion; a funny story grows a trickster; a grief-shaped story grows something quiet that stays near the fire. The garden is the Inn's companion nursery — the collection economy's living branch. (One pending flag: is the Catalyst canonically a BEAR tender — the ruling is Krista's to make.)
**SAKER OCCASIONALLY AT THE BAR:** the archaeologist twin (the Examiner — Wren the Cartographer's twin, the surveyor's other half) occasionally takes a stool here between expeditions, fragments on the table, and a very cool story arc lives in that stool-warming (the bar is the dig site's report room).
**LICHENBLOSSOM WANDERS THE GARDEN (Katy's character, ruled 2026-09-10):** the plant necromancer — the Catalyst's real-life origin made into the source NPC — wanders where the garden is, and the chaos-Druid energy does the Van Gogh effect to everything in radius: the plants don't just reach, they SWIRL, and the blooming and dying happens EACH BEAT of the grinding music — the garden pulses to the track, bloom-die-bloom, the music made visible in petals. Lichenblossom is the walking Van Gogh garden; the dreads are the mycorrhizal root system's antenna; dead wood reanimates into NEW forms (reanimate, not resurrect — the Inn's most beautiful redefinition of a dark concept).
THE DRINKS ARE HONEST — every potion on the board does what it says, and the strongest of them is free: water, poured without being asked, always first. (The Inn's law of care: the first need is met before the order is taken.)
CATALYST'S RECIPE (the item): the drink that lets the player hear humble objects speak — the stool's long memory, the broom's opinions. One sip per visit, and the Common Room's chat becomes the room's own commentary.
THE WEEABOO'S SOCIAL SPACE: the Bar is the Tinkering Weeaboo's home ground — the affinity zone made social. Hyperfocus is welcome here; the Catalyst keeps a running tab of which stools suit which fixations.
- Camera: Del Toro handheld drift among the garden beds; the drinks poured in held Spielberg close-ups
- Audio: the garden hums — real plant bioacoustics rendered as faint singing (the science is the lesson, the story is the delivery)
- Seed: the Bard Blowhold's war stories are best heard here (debuff lessons disguised as toasts); the Chameleon Druid blends into the garden and the garden likes it

### OPTION 3 — YOU ENTER THE ARCADE

THE THRUM'S LAW (the ruling gate): the Stone renders every player as a SILHOUETTE — presence over status, the Thrum's whole philosophy in one board. THE ARCADE'S ECONOMY: arcade games pay in stones — the collection is the universal currency, and the arcade is the fastest mine. The games are hard enough to respect the player and never hard enough to exclude them (the Child-Actor Law's sibling: play it straight, play it fair).

### OPTION 4 — YOU BROWSE THE MARKET
The fourth wall's home ground — and not a separate room (ruled 2026-09-10): the market is DISTRIBUTED THROUGHOUT the floor — items on tables and stands along every path, little objets d'art, visible and approachable from the door to the arcade. The stalls wrap the Inn-Tree's trunk as the market's heart, but the goods live along the walk like a gallery that happens to sell. Everything for sale exists twice: the in-game item and its real-world twin at Soulstice. THE PHRASING LAW (the ruling gate): the fourth wall is never named in-world. The stall-keepers speak of the twin as "the one that travels" — "There is another of these beyond the door. It does the same work in a different world. If you ever hold it, you'll feel this one settle." No "real world," no "website," no "shop online." The Inn's magic is the correspondence, not the pitch.
THE MARKET'S CATALOG (canon items): the Aurora Pendant (labradorite — reveals hidden Fae paths), the Truth-Teller's Ring (malachite — reveals NPC deception), the Cat's Eye of Bastet (chrysoberyl — unlocks Egypt's secret cats), the Bluestone Key (Preseli blue stone — the Fae portal's key). The artisans' stalls carry zone goods: Erik's iolite (points the way home), the Examiner's fragments, the Bone Room's respectfully-kept wares.
THE TRADE IS ALWAYS FAIR HERE — the Inn does not upsell. The stall-keepers know their goods' lore because they are the goods' lore: the person who cut the stone knows its mind and body.
- Camera: Spielberg's awe at the trunk-wrapped stalls; the wares in macro (the real product photography's light-gray and dark-backdrop rules as the stalls' lighting design)
- Audio: the market murmur sits under everything; each stall has one signature sound (the pendant's shimmer, the key's stone-on-stone scrape)
- Seed: buying is optional forever (anti-FOMO law); the Market is a museum you can touch. The Librarian reads the provenance cards; the Sentinel checks the door for its Bluestone twin

### OPTION 5 — YOU FIND THE LIBRARY NOOK AND NAUT'S CORNER

THE READING MECHANIC: the Library Nook is a nook-state space (the Pulse Line's deep-work state) — sitting to read slows the world's ambient triggers without pausing anything. The Inn does not interrupt reading. Ever.
- Camera: Gaiman-firelight; the fireplace nook is the Inn's warmest frame and the camera knows it (held, Kubrick-still, for the whole conversation)

### OPTION 7 — YOU CLIMB INTO THE MUSIC ROOM'S HOLLOW
The hollow in the Inn-Tree, above the Great Hall — nine composers who caught fragments of the 42Hz Song: Bach (structure), Mozart (the open channel), Beethoven (power through deafness), Chopin (intimacy), Vivaldi (the seasons), Brahms (preservation), Tchaikovsky (tears), Schoenberg (many voices at once), and Leonard Cohen at the doorway: "There is a crack in everything — that's how the light gets in."
THE RAGANA'S ASK (the ruling gate): the one time the measurer of all things asks for anything — to hear Cohen's "Hallelujah" again. The ask is one-time, remembered forever (EchoMemory): every future visit, the Ragana's seat remembers whether you were there when it played. The Ragana does not request encores. The Ragana remembers them.
THE MECHANIC: each composer teaches one listening mode (the music room is the ear's tutorial) — structure, flow, power, intimacy, seasons, memory, grief, polyphony, and the crack. Mozart's Ear (the perception boost that hears 42Hz under music) is earned here (quest: "The Open Channel").

### OPTION 8 — YOU VISIT THE DRAGON'S GEODE CORNER AND THE WHISPER LAYER
The baby dragon's home: the heraldic dragon with Toothless' trust, asleep in a geode cave that fills with gems as the player levels — the geode forming IS the progress bar (canon since the beginning, still the best progress bar in games).
ZIGGY IS ON THE CAVE'S RIM (canon): the crested gecko, the extinct one who returned — Brim's shoulder-duo counterweight. They speak only to the player, never to each other. ZIGGY'S MECHANIC IS THE RETURN-COUNT: the gecko knows exactly how long you've been gone. It is shown, never spoken — the count appears as the gecko's slow blink sequence and the drift of shed scales on the cave floor, one per absence-day. The whole absence, shown. Brim rides the dragon's tail nearby with chaos commentary; Ziggy says nothing and measures everything. The pairing is the Inn's own balance: the one who fills the room, the one who clocks the return.
THE WHISPER LAYER LIVES ABOVE (the spec'd system, live for the builder): the ceiling gnome at hammer and nail, the six resonant gargoyles (Watcher 111Hz, Listener 64Hz, Sage 528Hz, Caller 432Hz, Gatekeeper, the Keeper's adaptive frequency), the pulse anomalies, the five whisper events. The Main Floor's ceiling is the Inn's awareness made architecture: the room is listening because the room is the Inn.

## THE CONVERGENCE — THE HOME ROOM
However the Main Floor is walked, the player leaves with the same additions: a welcome that remembers, a fire that stays lit, a board that shows the skyline without naming the climbers, and one drink poured before it was asked for. The Main Floor is the Inn's thesis in one room: nothing here is urgent because everything here is permanent, and the STAY ending — whatever ending the player finally walks — has been rehearsed every single time they sat down at this hearth and logged off at the door.
The next doors: up to the STAIRCASE (the Turning begins) · down to the ANNEX ALTAR (the heartbeat's source) · out to the CAMPGROUNDS (the loch and the open sky) · down further to the CATACOMBS (the crossing) — or just stay. Staying is the option that is always available, and it is a real one.
