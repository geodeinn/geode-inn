# THE REDWOOD CATHEDRAL — Master Specification
## The Zone That Is Nothing But Exploring, Climbing, and Building

---

## WHAT THE REDWOOD CATHEDRAL IS

A zone that is nothing but enormous, beautiful, haunting redwoods. No other biome. No other architecture. No combat. No dissonance. No threats. No timer. No objectives. Just the forest — the ancient, 2,000-year-old forest — and the player.

The Redwood Cathedral is the Inn's PURE zone. Every other zone has mechanics, encounters, systems, progression. This zone has trees. The trees ARE the mechanic. The trees ARE the system. The player enters with empty hands — the pouch, the frequencies, the Song, the resonance, the timing all left at the threshold.

The Resonance Pulse is ABSENT here. The 50 BPM heartbeat goes SILENT. The player is, for the first time in the Inn, without a guide. No warmer/colder. No direction. Just the trees, the player's own attention, and the silence.

**Access:** Canopy City → bridge from the Neutral Platform descends into the forest
**Art Style:** Albert Bierstadt (but subdued — the grandeur without the spectacle)

---

## THE REDWOODS

Real redwoods. Not stylized, not cartoonified. 300+ feet tall. 20+ feet in diameter at the base. Bark a foot thick, reddish-brown, deeply furrowed. Canopy so high the ground is in permanent twilight — green-gold, diffuse, quiet.

The redwoods are the oldest LIVING things the player encounters. The oldest was a seedling when Rome was young. A sapling when the Minoan civilization collapsed. A mature tree when the first stone circle was built. The redwoods OUTLAST civilizations. The Inn's catacombs hold the MEMORY of ancient civilizations. The Redwood Cathedral holds the LIVING PRESENCE of time. The redwoods are the biological Rememberers — living memory, not stone memory.

### The Growth Rings as Messenger
Each ring is a year. Each year is a message. Wide ring = good year. Narrow ring = hard year. Scarred ring = fire year (survived, healed, recorded). The rings are TIMING — the same principle the Thrum teaches. The player who has learned to hear timing can READ the rings by touching the bark — the ridges and furrows mirror the growth rings underneath. The redwood is a library. The bark is the cover. The rings are the text. The reading is the touch.

### The Haunting
The redwoods are haunting because they are ALIVE and OLD and those two things don't usually go together. Stone persists. The redwoods are mortal. The redwoods will die. The haunting is the mortality of something that has outlived civilizations and will still, eventually, fall. The creak is not distress — the creak is the redwood breathing. Breathing by growing. Growing makes sound. The sound is the breath of the oldest living thing the player has ever encountered.

## THE CLIMBING

No equipment, no ropes, no skill check, no fall damage. The player climbs the way a child climbs a tree — hands on bark, feet in furrows, the slow, physical, full-body experience of going UP.

| Layer | Height | What's There |
|------|--------|-------------|
| 1. Forest Floor | Ground | Permanent twilight. Ferns, moss, fallen branches, root systems. The zone's home base. Where the player builds. |
| 2. The Trunk | 0-100 ft | Bark ridges as handholds. Texture changes (lower = spongy, upper = harder). Epiphytic ferns, moss gardens, lichen colonies. The redwood's skin — alive with smaller things. |
| 3. The Understory | 100-200 ft | First branches — thick enough to walk on. Canopy soil (soil ON the branches, from fallen needles + decomposing epiphytes). Air gardens. The middle world between earth and light. |
| 4. The Canopy | 200-300+ ft | Full light. Warm, open, WINDY. Interlocking branches create walkable paths between trees. The zone's reward — the view, the light, the perspective of looking DOWN on the forest that looked so enormous from below. |
| 5. The Crown | 300+ ft | The highest reachable point in the Inn. The Queen of Roots' throne — a branch worn smooth by centuries of sitting. The view is enormous. The silence is total. |

## THE QUEEN OF ROOTS' LEGACY

The Queen of Roots (Robin Wright) is the zone's absent guide. She's GONE — moved to the Inn-Tree to be near the Seelie King. But the zone remembers her: the worn branch, the canopy-soil shape, the peridot gift.

Her message, carved by growth (not by knife) into the bark near her throne:

"The roots push up. The canopy pushes down. The tree grows in the tension. I sat here for longer than your civilizations have lasted. The tree grew around me. I did not build. I did not fight. I sat. The forest remembered. That is enough. That is all I ever did. That is all you need to do here. Sit. The forest will remember you too."

The player's realization: the most important thing they can do in the Redwood Cathedral is not build, not climb, not explore. The most important thing is SIT. The sitting is the building. The presence is the contribution.

## THE BUILDING: PIT HOUSE

The player builds a pit house on the forest floor from what the forest has given: fallen bark, shed branches, fallen fronds. No tools. No machinery. Hands and materials.

The pit house persists across REINCARNATION. When the player reincarnates and returns, the previous pit house is still there — but the forest has been absorbing it. Moss on the bark. Ferns through the frame. The pit house is becoming part of the forest floor.

Three choices:
- Renovate (restore — "I'm back, I'm staying")
- Rebuild (dismantle + new — "I've learned, the house should be different")
- Leave it (a ruin — a memorial. The forest's record of the player's previous presence)

Over many reincarnations, the forest accumulates pit house RUINS — a personal archaeology of lives lived in the redwoods, each ruin a layer, each layer a memory, each memory a RING. The player's pit house ruins are the player's own growth rings.

## THE SOUNDSCAPE

The quietest place in the Inn. The Inn's systems STOP here.

| Sound | What It Is |
|-------|-----------|
| The silence | Not true silence — the silence of a forest. The absence of mechanical sound, the hum, the heartbeat, the Pulse. The silence of what's LEFT when the Inn's systems stop. |
| The creak | The redwood's voice. The wood moves — wind, growth, 2,000 years of widening. The creak is the sound of patience. The slowest frequency in the Inn — below 7.83Hz, below 14Hz, below 40Hz. The frequency of WOOD. |
| The thud | A redwood shedding a branch. Enormous, deep, felt in the ground before heard in the air. Not a death — a RELEASE. The branch falls and becomes a building material. The thud is the forest's delivery system. |
| The stream | A small stream — the only water in the zone. Doesn't connect to the Inn's water system. LOCAL. The stream is the zone's independence — the one thing that is entirely the forest's own. "This zone is not part of your systems. You are a guest here." |
