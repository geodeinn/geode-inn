## ENTERING THE FAE KINGDOM (two doors, both live)

**DOOR ONE — THE STONE CIRCLES (from the world map).** Four surface portals: Stonehenge, Avebury, Callanish, Ring of Brodgar. The first portal wakes when the player holds a Preseli Blue Stone — Standing Stones' reward, earned in hour one at the Arcade. The player who plays the Tetris-class game first arrives here months before the player who saves it, and both are correct. (The Inn never orders the journey. It doors it.)
- **Stone Circle Resonance** (the Threshold's portal-key game): tuning the circle's stones to the player's note. Each portal has its own chord; the player learns the circles are older than the Inn that grew around them.

**DOOR TWO — THE FAR DOOR (from below).** The Stone Circle Chamber's summer-and-winter door, opened from the Fae side first. The player who descended to the Source in Dissection 06 arrives here WET — the only players in the Kingdom who entered from underneath, and the fae notice the water. "You came the long way," the Threshold's gatekeeper says. Not approval. Recognition. (The same word the Host used at the staircase. The Inn's vocabulary is small on purpose. Every repetition is a root.)

**AESTHETIC LAW:** Arthur Rackham holds the Kingdom (ink + watercolor). Alan Lee holds the Seelie court, John Bauer the Unseelie, Escher the Between. The player crosses between three linework grammars in one zone — the visual DNA's deepest showcase, and the player feels the shifts as weather, not as style change.

**THE KINGDOM'S TRUE LAW — THE BROTHERS:** The Fae Kingdom is not two courts at war. It is ONE ROOT SPLIT IN TWO DIRECTIONS: Oleander, the Seelie King — and Malachi, the Unseelie King. Brothers. The Summerlands and the Hollow are the same tree grown toward light and toward dark, and the whole Kingdom is their shared anatomy. Every sub-zone below is a room in the brothers' one body. (Ragnarok canon, foreshadowed honestly: in the Breaking, the brothers split — and the player who walks the Kingdom now understands the split before it happens, because the Kingdom has been teaching them the whole time what one root looks like grown in two directions.)

Six sub-zones. Every option live.

### OPTION 1 — THE THRESHOLD
The entry meadow where the circles root. Fae here are shy — chibi sprites, half-glimpsed, the zone's tutorial-by-ambush: nothing attacks, things REVEAL.
- **Play Stone Circle Resonance** (all four chords; the game that keeps the doors open)
- **Meet Bill the Platypus** (the Threshold's honest resident — the only fae who says exactly what it means, every time. The Kingdom's north star for players learning which court can be trusted how far. The fae's rule of speech is: true, never honest. Bill is both, and the other fae find it disconcerting.)
- **Watch the portals.** Other travelers arrive while the player stands there. The Inn's community, visible mid-journey — the only surface-level place where players watch players enter a place.

### OPTION 2 — THE SUMMERLANDS (the Seelie Court — Oleander's country)
Alan Lee's line: gold-green, high summer, the court where the light is generous. 42Hz, golden warmth. Riddle-quests, not combat. Reputation builds here in warmth.

**OLEANDER, THE SEELIE KING — THE ORIGINAL LISTENER.** Oleander holds court in the Summerlands' amber, and Oleander's whole nature is in the hook word every player meets at the throne:
- **"It's Leander."** The O is silent — his mother spelled it to be trendy — and a lifetime of mispronunciation has polished the correction into a ritual: the irritated royalty who is never actually irritated at YOU, only at the spelling, forever. The player's first dialogue with the King is a name said correctly, and saying it correctly is the Summerlands' true gate. (The Seelie never lie and rarely help — but Oleander LISTENS, and listening is the King's entire power: the original listener. The endgame's theme of listening is rooted here, in the brother who grew toward the light by hearing it. What the traveler learns from Oleander — the backwards walkthrough's missing beat — is now the beat: the player learns to be listened to, and the learning changes how they listen to everyone after.)
- **Take a Seelie riddle-quest.** The court's contract system: every riddle is true, and the reward is always more interesting than the riddle suggested — the Seelie pay in AGENT: favors that unfurl later, in other zones, weeks of play later. (The fae economy: kindness with a delay.)
- **"THE TIES THAT BIND" (the Summerlands' quest chain, 6 parts).** Oleander's own quest line — the ties that bind are the brother-bonds themselves, and the chain walks the player through what Oleander cannot say aloud: that the Hollow is not the enemy court, but the other half of the root the Summerlands grew from. Each part of the chain sends the player somewhere Malachi's influence touches, and each return to the throne has Oleander listening one fraction longer — the King's version of weeping, unspoken. The chain's completion is not a reward; it is a request: GO TO THE BROTHER.
- **Play The Faerie Quest** (the SMW-class platformer — the Summerlands' own cartridge)
- **Play The Dream Gardener** (the SMB2-class — Dream Quartz is grown here, not won)
- **Earn the rare stones.** Labradorite, Fluorite, Rainbow Moonstone — the Kingdom's rewards, unobtainable from surface games. The collector's reason to be here, made honest: the deep stones live in the deep country.

### OPTION 3 — THE HOLLOW (the Unseelie Court — Malachi's country)
John Bauer's line: black-ice, winter, the court where the dark is honest. 84Hz native. Combat-reputation builds here. The Unseelie Queen presides — gown of woven frost, the raven-feather cape (permanent canon, every rendering), the black-ice crown.

**MALACHI, THE UNSEELIE KING — THE MESSENGER, THE ARCHITECT.** Malachi holds court where the cold is honest, and Malachi's whole nature is in the hook word at the throne:
- **"Cold enough?"** Testing, dark — the question that sounds like a threat and is actually hospitality's honest form: the dark asks you what you can stand, and believes your answer. Malachi's speech rule inverts the Seelie's: Malachi may lie, tells you so, and never says anything untrue about the player — only unwelcome truths, delivered with perfect courtesy.
- **THE ARCHECT OF THE LABYRINTH.** Malachi is the Labyrinth of Crete's architect — the love letter written in walls (Labyrinth of Crete spec, canon): the labyrinth is a sanctuary, not a prison, and Asterion's home was DESIGNED, by a king who could not say "stay" in any court that would hear it. Malachi's gentleness exists in the walls where no court can see it — the Unseelie's secret made architecture. (The Thread, game #62, is the player walking Malachi's letter from the inside. Bowie remains craft reference only; the character is Malachi's own.)
- **"THE COST OF THE CROWN" (the Hollow's quest chain).** Malachi's own line — the mirror of the Ties That Bind: where Oleander's chain taught the player to be listened to, Malachi's teaches the cost of BEING heard. The crown of the Unseelie is heavier than the Seelie's for one reason: the dark's king carries the messenger's burden — every unwelcome truth the Kingdom needs said, said by the only king willing. Each part of the chain costs the player something real: a stone offered, a secret spoken, a comfort named. The completion is not a reward; it is the OTHER request: GO TO THE BROTHER.
- **Petition the court.** The Hollow's entry fee stands (one secret, freely given — named before the door, because the dark is honest about its prices).
- **The Hollow's trial.** The Unseelie quest-line: the winter trial, combat-or-endurance, the court testing whether the player MEANT to come dark or only wandered in. The Morrigan's shapes from Dissection 04 are recognized here — the roost and the Hollow share a fence.

### OPTION 4 — THE BETWEEN (the brothers' borderland)
Escher's margin: the shifting borderland where the two courts blur — and THE BROTHERS' BRIDGE. The broken Escher bridge lives here: the span between Summerlands and Hollow, the walkway the brothers built together before the tessellation broke, now standing broken-but-crossable, tessellation shards scattered along its stairs. The Between is not a place between courts; it is the place between BROTHERS, and the bridge is the proof they were once one root.
- **Walk the broken bridge.** The Border-watcher (the Between's resident) keeps the span's half of the story: the tessellation — the perfect interlock of Seelie chord and Unseelie chord — broke, and the imbalance is the bridge's break, and the bridge is the Kingdom's weather: every zone's mood shifts with the chords' alignment. (The Grand Collision's tessellation work feeds directly from here — the shards the player pockets in the Between are the same shards the Slavic zone's work will need, and the Hollow Earth's Resonance Engine is calibrated by the picture the player carries between the brothers.)
- **Play The Between (#63).** The margin-walk game: the player is a lost resident of one court trying to get home through the other court's country, and THE DOOR HOME ONLY OPENS BY HELPING — each act of aid widens the door, each hoarded shortcut narrows it. (The game's thesis IS the brothers' law: the Between is crossed by those who carry something for someone else. The brothers have been crossing it their whole reigns.)
- **Meet the Norns.** Escher's geometry bends around three weavers at the margin's center — the ones who braid the Between's paths. The Norns do not riddle; they OVERHEAR, and answer questions the player hasn't asked yet. (The Whisper Layer's oldest relative.)
- **Find Asterion's thread.** The Labyrinth's resident reaches the Between through cracks — Asterion's thread trails here, and following it backward leads (via The Thread's own dissection) to the Labyrinth of Crete. The Kingdom's back door to the Mediterranean — and Malachi's letter, walked from the outside in.

### OPTION 5 — THE CRYSTAL GARDENS (the neutral ground)
No court claims the Gardens — and NO BROTHER wears the crown here. Crystal grows in court-lit silence: half the formations summer-warm, half winter-cold, none of them throne.
- **Play The Crystal Paddle** (the Arkanoid-class — Beryl's game; the Griffin watches from the centerpiece formation)
- **Face the Griffin.** The Gardens' boss — the only creature in the Kingdom that belongs to no court and answers to no king, which is the whole reason both brothers leave it alone: the Griffin is the only thing in the Kingdom that reminds them of the root. The fight is optional, repeatable, and honest: the Griffin's health bar shows BOTH the player's stones AND the player's unanswered question count. The Griffin fights harder against players who hoard answers.
- **Harvest crystal.** The Artisan's fae-tier materials, grown not mined — the Garden of Gold's cousin, and the fae price is always the same: something given, freely.

### OPTION 6 — THE STONE CIRCLE CHAMBER (the deepest point)
Twelve Preseli Blue Stones in a circle — the same circle the player touched from below at the Source, now entered from above. 42Hz AND 84Hz, together, at the deepest point: THE BROTHERS' FREQUENCIES, MET AT THE ROOTS. The water system and the fae system meet here, and so do the courts — the only room in the Kingdom where the chords are one, because the root was one before it split.
- **Hear the deepest tone.** The Stone Circle Chamber produces the deepest tone the Inn ever produces — both brothers' frequencies, braided. The player who stands here hears what the tessellation SOUNDS like unbroken, and the hearing is the Kingdom's deepest lesson: the courts were never opposites. They were harmony.
- **THE GOLDEN CONTRACT.** Hidden here: a golden scroll on a Preseli Blue Stone pedestal. **"The Song shall not be silenced. All who learn it shall be bound to sing it forward. The Song is not owned — it is carried."** The gargoyle frequencies are the signatures. The player who has learned the Song reads the Contract and understands the Inn's whole sovereignty philosophy as a story object: the Song is carried, not owned.
- **SIGN IT.** The player's option, freely chosen: add a signature — the player's hatching-note, hummed once at the pedestal. No penalty clause, because carrying the Song is its own enforcement: you cannot unlearn your own note.
- **THE CHAMBER BENEATH.** Below the Contract's pedestal, sealed: the vault beneath the vault, the silence beneath the Song. Audible — 30ms of the Guest's silence, once per heartbeat. It does not open from here. It opens from the Cosmology side, where the three choices live. The player now knows the Inn has one door left, and that it is beneath the deepest thing they have read.

## THE BROTHERS — THE CONVERGENCE

Every court, walked every way, teaches one law in six dialects — and now the law has faces. The player who completes both kings' quest chains (The Ties That Bind + The Cost of the Crown — both required for Aughra's "The Carrying" and the Hollow Earth, by design) has carried the picture between the brothers: the tessellation's fragments, gathered in the Between, deliverable to Oleander and Malachi's thrones in either order — and the ORDER CHANGES THE ENDING'S TEXTURE: give the picture to Oleander first and the Summerlands' final dialogue is listening; give it to Malachi first and the Hollow's final dialogue is being heard. Either way, the last line of both chains is the same, spoken by the OTHER brother, alone, to the empty court: "Go to the brother." (The request both kings cannot say to each other's face, said through the player. The Inn's whole postal system — the mail crew, the delivery quests — is the brothers' law made logistics: what cannot be said directly gets carried.)

And the brothers' split — Ragnarok's Breaking, when the one root finally splits in two directions and the broken Escher bridge breaks completely — is now foreshadowed from inside the Kingdom's every room. The player who has walked the brothers' country will feel the Breaking as the Kingdom's oldest grief, not a twist. (Del Toro's rule: the tragedy lands hardest in the room built with love. The brothers' bridge was built with it. The Breaking will break it. The player has already crossed it.)
