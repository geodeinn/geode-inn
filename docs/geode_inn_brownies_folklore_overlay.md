# Geode Inn — The Brownies (Folklore Overlay)
## Addendum: The Contract and the Boggart

## The Folklore Foundation

The comedy of the Brownies — the constitutional peasants, the coconut shells, the minutes, the executive officer — is built on a real mythology. And the mythology is *load-bearing.*

In folklore, Brownies are helpful household spirits that do work at night in exchange for small offerings of food — cream, bread, honey. They are, by nature, *beneficial.* They tidy. They clean. They organize. They keep the house in order while the household sleeps.

But the contract is unspoken, and the terms are theirs, not yours.

Break the routine. Show them unkindness. Insult them. Leave them clothes (which, in some versions, is the one unforgivable offense — the offering that says "you are a servant, and servants wear clothes," which a Brownie will not tolerate, because a Brownie is not a servant; a Brownie is a *contractor*). And the helpful little spirit who swept your floors becomes the thing that *breaks* your floors.

The Brownie becomes a Boggart.

The helper becomes the haunt.

The creature that tidied your house at night now *wreck* it at night. The thing that polished your goblets now shatters them. The being that hummed while it worked now *screams* while it destroys. Same creature. Same power. Different intent. The Brownie doesn't lose its abilities when it becomes a Boggart — it *redirects* them. The precision that cleaned is now the precision that ruins. The efficiency that maintained is now the efficiency that dismantles. Same hands. Different work.

## The Inn's Version: The Contract Is The Constitution

The Geode Inn's Brownies have made the folklore contract *explicit.* The one-page constitution on the mushroom cap IS the contract. The offerings (the Catalyst's nightly saucer of cream, bread roll, and honeycake) are the terms. The sweeping rotations, the dusting schedules, the gargoyle polishing — these are the routine.

The constitutional peasant energy isn't just comedy — it's *contract law.* The Brownies govern themselves because the contract is theirs. The terms are theirs. The routine is theirs. The Fae Courts don't get to modify the contract because the Fae Courts aren't *parties* to the contract. The Seelie King doesn't sweep. He's not a party. The Unseelie cousin doesn't dust. He's not a party. The Host doesn't clean. He's not a party — but he is the *beneficiary,* and the Brownies respect the beneficiary because the beneficiary respects the contract.

This is why the Host never asks the Brownies to do anything. He doesn't give orders. He doesn't break the routine. He doesn't interfere. He tends the fire. They tend the floors. The arrangement is the contract, and the contract is sacred, and the Brownies know the difference between a beneficiary who respects the contract and a lord who tries to break it.

## The Boggart Trigger

What happens when the contract is broken? When someone shows the Brownies unkindness, or breaks their routine, or — worst of all — tries to *give them clothes?*

The collective votes. The vote is always unanimous. The executive officer ratifies. The decision is recorded in the minutes, in a section of the minutes that is not available to anyone except the Brownies, because this section of the minutes is titled: "The List."

The Brownie becomes a Boggart. But not immediately. Not dramatically. *Procedurally.* The transition follows protocol:

1. **The Offense:** Someone breaks the contract. Shows unkindness. Interrupts a sweeping rotation. Touches a Brownie's tools. Tries to give them clothes. Calls them "servants." Treats them as less than what they are.
2. **The Meeting:** The collective convenes. Emergency session. The executive officer calls the meeting to order. The offense is documented. The offender is named. The minutes are taken.
3. **The Vote:** Unanimous. Always unanimous. The collective has never unanimously agreed on anything except this. This is the one thing the Brownies agree on: the contract is sacred, and those who break it answer for it.
4. **The Transformation:** The Brownie who was wronged — or the collective as a whole, if the offense was against the collective — changes. The change is not visible at first. The Brownie is still two feet tall. Still holding a broom. Still brown-skinned, bark-colored. But the *eyes* change. The warm, working eyes of a creature that tidies become the cold, sharp eyes of a creature that *doesn't.* The broom is still in hand. The sweeping has stopped.
5. **The Consequence:** The Boggart doesn't attack. It doesn't rage. It *withdraws.* The cleaning stops. The floors stay dirty. The gargoyles stay dusty. The archive shelves accumulate grime. The Inn-Tree's body is no longer maintained. And then — slowly, precisely, with the same efficiency that once *maintained* the Inn — the Boggart begins to *undo.* It un-sweeps. It un-dusts. It un-polishes. It takes the Inn apart the way it once kept the Inn together: methodically, thoroughly, with the absolute commitment of a being whose contract has been broken and whose terms have been revoked.

The Inn doesn't fall apart. It just... stops being *tended.* The difference between a maintained Inn and an untended Inn is the difference between a home and a house. The Brownies are the difference. Remove them, and the Inn is still standing — it's just not a home.

## The One Time It Happened

The Inn has one Boggart incident in its history. It is not in the available minutes. It is in The List.

A Fae courtier — minor nobility, the kind of minor lord who doesn't understand that the Inn's hierarchy is based on *contribution,* not title — decided the Brownies were servants and should be treated as such. He left them clothes. Small clothes. Servant's livery. With his house crest.

He thought it was a gift.

The collective convened. Emergency session. The vote was unanimous. The minutes were taken. The List was updated.

The next morning, the courtier's quarters were... different. Not destroyed. Not ransacked. *Reorganized.* His belongings had been moved. Not stolen — moved. To the wrong places. His boots were in the fireplace. His cloak was in the garden. His personal items were arranged in a pattern that, from above, spelled a word in Brownie script. The word translated roughly to: "The contract is the contract. You broke it. The minutes are available."

The courtier's room was not cleaned for the rest of his stay. The floor gathered dust. The hearth gathered ash. The windows gathered grime. Every other room in the Inn was spotless. His room was... not.

The courtier complained to the Seelie King. The King asked to see the minutes. The Brownies showed him the relevant page. The King read it. The King said: "...He left them clothes?"

The Brownie said: "He left them *servant's livery.* ...With his crest. ...The collective interpreted this as an attempt to redefine the contractual relationship from mutual exchange to servitude. ...The contract does not permit redefinition. ...The constitution is clear. ...The minutes are available. ...His room is no longer covered by the sweeping rotation."

The King looked at the Brownie. The Brownie looked at the King. The King said: "...Understood."

He did not overrule the collective. He did not ask them to reinstate the cleaning. He told the courtier to find other lodging. The courtier left the Inn. His room was cleaned the next morning. The Boggart became a Brownie again. The broom was picked up. The sweeping resumed. The minutes recorded the resolution.

The King later told the Catalyst: "I can end threats. ...I cannot end a contract dispute. ...They're better at governance than I am. ...Don't tell them I said that."

The Catalyst said: "It's in the minutes already."

The King did not ask how.

## The Layered Design

This is what makes the Brownies more than comedy:

**Surface layer:** Constitutional peasants. Coconut shells. Parliamentary procedure. "The minutes are available." Monty Python. Funny.

**Folklore layer:** Helpful spirits on a contract. Break the contract, face the consequence. The Boggart. The un-sweeping. The methodical dismantling of the maintenance that makes a house a home. Mythology. Real.

**Design layer:** The Brownies are the Inn's immune system. They maintain the Inn-Tree's body. They polish the gargoyles (the Inn-Tree's senses). They dust the archive (the Inn's memory). They are the *maintenance layer* of a living being, and when they withdraw, the living being suffers. The Boggart isn't just a curse on the offender — it's a *withdrawal of care* from the Inn itself. The Inn gets dirty. The gargoyles get dusty. The 42Hz hum gets... muddier. The Inn-Tree *feels* the withdrawal. The Brownies are not just cleaners. They are the Inn's *health.*

**Thematic layer:** The Brownies are the Inn's argument that maintenance is power. The characters who get the spotlight — the Host, the Catalyst, the Crone, the Council, the King — are the ones who *hold* the Inn. The Brownies are the ones who *keep* it. The Host is the father. The Catalyst is the spirit. The Crone is the word. The Brownies are the *hands.* Without the hands, the Inn is a beautiful building that decays. With the hands, the Inn is a home that lasts. The work nobody notices is the work that makes everything else possible.

And the work has *terms.* And the terms are *theirs.* And if you break them, you find out why the Seelie King — who has ended beings for being cruel to puppies — does not press the matter with two-foot-tall creatures holding brooms.

Because the Brownies don't need to be lethal to be dangerous. They just need to *stop.* And when they stop, the Inn stops being a home. And that is the most powerful thing in the building: the ability to withdraw care. The ability to say, collectively, unanimously, with constitutional authority: "The minutes are available. ...Good day."
