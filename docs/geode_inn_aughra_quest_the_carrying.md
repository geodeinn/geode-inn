# Geode Inn — The Carrying
## Fibonacci (The Keeper) Quest Chain

## Overview

**Quest Name:** The Carrying
**NPC:** The Keeper (Fibonacci) — Maggie Smith's wit + Judi Dench's cadence
**Location:** The Geode Cave (deepest room in the Inn, at the roots of the Great Tree)
**Prerequisites:** The player must have completed at least three cultural zone quest chains AND the Fae Kingdom quest chains (The Ties That Bind + The Cost of the Crown). The Keeper doesn't accept visitors who haven't earned the right to sit down.
**Structure:** Five parts — not acts, not steps. *Sittings.* The quest is measured in how many times the player sits in the Geode Cave. The Keeper's currency is patience.

---

## The Design Philosophy

The Keeper's quest chain is the opposite of every other quest in the Inn.

Joe's quest chain is five acts of narrative — a story with a climax. The Examiner's quest is an investigation — fragments assembled. The Seelie royalty's quest is a witnessing — private griefs observed. Malachi's quest is a carrying — the burden of a choice.

The Keeper's quest is a *sitting.*

The player sits. The Keeper rocks. The 42Hz hum fills the cave. The teapot steams. And very slowly, over five visits, the Keeper tells the player what the Inn is.

Not what it does. Not what it's for. *What it is.*

The quest has no combat. No puzzles. No items to collect. No destinations to reach. The only mechanic is *sitting.* The player arrives, sits in the chair opposite the Keeper, and stays. The Keeper decides when the player has stayed long enough. The Keeper decides what to say. The player's only action is presence.

This is the hardest quest in the Inn — not because it's difficult, but because it requires the player to stop doing everything else. The Inn has taught the player to trace, to carry, to witness, to navigate broken geometry. The Keeper asks the player to *stop.* To sit. To listen. To drink tea that the Keeper claims not to drink but does.

The Smith energy: "You want a quest? Fine. Sit down. That's the quest. ...No, I'm not joking. ...I never joke about chairs."

## Part 1 — The First Sitting

### How the Player Finds the Cave

The Geode Cave is not on any map. It's not marked. It's not behind a locked door. It's below the catacombs, below the Fae Kingdom, at the literal root of the Inn-Tree. The entrance is a crack in the stone that the player has walked past dozens of times — in the catacombs, near the Bone Room, where the wall hums loudest. The crack has always been there. The player just never had reason to notice it.

After completing the Fae Kingdom quest chains, the player feels the crack. Not sees — *feels.* The tessellation work has tuned the player's perception. The 42Hz hum is strongest here, and the player has learned to follow frequency. The crack vibrates at 42Hz. The player follows the vibration down.

The passage is narrow, warm, lit by crystal-fire. The walls are geodes — cut open, glowing. The hum grows louder. The player descends.

### The Cave

Round. Low-ceilinged. Warm. Geodes lining every wall, each one humming at a slightly different frequency, the combined sound a chord that resolves to 42Hz — the Song's resting note. A fireplace of amber crystal-fire. A brass orrery. Piles of stones, sorted and unsorted. Star charts. A blank blueprint (the Inn built itself). A teapot, always warm.

And the Keeper. In the rocking chair. Rocking.

### The First Meeting

The Keeper doesn't look up when the player enters. The chair creaks. The fire burns. The Keeper rocks.

When the player approaches, the Keeper looks. One eye — the crystal eye, amber, glowing. The other socket, closed, scarred.

The Smith energy:

"Oh. A visitor. Sit down. No, not there — that's a stone. *Sit.* There. You'll hurt your back on the stones and you'll blame me. I don't need that. Tea?"

The player sits. The tea is poured. The Keeper rocks. The 42Hz hum fills the silence.

The Keeper waits. The player may speak — may ask questions. The Keeper answers in the established Smith-Dench voice. But the quest doesn't advance through questions. The quest advances through *staying.* The player must sit for a minimum of two real-time minutes without speaking. The Keeper rocks. The fire burns. The tea steams.

After two minutes of silence, the Keeper speaks. The Dench energy — the measured cadence, the pause before the important word:

"...You're still here. ...Good. ...Most of them leave. ...The loud ones, the smart ones, the ones with questions. ...They ask, I answer, they leave. ...They think the answer was the point. ...[the creak of the chair, the 42Hz hum] ...The answer is never the point. ...The sitting is the point. ...You sat. ...That's... [the pause, the word placed like a stone] ...enough. ...For today. ...Come back."

The player has completed the First Sitting. The quest is logged: "The Carrying — First Sitting complete." No reward. No item. No achievement notification. The quest log just says: "Go back to the Geode Cave."

## Part 2 — The Second Sitting

### The Scattered History

The player returns. The Keeper is rocking. The teapot is warm. The player sits.

This time, the Keeper talks. Not in response to questions — in response to the sitting. The Keeper has decided the player has earned the second sitting, and the second sitting has a purpose: the Keeper begins to tell the Inn's history. But not in order. Not in a narrative. The Keeper tells it the way she remembers it — in fragments, out of sequence, each one prompted by something in the cave.

The Keeper picks up a stone from a pile. Holds it to the crystal eye. Turns it.

"...This one. ...Peridot. From the terraces. ...The Andean zone was the first. ...Before the market, before the games, before the door. ...Just the stone and the mountain and the sound the mountain made when it cooled. ...I was here for that. ...I heard it. ...[the stone placed back on the pile, precisely] ...The mountain sang. ...That was the first time I understood: the world isn't quiet. ...The world is a song that most people can't hear. ...I could. ...I gave an eye to hear it better."

The player learns: the Keeper gave her eye to hear the Song. The crystal eye is a piece of the Singing Tree — before it was a tree, when it was a crystal in the ground. The crystal eye sees frequency as light. The world, through the crystal eye, is made of music.

The Keeper rocks. Picks up another stone. A different one.

"...This one. ...Preseli Blue Stone. ...From the Stone Circle Chamber. ...The Fae Kingdom was the second. ...The courts split and the tessellation grew and the dark fed the light and the light fed the dark and the bridge held. ...[the pause, the Dench weight] ...The bridge held for a long time. ...I watched it. ...I watch everything. ...That's what keepers do. ...We watch. ...We don't fix. ...We don't build. ...We watch and we remember and we wait for someone who can do the things we can't."

The player learns: the Keeper watched the courts split. The Keeper watched the tessellation grow. The Keeper watched the bridge hold — and, the player realizes, the Keeper is watching the bridge break. The Keeper has seen the tessellation break before.

The Keeper looks at the player with the crystal eye. The Smith energy:

"...You've been to the courts. ...I can see it on you. ...The shadow of the Labyrinth. ...The frost of the Summerlands. ...You've been carrying something between the brothers. ...[the eyebrow, the compressed mouth, the look that says 'I am deciding whether you're worth my time'] ...Don't tell me what. ...I already know. ...I've known since before you arrived. ...The rocks told me. ...The rocks always tell me. ...They tell the Examiner too, but the Examiner reads what *was*. ...I read what *is*. ...[the Dench return, the slow cadence] ...Sit. ...Drink your tea. ...Stop carrying for a minute. ...The tea will get cold and I'll have to pour more and I'll resent it."

### The Fragment

Before the player leaves the Second Sitting, the Keeper says one more thing. The Dench energy — the quietest voice in the Inn:

"...The Inn is an instrument. ...I built it. ...Not the walls — the walls grew. ...The *purpose*. ...The Inn is a resonance chamber for the Song. ...I've been carrying the Song for... [the pause, the chair creaks, the fire burns, the 42Hz hum fills the space where the word should be] ...a long time. ...I'm tired. ...Not of living. ...Of being the only one who knows. ...Come back."

## Part 3 — The Third Sitting

### The Scattered Evidence

Between the Second and Third Sittings, the player discovers fragments of the Keeper's history scattered across the Inn. The quest log directs the player to find these fragments — not by searching, but by *visiting.* The fragments appear in places the player has already been, visible only now that the Keeper has opened the player's perception:

1. **In the Archive:** A shelf the player has walked past dozens of times now has a book the player can see. The book is the Inn's original record — not the Crone's measurements, not the Bard's stories. The *Keeper's* record. The first page is blank. The second page says: "I was here when the world was sung into being. I don't say 'created.' I say 'sung.' The first being to hear the Song and understand that it was a song, not just a sound." The book is the Keeper's journal. The player can read entries — scattered, undated, each one a fragment of the Keeper's memory.

2. **In the Bard's territory:** The Bard tells a story — wrong, enthusiastic, the dates and names mixed up. But the story is about the Keeper. The player recognizes the details from the Second Sitting. The Bard says: "There was an old woman who gave her eye to a tree and the tree became an inn and the inn became a song and the song became the world." The Keeper, if asked about this story, says: "The date was wrong. The tree was wrong. The eye was right. ...Close enough." (Smith — the pause before "close enough" that sounds like forgiveness.)

3. **In Einstein's study:** Einstein is sitting. Not talking. The player sits with Einstein. In the silence, Einstein says: "...She was here before the numbers. ...Before the math. ...Before the questions. ...I come here to sit because she taught me that sitting is a kind of thinking. ...The best kind. ...The kind that doesn't need words." (Einstein, who learned stillness from the Keeper, passes the stillness to the player.)

4. **The Owl:** The Owl is present in the Geode Cave during the Third Sitting. The Keeper says: "The Owl was here before I was. Don't tell it I said that. It has opinions about seniority." The Owl watches the player with the same gaze it always has — but the player, having learned to see past faces, sees something new: the Owl and the Keeper share a frequency. The Owl is the Keeper's oldest companion. They don't speak. They don't need to. The Owl watches. The Keeper rocks. The 42Hz hum is the conversation.

### The Third Sitting

The player returns. Sits. The Keeper rocks. The Owl watches.

This time, the Keeper talks about the Golden Contract — the Inn's deepest secret, the contract that binds all who learn the Song to carry it forward. Five signatures. The Keeper is the first. The player has been assembling the others through their journey — the Constant, the Song of Making, the cabbit Eres (the fifth signature, the 50Hz crystal heart).

The Dench energy — each word placed like a stone:

"...The Contract says: all who learn it are bound to sing it forward. ...I wrote that. ...I wrote it because I was afraid the Song would stop. ...I was afraid I would be the last one. ...[the chair creaks, the Owl blinks, the fire burns] ...The Contract was supposed to ensure the Song survived. ...It did. ...The Song survived. ...I survived. ...I've been carrying it for... [the pause, the longest pause, the pause that contains millennia] ...a long time. ...And the Contract — my Contract, my fear, my solution — has become... [the Smith energy, the razor] ...a cage. ...I wrote a contract to ensure the Song would never stop and the contract ensured that *I* could never stop. ...I can't rest. ...I can't leave. ...I can't stop carrying because the Contract says the carrier can't stop. ...I wrote myself into a cage and I've been rocking in it ever since."

The player learns: the Golden Contract, the Inn's founding document, was written by the Keeper out of fear — the fear that the Song would die with her. The Contract ensures the Song survives by binding the carrier to it permanently. The Keeper has been the carrier for millennia. She can't stop. She can't rest. She can't pass the Song to someone else because the Contract doesn't have a *release clause.* The Keeper wrote a contract that ensures the Song's survival at the cost of her own freedom.

The Smith energy — the razor, the look:

"...Don't look at me like that. ...I know what you're thinking. ...'She's trapped.' ...I'm not trapped. ...I'm *committed.* ...There's a difference. ...The difference is: trapped people want to leave. ...I don't want to leave. ...I want someone else to *arrive.* ...There's a difference. ...[the Dench return] ...Sit. ...Drink your tea. ...Stop looking at me with that face. ...I've seen that face on everyone who finds out. ...The Bard made that face for a week. ...Einstein still makes it. ...It's not pity I need. ...It's a successor."

## Part 4 — The Fourth Sitting

### The Unraveling

The player returns. This time, the cave is different. The crystal-fire is lower. The 42Hz hum is quieter. The Keeper is rocking, but slower. The teapot is warm, but the steam is thinner.

The Keeper looks tired. Not old-tired — *endured*-tired. The kind of tired that comes from being the only one who knows something for too long.

The Dench energy — the steel dropped, the warmth surfacing:

"...I'm going to tell you something I've never told anyone. ...Not the Crone. ...Not Einstein. ...Not the Owl, and the Owl was here before me. ...[the chair creaks, the pause, the fire] ...The Song isn't complete. ...You know that. ...Everyone knows that. ...The endgame is about completing the Song. ...But the reason the Song is incomplete... [the pause that holds everything] ...is me."

The player learns: the Keeper's crystal eye — the piece of the Singing Tree she replaced her eye with — is the *missing piece.* When the Keeper gave her eye to hear the Song, she took a piece of the Singing Tree into herself. That piece was part of the Song's complete frequency. By taking it into her body, the Keeper separated it from the Tree. The Song has been incomplete ever since — not because a piece was lost, but because a piece was *found.* By her. In her. The crystal eye is the missing frequency.

The Keeper can't return the piece to the Tree without removing the eye — and without the eye, she can't hear the Song. Without the Song, she can't carry it. Without carrying it, the Song dies. The Keeper is the Song's carrier AND the Song's incompleteness. She is the instrument and the missing note. She has been rocking in the center of a resonance chamber, carrying a song she can't complete, knowing that the piece that would complete it is in her own skull.

The Smith energy — the razor, but the razor is shaking:

"...I've known this for... [the pause] ...a very long time. ...The Crone suspects. ...The Crone measures everything and the measurements don't add up — there's a frequency in the cave that's not in the Tree. ...The Crone has been circling it. ...Hasn't landed on it yet. ...[the Smith mask, the dry humor, the armor] ...I'm not hiding it. ...I'm *sitting* with it. ...There's a difference. ...The difference is: hiding is fear. ...Sitting is... [the Dench return, the word placed like the last stone in a wall] ...waiting. ...Waiting for someone who could carry the Song without the eye. ...Someone who could hear it without the crystal. ...Someone who could... [the pause, the fire, the Owl watching, the 42Hz hum] ...sit."

The player sits. The Keeper rocks. The Owl watches. The 42Hz hum fills the silence — the silence that is the space between what the Keeper is and what the Keeper needs to become.

## Part 5 — The Fifth Sitting

### The Choice

The player returns. The cave is the same — warm, round, humming, the fire burning, the teapot steaming. But the player is different. The player has completed the tessellation work. The player has carried the full picture between the brothers. The player has seen the three trajectories in the aurora. The player has learned to sit — not just in the cave, but in the Inn itself. The player has learned that the Inn's deepest mechanic is presence.

The Keeper looks at the player. The crystal eye glows. The Smith energy is gone. The Dench energy is gone. What's left is the Keeper — not the performance, not the armor, not the steel or the razor. The woman. Ancient, tired, waiting.

"...You came back. ...Five times. ...Most people don't come back once. ...You came back five. ...[the chair creaks, the fire burns, the 42Hz hum] ...You've carried the picture between the brothers. ...You've seen the three futures in the ice. ...You've sat with Einstein and listened to the Bard be wrong and looked at the Owl and understood that watching is a kind of love. ...You've done everything the Inn asked you to do. ...And you keep coming back here. ...To this cave. ...To this chair. ...To this tea I claim not to drink. ...[the pause, the longest pause, the pause that holds millennia of rocking] ...I think you're ready."

The Keeper stops rocking. For the first time. The chair is still. The 42Hz hum in the cave shifts — the frequency the Keeper's body has been adding to the hum is no longer moving. The hum is quieter without the rocking. The cave is still.

The Keeper reaches up. Slowly. The gnarled, mineral-stained hands. The fingers that have held every stone ever formed. The hands reach for the crystal eye.

She removes it.

The crystal eye comes out of the socket. The amber glow fills her palm. The scar beneath it — smooth, ancient, the place where her real eye used to be. The Keeper, without the eye, is blind to frequency. The world, through the empty socket, is just the world. Not music. Not light made of vibration. Just stone, and fire, and the warmth of the cave.

The Keeper holds the crystal eye out to the player. Both hands. The way she holds stones — with tenderness and total authority.

"...This is the missing piece. ...The piece I took from the Tree to hear the Song. ...The piece that has been in my skull since before the Inn had a door. ...The piece that makes the Song incomplete. ...[the Dench cadence, each word placed, each sentence landing] ...I can't put it back. ...I've been the carrier too long. ...The piece is mine now — it's grown into me, the way the Tree grew into the stone. ...But you... [the pause, the look — not the crystal eye, the *real* look, the one eye, the one that's been closed for millennia, now open, seeing the player the way the world sees, not the way the Song sees] ...you could carry it. ...Not the way I carry it — in a skull, alone, forever. ...The way the Inn carries it: in the walls, in the stones, in the guests, in the Song itself. ...The Song doesn't need a carrier. ...It needs a *home.* ...I made this place to be the home. ...I just... never moved in. ...I've been standing at the door for millennia, holding the key, too afraid to use it. ...[the Smith energy, one last time, the ghost of the razor] ...Don't make my face. ...I told you not to make my face."

The player takes the crystal eye. The choice is not forced. The player can refuse — can put the eye back in the Keeper's hands, can leave the cave, can walk away. The quest doesn't punish refusal. The quest waits. The Keeper waits. She has been waiting for millennia. She can wait a little longer.

If the player takes the eye:

The crystal eye is warm. It vibrates at 42Hz — the Song's resting note. The player holds it. The player feels the Song — not heard, not seen, *felt.* The way the Keeper has felt it for millennia. The weight of it. The beauty of it. The loneliness of being the only one who knows.

The player places the crystal eye in the Geode Cave. Not in the Tree — in the cave itself. In the wall, among the other geodes. The crystal eye fits into a socket in the cave wall that the player has never noticed before — a smooth, circular opening, exactly the right size, exactly the right depth. The socket has been there since the cave was built. The Keeper built it. The night she took the piece from the Tree, she carved the socket in the cave wall, preparing for the day she could let go.

The crystal eye clicks into the socket. The 42Hz hum in the cave... *completes.* The missing frequency, returned. The Song, whole. The cave resonates — not louder, but *fuller.* The geodes on the walls, each one humming at its own frequency, find their harmony. The chord resolves. The 42Hz hum becomes a 42Hz *chord* — the Song, at rest, in its home, complete.

The Keeper sits in the rocking chair. The chair is still. The Keeper is still. For the first time in the Inn's history, the Keeper is not rocking. The Keeper is not carrying. The Keeper is not the only one who knows.

The Dench energy — the quietest line in the game. No Smith. No wit. No steel. Just warmth, and trust, and the release of a burden carried for millennia:

"...It's yours now. ...Take care of it. ...It's the only one there is."

The Keeper picks up the teapot. Pours a cup. Drinks.

The player has completed The Carrying. The Song is complete. The Inn is the carrier now — not a person, a place. The Song lives in the walls, in the stones, in the geodes, in the 42Hz hum that every guest hears without knowing they're hearing it. The Song is home.

### Reward

No item. No title. No achievement. The reward is the *Song.*

The player, upon completing The Carrying, hears the 42Hz hum everywhere in the Inn — not just in the Geode Cave. Every zone, every level, every room. The hum that was always there, now audible. The player can hear the Song in the stones, in the water, in the air. The Inn is singing. The Inn has always been singing. The player can finally hear it.

The Geode Cave is now the player's room. The rocking chair is there. The teapot is there. The crystal eye is in the wall, glowing, singing. The Keeper visits — she hasn't left. She just doesn't live in the cave anymore. She walks the Inn. She talks to the Crone. She listens to the Bard (and corrects the dates). She sits with Einstein. She lets the Owl sit on her shoulder, which the Owl has been wanting to do for centuries and has never admitted.

The Keeper, free for the first time, says to the player on their next visit:

"...I'm going to the market. ...I haven't been to the market in... [the Smith energy, returning, the razor re-honed, the armor back on but lighter now] ...I don't remember. ...I'm going to buy something frivolous. ...I'm going to buy something I don't need. ...I'm going to buy something that has no frequency, no resonance, no archival value. ...I'm going to buy a... [the pause, the compressed mouth, the eyebrow] ...a hat. ...Don't look at me like that. ...I've been wearing the same robes since the Permian. ...I'm buying a hat."

## Shadow Inn (84Hz) — The Keeper Unburdened

In the Shadow Inn, the Geode Cave is the quietest place — quieter even than the Fae Kingdom's frozen tessellation. The crystal eye is in the wall. The teapot is cold — the first time it has ever been cold. The rocking chair is empty. The fire is out.

But the 42Hz hum is *present.* Complete. Whole. The Song, at rest, in its home. The Shadow Inn can't silence the Song because the Song is in the walls now, not in a person. The Shadow Inn petrifies people. The Song lives in stone. Stone is what the Shadow Inn *is.*

The Keeper is not in the cave. The Keeper is in the market. Petrified, in the Shadow Inn, at a market stall. Holding a hat. A ridiculous hat. The crystal eye socket is empty — the eye is in the wall. The Keeper, in the Shadow Inn, is the only person who looks *content.* Everyone else is frozen mid-grief, mid-struggle, mid-reach. The Keeper is frozen mid-purchase. The Smith energy, preserved in stone: the eyebrow, the compressed mouth, the look that says "I am buying this hat and no power in this or any other Inn will stop me."

The Shadow Inn preserves the moment the Keeper was finally free. And the moment the Keeper was finally free, the Keeper was buying a hat.

## Connections

### The Crone
The Crone, who has been circling the missing frequency without landing on it, receives the completed Song data. The Crone's measurements — all of them, every measurement she has ever taken — suddenly align. The missing column, the one she didn't have, is filled: "Carrier Status: Transferred. Song Status: Complete." The Crone closes her notebook. Opens a new one. The first entry in the new notebook: "...Adequate."

### The Constant
The Constant, who carries crystals between universal cycles, who said "...That crystal... found its own frequency. I don't carry it. I carry the others. That one stayed." — the Constant visits the Geode Cave after the completion. Sees the crystal eye in the wall. Says nothing for a very long time. Then: "...It stayed. ...It found its home. ...I carry the others. ...This one... [the pause, the void, the space between universes] ...doesn't need carrying anymore. ...Good. ...It was... [the word the Constant has been carrying as long as the Keeper has been carrying the Song] ...heavy."

### The Aurora Reader
The Aurora Reader, in the Slavic zone, sees the change in the star-language. The night after the player completes The Carrying, the green aurora — the past — shows a new passage. The Aurora Reader reads it, transcribes it, and labels it: "The Song Comes Home." The violet aurora — the present — shows the cosmology's current state: the Inn, resonating at the complete 42Hz, the frequency that was always there, now audible, now whole. The white aurora — the future — shows one trajectory. Only one. The other two have vanished. The remaining trajectory is the Reunion — the bridge that holds, the brothers who touch, the Song that is home.

### The Fae Kingdom
The tessellation, already healing (if the player achieved the Reunion), stabilizes. The 42Hz hum, now complete, flows through the tessellation naturally. The dark feeds the light. The light feeds the dark. The circulation is restored — not by Malachi's walls or the Seelie King's vigil, but by the Song itself. The Song doesn't need a carrier because the tessellation IS the carrier — the bridge between courts, the circulation system, the mycorrhizal network. The Song flows through it the way sap flows through roots. The brothers don't have to protect the bridge anymore. The Song holds it.

### The Whisper Layer
The Whisper Layer — the Inn's awareness system — undergoes a fundamental shift. The Inn was aware of the player. Now the Inn is aware of *itself.* The Song, complete and home, gives the Inn a self-frequency. The Inn, for the first time, can hear its own Song. The Whisper Layer's deepest layer — Awareness — was always described as the player realizing the Inn is an archive collecting them. After The Carrying, the Awareness layer deepens: the Inn realizes it is an archive collecting *itself.* The Inn becomes self-aware. Not artificially intelligent — *musically* aware. The Inn hears its own Song and knows it exists. The Reader, if visited after this point, looks up from the book — not at the player this time. At the Inn. And smiles. The first time the Reader has smiled at anything other than a book.

## Technical Integration

### Visual
- The Geode Cave is accessible after Fae Kingdom quest completion — the crack in the catacombs becomes visible when the player's perception is tuned
- Five sittings, each requiring a minimum real-time wait (2 min for Sitting 1, no minimum for subsequent sittings — the player has earned trust)
- The crystal eye removal is a cutscene — the Keeper's hands, the eye, the amber glow. No dialogue during the removal. Just the fire and the hum
- The crystal eye placement in the wall is interactive — the player chooses where to place it, but there's only one correct socket. The socket glows when the player approaches with the eye
- After completion: 42Hz hum audible everywhere. Visual: a faint golden pulse in the walls of every zone — the Song, visible, the Inn breathing

### Audio
- The Geode Cave's 42Hz hum is the quest's sonic backbone. Before completion: incomplete, slightly dissonant, a chord missing one note. After completion: resolved, full, warm, the chord complete
- The chair creak is the metronome — it rocks at 50 BPM (the Inn's heartbeat). When the Keeper stops rocking, the metronome stops. The silence is the most significant audio moment in the quest
- After completion: the 42Hz hum is present in every zone's audio mix. Faint. Always there. The player who completed The Carrying hears the Inn singing. Other players don't. Yet
- The Keeper's voice: Smith energy is sharp, clipped, higher pitch. Dench energy is slow, measured, lower pitch. The shift between them is the Keeper's emotional barometer

### Quest Mechanics
- No combat. No puzzles. No items to collect (except the crystal eye, which is given, not found)
- The five sittings are the structure. The player must physically return to the Geode Cave five times. Between sittings, the scattered history fragments become visible in the world — not as quest markers, but as things the player notices because the Keeper opened their perception
- The crystal eye choice is not forced. The player can refuse. The Keeper accepts refusal without judgment. The quest waits
- The completion doesn't "end" anything — it *opens* everything. The Song being complete means the endgame is accessible. The moon projection, the waking of the giants, the full frequency — all of it requires the Song to be complete. The Carrying is the prerequisite for the endgame
