# Geode Inn — NPC Design: The Black Knight (Camelot Zone)
## "Tis But a Scratch" — The Living Price Tag

---

## The Character

A Camelot knight inspired by Monty Python's Black Knight. Full armor, Black Knight color scheme, standing (then hopping) in the path of the Camelot zone (Moreau artist style, Celtic British accents). The knight challenges the player to pass. The player must defeat the knight in a mini-encounter — but the knight never acknowledges defeat.

Each round of combat removes a limb. The knight never concedes:

- **Both arms gone:** "Tis but a scratch!"
- **One leg gone:** "I've had worse. Come on, you coward!"
- **Both legs gone (hopping on stumps):** "It's just a flesh wound! I'm invincible!" (hopping away)
- **Completely limbless (torso on the ground):** "Alright, we'll call it a draw."

The knight reappears later in the zone, fully intact, as if nothing happened. "Halt! Who goes there?" Same challenge. The cycle repeats. The knight is immortal and in complete denial.

## The "Cost an Arm and a Leg" Connection

The knight is the Inn's living price tag. When something is expensive — a rare stone, a premium crafting commission, an Egg hatch — the Black Knight hops past on the loading screen. The gag:

- Player sees the price. Expensive.
- Loading screen: the Black Knight hops across, missing an arm and a leg.
- Knight: "Tis but a scratch!"
- The visual pun: this costs an arm and a leg. The knight lost both. The knight says it's fine.
- Subtext: if you can afford it, it's worth it. If you can't, the knight would say that's just a scratch too.

The knight reframes expensive as survivable. You lost an arm and a leg? You're still standing. You're still invincible. It's just a flesh wound.

## Loading Screen Variations

- **Standard expensive item:** Knight hops past on one leg. "Tis but a scratch!"
- **Very expensive item:** Knight hops past on stumps, no legs. "It's just a flesh wound!"
- **Absurdly expensive item:** Knight is just a torso on the ground. "Alright, we'll call it a draw."
- **Free item:** Knight stands fully intact, looks confused, doesn't know what to do. "I... I have all my limbs. This has never happened before."

## Camelot Zone Encounter

The knight guards a bridge or path in the Camelot zone. The encounter is not a real boss fight — it's a scripted gag encounter. The player "defeats" the knight by progressing through the zone, and the knight's limb-loss tracks the player's progress:

- Quest 1: Knight loses an arm. "Tis but a scratch!"
- Quest 3: Knight loses the other arm. "Come on, then!"
- Quest 5: Knight loses a leg. "I've had worse!"
- Quest 7 (zone completion): Knight is a torso. "Call it a draw."

The knight respawns for the next player. The knight is the zone's comic relief and its progress tracker — the more limbs the knight has lost, the further you've gotten.

## Voice Profile

**Cultural Roots:** British (Camelot zone — Celtic British accent)
**Delivery:** Booming, overconfident, completely in denial. The voice never wavers regardless of how many limbs are missing. The conviction is absolute. The knight believes the knight is winning even when the knight is a torso.
**Key tell:** "Tis but a scratch!" — said with full conviction every time, regardless of severity. The phrase is the knight's identity. The knight would say it while on fire.

## Artist Style

**Zone:** Camelot (Moreau)
**Linework:** Heavy cel-shaded (tradesperson class) — the knight is a worker, a guard, a function
**Palette:** Black armor, silver highlights, the warm amber and stone of the Camelot zone, and the red of the "flesh wounds" that are never as bad as the knight claims

---

## Design Notes

The Black Knight is the Inn's permission to be expensive. The jewelry, the crafting commissions, the rare stones — these things cost real value. The knight's gag says: you can lose an arm and a leg and still be invincible. The cost is real. The survivability is also real.

The knight is also the Camelot zone's strongest argument for itself: this zone doesn't take itself too seriously. The Moreau art style is gorgeous and mythic, and then a knight hops past on stumps insisting it's fine. The contrast IS the zone's personality — Arthurian grandeur with Python irreverence living side by side, the same way they've lived together in British culture since 1975.
