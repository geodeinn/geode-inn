# Geode Inn — The Ruined Castle: The Left-Behind
## NPC Design — Dobby Visually, Hoggle in Spirit

## Overview

The Ruined Castle zone (Theme Brainstorm #30 — "The Ruined Castle — Rebuilding") features a small population of NPCs who were left behind when the castle fell. They are the small folk — the servants, the keepers, the ones who stayed when everyone else fled. They've been alone in the ruins for a very long time. They are ragged. They are grumpy. They are the most loyal creatures you will ever meet, and they will never admit it.

**Visual DNA: Dobby** (Harry Potter) — small, thin, enormous expressive eyes, oversized ragged clothing, the look of a creature that was made to serve but has outlasted its purpose. The pathos of something small and devoted in a space that has gone to ruin.

**Interaction DNA: Hoggle** (Labyrinth) — grumpy, calls you names, acts like helping you is an inconvenience, has complicated allegiances, helps you anyway, is secretly the most loyal character in the story, and will be furious if you point that out.

## The Species: The Keep-Folk

### Origin

The Keep-Folk are a type of brownie-adjacent household spirit from European castle tradition — the *coblyn* or *boggart* archetype. They are the spirits who tend castles, keeps, and great houses. Not the grand spirits — not the gargoyles or the Keepers. The *small* ones. The ones who sweep the floors, mend the tapestries, polish the armor, and know every stone by name.

When a castle is abandoned, most spirits leave. The gargoyles go dormant. The resident deity moves on. The magical protections fade. But the Keep-Folk don't leave. They *can't*. They are bound to the structure. The castle is their body, in a sense. When the castle falls, they fall with it — not dying, but *diminishing*. A Keep-Folk in a ruined castle is like a ghost of a ghost: still there, still working, still tending — but ragged, thin, barely holding together. They keep sweeping floors that no longer exist. They polish armor that has rusted to nothing. They mend tapestries that have rotted away. The work is the purpose. The purpose is all they have.

### Appearance (Dobby DNA)

- **Size:** ~2-3 feet tall. Small. Smaller than they should be — a Keep-Folk in a thriving castle is 3-4 feet, plump, healthy. In a ruined castle, they've shrunk. The ruin has taken from them.
- **Frame:** Thin. Almost gaunt. The Dobby thinness — not starved, but *diminished*. Like a candle that's been burning for too long.
- **Eyes:** Enormous. This is the Dobby signature. Huge, expressive, luminous eyes that take up a disproportionate amount of the face. The eyes are the part of them that hasn't diminished — they're still full size, still full of feeling, still too big for the face they're in. The eyes are the tell: these creatures feel everything. They can't hide it. The eyes betray every emotion, even when the mouth is saying "go away."
- **Ears:** Large, pointed, slightly bat-like. Mobile — they move with emotion. Flat when angry, perked when curious, drooping when sad.
- **Skin:** Grey-brown, like old stone. Slightly translucent at the edges — the tips of the ears and fingers are faintly see-through, a sign of their diminishing. In a thriving castle, they'd be opaque. In the ruin, the light shows through.
- **Clothing:** Ragged. Oversized. Each Keep-Folk wears the remains of a livery — a castle servant's uniform from whatever era the castle was last inhabited. The clothes are centuries out of date, patched with whatever fabric they've scavenged from the ruins — tapestry fragments, curtain scraps, even old banners. The effect is Dobby's pillowcase aesthetic but more layered, more historical. Each patch is from a different era of the castle's history.
- **Hands:** Long-fingered, delicate, always busy. The hands are the most alive part of them — even when standing still, the hands are working: straightening a rag, brushing dust, touching a wall. The hands remember the work even when the work is gone.

### Personality (Hoggle DNA)

The Keep-Folk interact like Hoggle from Labyrinth:

- **Grumpy by default.** The first response to anything is complaint. "What do you want?" "Why are you here?" "Can't you see I'm busy?" (They are never busy. They are always busy. Both are true.)
- **Insulting, but not mean.** They call the player names — "tall one," "soft hands," "castle-stomper," "dust-bringer" (because the player tracks dust into their ruins). The insults are not cruel — they're *affectionate*, but the affection is buried under six layers of grump. The player who pays attention notices the insults get gentler over time. "Tall one" becomes "the tall one" becomes "our tall one."
- **Self-interested on the surface.** Everything is a transaction. "You want me to show you the armory? What's in it for me?" The player must offer something — a polished stone, a clean cloth, a repaired wall. The Keep-Folk always drive a hard bargain. The bargain is never really about the payment. The bargain is about the player *asking* — about acknowledging that the Keep-Folk have something worth bargaining for.
- **Secretly loyal.** This is the Hoggle core. The Keep-Folk will help the player. They will guide them through the ruins, warn them of dangers, show them hidden passages. They will do this while complaining the entire time. "Fine. I'll show you. But only because if you fall down a well, I'll have to fish you out, and I don't want to fish you out. I have better things to do." (They do not have better things to do. They have nothing else to do. That's the tragedy.)
- **Conflicted allegiances.** Like Hoggle working for Jareth while helping Sarah, the Keep-Folk have a complicated relationship with whatever force ruined the castle. Some of them served the lord who fell. Some of them served the force that brought the ruin. They don't all agree about which side they're on. This creates faction dynamics among the Keep-Folk — some want the castle rebuilt, some want it left in ruin, some want it rebuilt *differently*.
- **Furious if you call them kind.** The Hoggle rule. If the player says "you're a good friend" or "you're kind," the Keep-Folk recoils like they've been burned. "I am NOT kind. I am *practical*. Kindness is a luxury. I don't have luxuries. I have ruins. Now stop talking and start rebuilding." (They are kind. They know they are kind. They cannot afford to be kind. Being kind means caring, and caring means losing, and they've already lost everything except the ruins.)

## Individual Keep-Folk (Beginning Roster)

### 1. Wick — The Sweeper

**Role:** Guide NPC — the first Keep-Folk the player meets. Introduces the castle zone and the rebuilding mechanic.

**Appearance:** The most Dobby-like — thin, huge green eyes, a ragged curtain draped over one shoulder like a toga. Holds a broom made from a branch and dried thistle. The broom is mostly bristles-less. He sweeps anyway. The sound of the broom on stone is the first thing the player hears entering the castle.

**Personality:** The grumpiest. The most Hoggle. Every sentence starts with a complaint. Every interaction ends with "now leave me alone." He has been sweeping this hall for 300 years. The hall has no roof. He sweeps the rain off the floor.

**Key dialogue:**
- *(First meeting):* "Oh. A visitor. Wonderful. I love visitors. Visitors bring dust. Dust is my favorite thing. I can't get enough dust. I sweep dust all day and then visitors bring MORE dust. It's a gift. You're a gift. ...What do you want?"
- *(When the player offers to help rebuild):* "Rebuild? ...You want to rebuild? Ha. Everyone wants to rebuild. Nobody wants to SWEEP. Sweeping is what keeps a castle alive. Not walls. Not roofs. Sweeping. I have been keeping this castle alive with a broom for three hundred years and nobody — NOBODY — has ever said 'thank you, Wick, for sweeping.' ...I don't want a thank you. I want a new broom. This one's been dead since before you were born."
- *(When the player gives him a new broom — a crafted item):* He takes it. He runs his hand along the bristles. He doesn't say thank you. He sweeps. The sound is different — fuller, richer. The floor is cleaner. He says, without looking up: "...It's a decent broom. Not great. Decent. ...You can stay. Not because of the broom. Because the hall needs sweeping and you're already here and I'm not carrying you out."
- *(After the player has helped rebuild a section of the castle):* "You rebuilt the east wing. ...It's not bad. The walls are straight. The floor is level. ...You swept before you left, didn't you. I can tell. The corners are clean. ...I didn't ask you to sweep. I can sweep my own corners. ...They're good corners. ...Stop looking at me like that. I'm not being NICE. I'm being ACCURATE. The corners are good. That's a fact. Facts are not feelings. ...Go away. I have corners."

### 2. Rindle — The Stitcher

**Role:** Crafting NPC — mends tapestries, clothing, and (reluctantly) the player's gear.

**Appearance:** Slightly plumper than Wick (the Dobby variation — not all Keep-Folk are gaunt; Rindle has kept some of her fullness because her work keeps her connected to the castle's past). Huge amber eyes. Wears a coat made from dozens of tapestry fragments, each from a different room of the castle. Her hands are never still — always stitching, even while talking. Thread trails from her fingers like spider silk.

**Personality:** Less grumpy than Wick, more *sarcastic*. The Hoggle quality is in her willingness to help combined with her insistence that she's only doing it because the alternative is worse. She mends things. She's been mending things that don't exist anymore — tapestries that have rotted, clothes that have turned to dust. She mends the memory of the object. When the player brings her something real to mend — actual gear, actual cloth — she is furious. Because real things can be ruined again. Memory can't be ruined. It's already as ruined as it gets.

**Key dialogue:**
- *(When the player brings damaged gear):* "Oh, wonderful. Something else to fix. Because I don't have enough to fix. I fix tapestries that fell apart in 1423. I fix gloves that evaporated in the Reformation. I fix THREAD that doesn't exist anymore. And now you bring me — what is this? Leather? Real leather? Stitched with — is this cotton thread? ...It's so beautiful. ...I mean it's AWFUL. The stitching is crooked. The leather is scuffed. Who taught you to sew? A troll? ...Give it here. I'll fix it. Not because I want to. Because if I don't, you'll die in the ruins, and then you'll become a ghost, and ghosts don't need mending, and I'll have NOTHING TO DO."
- *(When the player compliments her work):* "Don't. Don't compliment me. I'm not doing this for compliments. I'm doing this because the thread is fraying and I CANNOT ABIDE fraying thread. It's not about you. It's about the thread. The thread has a standard. I meet the standard. That's the whole arrangement."
- *(When the player brings her a tapestry fragment found in the ruins):* She takes it. Her hands go still for the first time. She looks at it. The amber eyes fill. "This is from the Great Hall. ...The hunt scene. The stag. The dogs. ...I stitched this. Not this version — the original. 1487. Three months of work. The dogs had individual spots. I gave each one a name in the stitching. ...You can't see it anymore. The thread's gone. But the pattern is still there. In the weave. Like a skeleton. ...I'll fix it. I'll fix it right now. Not because you asked. Because the dogs deserve their names back."

### 3. Cobble — The Stone-Knower

**Role:** Building guide NPC — knows every stone in the castle, which ones are load-bearing, which ones are decorative, which ones are *singing*.

**Appearance:** The thinnest of the three — almost translucent at the edges. His skin is the color of the castle's limestone, as if the stones have been leaching into him for centuries. Huge grey eyes — the color of wet stone. He touches walls constantly, his hand pressed flat against the stone, listening. He doesn't sweep or stitch. He *listens*. He knows the castle's bones.

**Personality:** The quietest Keep-Folk. Less Hoggle, more... resigned. He doesn't complain because complaint requires energy he doesn't have. He simply states facts. "This wall is load-bearing. It holds up the east tower. The east tower is fallen. So this wall holds up nothing. ...It still likes to be held. I hold it." The pathos here is Dobby's — the creature devoted to a structure that can no longer return the devotion.

**Key dialogue:**
- *(When the player asks about the castle's structure):* "The stones remember. Not like people remember — not in stories. In pressure. In weight. This stone has been holding up that stone for 800 years. If you remove this one, that one falls. ...The one above it remembers too. And the one above that. All the way to the top. The whole castle is a stack of stones remembering what they're supposed to do. ...Most of them are doing it wrong now. The walls that still stand don't hold up what they used to hold up. They hold up... each other. That's enough. That's enough for stones."
- *(When the player rebuilds a collapsed wall):* He places his hand on the new stones. He's quiet for a long time. "These stones are... new. They don't know the castle yet. They don't know what they're holding. ...I'll tell them. I'll put my hand on them every day and tell them what they're for. ...You can't just place a stone and walk away. You have to *introduce* it. To the stones next to it. To the stones above it. To the foundation. ...It's slow. Everything I do is slow. But the stones know me. They've known me for 800 years. ...The new ones will learn."
- *(If the player asks him to leave the castle):* "Leave? ...I can't leave. I'm the one who knows the stones. If I leave, who tells the new stones what they're for? Who holds the old stones when they're tired? ...No. I'm staying. The castle needs me. ...The castle has needed me for 300 years and I've been here for every day of it and I will be here for every day after. That's not loyalty. That's... geology. Stones don't leave. Neither do I."

### 4. Thatch — The Roof-Watcher

**Role:** Quest NPC — obsessed with the castle's missing roof. Every quest he gives relates to roofing, covering, sheltering. The rebuilding mechanic's structural progression flows through Thatch — the castle can't be fully restored until the roof is rebuilt, and Thatch is the one who knows what the roof looked like, how it was built, and why it fell.

**Appearance:** The most ragged — his clothing is entirely made from roofing materials: bits of thatch, slate fragments, strips of lead flashing. He is constantly looking up. His eyes — huge, pale blue, like sky through a hole in a roof — are always fixed upward, even when talking. The Dobby quality is in his obsession — the single-minded devotion to one thing (the roof) that defines his entire existence.

**Personality:** Anxious. Not grumpy like Wick or sarcastic like Rindle — *anxious*. The Hoggle quality is in the way his anxiety makes him seem selfish ("I need the roof! I don't care about the walls!") when really it's the opposite — he's anxious because he cares too much about everything and the roof is the one thing he can focus on to keep from being overwhelmed.

**Key dialogue:**
- *(First meeting — he's standing in the great hall looking up at the open sky):* "Do you see that? That's sky. SKY. Do you know what's supposed to be between us and the sky? ROOF. There is supposed to be ROOF. Do you know what rain does to a floor? I assume you do. You seem like someone who walks on floors. Do you LIKE walking on wet floors? Because I don't. I don't like it at all. I have been looking at sky for 200 years and I am TIRED of it. ...Can you fix a roof? Can you? Please? ...I didn't say please. I said 'if.' 'If you can.' That's not please. That's conditional. Don't look at me like that."

- *(When the player begins rebuilding the roof):* He watches. He can't help. He's too small, too weak. But he stands below and watches every stone, every beam, every slate. He directs: "No. No, that beam goes THERE. Not there. THERE. The load goes to the east wall. The east wall is Cobble's. Cobble says it's ready. PUT IT THERE. ...Yes, I know I'm not building. I'm SUPERVISING. Supervising is important. ...It IS important. Don't patronize me. I've been supervising this roof for 200 years. I know where every beam goes. ...They fell. But I REMEMBER where they went."

- *(When the roof is complete):* He stands under it. He looks up. He sees ceiling, not sky. He's quiet for a long time. The pale blue eyes fill. "...It's dry. It's dry under here. ...I forgot what dry sounded like. The rain... it used to hit the roof and you could hear it. Tap tap tap. Like fingers. I missed that sound. ...Thank you. ...I DID say thank you. I'm ALLOWED. It's been 200 years. I'm ALLOWED ONE. Don't tell Wick. He'll be insufferable."

## The Rebuilding Mechanic

The Keep-Folk are the NPCs who drive the castle's rebuilding. The castle starts as a ruin — walls fallen, roof gone, floors collapsed. The player, with the Keep-Folk's guidance, rebuilds it section by section:

1. **Wick** guides the clearing — sweeping debris, removing rubble, preparing the space.
2. **Cobble** guides the walls — identifying which stones to reuse, which to replace, how to place them so they remember their purpose.
3. **Rindle** guides the interiors — tapestries, furnishings, the soft elements that make a castle a home.
4. **Thatch** guides the roof — the final stage, the covering that makes the castle whole again.

As each section is rebuilt, the Keep-Folk change — they get slightly plumper, slightly more opaque, slightly less ragged. The castle's restoration restores *them*. They are, after all, bound to the structure. Rebuild the castle, rebuild its people.

When the castle is fully restored, the Keep-Folk are no longer Dobby-thin. They're healthy, round, bright-eyed — the way they looked when the castle was alive. Their grumpiness remains (it's personality, not pathology — Hoggle is grumpy even when things are fine), but the edge is softer. The anxiety is gone. The pathos is replaced by contentment.

And Wick, standing in the great hall, sweeping a floor that has a roof over it for the first time in 200 years, says: "It's a decent castle. Not great. Decent. ...The corners are good. ...Stop looking at me."

## The Hoggle Moment

Every Keep-Folk has a Hoggle moment — the scene where their grumpiness breaks and the loyalty shows. It happens differently for each:

- **Wick:** When the player is in danger in the ruins (a structural collapse, a hostile creature), Wick appears with his broom — the new one the player gave him. He doesn't fight. He sweeps. He sweeps the debris away. He sweeps a path. He sweeps the player to safety while saying: "I am NOT rescuing you. I am SWEEPING. You happen to be ON the floor. The floor is my job. I'm doing my job. This is not a rescue. This is janitorial work. ...MOVE YOUR FEET. I can't sweep your feet."

- **Rindle:** When the player's gear is destroyed in a critical moment, Rindle appears with a fully repaired set — better than new, reinforced with tapestry-thread from the Great Hall. "I didn't fix this for you. I fixed it because the THREAD was FRAYING and I CANNOT ABIDE fraying thread. The fact that you're wearing it is... coincidental. ...The reinforcement is because the original stitching was AWFUL. Not because I care if you survive. Because I don't. I care about THREAD QUALITY. ...Go. Before the thread gets dirty."

- **Cobble:** When a rebuilt wall is about to collapse (the player rushed the construction, didn't let Cobble introduce the stones), Cobble throws himself against the wall — his small body pressing against the stones, holding them. He's too small. He can't hold it. But he does. The stones hold for him — not because of his strength, but because they know him. They've known him for 800 years. The wall stabilizes. Cobble, crushed against the stone, says: "The stones... know me. ...I told them to hold. They held. ...That's not loyalty. That's... masonry. Good masonry listens. ...I'm fine. Don't touch me. I'm part of the wall now. ...I said don't touch me."

- **Thatch:** When the final roof beam is placed and the castle is whole, Thatch stands in the rain — *on top of the roof*, in the rain, by choice. He looks up. The rain hits his face. He's crying. But it's raining, so you can't tell, and he will deny it forever. "I'm not crying. It's RAINING. I'm on a ROOF. In RAIN. This is what happens on roofs. ...I'm staying up here. The roof needs me. ...The roof has needed me for 200 years and I'm finally HERE. ...Go away. I'll come down when it stops raining. ...It might not stop. That's fine. I have time."

## Connection to the Inn

- **The Ruined Castle is a rebuilding zone.** The Keep-Folk are the NPCs who make rebuilding meaningful — not just placing stones, but restoring the creatures who were bound to the structure. The player who rebuilds the castle isn't just fixing a building. They're saving the small folk who were slowly dying with it.
- **The Dobby DNA connects to the brownies** (Brindle, Cobble, Thatch — the Keeper's Brownie Trio). The Keep-Folk are cousins of the brownies — the same species, different context. A brownie in a thriving inn is plump and mischievous. A Keep-Folk in a ruined castle is thin and grumpy. Same creature, different circumstances. The player who has met the brownies first will recognize the family resemblance — and will understand what the Keep-Folk *could be* if the castle is restored.
- **The Hoggle DNA connects to the Labyrinth aesthetic** that runs through the Inn's design (the Owl is Labyrinth-inspired, the Architect of Stairs is Henson-era Fae). Hoggle is the Labyrinth character who is morally complicated, secretly good, and furious about being good. The Keep-Folk carry that same energy: they are the characters who help you while insisting they're not helping.
- **The Song of Making:** As the castle is rebuilt, the Keep-Folk begin to hum. At first it's faint — barely audible, like wind through empty halls. As more sections are restored, the humming grows. When the castle is complete, the Keep-Folk hum together — and the hum is 42 Hz. The castle becomes a node of the Song. The ruined castle, restored, joins the Inn's resonance network. The Keep-Folk were always part of the Song. They just couldn't sing it in a ruin.
