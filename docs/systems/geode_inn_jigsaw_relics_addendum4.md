### 17. The Water Zone — Drift Pieces
**Zone:** Water Zone (Vampire Hunter D gothic anime aesthetic)
**The Mechanic:** The pieces won't stay still. They float around the board in slow currents — drifting, rotating, sinking, rising. You have to catch a piece and place it before it drifts away. Some pieces sink deeper and take longer to retrieve. You're not just assembling — you're fishing.

**Details:**
- 40 pieces, all buoyant, all slowly drifting on invisible currents
- Pieces move at different speeds and directions — some cluster in eddies, some drift alone
- Deeper pieces (cooler colors, heavier shapes) sink toward the bottom edge and must be "retrieved" by dragging upward through the water
- A piece caught and held stops drifting — but the current still pushes it slightly, so you feel the water resisting your placement
- If you release a piece without placing it, it resumes drifting from where you let go
- Placed pieces are anchored — they stay put, but the current flows around them, subtly affecting nearby unplaced pieces
- No timer, but the drift creates natural urgency without pressure

**Difficulty Levels:**
- Apprentice: slow drift, pieces cluster near the frame edges
- Journeyman: standard drift speed, pieces spread across the board
- Master: fast drift, pieces rotate as they move (must catch the right rotation)
- Archive Keeper: fast drift + rotation + pieces are semi-transparent (water is murky at depth)

**Narrative Intro:**
"The water does not hold still for anyone. The creatures here have learned to move with it, not against it. The pieces you seek are drifting — they have been drifting since before you arrived. Catch what you can. Place what you catch. The water will not wait, but it will not hurry either. Rebuild the depth."

**Audio:**
- Ambient: deep underwater drone, 42Hz filtered through water
- Piece catch: a soft splash, a bubble
- Piece place: a deep resonant click, muffled by water
- Piece drift (release): a soft whoosh of current
- Full completion: the water goes still. All drift stops. A single clear tone rings through still water — the first moment of silence the Water Zone has ever known.

**Reward:** Stone chips + "The Fisherman" achievement + Water Zone wallpaper

### 18. The Faerie Kingdom — Shifting Pieces
**Zone:** Faerie Kingdom (Dalí surrealist aesthetic)
**The Mechanic:** The tabs and blanks aren't fixed. A piece that fit a moment ago doesn't fit now because the Fae changed the shape. You have to watch for the moment the piece stabilizes — a brief stillness in the shifting — and place it then. Miss the window and the shape changes again.

**Details:**
- 36 pieces, all subject to shape-shifting
- Each piece's tabs and blanks morph slowly and continuously — a tab elongates, a blank fills in, a corner rounds, an edge straightens
- Every piece has a "stable moment" — a brief window (2-3 seconds) where its shape locks into a valid jigsaw form
- You can see the stable moment coming: the shifting slows, the edges sharpen, the piece "decides what it is" for just long enough to place
- Miss the window and the piece resumes shifting — you have to wait for the next stable moment
- Some pieces shift faster (wild fae), some slower (seelie court pieces are more cooperative)
- Placed pieces stay fixed — the Fae respect what's already been built, they only shift what's unplaced

**Difficulty Levels:**
- Apprentice: long stable windows (5 seconds), slow shifting
- Journeyman: standard windows (3 seconds), moderate shifting
- Master: short windows (1.5 seconds), fast shifting, pieces also shift COLOR during unstable phases
- Archive Keeper: very short windows (1 second), fast shifting, color shift, AND pieces swap positions with each other when you're not holding them

**Narrative Intro:**
"The Fae do not keep their shape. A thing that was a door is now a window. A thing that was a window is now a mirror. The trick is not to force them. The trick is to watch. When the piece decides what it is — for just a moment — you place it. If you are too slow, it becomes something else. The Fae are not cruel. The Fae are simply not done deciding. Rebuild the becoming."

**Audio:**
- Ambient: shimmering, unstable tones — notes that bend and shift pitch
- Piece shift: a soft crystalline chime (the sound of shape changing)
- Stable moment: a clear sustained note — the piece has decided
- Piece place (during stable): a satisfying click + the stable tone resolves
- Missed window: the stable tone bends back into shimmering
- Full completion: all shimmering stops. A pure, fixed, stable chord plays — the Fae, for one moment, have agreed on what they are. It won't last. But it's beautiful.

**Reward:** Stone chips + "The Observer" achievement + Faerie Kingdom wallpaper

### 19. The Catacombs — Buried Pieces
**Zone:** Catacombs (Goya — dark, weight of history)
**The Mechanic:** Nothing is visible at the start. Pieces are buried under a layer of dust and stone. You excavate by brushing — mouse drag or finger swipe clears the surface gradually. Some pieces are deeper than others. You assemble as you uncover.

**Details:**
- 40 pieces, all buried under a dark, dusty surface
- Brushing mechanic: drag across the surface to clear dust — the more you brush, the more is revealed
- Dust particles drift and settle (visual effect) — cleared areas slowly re-accumulate if left alone, so you must work steadily
- Some pieces are near the surface (light brushing reveals them), others are deep (require sustained brushing in one spot)
- As pieces are uncovered, they emerge into dim candlelight — their colors are muted, dusty, ancient
- Once a piece is fully uncovered, it can be dragged to the assembly area
- The assembly area is a clean stone surface — pieces placed there show their true colors (the dust stays in the catacombs, not on the puzzle)
- You can only see what you've uncovered. The rest is darkness and dust.

**Difficulty Levels:**
- Apprentice: dust clears easily, re-accumulation is slow, pieces are near the surface
- Journeyman: standard brushing effort, moderate re-accumulation, varied depths
- Master: hard-packed surface (requires sustained brushing), fast re-accumulation, some pieces buried very deep
- Archive Keeper: hard surface + pieces are buried in layers (must clear through multiple strata), and some pieces are buried UNDER other pieces (must remove the top piece to access the one beneath)

**Narrative Intro:**
"Everything here is buried. Not hidden — buried. There is a difference. Hidden means someone doesn't want you to find it. Buried means time didn't ask permission. The dust does not malicious. The dust is patient. Brush. Uncover. Remember what was here before the forgetting. The catacombs do not forget. They simply accumulate. Rebuild the memory."

**Audio:**
- Ambient: deep, muffled silence — the sound of being underground, occasional distant stone settling
- Brushing: soft scraping, dust particles, dry and intimate
- Piece uncover: a soft exhale — like the piece has been holding its breath for centuries
- Piece place: heavy stone-on-stone, definitive, permanent
- Full completion: the dust stops settling. The candlelight brightens. A low, warm chord builds — the sound of something ancient being remembered. Not celebrated. Just... acknowledged.

**Reward:** Stone chips + "The Excavator" achievement + Catacombs wallpaper

### 20. The Miskatonic Annex — Watching Pieces
**Zone:** Miskatonic Annex (Lovecraftian, Rousseau aesthetic, Naut the Scholar's domain)
**The Mechanic:** Pieces move when you're not looking at them. You place a piece, look away to grab the next one, and the first piece has shifted. The horror isn't a monster — it's that the puzzle is aware of you.

**Details:**
- 40 pieces scattered around the frame
- When you look directly at a placed piece, it stays put
- When a placed piece is off-screen (or when your cursor/touch is elsewhere), it may shift — rotate, swap with a neighbor, or slide slightly
- The movement is subtle and slow — not chaotic, but unsettling. You place a piece, turn to grab another, and when you look back, something has changed. You're not sure what.
- You must build in a specific order, or find a way to "pin" pieces in place
- Pinning: if you hold a piece for 3 seconds, it locks and cannot shift. But you can only pin one piece at a time (pinning a new one unpins the old one)
- Some pieces NEVER move (the stable ones — usually the corners and edges, the structural facts). The shifting pieces are always interior.
- The more pieces you place, the more the remaining pieces shift — the puzzle gets harder as you near completion

**Difficulty Levels:**
- Apprentice: pieces shift slowly, shifts are obvious (easy to spot and correct)
- Journeyman: standard shift speed, shifts are subtle, one piece moves at a time
- Master: multiple pieces shift simultaneously, shifts include rotation, some placed pieces SWAP positions
- Archive Keeper: all interior pieces shift, shifts happen faster the fewer unplaced pieces remain, AND the assembled image itself subtly changes (objects in the painting move — a door that was on the left is now on the right). The picture is alive.

**Narrative Intro:**
"Something in this room is watching you. Not the scholar — the scholar is a friend. Something older. Something that does not like to be seen assembling it. You will place a piece. You will turn away. You will turn back, and the piece will have moved. You are not losing your mind. The puzzle is. Build quickly. Pin what you can. Do not look away from the center for long. Rebuild the truth — before it rebuilds itself."

**Audio:**
- Ambient: near-silence with very faint, very deep tones — almost subliminal. Occasional almost-sounds that might be breathing.
- Piece place: a soft, uncertain click — not the satisfying stone-on-stone of other puzzles. Something less... committed.
- Piece shift (off-screen): a barely audible whisper, like fabric moving. You might not hear it. That's the point.
- Pin: a definitive click — the sound of something being forced to stay
- Full completion: everything stops. The watching stops. A single note plays — not warm, not cold. Just... present. The picture is complete. Whatever was watching has nothing left to hide.

**Reward:** Stone chips + "The Watched" achievement + Miskatonic Annex wallpaper (the static version — the one that doesn't move)

### 21. The Hollow Earth — Refraction Pieces
**Zone:** Hollow Earth (O'Keeffe — pure form, luminous crystal city)
**The Mechanic:** Crystal facet pieces. The image through each piece is distorted by refraction — what you see isn't what's actually on the piece. You have to mentally account for the bend of the crystal to know where it goes.

**Details:**
- 50 pieces, each rendered as a crystal facet — translucent, angled, refracting light
- The image on each piece is warped by the crystal's angle — straight lines bend, colors shift toward the spectrum edges, sizes distort
- A piece that looks like it shows the left side of the crystal city actually shows the right — the refraction flipped it
- You must learn to "see through" the refraction — to imagine what the image looks like without the crystal bend
- Pieces with steeper facet angles refract more (harder to read); pieces with shallower angles are closer to true (easier)
- The assembly area is a flat surface — placed pieces show their TRUE image (the refraction effect only exists on the scattered, unplaced pieces)
- As you place pieces, the flat assembled image builds in true colors — the crystal city as it actually is, not as the crystal bends it
- The contrast between refracted (unplaced) and true (placed) is the puzzle's visual signature

**Difficulty Levels:**
- Apprentice: shallow refraction angles (minor distortion), easy to read through
- Journeyman: standard refraction (moderate bending, color shifting at edges)
- Master: steep refraction (significant distortion, some pieces show mirror images, color spectrums shift dramatically)
- Archive Keeper: extreme refraction + pieces are ALSO translucent (you can see the piece behind them through the crystal, creating double-images). Must distinguish what's on THIS piece from what's on the piece behind it.

**Narrative Intro:**
"The Earth is the geode. Rough without, crystal within. The city below has always been here — but the crystal bends what you see. A spire that leans left is actually straight. A color that burns blue is actually gold. The crystal does not lie. The crystal refracts. There is a difference. Learn to see through the bend. Rebuild what was always there — not what the crystal showed you, but what the crystal was hiding."

**Audio:**
- Ambient: resonant crystal tones — long sustained notes that ring and decay slowly, the sound of a crystal cathedral
- Piece pickup: a crystalline chime (pitch varies by piece — each facet rings at its own frequency)
- Piece place: a deep, warm resonance — the crystal settling into its true position
- Full completion: all the individual crystal tones resolve into a single harmonic — the 42Hz Song, amplified through crystal. The sound of the Source, heard clearly for the first time without the refraction's interference.

**Reward:** Stone chips + "The Seer" achievement + Hollow Earth wallpaper (the true, un-refracted image)
