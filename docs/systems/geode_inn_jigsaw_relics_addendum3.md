### 16. Occam's Razor — Shape-Logic Assembly
**Zone:** The Alchemical Staircase (philosopher's territory)
**Aesthetic:** Escher-adjacent but distinct — clean, geometric, almost diagrammatic. The philosophy chamber.

**The Mechanic:**
- Every piece's image is DECEPTIVE until placed. Colors don't connect. Patterns break. A piece that looks like it belongs in the sky is actually part of the ground. The images lie.
- The ONLY reliable guide is the piece's SHAPE — the tabs and blanks. If the shape fits, the piece belongs there, regardless of what the image looks like.
- When a piece locks into the correct position, its image RESOLVES — the colors shift, the pattern connects, the piece reveals its true face. The deception drops.
- The image builds itself as a reward for trusting logic over appearance. You literally cannot see the full picture until every piece is in place.
- No ghost outline (useless — the outline would also be deceptive)
- No color matching (useless — colors lie)
- No pattern matching (useless — patterns lie)
- Pure shape logic. Tab and blank geometry. The simplest answer.

**Philosophy:**
- Occam's Razor: the simplest explanation that fits the facts is the truth. Here, the simplest structural fit IS the truth.
- The puzzle teaches its own lesson: you cannot judge by appearance. You must trust the underlying structure. The shape doesn't lie, even when the image does.
- The image only makes sense in retrospect — once assembled, you see the whole picture and wonder how you ever thought that piece was sky when it was clearly part of the root system all along.

**Piece Count:** 40 pieces
**Difficulty:** No levels. Occam's Razor is always what it is. The mechanic doesn't need difficulty modifiers — it's already a paradigm shift from every other puzzle.

**Narrative Intro (tabletop questline voice):**
"The philosophers argue about what is true. Socrates dissolves. Plato purifies. Aristotle categorizes. But William sits quietly with a razor and says: the simplest answer that fits is the truth. The image lies. The shape does not. Trust the shape. Place the piece. See what was always there. Rebuild the clarity."

**Visual Details:**
- Before placement: pieces show mismatched colors and patterns — a piece might show blue sky imagery but have the shape that only fits in the lower-left corner. The disconnect is jarring and deliberate.
- During placement (lock moment): the piece's image morphs/shifts — colors realign, patterns connect. A brief shimmer (not gold — silver, the philosopher's metal) marks the resolution.
- After placement: the piece now shows its true image, connecting seamlessly with surrounding resolved pieces.
- Partially completed: the resolved sections form a coherent image surrounded by unresolved (deceptive) pieces — the contrast between truth and deception is visible on the board.
- Fully completed: the entire image is resolved, coherent, and clear. The picture was always there. You just couldn't see it until you stopped trusting your eyes.

**Audio:**
- Piece pickup: standard stone sound
- Piece lock (correct): a clean, clear bell tone — the sound of clarity arriving. Pitch rises slightly with each resolved piece (the picture getting clearer, the frequency rising toward truth)
- Incorrect drop: standard soft placement (no penalty, but no resolution either — the image stays deceptive)
- Full completion: a single pure sustained tone — the clearest sound in the Inn. No harmony, no chord. Just one note. The simplest answer.

**Reward:** Stone chips + "The Razor" achievement + the completed image unlocks as a wallpaper (the image that was hidden all along, now visible)

**Frame:** Universal Celtic knotwork gold border, same as all puzzles

**Note on the Staircase Escher puzzle:** The Escher puzzle (#5) breaks spatial logic. The Occam's Razor puzzle (#16) breaks visual trust. Together they form the Staircase's puzzle pair — two different philosophical challenges on the philosopher's staircase. Escher says "the geometry is impossible." Occam says "the image is lying." Both are true on the staircase.
