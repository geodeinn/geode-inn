## THE SONG

"Bohemian Rhapsody" by Queen. Written by Freddie Mercury. Released October 31, 1975.

Six acts. One mini-opera. No chorus. No repeated structure. A song that shouldn't work and works completely — the way a geode shouldn't hold light and does.

Key: Bb major with modulations to A major and Eb major.
Tempo: ~72 BPM (ballad sections), variable.
Vocal range: Bb2 to F5 (nearly three octaves).

The song has no mathematical structure underneath it — no Fibonacci sequence like Lateralus, no golden ratio architecture. Bohemian Rhapsody is organized as **narrative**, not mathematics. Six acts. Each act has its own emotional register, its own key, its own mineral palette. The bead design must reflect this structure: six sections, six mineral palettes, one continuous thread.

## THE MAPPING

**Total beads: 159**
**Unique stones: 13**
**Method:** Each melodic note's frequency (Hz) is matched to the nearest stone in the sonic mineral signature database. The stone's average frequency (density frequency + hardness frequency / 2) is the matching point. Where the match is imperfect (Δ > 20 Hz), the gap is interpreted as a **"cover interpretation"** — the mineral system's version of the note, not a direct reproduction.

**Foundation:** The 42Hz Song runs underneath all 159 beads, as it does for every Geode Inn sound. The 42Hz is the universal carrier wave. The stones are the content.

## THE AMBER PRINCIPLE

Amber is the bookend stone. 35 beads — the most used stone in the design.

Amber is fossilized resin — life that became stone through time and pressure. It carries the lowest frequencies in the palette (244 Hz). It appears in every section, but it DOMINATES the Intro (7 of 18 beads) and the Outro (12 of 26 beads).

The Intro and Outro share the same three stones (Amber, Jet, Shungite), but Amber's proportion increases from Intro to Outro. The song ends more Amber than it began. The confession, the opera, the rock — all of it was a journey, and the journey ends with more of the oldest, warmest material. The song returns to Amber the way a breath returns to stillness.

Amber also carries the "cover interpretations" — the lowest notes of the Guitar Solo and Outro, where no stone exists in the 155-210 Hz range. Amber covers the subsonic territory. The stone that was once alive carries the notes that are almost silent. This is not a flaw. This is the mineral system's version of the note — the cover, not the original. The gap IS the interpretation.
