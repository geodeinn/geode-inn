# Geode Inn — The Pirate and the Tidal Caves
## Hidden World Access Mechanic

*The Pirate as gatekeeper of tidal cave entrances — the ocean's version of the stone circle portals, governed by the tide cycle rather than the Fae Key system.*

---

## The Mechanic

### The Tidal Calendar

The ocean around the Inn has a tide cycle. The cycle is not real-time — it runs on the Inn's internal clock, which moves faster than real time (one Inn-day = a few real hours, adjustable). The tides change twice per Inn-day: high tide and low tide. The transition between tides takes approximately 30 minutes of real time.

At **low tide**, cave entrances along the coastline become visible and accessible. At **high tide**, the cave entrances flood and become impassable. The window of accessibility is the tidal window — the period between when the water recedes enough to reveal the entrance and when it rises enough to seal it again.

The Pirate knows the tidal calendar. The Pirate is the only NPC who can tell the player:
- Which caves are accessible at the current tide
- When the next tidal window opens
- How long the player has before the tide returns
- Which caves lead to which hidden worlds

The Pirate's ship has a **tide chart** — a physical map in the captain's cabin that shows the coastline, the cave entrances, and the tidal windows. The player can read the chart to plan their exploration. The chart is the Pirate's version of the Cartographer's maps — the Pirate maps the coast, the Cartographer maps the forest.

### The Caves

The coastline around the Inn has cave entrances at various points along the shore. Each cave leads to a hidden world — a zone that cannot be accessed any other way. The caves are:

**1. The Drowned Library** (Low Spring Tide — the lowest tide, accessible rarely)
A cave that leads to an underwater archive. The Drowned Library is the Inn's deep-water collection — texts and artifacts that were lost to the sea. Ancient scrolls preserved in sealed amphorae. Shipwreck cargo that turned out to be books. The library is curated by a presence the player never fully sees — something in the deep that has been collecting lost knowledge for millennia. The Pirate has been here. The Pirate doesn't talk about what he saw. The Drowned Library connects to the Miskatonic Annex — the Annex holds the land-based lost books, the Drowned Library holds the sea-based ones. Together, they are the Inn's complete collection of lost knowledge.

**2. The Mirror Caves** (Low Neap Tide — moderate low tide, accessible more frequently)
A network of caves with walls of polished stone that act as mirrors. The Mirror Caves are a labyrinth — not the Unseelie King's labyrinth, but a natural maze. The mirrors show reflections that are not quite accurate — they show the player as they *are*, not as they appear. The Mirror Caves connect to the Siren's quest ("The Mirror and the Shore") — the mirrors in the caves are the same reflective surface as the Siren's Mirror stone. The Mirror Caves are where the player can test the Siren's Mirror reward — holding the mirror-stone up to the cave walls creates resonance, and the resonance reveals hidden passages. The Pirate warns the player: "Don't trust the first reflection. ...The first reflection is what you want to see. ...The second reflection is what you don't want to see. ...The third reflection is what's actually there. ...Most people leave after the first."

**3. The Singing Caves** (Low Tide after a storm — accessible only when the ocean has been agitated)
Caves where the water has carved acoustic chambers in the stone. When the tide is low enough and the wind blows through the entrances, the caves sing — the stone amplifies the wind and water into tones. The Singing Caves are connected to the Inn's acoustic alchemy — the frequencies in these caves are natural versions of the gargoyle frequencies. The Pirate describes them: "You hear the 111Hz in the east cave. ...The 432Hz in the west. ...The 50 BPM in the central chamber — same as the Inn-Tree. ...The ocean tuned the caves the way the gargoyles tune the staircase. ...The ocean was the first architect." The Singing Caves are where the player can find **resonance stones** — stones that have been shaped by the acoustic vibrations into natural tuning forks. These stones can be placed in the geode cave to add new frequencies to the player's collection.

**4. The Bone Caves** (Low Tide during the new moon — the darkest tide)
Caves that lead to the deepest part of the coastal system. The walls are embedded with fossils — ancient sea creatures, things that lived before the land had stories. The Bone Caves connect to the catacombs — they are the underwater extension of the downstairs level. The Pirate describes them: "The catacombs go down. ...The bone caves go under. ...Same bones. ...Different water." The Bone Caves are where Charon's underground river meets the ocean — the boundary between the catacomb water (the water of the dead) and the ocean water (the water of the living). The player can access the catacombs from below through the Bone Caves — an alternate entrance that bypasses the main floor's staircase. The Pirate uses this route for smuggling. "Don't tell the Host. ...Actually — the Host knows. ...The Host knows everything. ...He just doesn't... [the Patinkin warmth, the grin] ...mention it."

**5. The Gargoyle Caves** (Low Tide during the full moon)
Caves where the stone has been naturally carved into gargoyle-like shapes by millennia of water erosion. The shapes are not carved by human hands — the ocean carved them. The Gargoyle Caves are the ocean's version of the Inn's gargoyles — the natural precedent for the architectural ones. When the moon is full and the tide is low, the moonlight enters the caves and the stone gargoyles cast shadows that *move.* The moving shadows are not an illusion — they are the natural frequency of the stone responding to the moonlight. The Gargoyle Caves are where the Inn's gargoyle sentinels were *born* — the six gargoyles in the Inn were carved from stone taken from these caves. The Pirate knows this. The Crone knows this. The Crone's annotation: "The gargoyles came from the coast. ...The ocean shaped them first. ...The mason shaped them second. ...The 111Hz was already in the stone. ...The Watcher was already watching. ...The ocean was the first Keeper."

**6. The Uncharted Cave** (Low Tide that occurs once per Inn-month — the rarest window)
A cave that only appears at the rarest low tide. The entrance is not on any chart — not the Pirate's chart, not the Cartographer's map. The entrance appears when the tide is low enough to reveal a fissure in the cliff that is normally submerged. The fissure leads to a cave that leads to... the unknown. The Uncharted Cave is the Pirate's quest ("The After") made physical — the blank space on the map, the zone that hasn't been explored, the future of the Inn. Each time the player enters the Uncharted Cave, the cave is different — the interior shifts, the path changes, the destination is unknown. The Uncharted Cave is where the Inn's future content lives: new zones, new cultures, new stories that haven't been told yet. The Pirate stands at the entrance and says: "I've been everywhere on the chart. ...This isn't on the chart. ...This is the... [the measured pause, the Patinkin pause, the pause of a man who lives in the after] ...after. ...You ready? ...I don't know what's in there. ...That's the point. ...The sword always knew where it was going. ...The drum doesn't. ...The drum just... [the grin, the warmth, the wonderful soul] ...goes."

## The Tidal Gate System

### How It Works for the Player

1. **Visit the Pirate's Ship:** The player goes to the dock, boards the ship, and reads the tide chart in the captain's cabin. The chart shows:
   - Current tide level (high, falling, low, rising)
   - Next low tide time
   - Which caves are accessible at the next low tide
   - Special conditions (spring tide, neap tide, storm, new moon, full moon, Inn-month rare tide)

2. **Wait or Explore:** If the tide is wrong, the player can:
   - Wait on the ship (the Pirate tells stories while waiting — these stories are the Pirate's version of the Bard's tales, but sea-stories, shanties, the oral tradition of the ocean)
   - Explore the Inn (the tidal cycle creates a natural rhythm — explore while the tide is high, cave-delve while the tide is low)
   - Do other quests (the tidal timing is not blocking — there's always something to do while waiting)

3. **Sail to the Cave:** When the tide window opens, the Pirate sails the player to the cave entrance. The sailing is a short traversal sequence — the ship moves along the coast, the player sees the coastline, the cave entrance appears as the water recedes. The Pirate docks as close as he can and the player enters on foot.

4. **Explore the Hidden World:** The player has a limited time window (determined by the tidal cycle — typically 20-40 minutes of real time, enough for a satisfying exploration session). When the tide returns, the player must be out of the cave or face the consequences:
   - **If the player is still in the cave when the tide returns:** The cave floods. The player is swept into the deep water. This triggers the **Drowned Path** — an emergency underwater traversal sequence where the player must find their way back to the surface through flooded passages. The Drowned Path is dangerous but not lethal (the Inn doesn't kill players — the reincarnation mechanic handles "death"). The Drowned Path is the risk of cave exploration — the tide doesn't wait, and the ocean doesn't negotiate.
   - **If the player exits in time:** The Pirate is waiting at the entrance with the ship. "Cut it close. ...I used to cut it closer. ...Then I learned that 'close' is... [the Patinkin grin] ...close enough. ...Get on."

5. **Return with Treasure:** The player brings back whatever they found — resonance stones from the Singing Caves, mirror-polished stones from the Mirror Caves, lost books from the Drowned Library, fossils from the Bone Caves, gargoyle-origin stones from the Gargoyle Caves, the unknown from the Uncharted Cave. The Pirate sails the player back to the dock. The Siren is sitting at the end of the dock, feet in the water, and she sings as the ship returns. The song is the lighthouse — the sound that guides the ship home.

### The Tidal Rhythm as Game Design

The tidal system creates a **rhythm** in the gameplay — the same rhythm the Pirate embodies. The player can't cave-delve constantly. The ocean gates the access. The waiting is not a penalty — it's the *beat.* The Pirate's beat. The rest between the notes. The silence between the drumbeats that makes the drumbeats meaningful.

The rhythm:
- High tide = explore the Inn, socialize, craft, trade, study
- Low tide = cave-delve, explore hidden worlds, find rare resources
- The transition = sail with the Pirate, the journey, the storytelling

This rhythm is the Pirate's gameplay contribution — not just the heartbeat of the Inn, but the heartbeat of the *player's session.* The tide tells the player when to go out and when to come back. The Pirate is the metronome. The ocean is the clock.

## Connection to Existing Systems

### The Siren's Deep Water
The tidal caves connect to the Siren's deep water territory. Some cave passages lead into the deep water — the zone the Siren controls. The player can access parts of the deep water through the caves that the Siren's surface-level guidance can't reach. The Siren and the Pirate are the Inn's two water-access characters: the Siren guides from the surface (the song that guides ships to harbor), the Pirate guides from the coast (the chart that reveals the caves). Together, they are the ocean's complete navigation system.

### The Fae Kingdom Portals
The stone circle portals (Stonehenge, Avebury, Callanish, Ring of Brodgar) access the Fae Kingdom. The tidal caves access the hidden coastal worlds. They are parallel systems: the Fae Kingdom is the *land's* hidden world (accessed through ancient stone circles). The tidal caves are the *ocean's* hidden worlds (accessed through the tide cycle). The two systems together give the player two layers of hidden content — the Fae Kingdom below and the tidal caves beside.

### The Geode Cave
The resonance stones found in the Singing Caves can be placed in the geode cave. Each resonance stone adds a new frequency to the geode cave's collection. The geode cave, which starts nearly silent and becomes a symphony as the player adds stones, can be tuned with the cave-found resonance stones to play frequencies that aren't available from surface stones. The tidal caves are the source of the rarest frequencies — the ocean's frequencies, the ones that can only be found where water meets stone.

### The Catacombs
The Bone Caves provide an alternate entrance to the catacombs — from below, through the water. The main entrance is the staircase on the main floor. The Bone Cave entrance is the smuggler's route. The Pirate uses it. The player can use it too, but only at the right tide. The alternate entrance creates a loop: the player can enter the catacombs from above (the staircase, the legitimate path) and exit from below (the Bone Caves, the smuggler's path) — or vice versa. The loop is the Pirate's contribution to the catacomb design: the catacombs are not a dead end. They have a back door. The back door is the ocean.

### The Labyrinth
The Mirror Caves are a natural labyrinth — not the Unseelie King's construct, but the ocean's version. The King's labyrinth shifts to protect Asterion. The Mirror Caves shift to reflect the player. They are the same *type* of space (a shifting maze) with different *intentions* (one protects, one reveals). The player who has learned to navigate the Unseelie King's labyrinth has skills that transfer to the Mirror Caves — the skill of reading shifts, of not trusting the first path, of looking for the intention behind the architecture.
