# Geode Inn — The Wandering Program
## System: Inn Creatures in the Real World
## Access: Main Floor → World Map → National Parks → Any park
## Prerequisite: Beorn's border test (Mediterranean Zone Quest 5)

### Overview

The Wandering Program is the system that lets Inn creatures wander into the real world — specifically, into the National Park zones. The Inn's creatures (brownies, pixies, fizgigs, dragon kittens, gargoyle puppies, and others) can leave the Inn and explore the external zones. Their encounters with the real world become the Modern Legends archive.

The program's core principle: cryptids are Inn creatures perceived at a two-degree frequency tilt. Bigfoot is a brownie. Nessie is a water zone creature. Mothman is a pixie at the wrong frequency. The names humans give these creatures are the SAVING MECHANISM — when a human names a cryptid, the human performs Bastion's act (naming = seeing = acknowledging the existence of something you don't understand).

The Wandering Program connects three systems: the National Park zones (where wandering happens), the Modern Legends archive (where encounters are recorded), and the Inn's creature population (the wanderers themselves).

## How Wandering Works

### Step 1: Assignment
Each Inn creature has a "wander score" — a hidden stat that determines how likely they are to wander. Brownies wander most (they're curious and small). Dragon kittens wander rarely (they're attached to the hearth). Fizgigs never wander (they're too busy fixing things).

The player can ASSIGN a creature to wander by visiting the Wandering Office (a small desk near the main floor, staffed by a chibi NPC called the Wandermaster). The Wandermaster is a mole-like creature with tiny spectacles and a travel journal. The Wandermaster tracks all wandering assignments.

### Step 2: The Encounter
When a wandering creature enters a National Park zone, there is a CHANCE of an encounter with a human. The encounter is calculated based on:
- The creature's size (smaller = more likely to be seen as a cryptid)
- The creature's frequency (if the creature's frequency is close to 42Hz, it appears as itself; if it's off by 2 degrees, it appears as a cryptid)
- The park's human traffic (Yellowstone = high traffic, Superstition Mountains = low traffic)

When an encounter occurs, the creature appears to a human as a CRYPTID — a blurred, misidentified version of itself. The human sees a shape, a shadow, a movement. The human gives the creature a NAME: Bigfoot, Mothman, Skunk Ape, Jersey Devil, Flatwoods Monster.

### Step 3: The Recording
When a human names a wandering Inn creature, the encounter is recorded in the Modern Legends archive. The recording includes:
- The creature's real identity (brownie, pixie, etc.)
- The human's name for it (Bigfoot, Mothman, etc.)
- The location (which National Park)
- The frequency difference (how far off 42Hz the creature was)
- The story (what happened, told in the tabletop voice)

The Modern Legends archive is the Inn's living record of human-imagination-meets-Inn-reality. Each entry answers the question: "Will this still be worth telling in 2126?"

### Step 4: The Return
The wandering creature returns to the Inn after a set time (12-48 real hours). The creature brings back:
- The name the human gave it (if any)
- The story of the encounter (if any)
- A stone from the park (if the creature found one)
- A "wander buff" — the creature gains a temporary stat boost from the experience

## The Wandermaster

The Wandermaster is the Wandering Program's administrator — a chibi mole NPC with tiny spectacles, a travel journal, and a filing system made of acorn caps. The Wandermaster tracks every wandering assignment, every encounter, every name.

**Voice:** Quick, bureaucratic, fond of lists. The Wandermaster speaks in inventories. "Three brownies out. Two returned. One named — Bigfoot, again. The humans are very consistent with that one. ...Two pixies out. One returned. One missing — no, not missing. ...Delayed. ...Mothman delay. The human took a photograph. The pixie is hiding until the photograph fades. ...These things take time."

**The Wandermaster's office:** A small desk near the main floor's exit, between the Modern Legends nook and the world map. The desk has:
- A wander assignment board (which creatures are available to wander)
- A return log (which creatures have come back, and what they brought)
- A naming registry (the cryptid names humans have given)
- A frequency meter (showing each creature's current frequency offset)

## Quest: "The Naming"

### Giver: The Wandermaster
### Prerequisite: Mediterranean Zone Quest 5 (border crossing) + at least 1 National Park zone visited

**Quest Text:**
*"A brownie has been in the Appalachian Range for six hours. No encounter yet — the brownie is good at hiding. ...Too good. ...If the brownie isn't seen, the brownie doesn't get named. ...If the brownie doesn't get named, the encounter doesn't get recorded. ...The Modern Legends archive stays empty. ...The archive needs entries. ...The archive needs STORIES. ...Go to the Appalachian Range. Find the brownie. ...Don't scare it. ...Don't name it yourself — a human has to name it. ...Just... make sure the brownie is in the right place at the right time. ...Subtly. ...The brownie doesn't know you're helping. ...The brownie thinks it's just walking."*

**Objective:**
- Travel to the Appalachian Range (National Park zone)
- Locate the wandering brownie (the brownie is hiding in the forest — the player must use Form Sight to find it; the brownie's frequency offset makes it visible as a "Bigfoot-shaped" blur)
- Guide the brownie toward a hiking trail where a human NPC is walking (the player can't touch the brownie — the player must use the Stone Vibration Touch System to create a "warm path" of 42Hz vibrations that the brownie follows naturally)
- The human NPC sees the brownie (as a blur, a shape, a shadow)
- The human NPC names the creature: "Bigfoot" (if in the Appalachians) or a region-appropriate name
- The encounter is recorded in the Modern Legends archive

**Mechanic:** Stealth + herding. The player guides the brownie without the brownie knowing, and without the human NPC seeing the PLAYER. The player is the invisible matchmaker between Inn creature and human perception. The player is the reason the legend exists.

**Reward:** The first Modern Legends entry — a story in the archive, written in the tabletop voice, telling the tale of the encounter from BOTH perspectives (the brownie's and the human's). The entry includes the brownie's real name, the human's cryptid name, and the frequency offset. The entry is the player's first contribution to the Modern Legends archive.

## Modern Legends Archive Format

Each entry follows this structure:

```
### Entry #[N]: "[Cryptid Name]" — [Real Creature], [Park Zone]

**The Human's Story:**
[What the human saw, told in plain language — the sighting, the fear, the wonder, the naming]

**The Creature's Story:**
[What the creature experienced, told in the tabletop voice — the wandering, the hiding, the moment of being seen, the confusion of being named]

**The Frequency:**
[The creature's frequency offset — how far from 42Hz, what the offset means]

**The Question:**
"Will this still be worth telling in 2126?"
[Answer: Yes / No / Unknown — and why]

**The Stone:**
[The stone the creature brought back from the park, if any]
```

## Connections

- **Modern Legends Nook** (Main Floor) — the archive where all entries are stored and readable
- **National Park Zones** — the wandering destinations
- **Uhtred** (Norse Zone) — the first registered wanderer; his mobile campfire is the model for the program
- **The Whisper Layer** — the frequency offset (42Hz ± 2 degrees) is the same "veil" that the Whisper Layer operates behind. The cryptids are the Whisper Layer's real-world manifestation.
- **Bastion** — the naming mechanism. When a human names a cryptid, the human performs Bastion's act: acknowledging the existence of something they don't understand. The name is the bridge between the Inn and the human world.
- **The Bead Board / Sonic Mineral Codex** — wandering creatures can bring back stones from the parks. These stones have park-specific frequencies that can be used in the Bead Composer.
