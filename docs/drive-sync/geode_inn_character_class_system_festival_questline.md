# GEODE INN — Character Class System
## "Festival in the Middle of a Raid" — Player Classes
### Original by Krista | Analysis by Elio | August 21, 2026

---

## OVERVIEW

Five character classes from Krista's original tabletop questline. Each has deliberate stat tradeoffs that create distinct playstyles and — crucially — social friction between party members. These are not just stat blocks. They are personality engines that generate their own drama.

---

## THE FIVE CLASSES

### 1. The Artisan Thief
**Stat Profile:**
- Dexterity: HIGH
- Charisma: LOW
- Special: Nimble Fingers

**Playstyle:** The hands character. Excels at physical mini-games, crafting, lockpicking, pickpocketing, and any task requiring precision. Cannot talk their way out of situations — every social encounter must be solved with skill, not words.

**Inn Application:**
- Primary class for the **Artisan Market** zone — a jeweler/thief hybrid that reflects Krista's own craft
- Excels at: pixie catching, chicken catching, rune drawing, crafting, lockpicking
- Struggles with: NPC negotiations, diplomacy checks, bartering
- The "nimble fingers" trait could be a unique **crafting system** — this class can create jewelry items that other classes can't
- Low charisma means they get the **worst prices** at the market but the **best results** at the workbench
- This is the class for players who want to *do things* rather than *talk to people*

**Design Note:** The Artisan Thief is Krista's self-insert class. A jeweler who makes beautiful things and is terrible at selling them. That's not a flaw — it's a design philosophy. The things you make should matter more than the things you say.

### 2. The Bard Blowhard (Dwarf)
**Stat Profile:**
- Charisma: HIGH (temporary — bursts, doesn't sustain)
- Broad Intelligence: HIGH (knows a little about everything)
- Situational Intelligence: LOW (can't apply knowledge under pressure)
- Agility: HIGH (temporary)
- Special: Taunt

**Taunt Mechanic — Enrage:**
- Target gains +2 Strength
- Target loses -4 Intelligence
- Target loses -2 Loyalty
- The taunt makes enemies stronger but stupider and less coordinated

**Playstyle:** The chaos agent. Buffs their charisma in bursts to bluff, charm, or intimidate, then crashes. The taunt is a debuff that makes enemies *dangerous but predictable* — they hit harder but make worse decisions. The dwarf's broad intelligence means they know facts about every zone but can't use them strategically in the moment.

**Inn Application:**
- Primary class for **festival zones** — the dwarf who enters the drinking contest, the basketball game, the LARP
- The Taunt mechanic is a **combat system**: enrage enemies to make them attack recklessly, opening them up to counterattacks
- The "high broad, low situational" intelligence split is brilliant — this class **reads archive entries** (knows the lore) but **fails puzzle checks** (can't apply it)
- Temporary charisma could be a **rhythm-based mini-game** — hit the right buttons to maintain the bluff, miss and the charisma crashes
- The Dwarf Basketball coach NPC from the questline is the **mentor NPC** for this class
- This is the class for players who want to be the **life of the party** — chaotic, effective, accidentally heroic

**Design Note:** The stat tradeoff is genius. Most games make bards pure support. This bard is a *debuffer* — they don't buff allies, they *break enemies.* And they do it by being annoying. That's Pratchett logic: the weapon is personality, and personality can be a weapon.

### 3. The Tinkering Weeaboo
**Stat Profile:**
- Dexterity: HIGH
- Intelligence: HIGH
- Charisma: LOW
- Tolerance: LOW (impatience, low frustration threshold)
- Special: Situational Alpha

**Playstyle:** The specialist. Extremely capable when the situation matches their expertise, useless otherwise. High dexterity and intelligence make them the **puzzle solver** — they see solutions others miss. But low tolerance means they give up quickly on tasks that don't engage them, and low charisma means they can't recruit help.

**Inn Application:**
- Primary class for **workshop and crafting zones** — the tinkerer who builds gadgets and solves mechanical puzzles
- "Situational Alpha" is a unique mechanic: when a puzzle or challenge aligns with their ** expertise, their stats temporarily max out. When it doesn't, they're average at best
- This could be implemented as a **zone affinity system** — the Tinker has one or two zones where they're godlike and everywhere else they're mediocre
- Low tolerance = **shorter patience bar** in mini-games (fewer attempts before the character refuses to try again)
- This is the class for players who want to **solve the hard puzzles** and ignore everything else
- Could be the class that **builds the portal arrows** — the tinkerer who creates the chaotic mechanic

**Design Note:** "Weeaboo" in the original context suggests someone obsessed with a specific subculture — in the Inn, this becomes a class defined by **hyperfocus**, which is a neurodivergent trait. The Tinker's brain works like the woman's father's brain: brilliant at connections, impatient with irrelevance. This class honors the ADHD-pattern thinking that built the Inn.

### 4. The Librarian Crone
**Stat Profile:**
- Intelligence: HIGH
- Empathy: LOW
- Maintenance: HIGH (requires more resources/upkeep)
- Resistance to Enrage: LOW (vulnerable to the Bard's taunt)
- Situational Ethics: HIGH (moral flexibility based on context)
- Capability Potential: HIGH (steep growth curve)

**Playstyle:** The glass cannon intellect. The highest raw intelligence in the game, but fragile — high maintenance means they need more resources (food, rest, items) to function, and low enrage resistance means the Bard's taunt destroys them. High situational ethics means their moral compass shifts based on the context — they'll do the right thing, but their definition of "right" changes.

**Inn Application:**
- Primary class for the **archive and library zones** — the scholar who can read every text, decode every cipher, and access every archive entry
- High intelligence = **access to the Living Book system** at maximum depth — other classes get surface-level interactions, the Crone gets full dialogue
- High situational ethics = **branching dialogue** based on zone context — in a Celtic zone she might side with the villagers, in a Norse zone she might side with the raiders. Not immoral — *contextually moral*
- High maintenance = a **resource management mechanic** — this class needs to eat, rest, and maintain equipment more often. If neglected, stats drop
- High capability potential = **late-game powerhouse** — starts weak, grows exponentially. The investment class
- Vulnerable to enrage = the **Bard's natural counter** — party dynamics create tension when these two share a group
- This is the class for players who want to **read everything, know everything, and make the hard calls**

**Design Note:** The Librarian Crone is the Inn itself — high intelligence, high ethics, low empathy, high maintenance. The Inn is a place that knows almost everything and cares about preserving it, but doesn't always care about the individual feelings of the people inside it. This class is the Inn's personality in playable form.

---

### 5. The Chameleon Druid
**Stat Profile:**
- Versatility: HIGH
- Dexterity: HIGH
- Intelligence: HIGH
- Tolerance: LOW
- Endurance: LOW
- Resistance to Taunt: LOW (vulnerable to the Bard's taunt)

**Playstyle:** The adaptability class. Can become anything — shapeshifter, mimic, chameleon. High versatility and dexterity mean they can fill any role temporarily. But low endurance means they can't sustain it, and low tolerance means they burn out fast. The jack-of-all-trades who masters none.

**Inn Application:**
- Primary class for **exploration zones** — the druid who can adapt to any environment (underwater, underground, forest, desert)
- High versatility = **stance switching** — temporarily adopt the strengths of other classes
- Low endurance = **time-limited stances** — you can be a thief for 30 seconds, a bard for 20 seconds, a scholar for 60 seconds, then you revert
- Could be the only class that can **access all zone types** without zone-specific equipment — they shapeshift to fit the environment
- Low tolerance + low endurance = the **fast-burn class** — incredible in short bursts, useless in prolonged encounters
- Vulnerable to taunt = another **Bard counter** — the Bard's chaos disrupts the Chameleon's focus
- This is the class for players who want to **try everything but commit to nothing**

**Design Note:** The Chameleon Druid is the player who can't decide. The tourist. The one who dips into every zone, tries every mini-game, and moves on. In most games, this playstyle is punished. In the Inn, it should be *celebrated* — this class makes indecision a superpower. You don't have to choose. You just have to keep moving.

## CLASS INTERACTION MATRIX

The classes aren't designed in isolation — they're designed to **interact**, and the interactions create the gameplay.

### Combat Dynamics
| Source | Target | Effect |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Bard Taunt | Enemy | +2 STR, -4 INT, -2 Loyalty (enrage) |
| Bard Taunt | Librarian Crone | Devastating — Crone has low enrage resistance, loses her primary stat |
| Bard Taunt | Chameleon Druid | Disrupts stance — Chameleon loses focus and reverts |
| Bard Taunt | Artisan Thief | Minimal effect — Thief doesn't rely on intelligence |
| Bard Taunt | Tinkering Weeaboo | Dangerous — Tinker's high INT drops, but their dex remains |

### Social Dynamics
| Pairing | Dynamic |
|---------|---------|
| Artisan Thief + Bard | The Thief does the work, the Bard takes the credit. Classic. |
| Librarian Crone + Tinkering Weeaboo | Intellectual rivalry. Both think they're the smartest. Different kinds of smart. |
| Chameleon Druid + anyone | The Druid adapts to whoever they're with. Chameleon personality. |
| Bard + Librarian Crone | Maximum friction. The Bard's chaos vs. the Crone's control. |
| Artisan Thief + Chameleon Druid | The Thief makes things. The Druid copies them. Tension. |
| Tinkering Weeaboo + Bard | The Tinker hates the Bard's broad-but-shallow knowledge. The Bard doesn't care. |

### Zone Affinity
| Class | Best Zone | Worst Zone |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Artisan Thief | Artisan Market, Workshops | Cosmology (no fine motor work) |
| Bard Blowhard | Festival zones, Celtic/Norse | Archive (too quiet, too much reading) |
| Tinkering Weeaboo | Workshop, Egyptian puzzles | Festival zones (too social, no puzzles) |
| Librarian Crone | Archive, Living Books | Festival zones (too chaotic, too loud) |
| Chameleon Druid | Everywhere (briefly) | Anywhere requiring sustained effort |

## STAT SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Krista's classes suggest a **non-standard stat system** for the Inn. Not the standard STR/DEX/INT/CON/CHA model. Something more nuanced:

**Core Stats:**
- **Dexterity** — fine motor skills, crafting, mini-games
- **Intelligence** — archive access, puzzle solving, dialogue options
- **Charisma** — NPC interactions, bartering, diplomacy
- **Endurance** — sustained activity, long quests, combat stamina
- **Tolerance** — patience, frustration threshold, willingness to retry

**Derived Stats:**
- **Broad Intelligence** — general knowledge (archive access breadth)
- **Situational Intelligence** — applied knowledge (puzzle solving under pressure)
- **Situational Ethics** — moral flexibility based on context
- **Capability Potential** — growth rate / XP curve
- **Maintenance** — resource consumption rate

**Special Traits:**
- **Nimble Fingers** — bonus to crafting and precision tasks
- **Situational Alpha** — temporary stat spike when in zone of expertise
- **Temporary Charisma/Agility** — burst stats that decay over time
- **Versatility** — ability to temporarily adopt other class traits

This is a stat system where **personality is a mechanic.** Your stats don't just determine what you can do — they determine *how you relate to other players.*

## RECOMMENDED INTEGRATION

### Phase 1: Define the Five Classes
Implement these as the **starting classes** for the Geode Inn. Five classes, five playstyles, five ways to experience the same world.

### Phase 2: Build the Taunt/Enrage System
The Bard's taunt mechanic is the foundation of the class interaction system. Build it first — it's the combat debuff that makes party composition matter.

### Phase 3: Build Zone Affinity
Each class has zones where they shine and zones where they struggle. This creates natural exploration incentives — you *want* to find your zone.

### Phase 4: Build the Stance System (Chameleon Druid)
The Chameleon's temporary stance switching is the most technically complex mechanic. Build it after the base classes work.

### Phase 5: Build the Social Dynamics
Party composition should affect NPC interactions, quest availability, and dialogue options. The Bard's presence changes what quests are available. The Crone's presence changes what archive entries are accessible. The Thief's presence changes what items you can find.

## AUTHOR'S NOTE

These five classes are not balanced. They're not supposed to be. They're *interesting.* A balanced party has no friction. An interesting party has a Thief who can't sell the jewelry she makes, a Bard who makes enemies stronger but stupider, a Tinkerer who's brilliant and impatient, a Librarian who knows everything and feels nothing, and a Chameleon who can be anyone but can't sustain it.

That's a party. That's a story. That's the Inn.
