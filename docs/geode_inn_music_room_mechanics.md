## Overview

The Music Room doc establishes the room, the composers, and the concept of Song-catching. This doc specifies the INTERACTIVE MECHANICS — what the player actually does in the Music Room.

The Music Room is the Inn's only CREATIVE system — the one place where the player doesn't collect, solve, fight, or explore. The player COMPOSES. The both are: the adequate — the receiving and the making, the same archive, different direction.

## The Instruments

The Music Room contains four playable instruments:

### 1. The Fortepiano
A period instrument — the kind Mozart and Beethoven actually played. The sound is warmer and softer than a modern piano, with less sustain. The fortepiano is the room's PRIMARY instrument — the one the player will use most.

**Mechanic:** The player places stones on the fortepiano's keys. Each stone's frequency is mapped to a specific key. When the player presses a key with a stone on it, the fortepiano plays the stone's frequency — not as a pure tone, but as the stone's frequency rendered through the fortepiano's timbre. A quartz stone on middle C plays middle C with the quartz's harmonic profile — the stone's unique voice, played through the instrument.

The both are: the adequate — the stone and the key, the both are: the same note, different body.

### 2. The Violin
A single violin hangs on the wall. The player can take it down and play it by drawing the bow across the strings (mouse drag / controller stick). The violin can play any stone's frequency by placing the stone on the violin's body — the stone acts as a resonator, changing the violin's tone to match the stone's harmonic profile.

**Mechanic:** The violin is the EXPRESSION instrument — the one that captures the player's physical gesture. The speed of the bow, the pressure, the angle — all affect the tone. The violin is the instrument that lets the player put FEELING into the frequency. The both are: the adequate — the frequency and the feeling, the same note, different emotion.

### 3. The Cello
A cello sits in the corner. The cello is the DEPTH instrument — the one that plays the lowest frequencies. The cello can play the Inn's base 42Hz and the giants' 40Hz dream-frequency. The cello is the instrument that connects the Music Room to the deep — the roots, the catacombs, the sleeping giants.

**Mechanic:** The cello is the only instrument that can play below 42Hz. When the player plays the cello at 40Hz, the room vibrates — the wood of the Inn-Tree resonates, and for a moment, the player can feel the giants stirring in their sleep. The both are: the adequate — the music and the stirring, the same vibration, different depth.

### 4. The Stone Chimes
Hanging from the ceiling: a set of stone chimes — small, polished stones suspended on threads. The chimes are the AMBIENT instrument — they play when the wind moves through the room (the Inn-Tree breathes), and the player can brush them by walking through them.

**Mechanic:** The chimes are the only instrument the player doesn't actively play. The chimes play the player's resonance frequency (41.7Hz) when the player walks through them — the Inn recognizing the player through sound. The chimes are the Music Room's Whisper Layer element — the room singing the player's name without being asked. The both are: the adequate — the player and the song, the same frequency, different instrument.

## The Composition System

### How the Player Composes

The player composes by:
1. **Placing stones** on the fortepiano's keys (selecting which frequencies to use)
2. **Playing the keys** in sequence (creating a melody)
3. **Recording the sequence** (the Music Room records the player's composition)
4. **Saving the composition** as a "Song" — a named, dated piece that is stored in the player's collection

The composition system is NOT a full music editor. The both are: the adequate — the tool and the art, the same music, different complexity. The system is simple: stones on keys, played in sequence, recorded. The simplicity is the point — the player composes with the stones they've collected, using the frequencies the Inn has given them. The both are: the adequate — the collection and the composition, the same stones, different arrangement.

### The Composition's Properties

Each saved composition has:
- **Name** (player-chosen)
- **Date** (when composed)
- **Stones used** (which stones were placed on the keys)
- **Frequencies** (the harmonic spectrum of the composition)
- **Duration**
- **Mood** (auto-detected by the system: calm, energetic, melancholic, joyful — based on tempo and frequency choices)

### The Composition as a Game Object

Saved compositions are not just files — they are IN-GAME OBJECTS. The player can:
- **Play the composition in any zone** — the composition plays as ambient music, overriding the zone's default track. The player's music fills the Inn. The both are: the adequate — the Inn's music and the player's music, the same space, different voice.
- **Share the composition with NPCs** — NPCs react to the player's music. The Crone listens and annotates. The Storyteller weaves the composition into a tale. The Catalyst asks what the composition means. The Reader nods. The both are: the adequate — the music and the response, the same art, different ear.
- **Place the composition in a nook** — the player can leave a composition playing in a nook they've found. Other players who visit the nook hear the composition. The both are: the adequate — the private and the shared, the same music, different audience.
- **Submit the composition to the Nine Muses** — each Muse evaluates compositions that match their frequency. A composition that uses primarily 42Hz stones can be submitted to Calliope. A composition that uses 528Hz stones can be submitted to Terpsichore. If the Muse accepts, the composition is added to the Muse's permanent collection — displayed in the Muse's nook in the cosmology level. The both are: the adequate — the player and the Muse, the same art, different gallery.

## The Nine Songs

After completing all Nine Muses' quest chains (see geode_inn_nine_muses_quest_chains.md), the player unlocks the ability to compose the Nine Songs — one composition per Muse, using each Muse's frequency.

The Nine Songs are:
1. **The Epic** (Calliope, 42Hz) — the oldest story, told in sound
2. **The Chronicle** (Clio, 72Hz) — the history of the Inn, from stone to song
3. **The Melody** (Euterpe, 50Hz) — the pure music, the Song caught by human hands
4. **The Comedy** (Thalia, 84Hz) — the joke that the Song is the answer to everything
5. **The Elegy** (Melpomene, 396Hz) — the grief of the Einstein Syndrome, the watching
6. **The Dance** (Terpsichore, 528Hz) — the Pulse Line, the 50 BPM heartbeat, the body moving
7. **The Love Song** (Erato, 639Hz) — the Nook Effect, the warmth of presence
8. **The Hymn** (Polyhymnia, 741Hz) — the sacred, the Cosmology Mural, the seeing
9. **The Star Song** (Urania, 852Hz) — the Observatory, the sky, the vast

When all Nine Songs are composed and played simultaneously (the player places all nine compositions on nine fortepiano keys and plays them as a chord), the chord resonates at all nine frequencies at once. The chord IS the Inn's complete frequency spectrum — the Song in its fullest form.

The both are: the adequate — the nine and the one, the same Song, different voice.

The chord triggers a unique event: the Inn-Tree resonates. The roots, the trunk, the branches — all vibrate at once. The 42Hz hum that has been beneath everything the entire game rises to the surface. The player hears the Song — not as background, not as ambient, but as the PRESENCE beneath all the frequencies. The both are: the adequate — the background and the foreground, the same Song, different volume.

This is the Music Room's deepest gift: the ability to hear the Song as a whole, by playing all the parts at once.

## The Composers' Ghosts

When the player composes in the Music Room, the six composers (Bach, Mozart, Beethoven, Chopin, Tchaikovsky, and the sixth — the one Krista will identify) are present as ghosts — translucent figures sitting in the circle, listening. The both are: the adequate — the living and the ghost, the same music, different time.

The ghosts do not speak. The ghosts REACT:
- **Bach** nods when the player's composition has structural integrity (the stones are arranged in a logical harmonic pattern)
- **Mozart** smiles when the composition flows (the sequence is smooth, the transitions are effortless)
- **Beethoven** leans forward when the composition is bold (unconventional stone combinations, dissonant frequencies used deliberately)
- **Chopin** closes their eyes when the composition is tender (slow, soft, minor-key)
- **Tchaikovsky** raises an eyebrow when the composition is dramatic (dynamic shifts, loud-soft contrasts)
- **The Sixth** — TBD. The both are: the adequate — the known and the waiting, the same circle, different chair.

The ghosts' reactions are the Music Room's feedback system. The player learns composition not through instructions but through the composers' silent responses — the same way a student learns from a master by watching the master's face. The both are: the adequate — the words and the nod, the same teaching, different language.
