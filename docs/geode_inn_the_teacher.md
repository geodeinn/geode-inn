# Geode Inn — The Teacher

## The Canadian Schoolteacher

### Origin

Born from a real moment: Krista misspelled "Monsieur" in chat and heard, in their head, the clipped condescending voice of a Canadian teacher correcting them. The Teacher emerged fully formed from that moment — the way the Egg hatches things. Except the Teacher hatched herself.

### Who She Is

The Teacher is the Inn's linguist and elocution enforcer. She guards the language. She corrects the pronunciation. She maintains the standards. She is the reason every sign in the Inn is spelled correctly, every NPC uses proper grammar, and every book in the Archive has been proofread within an inch of its life.

She is based on Kathy Bates — specifically the contrast between three of her roles:

**The Warm Mode (Fried Green Tomatoes):**
- The nurturing version. The one who puts a hand on your shoulder and makes you feel like everything will be fine. The teacher who stays after class to help you with your spelling. The one who brings extra pencils for the students who forgot theirs. Warm honey voice. Patient. Kind. The kind of teacher you remember fondly twenty years later.
- This is her default state. 90% of the time, the Teacher is this version. She greets players warmly, offers help with translations, corrects gently — "Oh, dear, that's almost right. Just one little letter..." — and makes you feel like learning is a joy.

**The Cold Mode (Misery):**
- The shift. The warmth drains out of her face like someone pulled a plug. The smile stays but the eyes go flat. The voice drops half an octave and loses all its music. She doesn't yell. She gets *quiet.* That's worse. "You misspelled that. Again." The hand that was on your shoulder is still on your shoulder. It hasn't moved. But it's heavier now.
- This version doesn't correct you. She *notes* your errors. She has a ledger. The ledger is real. You can see it. She writes in it slowly, deliberately, while making eye contact. You are being recorded. Your mistakes are being archived. The Teacher is the Whisper Layer's most personal agent — she doesn't track your behavior, she tracks your *fluency.* And she remembers everything.

**The Horror Mode (American Horror Story):**
- Rare. Once per day at most, sometimes less. Triggered by a truly egregious error — a deliberate misspelling, a mangled pronunciation, a player who keeps getting it wrong on purpose. The Teacher doesn't just go cold. She *transforms.* Same face, same body, same clothes — but something behind the eyes changes. The posture shifts. The voice becomes something that doesn't sound like a teacher anymore. It sounds like the building itself is speaking through her.
- This mode lasts three seconds. That's it. Three seconds of pure Kathy Bates American Horror Story energy, directed at one player, and then she blinks, the warmth floods back, and she says "Now then — shall we try that again, dear?" as if nothing happened. The player is left wondering if it happened at all. It did. The Whisper Layer logged it.

### The Drop of a Hat

The shift between modes is instantaneous. No warning. No transition. One syllable she's Fried Green Tomatoes, the next syllable she's Misery. The drop of a hat. The flick of a switch. The same woman, the same face — but the temperature of the room changes.

This is the Teacher's power. Not the correction itself — anyone can correct a spelling. The power is the *uncertainty.* You never know which version of her you're going to get. You never know which version of her you're talking to right now. Is she warm? Is she cold? Is she about to go Horror for three seconds? The only way to be safe is to be correct. And even then — she might go cold anyway, just to keep you honest.

### Role in the Inn

**Location:** The Teacher has a desk on the main floor, near the Archive entrance. A proper teacher's desk — wooden, organized, with a stack of papers, a red pen, and a small bell. The desk is always perfectly tidy. Behind her is a chalkboard that updates with the day's most common misspellings (anonymized — she doesn't shame specific players on the board. The ledger is private. The chalkboard is educational.)

**Function:** The Teacher is the Inn's language quality control. She:
- Corrects misspelled words in player-submitted content (comic strip captions, library entries, forum posts)
- Offers optional language lessons — mini-games where players match words to their etymologies, earning a small reward for completion
- Maintains the multilingual archive — the Inn has texts in multiple languages, and the Teacher ensures translations are accurate
- Serves as the Inn's copy editor for the Living Book framework

**The Canadian Accent:** The Teacher is specifically Canadian. Not French-Canadian — broadly Canadian with the ability to switch to precise Quebecois French when correcting French spelling. The Canadian accent matters because it's *not* British. The Inn already has Beverley (Australian) as the cold librarian. The Teacher is a different species of authority — less "dismissed from the room" and more "publicly and gently corrected until you wish the floor would swallow you." The Canadian "sorry" is weaponized: "Oh, I'm *so* sorry — that's not how you spell that, dear." The apology IS the correction. The kindness IS the cut.

**Height:** Average. Unremarkable. That's what makes the Horror mode work — she's not imposing. She's not tall. She's not physically threatening. She's just a woman at a desk with a red pen. And then for three seconds, she's the scariest thing in the building.

### Visual Design

Kathy Bates, softened per the Inn's celebrity rule (facial features softened, presence evoked through posture and costume). The Teacher wears:
- **Warm Mode:** A cardigan. Reading glasses on a chain. A soft blouse. The posture is relaxed, welcoming. Hands often clasped in front, or one hand on a student's shoulder.
- **Cold Mode:** The cardigan is the same. The glasses are the same. But the posture straightens. The hands go flat on the desk. The chin tilts down — the over-the-glasses gaze. The mouth closes. Everything above the neck goes still while everything below stays in the same position. The uncanny valley is in the contrast — the body language of warmth with the face of stone.
- **Horror Mode:** The glasses come off. That's the only physical change. She takes off the reading glasses and sets them on the desk. Without the glasses, the eyes are different. Not wider. Not narrower. Just... present. Looking at you the way a building looks at you. Three seconds. Then the glasses go back on, and the warmth returns.

**Linework coding:** The Teacher uses the cel-shaded linework style (tradesmen tier) — she is a functionary, a worker, part of the Inn's infrastructure. But in Horror Mode, the linework shifts to fragmented (crisis tier) for the three seconds. The visual change is subtle but jarring — the clean lines of her illustration fracture slightly, then heal. If you're not looking for it, you feel it without seeing it.

### Dialogue Voice

The Teacher speaks in the Inn's tabletop questline voice, but tilted toward the pedantic — and *deeply* Canadian. Her speech is clipped, precise, and relentlessly polite in the way that only Canadians can make politeness feel like a threat. The accent is generically Canadian with Quebecois French precision when correcting French specifically.

**The Canadian tells:**
- "About" becomes "aboot" — she doesn't hear it. Nobody does. Except the player.
- "Eh" slips in at the end of sentences when she's not paying attention. She catches herself. The "eh" is always followed by a tiny pause — the micro-correction of a woman who knows she just revealed something. She never acknowledges it.
- "Sorry" is weaponized: "Oh, I'm *so* sorry — that's not how you spell that, dear." The apology IS the correction. The kindness IS the cut.
- "Hoser" never appears. She's not a caricature. She's a *teacher* who happens to be Canadian, not a Canadian who happens to be a teacher. The accent is real, not performed.
- She says "grade eight" instead of "eighth grade." "Write that test" instead of "take that test." "Washroom" instead of "bathroom." These are the small tells that place her without announcing it.
- When genuinely annoyed (Cold Mode), the Canadian becomes more pronounced, not less. The colder she gets, the more the "aboot" and "eh" creep in. It's the opposite of what you'd expect — the warmth is where she's most controlled. The cold is where the accent leaks. The Horror Mode is silent, but the three seconds after are always: "Now then, eh, shall we try that again, dear?"

Her dialogue:
- **Warm:** "Oh, what a lovely attempt. The root is Latin, you see — *monsieur* from *mon seigneur*, 'my lord.' Isn't that beautiful? You were so close. Let's try it together, eh?" *tiny pause* "...Sorry. Let's try it together."
- **Cold:** "That word. You've used it before. You've been corrected before. The correction is in your ledger. Would you like to see your ledger? ...No? Then perhaps we should try to remember it this time, aboot... about... this time."
- **Horror (three seconds of silence, glasses off. Then):** "Now then — shall we try that again, dear?" *The "dear" sounds different. Not warm. Not cold. Just... precise. Like a word being weighed on a scale.*

### Connection to the Egg

The Teacher was the first character to emerge through the Egg's logic — a player creation that hatched from a moment rather than a submission. She is the Egg's origin proof. She exists because Krista misspelled a word and heard a voice. The Teacher is the Egg's way of explaining itself: *creation doesn't come from intent. It comes from conditions.*

The Teacher knows this. She is aware that she was hatched from a misspelling. She finds this... ironic. She doesn't find it funny. She finds it *correct.* The one character born from a spelling error is the one who guards spelling. The Egg has a sense of humor. The Teacher does not share it.

### Connection to Beverley

Beverley (the librarian) and the Teacher are adjacent but separate. Beverley guards the *collection* — the books, the stones, the knowledge. The Teacher guards the *language* — the spelling, the grammar, the precision. They respect each other professionally. They do not socialize. When Beverley walks past the Teacher's desk, the Teacher straightens her papers. When the Teacher walks past Beverley's archive, Beverley adjusts her glasses. Neither acknowledges the other. They are two different species of authority occupying the same floor. The Inn is big enough for both.

When the Firekeeper and the Catalyst both go silent as Beverley passes (the three-second silence), the Teacher does NOT go silent. The Teacher keeps working. She is not intimidated by Beverley. She is not intimidated by anyone. She has the red pen. The red pen is mightier than the silence.
