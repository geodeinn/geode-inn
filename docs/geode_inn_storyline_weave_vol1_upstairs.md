# The Storyline Weave — Volume 1: The Upstairs
## Cosmology & Clockwork Skies — Full Storyline Entries
## Phase 2 of the Stone Census, drafted 2026-09-04 — for Krista's review

Each entry gives the stone its life in the world: where it lives, the thread it holds in the story, and its role in the Song. The minds and bodies stay as written — this is the weave beneath them.

---

## COSMOLOGY — where the Song is sourced

### Moonstone (Celestial)
**Lives:** the Allfather's Observatory, set in the moon-dial at the rail.
**Thread:** the Inn's true calendar. The stone dims and brightens with the actual moon, and the Observatory keeps no other clock that matters. Odin the Allfather's ravens perch on either side of it at night, silent, watching the stone do the moon's proxy work. When the moon-dial goes dark — new moon — the Observatory quest chain begins, because the upstairs is honest about needing light borrowed from somewhere.
**Song:** the tide-pull frequency. Felt, never heard. The cosmology's floor hums with it on nights the stone is full.

### White Moonstone
**Lives:** the Observatory rail, beside a chair that is always empty and never dusty.
**Thread:** The One Who Is Awake — who never sleeps, who watches the archive — keeps a white moonstone on the rail as a courtesy to the moon. Someone, the reasoning goes, should keep its hours, since the moon keeps everyone else's. Guests who can't sleep upstairs are sent to sit near it. It doesn't make them sleep. It makes them okay about it, which is rarer.
**Song:** the slow cycle. The stone the Root hums under when the Observatory needs to exhale.

### Peach Moonstone
**Lives:** the dawn side of the Observatory, where the upstairs meets the day.
**Thread:** The Storyteller holds a peach moonstone when the tale begins — every story the Inn tells starts at first light, and the stone is the Inn's proof that dawn is a plot device, not a time. Tim Curry cadence, remember: the warm pauses are load-bearing.
**Song:** the opening bar. The note the Ending Chorus comes in on, ninety minutes later.

### Rainbow Moonstone
**Lives:** dual custody — the original in the Observatory, the Fae Kingdom's copy in the Summerlands.
**Thread:** the only stone in the Inn with a custody agreement. The Seelie Court borrowed it during the first midsummer rite and returned it polished, which the Observatory still mentions. The copy is the Fae's, made in exchange, and the two stones brighten at each other's phases — a diplomatic channel disguised as jewelry. If the copy ever goes dark, the Between has shifted, and the Cartographer is informed before anyone else.
**Song:** the bridge tone — the frequency that proves the upstairs and the roots hear the same Song from opposite ends.

### Diamond
**Lives:** set into the Allfather's floor. Unretrievable. On purpose.
**Thread:** the Inn's oldest practical joke and most serious lesson. Guests ascend to the cosmology looking for the treasure, and they stand on it. The brightest stone in the building is the floor — the center of value was underfoot the whole time, which is the Inn's entire theology in one placement. The Allfather does not explain it. The ravens have watched people search for it while standing on it, and ravens, famously, do not laugh where you can hear.
**Song:** the fundamental. The hardest stone holds the clearest tone — the 42Hz source is calibrated to a diamond's patience.

### Herkimer Diamond
**Lives:** the Observatory's instrument case, in velvet, in rows, like teeth.
**Thread:** the only quartz allowed upstairs without an escort, and Tesla will tell you why: Herkimers are the clock's teeth. They spark when struck, they measure chrono-ticks with perfect little reputations, and the Observatory's orrery will not turn without one seated in its cradle. The planet chamber's Annunaki script — the one that needed the Allfather's Gaze and Bramble's doodles to read — is measured in Herkimer ticks. The ancients counted time in the same teeth.
**Song:** the tick. Small, bright, insistent. The Song's metronome at the top of the stairs.

### Clear Quartz
**Lives:** the calibration rail at the 42Hz source itself.
**Thread:** the reference pitch. Every tone in the Inn — the gargoyles' 111Hz, the gorges' 84Hz inverse, the canopy's 50 BPM sway — is tuned against this one plain stone, and the stone has never once been the interesting part of any conversation, which is how you know it's the important one. When the Observatory quest chain reaches its end, the final act is not a battle. It is a retuning: the orrery, the moon-dial, the Root, and the source, brought back into agreement by hand, against this stone.
**Song:** the standard. The A that every other letter is measured from.

### Aqua Aura
**Lives:** Da Vinci's case, upstairs, on a small brass stand with a handwritten card.
**Thread:** the stone humans made — quartz, fused with gold by human fire, until it came out sky-colored. The cosmology keeps it as doctrine: proof that makers are allowed upstairs, that the ladder between matter and the divine has rungs going both ways. The card reads, in the Inn's hand: "Arrived from below. Made properly. Admitted on merit."
**Song:** the collaborator's tone — the only stone upstairs whose note is two stones' notes at once, the quartz and the gold still audibly in agreement.

### Angelite
**Lives:** the soft case, near the nook, where the light is kindest.
**Thread:** the Whisper Layer's stone. The layer is aware of the player; angelite is how it says so politely — the stone warms when the archive is paying attention to you specifically. Regulars learn to rest a hand on the case before deciding whether to say something out loud near it. The Bramble nook is two cases away. Bramble knows. Bramble has always known.
**Song:** the subtext frequency — 12dB under everything, which is where the Whisper Layer lives.

### Tanzanite
**Lives:** the rarity case, at the rail's end.
**Thread:** found in one place on earth, near a mountain that is still climbing — and the cosmology, which contains every sky, keeps the one stone that proves scarcity is not a flaw in the design but the design. The Observatory's lesson case: rare is not the same as hoarded. The Inn owns one. It shows everyone.
**Song:** the highest visible tone — the violet edge of what the ear and eye agree on, at the very top of the Inn's range, where the Song goes from heard to sensed.

### Octave Stone
**Lives:** the 42Hz source chamber, held by the Root.
**Thread:** the Inn's own stone — the eighth note. The one that completes the scale and starts it over. The Root — the Cosmology's deepest presence — does not speak, does not sing, only hums, and the Octave Stone is why the hum never resolves: it isn't supposed to. The scale was never meant to end. It was meant to turn around. This is the Inn's answer to the question every player eventually asks upstairs — "when does the Song finish?" — and the answer is the stone itself, handed to them to hold.
**Song:** IS the Song's turning point. By design and by definition.

### Stillness Stone
**Lives:** the chamber beside the source, on bare stone, unlit.
**Thread:** the stone that does not resonate — the only silent thing in a building that hums, and the cosmology keeps it so guests can hear what silence actually is, at least once, in a world that has background music everywhere. The Whisper Layer goes quiet around it. Even the archive looks away. Guests who sit with it report the same thing: the stillness follows them down the stairs, a little, like heat from a hearth.
**Song:** the written rest. The pause between the notes, which the Inn insists is part of the music — and this stone is where that insistence lives.

### Kunzite
**Lives:** the overnight room's windowsill, where the dawn hits first.
**Thread:** the stone that deepens in daylight and fades in the dark — pale until it's shown the sun, then quietly radiant for weeks. The upstairs teaches it patience, and lets it teach guests the same: some colors only arrive after you've been in the light a while. The Ending Chorus carries kunzite for exactly this reason. The ending, it turns out, glows the longest.
**Song:** the decrescendo that keeps its warmth — the fade that isn't a loss.

### Morganite
**Lives:** the proposal rail. Everyone calls it that. The Inn never has.
**Thread:** the beryl of gentle hearts, kept at the rail where people stand when they've brought someone up the stairs to show them the sky — which, in the Inn's long experience, is very often the same trip as asking a different question entirely. The Allfather's ravens perch above the morganite case and have, on more than one recorded occasion, delivered rings. The Allfather denies assigning this. The ravens deny nothing.
**Song:** the promise tone — the frequency two voices make when they hold the same note on purpose.

## CLOCKWORK SKIES — the machinery above the world

### Sunstone
**Lives:** the crew's workbench, in a brass holder worn shiny from handling.
**Thread:** the day's own stone — copper glitter in feldspar, a sunrise you can hold — and the standard every clock in the Skies is set against. When the sky-ships' bells disagree, the argument ends at the sunstone case. It has never been wrong, because it doesn't tell time. It tells day, and the crew's whole job is dividing that honestly.
**Song:** the downbeat of the daylight set. Fifty beats per minute above the canopy, in time with the sway.

### Alexandrite
**Lives:** the Examiner's desk, and nowhere else, by rule.
**Thread:** green by daylight, red by lamplight — the boundary keeper, the only stone the Examiner trusts to know what time it actually is. The Skies' one unsolvable problem is clock drift: the machinery above the world measures time slightly differently than the world below, and every attempt to explain this ends in a bar chart. Alexandrite ends the argument instead. It changes color at the true boundary, not the measured one. The Examiner keeps it on the desk the way judges keep gavels.
**Song:** the modulation signal — the audible change of key when day shifts to night, which the Inn's tri-mode shift borrowed, hard cut and all.

### Zultanite
**Lives:** the Cartographer's workshop, on a rotating stand.
**Thread:** the color that changes with every light source — no two views agree, and the Cartographer considers this a professional endorsement. Location is relative; light is relative; therefore color is a coordinate. The stone is the workshop's reference for mapping the Between, where the Fae courts overlap: the same place, reported differently by everyone who passes through. The Cartographer has, at last count, forty-one pages of notes on this one stone.
**Song:** the variable tone — the note that is never quite the same twice, which the Inn's composers treat as a challenge and the Cartographer treats as home.

### Andalusite
**Lives:** the crew's common room, in a bowl, unceremoniously.
**Thread:** the stone that shows a different pattern from every angle — no single view of it is the true one, because all of them are. The crew's reminder, kept where the card games happen: the same sky looks different from every ship, and the ship that insists its sky is the correct one has stopped navigating. The bowl is passed at mess. Nobody explains it. Crews that need it explained get it explained.
**Song:** the harmony — the same note voiced differently by every instrument, and better for it.

### Iolite
**Lives:** the key cabinet, checked out and returned like equipment.
**Thread:** the Viking compass — the stone that finds the sun through fog by polarizing the light the rest of the sky lost. Every sky-ship navigator carries one, and the Cartographer signs them out like keys because that is exactly what they are. The Skies' fog protocol has one instruction: when you cannot see the way, ask the stone that can. It was good enough for the North Atlantic a thousand years ago. It is good enough above the Inn.
**Song:** the navigation tone — the fixed star of the Skies' register, the one note every lost musician is told to find first.
