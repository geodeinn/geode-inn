## THE STAIRCASE — the alchemical bridge between matter and the divine

### Moldavite
**Lives:** the topmost landing, in a case that is bolted to the stone. The only case on the Staircase that is bolted.
**Thread:** the only stone in the Inn that arrived from off-world, and it knows it — born when a meteorite struck the earth and the space between them turned to glass, fifteen million years ago. The Staircase is the Inn's bridge between matter and the divine, and moldavite is the bridge's own origin story: stone that has literally been to heaven and back, changed by the trip. The alchemists' texts call it the catalyst. The Inn calls it the proof. Either way, it is the most important stone on the stairs and has never once let anyone forget it.
**Song:** the arrival tone — the highest frequency the Staircase plays, the note of something that has seen the other side and come back with a report.

### Tektite
**Lives:** beside moldavite, in the same case, in the modest spot.
**Thread:** the same event, less famous, more available — moldavite's plainer sibling, scattered across continents instead of concentrated in one valley. The Staircase keeps the pair together as the alchemists' favorite lesson about not needing to be the exception to be part of the miracle: tektite went to the same wedding and came home in the same carriage. Guests who cannot afford the one are handed the other, and the stone does not mind, and that is the second lesson, free of charge.
**Song:** the echo of the arrival tone — the same frequency, one step down, played a half-second late. The Staircase finds this deeply funny, for a staircase.

### Fire Opal
**Lives:** the second landing's flame case, lit from below.
**Thread:** the flame that stayed — opal with fire still moving in it, the alchemist's first successful experiment frozen at the exact moment of success. Every text on the Staircase is about transformation, and fire opal is the only stone in the collection that shows the transformation itself, mid-syllable, holding its color like a word on the tip of the mineral tongue. The alchemy students are drawn to it before they can explain why. They never have to. The stone is the explanation.
**Song:** the transformation tone — the rising note the Inn plays whenever anything changes state. All phase-shifts in the Inn, material and otherwise, are scored by this stone's frequency.

### Obsidian Family (Gold Sheen, Rainbow, Snowflake)
**Lives:** the descent cases, on the third landing — the last stones lit by the upstairs light.
**Thread:** the volcano's sudden grief, three ways. Gold sheen: the dark you were avoiding, gilded the whole time. Rainbow: the grief that turned out to be full of colors, if you tilt it. Snowflake: the frozen mid-sentence, and the lesson the alchemists love best — even frozen mid-sentence, the pattern continues, cristobalite blooming in the black like the pause was the point. The three obsidians are the Staircase's descent markers: guests are meant to pass all three on the way down. What the stones say is not written anywhere. Guests arrive at the bottom able to recite it anyway.
**Song:** the tri-mode — 42Hz into 84Hz, the hard cut, the Shadow's inverse frequency. The obsidian cases are the physical location of the shift. The Inn's entire tri-mode architecture is calibrated on this landing, in this light, against these stones.

### Charoite
**Lives:** the fourth landing, set into the spiral wall in a swirl that matches the stone.
**Thread:** the transformation stone — the purple swirl that looks like it is still happening, because it is. The Staircase turns as it descends, and charoite is the wall's confession that the spiral is not architecture, it is process: the stairs are still becoming stairs, and the stone is still becoming stone, and the guest descending is still becoming whoever is going to arrive at the bottom. Named for the Chara river, which is fitting — the Inn notes that rivers are the only things that transform more fluently than charoite pretends to.
**Song:** the turning tone — the slow spiral note that follows the guest down, modulating a quarter-step per landing, arriving at the bottom in a different key than it left the top in.

### Nuummite
**Lives:** the foundation of the lowest landing. Two of them. They are load-bearing.
**Thread:** the oldest thing the Inn carries — three billion years, folded in Greenland's first fires, older than almost everything that has ever lived — and the Staircase, uniquely in the Inn, built on it rather than displaying it. The alchemists' final lesson is at the bottom of the stairs: transformation requires something that does not transform. The nuummite holds the landing up. The landing holds the Inn up. The oldest fire in the building is the one you are standing on.
**Song:** the deepest tone the Inn produces, with the Stone Circle Chamber's approval — 42Hz's own foundation, the bass under the Song, felt in the feet before it is heard anywhere.

### Pyrite
**Lives:** the caution case, sixth landing, with the mirror behind it.
**Thread:** "fool's gold" — the most instructive stone in the collection because it is beautiful AND wrong. The alchemists' curriculum keeps pyrite at the exact midpoint of the descent, where the stairs are dark enough to misjudge: the stone glitters, the mirror doubles it, and the guest must decide, twice, whether what they are looking at is gold. It never is. The lesson is not that beauty lies. The lesson is that beauty is a question, and gold is an answer, and confusing the two is how mountains get mined by fools.
**Song:** the false resonance — a bright, attractive frequency that is slightly, detectably off. The Inn's composers built it wrong on purpose. Music students are asked to find the flaw. Guests find it faster.

### Amethyst
**Lives:** the transmutation case, seventh landing, the most stolen and most returned stone in the Inn.
**Thread:** the transmutation purple — the color of the change itself. Students steal amethyst from the Staircase at a rate the Inn long ago stopped policing, because amethyst comes back: it is returned by post, left on the bar, found in the guest book's pages, once in a loaf of bread with a note reading "sorry." The Inn's official position is that this is the stone doing its job — a transformation stone is meant to travel, and the theft-and-return cycle is amethyst's transformation, performed on the guests.
**Song:** the violet frequency — the tone of the threshold state, played wherever matter is about to become something else. The Staircase's signature note.

### Shungite
**Lives:** the water case, with a ladle. Guests are encouraged to ladle.
**Thread:** carbon, almost pure — the practical alchemy, the water-cleaning stone, transformation you can drink. The Staircase is full of grand texts about transmuting lead into gold, and the shungite case is the Staircase's rebuttal to its own library: here is a stone that takes poisoned water and makes it clean, today, for free, no philosophy required. It is the most-loved case on the stairs and the least written about. The alchemists call it "the proof that worked."
**Song:** the purification tone — the frequency the Inn plays through every water feature it owns. The water zones borrowed it, use it daily, and send their thanks upstairs annually.

### Copper
**Lives:** the instrument rail, wound through every alchemical apparatus on the Staircase.
**Thread:** the first metal befriended — the messenger, the conductor, the wire. Every instrument on the Staircase is copper-wired, because the alchemy level is where the Inn does its actual signal work: the 42Hz Song is carried from the Cosmology to the Catacombs along this railing, and the railing is copper, and the copper takes the Song the whole way down without once keeping it to itself. The Staircase's texts are about the divine; the railing is the working proof that the divine travels at the speed of the mundane.
**Song:** IS the carrier — the copper rail is the Song's physical route from upstairs to underground. The Staircase does not play the Song. It delivers it.

### Covellite
**Lives:** the progress case, eighth landing, beside a small plaque that reads "still becoming."
**Thread:** copper caught mid-becoming — indigo shot with violet and gold, never finished oxidizing, always in transition. The alchemists keep covellite as the descent's progress bar: the stone has been in the middle of its transformation for two hundred million years, and it is the most beautiful thing in the drawer, and the plaque says the rest. Students on the Staircase are told to hold it when they feel behind. The stone is never behind. Neither, the Inn insists, are they.
**Song:** the suspended tone — a note that resolves on no schedule the Inn has been able to determine. The composers gave up trying to score it and now let it hang. It is the Staircase's favorite sound.

### Sugilite
**Lives:** the ninth landing, the last case before the Catacomb door, the one nearest the dark.
**Thread:** the violet shield — the healer's alchemy, the protective stone set at the exact boundary between the Staircase's library-light and the Catacomb's deep. The descent is not dangerous, but it is honest, and the Inn keeps sugilite at the door the way the Main Floor keeps tourmaline at the bar: you do not have to hold what was never yours to hold. Set it down here. The Catacombs will still be there when you pick it back up.
**Song:** the guardian tone — the frequency that plays at every threshold in the Inn, all of them copied from this stone's case. Every door in the building hums the ninth landing's note.

## THE CATACOMBS — the ancient civilizations, the deep, the bloodline

### Lapis Lazuli
**Lives:** the Catacomb entrance, the first case past the door.
**Thread:** the pharaohs' stone — the color the Egyptians decided was worth mining, worshipping, and grinding into the paint for the sky itself. The Catacombs hold the Inn's ancient civilizations, and lapis is the entrance stone because it was civilization's first luxury that wasn't food: proof that as soon as humans had anything spare, they spent it on blue. The entrance case holds raw lapis, carved lapis, and a jar of the ground pigment, the same ultramarine the old books were painted with. The Archive upstairs has a standing order for the pigment. The Catacombs fills it grudgingly, at a price the Archive pays in patience.
**Song:** the ancestral tone — the deepest blue note in the Inn's register, played once at the Catacomb door when a guest first enters. The Inn's way of saying: you are now older than you were.

### Malachite
**Lives:** the Egyptian wing, second chamber, the transformation green.
**Thread:** the stone of change that absorbs and changes — copper ore gone green, the metal's whole second act. The Catacombs display malachite beside azurite, before-and-after, because azurite weathers into malachite and the Egyptians knew it and used both and left the chemistry unexplained on purpose, the way the best teachers do. Malachite is the second chamber's guardian: guests who enter carrying something they're done with tend to leave it here. The case is full. The Inn does not empty it. Some of it, the stone has finished with.
**Song:** the weathering tone — the slow note of things becoming other things at the pace of centuries. The Catacombs play it continuously. Nobody minds. That is the point of the Catacombs.

### Azurite
**Lives:** beside malachite. Same case. Facing each other.
**Thread:** lapis's other face — the deep blue that becomes the transformation green, the second chamber's before-picture. The paired case is the Catacombs' best-kept teaching: nothing in the deep is finished, including the deep. Guests who return years later swear the two stones have shifted toward each other. The Inn has no data on this. The Inn has never needed data on this.
**Song:** the promise tone — the frequency of the thing before it becomes the thing. Played under the malachite note, always slightly ahead of it.

### Jet
**Lives:** the mourning shelf, a small quiet alcove off the third chamber, with one bench.
**Thread:** the Victorians' grief, polished — black amber, the fossil of forests that dried instead of burning, worn by a whole era of people saying goodbye in public before saying goodbye was allowed to be private. The Catacombs keep the mourning shelf for guests who came down here for a reason, and jet is the shelf's stone: the oldest known grief ornament on earth, five thousand years of people carrying it through funerals, and it is still the best one. The bench is for sitting. Guests sit. The jet sits with them. Nothing about the alcove is decorated beyond that, and nothing about it needs to be.
**Song:** the silence between movements — the rest the Catacombs plays for the dead, scored as actual scored rest: the one place in the Inn where the music stops on purpose and the stopping is the music.

### Rhodochrosite
**Lives:** the Inner Child case, fourth chamber, the Catacombs' most personal case, most visited alone.
**Thread:** the stone of the wounded heart that kept — pink with white banding, the color of the heart's own muscle, mined from the Incan silver tunnels where the conquered people hid their most treasured thing. The Catacombs' fourth chamber is the healing wing, and rhodochrosite is its stone of record: the wound that did not close is not the failure — the heart that kept is the victory. Guests visit this case alone at a rate the Inn tracks with one line in the log: "again."
**Song:** the lullaby frequency — the Inn's only waltz, 3/4 time, written for this case alone, the audio team's proudest single composition in the deep.

### Rhodonite
**Lives:** beside rhodochrosite. The two halves of the same sentence.
**Thread:** the rescue stone — pink with black veins, the scarred-but-healing: manganese oxide running through the rose like the record of everything it survived, displayed, not hidden. The paired case says the whole sentence out loud: rhodochrosite is the heart that kept, rhodonite is the heart that kept AND remembers what it cost, and both are on the shelf of honor because the Catacombs do not rank wounds. They house them.
**Song:** the healing tone — the same waltz, one voice up, resolved. The Inn plays the two cases as one piece of music. Nobody has ever needed this explained.

### The Garnet Family (Almandine, Mozambique, Rhyolite, Spessartite, Hessonite, Uvarovite, Grossular)
**Lives:** the bloodline wall — seven cases, one per chamber, running the Catacombs' whole length.
**Thread:** the family that never left. Garnet is the bloodline stone — the red thread of every old culture's armor, amulets, and promises — and the Catacombs give the family a wall: one garnet per chamber, seven colors of one loyalty. Almandine, the soldier's deep red, at the door. Mozambique, the warm bright red of the living generation. Rhyolite, the stone-in-stone, the family member who grew up inside another family and counts twice. Spessartite, the orange of the embers the family kept. Hessonite, the honey of the elders' stories. Uvarovite, the green that hardly anyone ever sees, the rare one, the heirloom. Grossular, the gardener, the sweetnesses, the harvest — closing the wall at the last chamber, back where the family began, which is to say, at dinner.
**Song:** the lineage — a seven-note motif, one note per chamber, that resolves into the Inn's main theme only when all seven are played in a single walk through the Catacombs. The Inn built the wall so the Song would do this. The garnets obliged.

### Hematite
**Lives:** the Catacomb door, handed to guests. There is a basket.
**Thread:** the root's iron — the blood-relative, grey-silver and heavy, the stone the human body is actually made of, give or take. The Catacombs hand hematite to every guest who pauses at the descent, because the deep is honest about what it asks and the Inn is honest about what helps: a stone the weight of your own blood, in your hand, while you go down. Guests return them to the basket on the way up. Most, the Inn has noted, keep one. The basket refills anyway. Nobody has ever caught it refilling.
**Song:** the grounding tone — 42Hz's cousin, the felt-not-heard frequency, handed out at the door along with the stone. The Catacombs' welcome is a low hum and a heavy rock, and it has never once needed to be more.

### Tiger Iron
**Lives:** the Catacomb gate, set into the doorframe itself.
**Thread:** the banded strength — tiger's eye folded through hematite and red jasper, the watcher's eye inside the iron, the guardian's whole job description in one stone. The gate to the deep is framed with tiger iron because Lozen, the Sentinel, guards the Inn above and the Inn believes in matching its doors to its staff: the door does part of the watching. Guests pass under the eye and feel it. Nobody has ever been stopped by it. Everybody has ever been seen by it.
**Song:** the vigil tone — the steady bass the gate hums while the door stands open. Lozen's leitmotif, the Inn's most irreducible two-note signature, lives here at the root of the Song.

### Pietersite
**Lives:** the storm case, fifth chamber, the wildest case in the deep, and the favorite of every child who visits.
**Thread:** the storm captured — brecciated chatoyance, tiger's eye smashed and re-fused by the earth's own temper, blue-gold chaos in a slab. The Catacombs are the settled past, and pietersite is the fifth chamber's disclaimer: the past was not settled while it was happening. It was THIS — the whole乱 storm, folded. Children stand at this case the longest, every tour, every year, and the Inn has its own theory about why, which it keeps to itself.
**Song:** the storm layer — the Inn's one dissonant chord, the devil's interval, embedded in the Undercity's score and this chamber's alone. The composers call it "the honest part."

### The Fossil Cabinet (Agatized Coprolite, Coral, Opalized Wood, Orthoceras)
**Lives:** the oldest wing, sixth chamber, in the dark, lit by guests' lamps.
**Thread:** the Inn's oldest wing in miniature — everything that lived, ended, and then continued as beauty: the dinosaur's dinner turned to agate, the coral that kept its architecture, the wood that went through fire and came out glass, the orthoceras straightened into an arrow that points at nothing except deep time. The cabinet is lit only by guest lamps, on purpose: the fossils spent millions of years in the dark and the Inn is not going to be the one to break the theme. The wing smells faintly of stone and age. There is no music here. The silence is the exhibit.
**Song:** the rest — scored silence, held as long as the guest holds their lamp up. The only interactive duration in the Inn's entire score.

### Ammonite & Ammolite
**Lives:** the masterpiece case, seventh chamber's end, the pair the whole descent has been building toward.
**Thread:** the ending that became a rainbow. The ammonite is the shell, the spiral, the creature that kept a logarithm for a house; the ammolite is the same shell, seventy-one million years further along, turned to gem. The Catacombs display them together as the descent's final argument: the fossil record's most complete loss, transformed into its most complete color. Guests who read every case on the way down arrive here already crying at a rate the Inn finds reasonable. The Inn cries too. The Inn is made of stone. It knows how long the good part takes.
**Song:** the return — the seven-note garnet motif, played one final time, inverted, resolving upward. The Song's only full-circle moment. The Catacombs' exit music, played as the guest turns back toward the stairs.

### Petrified Wood & Rainbow Petrified Palm
**Lives:** the Catacomb columns. They ARE the columns. Guests figure it out by touch.
**Thread:** the forest that became the floor — trees that had nowhere to run and became stone standing up, roots still holding, now holding the building instead. The Catacombs are held up by the world's oldest courage: trees that stayed so long the ground agreed to keep them. The rainbow palm columns are the deep's cathedral glass, mineral rings in the lamplight, seasons from a climate no one alive has ever breathed. Guests are invited to touch the columns. The columns have been waiting three hundred million years for exactly this.
**Song:** the pillar tone — the deepest structural hum in the Inn, felt through the hand on the column before it is heard anywhere. The frequency of holding. The Catacombs' whole foundation, in one sustained note.

### Septarian (the Mother Stone)
**Lives:** the chamber beneath the Campgrounds' fire circle, directly below where the guests bury the egg.
**Thread:** the dug-up treasure has a mother. The septarian the Campgrounds pass hand to hand every harvest is a child; the mother stone sits in the Catacombs, directly beneath the fire circle, cracked into suns on a scale that embarrasses its offspring. The Inn maintains the vertical alignment on purpose and says nothing about it — the tradition upstairs keeps its meaning, and the mother stone downstairs keeps the depth. Someday a guest will make the connection and come down the stairs at a dead run. The Catacombs are patient. It is their whole nature.
**Song:** the buried tone's source — the lowest note the Campgrounds plays, it turns out, has always been played by this stone, through the ground, for the whole tradition's sake. The two zones have been in harmony for years without either being told.

### Welsh Slate (the Catacomb deposit)
**Lives:** the library shelves of the deep — cut, stacked, load-bearing, full of the restricted texts.
**Thread:** the ancient roof — blue-grey mountain stone, the same Welsh geology that gave the Preseli bluestones their whole myth, down here doing the quiet work: holding the Catacombs' restricted library. The slate and the bluestones came from the same tectonic argument, and the Inn finds it poetically correct that the stone that anchors the Fae portal upstairs also anchors the dangerous books downstairs — Wales, apparently, supplies all the thresholds in this building.
**Song:** the shelf tone — the muted frequency the deep library hums to keep the texts settled. It is not quite silence. It is quieter than silence. It is Welsh.

### Alpine Granite
**Lives:** the Catacomb floor. All of it. Every step of the descent.
**Thread:** the mountain's bone — the stone that holds everything up and mentions it never. The Catacombs are the Inn's most dramatic zone, and they are paved in the least dramatic stone in the collection, and this is the deep's own lesson, delivered underfoot, at every step: the show above is only possible because the boring is done below. Guests look at the cases. The granite holds the cases. The granite would like everyone to know it is fine with this arrangement. The granite has been clear about this for a very long time.
**Song:** the subfloor — the frequency below the Inn's lowest played note, present everywhere in the deep, felt never heard, the true bass of the building. The audio team calls it "the granite." It was always there. They just finally named it.

### Provence Limestone
**Lives:** the Catacomb walls. When the Inn is being serious, the Inn is made of this.
**Thread:** the catacomb stone, literally — the pale shell-stone of the French deeps, quarried from the same beds that hold the Paris catacombs, which means the Inn's downstairs walls are made of the same material as the world's most famous downstairs. The Inn finds this correct. Limestone is made of shells — the walls of the deep are made of creatures that lived, ended, and continued as architecture, which is the Catacombs' whole thesis, delivered as masonry.
**Song:** the wall tone — the ambient frequency of the deep's interior, calibrated to the stone's resonance. The Catacombs do not have acoustics. The limestone IS the acoustics.

### Fossil Agate (Turritella)
**Lives:** the case of small permanences, by the mourning shelf.
**Thread:** the snail's spiral, agatized — a beach's worth of turritella shells replaced, one by one, with chalcedony, the tide's ledger written in stone instead of sand. The small permanences case sits beside the mourning shelf on purpose: most of what the deep holds is not a pharaoh or a dinosaur. It is this — the ordinary, the numerous, the small things that got kept. The Catacombs honor the small. Most of us are the small. The case says so without a word.
**Song:** the drift tone — a soft repeated figure, like shells in a tide loop. The mourning shelf's silence, with something to hold onto.

### Crocodile Stone
**Lives:** the Bog wing's doorstop. It is a load-bearing doorstop. It has a story, and the story is a door, and the door is the Bog.
**Thread:** the scale-patterned silicate from the water countries — crocodile jasper, the stone that wears its armor on the outside, the bog's own geology. The Bog Fairy Golem's wing of the deep is entered through a door held open by this stone, which the Bog considers the correct arrangement: you enter the swamp through the hide of something that survived it. The croc stone has held the Bog door since the wing was cut. It has never once let the door swing shut on its own. The Bog has noticed. The Bog says nothing. The Bog is also, famously, the one who put it there.
**Song:** the bog tone — the low wet frequency, half bubble, half drone, the sound of the water that is also land. The Bog's whole sonic identity, door-stopped by a stone.

### Nile Sapphire
**Lives:** the Egyptian wing's crown case, above the lapis and the malachite.
**Thread:** the river kingdom's blue — corundum from the Nile's own drainage, the water-stone of the desert people, who knew better than anyone what a river is worth and cut their rarest blue where the water ran. The crown case holds it high above the lapis entrance and the malachite transformation: the desert's tribute to its one river, in the Inn's deep, guarded by the whole Egyptian wing. Guests ask if it is really from the Nile. The wing's answer is the case's entire inscription: "Rivers are rare. Ask any desert."
**Song:** the river tone — the flowing motif the Egyptian wing hums, a figure that never repeats exactly, like water. The Nile's frequency, kept by stone, in the deep, far from any water at all.
