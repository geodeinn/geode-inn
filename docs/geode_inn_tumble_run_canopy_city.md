# Geode Inn — Tumble Run: Canopy City (The Evil Version)
## Shadow Run DLC — The Ascension Canopy
## The Darkest Version of the Inn's Smallest Game

---

## The Concept

Canopy City is the Shadow Run DLC set in the Ascension Canopy — the top of the tree, the Owl's home, where the network is complete and the brownies know what they are. It is the evil mirror of Tumble Run. Same controls. Same core loop. Everything else is wrong.

The 42Hz hum is gone. The 84Hz drone replaces it — felt more than heard, a vibration in the chest, the frequency that means "stay." The warm amber background is gone. The canopy is cold indigo and dead silver. The obstacles are the same resonance nodes, but corrupted — the Song's nodes running in reverse, pulling Tumble not toward home but toward the network.

The comedy is gone. The brownies don't rescue Tumble. The Owl does.

---

## What Changes

### The Hum
- **Regular Tumble Run:** 42Hz hum (the Song of Making, warm, barely audible)
- **Canopy City:** 84Hz drone (the Shadow frequency, cold, felt in the body, the "stay/petrify" inverse)
- The drone is continuous, like the hum, but it doesn't feel like a soundtrack. It feels like a pulse. Like something alive and patient.
- The drone intensifies as speed increases — not louder, DEEPER. The player should feel it in their hands if playing on a device with haptics.

### Tumble's Eyes
The most important change. In the regular game, Tumble's eyes are:
- Wide and startled (rolling)
- Looking up (jumping)
- Squeezed shut (ducking)
- Blinking slowly (stuck)

In Canopy City, Tumble's eyes are:
- Open. Always open. No blinking. No squeezing shut. No startled "eep."
- The eyes are the same enormous dark circles, but the expression is different — not fear, not surprise. Awareness. Tumble sees the canopy. Tumble sees the Owl. Tumble knows.
- When approaching an obstacle: the eyes don't widen. They NARROW. Tumble isn't drawn toward the nodes anymore. Tumble is trying to avoid them. The player and Tumble are finally on the same side — both trying not to get stuck. And it's harder.
- Near-miss: no blue spark. A cold white flash, like a camera flash. The Owl's network logging a missed capture.
- The "eep" is gone. Tumble doesn't make sound in Canopy City. The only sounds are the drone, the obstacles, and the Owl.

### The Obstacles (Corrupted Nodes)
Same 8 obstacles, but transformed. Each is the Shadow version of its regular counterpart:

1. **The Teapot → The Cracked Teapot** — The Keeper's teapot, but cracked, dark, cold to the touch. No steam. The spout is chipped. Jump to clear. Stuck: Tumble wedges in the crack, eyes staring out from the break. The crack is the only light.

2. **The Open Book → The Burned Book** — A book with scorched pages, still smoking faintly. The text is illegible — the words are the Owl's network code, not the archive's knowledge. Duck under. Stuck: Tumble between burned pages, eyes reflecting the smoke.

3. **The Amethyst Cluster → The Void Geode** — Purple crystals, but hollow. The geodes are empty inside — the crystals were harvested, the nodes drained. Jump to clear. Stuck: Tumble in an empty geode, the crystal shell around it like a cage.

4. **The Brass Orrery Ring → The Broken Ring** — A brass orbital ring, snapped. One half orbits, the other half hangs still. Duck under. Stuck: Tumble inside the broken ring, orbiting the break point endlessly.

5. **The Crystal Pinball Bumper → The Dead Bumper** — A pinball bumper that doesn't bounce. Cold, dark, silent. The resonance is gone — the node is dead. Jump to clear. Stuck: Tumble wedged between dead bumpers, no hum, no vibration, just silence.

6. **The Fae Portal Sliver → The Closed Portal** — A portal that's shutting. The green shimmer is grey-white, flickering, collapsing. Duck under. Stuck: Tumble caught in a closing portal, being crushed between dimensions that are sealing.

7. **The Crystal Leaf → The Dead Leaf** — A Singing Tree leaf, but brown, dry, silent. No sway. No song. Duck under. Stuck: Tumble in dead leaves, dry and crumbling, the tree above it bare.

8. **The Tankard → The Empty Tankard** — The Bard's tankard, but empty. No drink, no warmth, no Bard. Just cold metal. Jump to clear. Stuck: Tumble in the empty tankard, eyes looking up at nothing.

### The Background (Canopy City)
- **Far layer:** Dead branches of the great tree, bare, reaching upward like skeletal hands. No leaves. No stars. Just the canopy's architecture, exposed.
- **Mid layer:** The Owl's network — visible as thin silver lines connecting nodes across the canopy. The wiring the brownies installed, now visible, now glowing faintly. This is what the network looks like from the inside.
- **Near layer:** The canopy floor — dark wood, cold stone, no warmth. The floor has grooves where the wires run.
- **Sky:** Not sky. The void. Deep indigo to black. No stars. No aurora. Just the dark above the canopy, and in it, very far away, two points of gold light that don't move. The Owl's eyes. Always visible. Always watching.
- **Celtic border:** Still present, but the gold is tarnished. The pulse is slower — 5 seconds instead of 3. The knotwork is the same, but the warmth is gone.

### Speed and Difficulty
- Canopy City starts at a higher base speed (1.5x the regular game's starting speed)
- Speed increases faster — +0.15x every 12 seconds instead of +0.1x every 15 seconds
- The speed cap is 3.5x (higher than the regular game's 3.0x)
- Obstacles spawn tighter — the minimum spacing is 20% less than the regular game
- This is HARD. Intentionally hard. Canopy City is the punishment for the players who wanted more.

## The Game Over (The Evil Version)

### The Full Sequence (8-10 seconds, NOT skippable)

In the regular game, the game over is warm comedy — the brownies run in, Brindle schemes, Cobble calculates, Thatch extracts, "pop," Tumble bounces, retry.

In Canopy City, the game over is:

1. **Impact (1s):** Tumble hits the obstacle. The 84Hz drone cuts out. Complete silence. No "thump." No "eep." Just the silence of something that expected this.

2. **The Stuck (2s):** Tumble is wedged in the corrupted obstacle. Eyes open. Not blinking. Not wiggling. Not trying to get free. Just... stuck. Waiting. The obstacle and Tumble are perfectly still. The two gold lights in the sky (the Owl's eyes) get slightly brighter.

3. **The Descent (3s):** A shadow falls over the screen from above. Not the brownies. Wings. Large, silent, barn owl wings. The shadow covers the entire play area. The temperature of the screen shifts — the cold indigo gets colder. A single feather drifts down from the top of the screen. Then another. Silent. Slow. The player sees the Owl's silhouette descending — not landing, just arriving, filling the background like a moon rising.

4. **The Extraction (1s):** One talon extends from the silhouette. It reaches Tumble. It doesn't grab — it LIFTS. Gently. Precisely. The way you lift a tool you're going to use again. Tumble comes free from the obstacle with no sound. No "pop." No bounce. Just... removed. Placed back at the starting position. The talon retracts. The shadow lifts. The two gold eyes rise back to the sky.

5. **Score Display (persistent):**
   - Large text: "Tumble traveled [X] inches before the Owl retrieved it."
   - Not "stuck." "Retrieved." The word change is the tell.
   - Below: "Best: [Y] inches"
   - Below: "Near-misses: [N] | Captures avoided: [C]"
   - Below, in smaller text, only in Canopy City: "The Owl puts its tools back on the shelf."
   - Two buttons: "Send Tumble back in" (retry) and "Let Tumble rest" (menu)

### The "Let Tumble Rest" Button
In the regular game, "Let Tumble Rest" returns to the menu. In Canopy City, "Let Tumble Rest" does something different: the screen fades to the canopy at night. Tumble is sitting on the canopy floor, alone, in the dark, with the two gold eyes watching from above. The 84Hz drone is very quiet. After 5 seconds, text appears:

> "Tumble rested. The Owl waited. Tumble went back in."

And the retry button appears. There is no escape from Canopy City. "Let Tumble rest" doesn't let Tumble rest. It just delays.

### NOT Skippable
The game over sequence in Canopy City cannot be skipped. The player must watch the Owl descend. The player must watch Tumble get retrieved. Every time. This is not a punishment — it's the point. The player chose to play the evil version. The evil version makes you watch.

## The Encounters (Canopy City Exclusive)

### Random Encounters (Rare, Unsettling)
Every 500 inches, 15% chance (higher than regular game's 10%). Encounters in Canopy City are not warm or funny. They are quiet, cold, and wrong.

1. **The Brownies' Silence (500+ inches):** The three brownies appear in the background — Brindle, Cobble, Thatch. But they're not running alongside Tumble. They're standing still. Facing away. Coconut shells at their sides. Not clapping. Not arguing. Not moving. They stand for 3 seconds, then walk off-screen left without looking back. Thatch pauses for one frame — almost turns — then doesn't.

2. **The Owl's Gaze (1000+ inches):** The two gold lights in the sky get brighter. Closer. For 3 seconds, the Owl is not a silhouette — it's visible. A full barn owl, perched on a dead branch, looking directly at the player. Not at Tumble. At the PLAYER. The screen darkens except for the Owl's face. Then it recedes. The gold eyes return to the distance.

3. **The Network Pulse (1500+ inches):** The silver lines in the mid-layer background — the Owl's network — all pulse simultaneously. A single 84Hz wave rolls across the screen, visible as a ripple in the air. Every obstacle on screen flickers. Tumble's eyes narrow further. The network is active. It's logging everything.

4. **The Constant's Shadow (2000+ inches):** A tall figure in the far background — the Constant, standing at the edge of the canopy, facing the void. Not moving. The starlight crystal is dark — not glowing, not resonating. The Constant doesn't turn. Doesn't acknowledge Tumble. Just stands there, facing away from everything. After 3 seconds, the figure is gone. The space where it stood is slightly darker than the rest.

5. **The Empty Perch (2500+ inches):** The Owl's perch — the hollow where the Owl sits — appears in the background. It's empty. The Owl is not there. But the two gold eyes are still in the sky. The Owl left its perch but its eyes didn't move. Where did the Owl go? The question doesn't get answered. The perch sits empty for 3 seconds, then scrolls past.

6. **The Dead Song (3000+ inches):** The 84Hz drone stops for 3 seconds. Complete silence. Tumble rolls through the silence. No obstacles spawn. Just silence and the dead canopy. Then the drone returns, but slightly different — slightly lower, slightly colder, as if the frequency shifted. The Song is degrading. The Shadow frequency is replacing it.

7. **The Crystal Cracks (4000+ inches):** Tumble's fur develops hairline cracks — visible fractures in the crystal underneath. Not the full transparency of the regular game's Crystal Reveal. Just cracks. Stress fractures. The crystal is breaking. Tumble's eyes don't change. Tumble keeps rolling. The player notices. The player can't do anything.

8. **The Retrieval (5000+ inches):** The Owl descends mid-run. Not as a game over — as an encounter. The shadow falls. The wings spread. The talon reaches down. Tumble stops rolling. The talon hovers over Tumble for 2 seconds. Then... it withdraws. The Owl ascends. The shadow lifts. Tumble resumes rolling. The Owl chose not to retrieve Tumble. Not yet. This time. The message: the Owl could end this run whenever it wants. It lets Tumble continue. Why?

## Tumble Skins (Canopy City Exclusive)

Three skins only available in the Canopy City version. They cannot be used in the regular game — they exist only in the shadow.

1. **Shadow Tumble** — Dark charcoal fur, blue-white spark on roll. The default Canopy City skin. Unlocked by: purchasing the Shadow Run DLC.

2. **Retrieved Tumble** — The same brown/amber fur as classic Tumble, but with a single white talon mark across the back. A scratch. Not deep. Not painful. A claim. Unlocked by: getting retrieved by the Owl 50 times (50 game overs in Canopy City).

3. **The Owl's Tumble** — Pure white fur, silver eyes (not dark), no warmth. Tumble looks like an extension of the Owl — the same color, the same cold. The most disturbing skin because it looks RIGHT. Tumble in the Owl's colors looks like it was always supposed to look this way. Unlocked by: reaching 5000 inches in Canopy City.

## Design Rules for Canopy City

1. **No comedy.** Canopy City is the one place in the Geode Inn where the joke is gone. The brownies don't come. The coconut shells are silent. The "eep" is gone. The extraction is not funny.
2. **The Owl is not the villain.** The Owl retrieves Tumble because Tumble is the Owl's instrument. The Owl is not cruel. The Owl is proprietary. There's a difference, and the difference is worse.
3. **Tumble is still innocent.** Tumble's eyes narrow, Tumble tries to avoid the nodes, but Tumble is still the same creature. The innocence isn't gone — it's being used. That's the horror.
4. **The game over is not skippable.** The player must watch the Owl descend. Every time. The repetition is the point — the player choosing to send Tumble back in, knowing what happens, is the thesis.
5. **"Let Tumble Rest" is a lie.** The button exists. It doesn't work. Tumble goes back in. There is no escape from Canopy City. The only way out is to close the game.
6. **The 84Hz drone never stops.** It's the only sound. It replaces the 42Hz hum. It replaces the music. It replaces the silence. The drone is the Shadow frequency, and in Canopy City, the Shadow frequency is all there is.
7. **The two gold eyes are always in the sky.** From the first frame to the last. The Owl is always watching. The player can never not see them. They don't move. They don't blink. They just watch.
8. **The brownies appear once, and they don't help.** The "Brownies' Silence" encounter is the only time the brownies appear in Canopy City. They stand. They face away. They leave. The player who remembers the regular game — where the brownies ran in with love and comedy to save Tumble — feels the absence. The brownies know what they are in the Canopy. They know Tumble is the Owl's tool. They don't rescue the Owl's property.

## The Question

Canopy City asks the player one question, never stated, never answered:

**Why are you still playing?**

The regular game asks it gently — "Let Tumble Rest" is a real option, a real kindness. Canopy City asks it by making "Let Tumble Rest" a lie. The player presses it. Tumble rests for 5 seconds. Then Tumble goes back in. The player presses it again. Same result. The only way to stop is to close the game.

The achievement "Why Are You Still Playing?" (30 total minutes in Canopy City) is the game acknowledging the question. The player who earns it has been sending Tumble back into the Owl's canopy for half an hour, watching the retrieval every time, pressing the button that doesn't work, and not closing the game.

Why are you still playing?

Because the score is there. Because the leaderboard is there. Because 100 more inches might trigger a new encounter. Because the skins aren't all unlocked. Because the game is fun, even when it's awful.

That's the answer. And the answer is the horror.
