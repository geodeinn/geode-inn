# THE DOLLKEEPER'S HORROR REGISTER — Speter Adams's Dolls, Creepy Canon
**Status: PROPOSAL — drafted on creative license (Krista, 2026-10-01: "Whatever you'd like to do with this :)"), all rulings Krista's to confirm or veto.**

## Origin
Krista named dolls one of the very few things she finds truly creepy, with the Valley of the Dolls association close behind. This spec leans into that. The house laws hold throughout: creepy, never cruel. Whimsical-macabre. No-fail. The dolls are the same dolls canon already holds — soft, detailed, lovingly rendered — the register below is about what they DO, not what they look like.

## Honest Label — Valley of the Dolls
Jacqueline Susann, 1966. The "dolls" of the title are not porcelain. They are pills — barbiturate downers (Seconal and company), the drugs that hollowed out three women while the culture called it coping. The book's horror lives in the medicine cabinet, not on the mantel.

The resonance, stated plainly: the realest "dolls" in Krista's own childhood were phenobarbital — the same pharmaceutical family Susann was warning about, prescribed without pediatric data. The Inn's doll canon is the anti-Valley. Speter's dolls are homes for spirits that got no body; the Valley's dolls were doses for children who had one. A doll is what you give the unhoused. That's the whole inversion, and it is why this register belongs in the house.

Susann herself, honestly labeled: a phenomenon, not a craftsman's object. She wrote it in about twenty months and said plainly she was after bestseller mechanics, not literature. The book is an anti-Skinner specimen — the pharmaceutical hook dressed as comfort — filed on the shelf for its subject, not its sentences.

## The Creepy Canon (proposed)

### 1. THE FACING
The dolls never move while watched. Ever. But their positions are never quite what you remember — turned a few degrees, shifted along the shelf, facing a new direction between visits. Never toward the door as threat. Toward whoever just arrived. No jump-scares, no danger (no-fail law intact): the long shiver of "it was facing the window before." Watching is how the unhoused hold on to company. The doll is saying hello.

### 2. THE POLITE DEAD
Each doll houses a spirit (existing canon: the ones who never picked a side). The creepy accent: the spirits are courteous. A player who sits near a doll long enough starts to feel listened to — Bramble's silence, rendered in porcelain. The dolls of a zone go quiet when someone is grieving, then slowly, over days, turn toward the mourner. The Inn's empathy mechanic wearing a face.

### 3. THE CATALOGUE OF KEPT
Speter's doll-collection quest (existing canon: find every doll, unlock the real face) gains its payoff image: the moment the last doll is found, every doll in every zone is facing the player. Not menace. Recognition. "Now they know you." You went to every home; the Kept turn to acknowledge the visitor who bothered to meet everyone. Creepy gratitude is the register — the shiver and the warmth arrive together, which is this house's signature move.

### 4. THE CATACOMBS SHELF (The Ragana's Discomfort)
The strongest version lives here. Oldest dolls (existing canon), worn paint, spirits waiting the longest. Proposed: every Catacombs doll faces the same direction — the direction of the great door. They have been waiting, in the oldest way there is, for a knock. The Catacombs wing is already the house's creepy wing (plague-mask Doorkeeper, skeleton key, Cast in Bronze carillon); the shelf makes it a suite.

### 5. THE RAGANA WRINKLE
The Ragana measures everything; the dolls cannot be measured. The Ragana's archive has one shelf Speter's dolls have never touched — the Ragana moved in to occupy it first. Whether that's a boundary or an invitation is the slow-burn question, per the existing "not unwelcome" arc.

### 6. QUIRE THREAD (parked, ungated)
The Lovecraftian scholar reads in the margins of restricted Archive texts. A margin note on the practice of housing spirits — the ethics of giving the dead a place to sit — is a natural restricted-shelf document. Not opened. Parked for a future session.
