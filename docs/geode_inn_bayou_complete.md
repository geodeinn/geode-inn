## THE CENTRAL NPC: THE KEEPER

### Mama Coumine

The Bayou's central NPC is **Mama Coumine** — the swamp's keeper, the colonial structure's caretaker, and the door's guardian. She is not a young woman. She is not an old woman. She is a woman who has been in the swamp long enough that the swamp is in her.

**Name origin:** Coumine is a corruption of "coumarine" — the sweetgrass that grows at the bayou's edge, the grass used in Louisiana Native American basket-weaving. The name connects her to the land, the indigenous craft, and the sweet smell that hangs in the bayou's humid air. "Mama" is the honorific — not a family title, but the Louisiana term of respect for a woman who holds a community together.

**Appearance:** Mama Coumine is mixed-race — the Louisiana Creole heritage, the blend that the bayou itself represents (land and water, European and African and Indigenous, the biological and the built). She wears a tignon (the head wrap that Creole women were required by law to wear in Spanish colonial New Orleans — the restriction that became a fashion, the oppression that became a statement). Her clothes are swamp-colored — grey-green, brown, the palette of the bayou. Her hands are stained with clay — the same clay the player crafts with in the survival game. She carries a walking stick made of cypress knee — the same wood the swamp is made of.

**Voice:** Mama Coumine speaks with a Louisiana Creole accent — French-inflected English, the accent of the bayou, the accent of the Mississippi Gulf Coast. Her speech is SLOW — the pace of the swamp, the pace of biological time. She doesn't rush. She doesn't repeat. She says a thing once, and the thing sits in the humid air the way Spanish moss hangs from a cypress — present, swaying, not going anywhere.

**Role:** Mama Coumine is the keeper of the colonial structure — the church/mission that the swamp is consuming. She lives in the structure. She keeps the structure standing (barely — the structure is losing the battle with the swamp, and Mama Coumine is not fighting the battle, she is WITNESSING it). She is also the guardian of the basement door — the door that leads from the colonial structure to the buried cities.

**Mama Coumine's truth:** Mama Coumine is not indigenous. Mama Coumine is the colonial layer's last resident. She is the one who STAYED — the one who didn't leave when the swamp took the structure, the one who didn't abandon the building when the water rose. She stayed because someone had to stay. Someone had to keep the door. Someone had to make sure the door to the buried cities wasn't lost under the kudzu.

Mama Coumine's thesis: the colonial layer was built ON TOP of something older. The colonial builders didn't know about the buried cities — they built on the land because the land was high enough. The door in the basement was there before the church. The church was built above the door. Mama Coumine discovered the door. Mama Coumine chose to stay and guard it. Not because the colonial structure is worth guarding — the structure is colonial, it's an overlay, it's 300 years of a much older story. But the DOOR is worth guarding. The door is the access point to the 1,500-year-old cities. The door is the Inn's refusal to let the colonial overlay erase the pre-colonial truth.

**Mama Coumine is the Geode Inn's position on colonialism made into a character:** she is not the colonizer. She is not the colonized. She is the one who inherited the colonial structure and chose to use it as a door — to hold the colonial building open long enough for the traveler to find what was there before. The colonial structure is not condemned. The colonial structure is REPURPOSED — the way the Inn repurposes everything. The structure becomes the door's frame. The colonial layer becomes the ACCESS LAYER to the pre-colonial truth.

### Dialogue Style

Mama Coumine speaks in proverbs and observations. She doesn't give orders. She doesn't give quests in the traditional sense. She SAYS things, and the things she says are the quests — the traveler hears them and knows what to do.

> "The swamp don't hide nothing. The swamp just... grows over it. [She adjusts the tignon.] People say the swamp is hiding the cities. No. The swamp is doing what swamps do. Swamp grows. Cities stay. Swamp covers cities. That's not hiding. That's... time."
> 
> "This church? This church is 300 years old. [She puts her hand on the wall, and the wall gives slightly — the wood is soft with moisture.] The cities under this church are 1,500 years old. The jungle under the cities is 60 million years old. [She looks at the traveler.] I'm the youngest thing here."
> 
> "You want to go down? [She gestures at the basement door — cypress wood, warped with moisture, the handle green with patina.] I'm not stopping you. I'm not helping you. I'm... holding the door. That's what I do. I hold the door. What you find down there... [She pauses. The Spanish moss sways.] ...that's between you and them."

### Mama Coumine's Connection to the Inn

Mama Coumine is the Bayou's version of the Firekeeper — the one who stays, the one who keeps. The Firekeeper keeps the fire. Mama Coumine keeps the door. Both keepers chose to stay in a place that is losing its battle with something larger (the fire loses its battle with silence; the structure loses its battle with the swamp). Both keepers find: adequate.

Mama Coumine is also the zone's FREQUENCY ANCHOR — the one person in the bayou whose personal frequency is STABLE enough to navigate. The Spanish moss sways with every frequency shift. The water moves with every tide. The structure settles with every season. Mama Coumine is the one thing that doesn't shift. The traveler who is disoriented by the swamp's flatness and the moss's movement can orient on Mama Coumine — she is the bayou's compass.

## THE QUEST CHAIN: "What Was There Before"

### Quest 1: "The Reclamation" (Survival Game)
**Location:** The Swamp (surface layer)
**Giver:** None — the quest begins when the traveler enters the bayou

The traveler enters the bayou from the world map. The bayou is flat, humid, disorienting. The traveler must survive — build shelter, maintain sanity, navigate by the moss — until the colonial structure is visible through the cypress canopy.

**Objective:** Survive 7 in-game days in the swamp. Craft a lantern (cypress wood + swamp gas bioluminescence), build a raised shelter, and navigate to the colonial structure.

**Reward:** Anthracite stone. Access to the colonial structure. Mama Coumine's acknowledgment.

### Quest 2: "The Keeper's Door"
**Location:** The Colonial Gothic Structure
**Giver:** Mama Coumine

The traveler reaches the structure. Mama Coumine is at the door — not blocking it, standing beside it. The structure is half-consumed by the swamp: the steeple has cypress trees growing through it, the stained glass is broken and replaced with Spanish moss curtains, the pews are rotting, the altar has a cypress knee growing through it.

**Dialogue:**
> "You survived the swamp. Good. That means you can be patient. [She opens the door — the structure's front door, not the basement door.] Come in. Sit. [She gestures at a pew that is only half-rotted.] This church was built 300 years ago by people who thought they were building on solid ground. [She sits across from the traveler.] They were. The ground IS solid. It's just... the solid ground is on top of something else."
> 
> "The door in the basement — I found it 40 years ago. The floor gave way. The floor was always going to give way. The colonial builders poured their foundation on top of a chamber, and the chamber's ceiling was thinner than they knew. [She stands.] I've been holding the door ever since. Not locked. Not hidden. HELD. The door is a threshold, and thresholds need keepers."
> 
> "Before I open it — you should know what's down there. Not what you'll FIND. What's THERE. What was there before the church, before the colony, before the colonial name for this land. [She adjusts the tignon.] The cities down there are old. Not old like the catacombs. Old like the ROOTS of the catacombs. The people who built those cities built them when the swamp was a savanna. The climate changed. The water rose. The jungle grew. The cities stayed. The cities didn't fall — the cities were COVERED. The way a garden covers a path. The way moss covers a stone. Not destroyed. Grown over."
> 
> "Go down. But go slow. The cities are patient. The cities have been waiting 1,500 years. They can wait for you to take your time."

**Objective:** Enter the basement. Open the door. Descend to the buried cities.

### Quest 3: "The Buried Cities"
**Location:** The Buried Cities (deep layer)
**Giver:** None — the cities speak for themselves

The traveler descends through the basement door and enters the buried cities. The cities are not ruins — they are INTACT. The jungle grew OVER them, not THROUGH them. The cities are sealed — preserved by the jungle's canopy the way a bog preserves what falls into it.

**The cities are three:**

**City 1: The Mound City** — the oldest. A city built around and on top of a great earthwork mound — the type built by the Mississippian culture (the mounds at Poverty Point, Cahokia, Etowah). The mound is the city's center, and the mound is STILL STANDING — 1,500 years of jungle growth couldn't topple a structure made of earth. The mound is the oldest architecture the traveler has seen in the Inn — older than the pyramids, older than the ziggurats, older than the stone circles. The mound is EARTH. The mound is the simplest possible architecture: a pile of dirt, shaped by human hands, surviving because it is the most adequate structure ever built.

The Mound City's NPC: **The Mound-keeper** — a figure made of packed earth, the color of the bayou clay, standing at the mound's base. The Mound-keeper doesn't speak. The Mound-keeper HUMS — at 42Hz, the Song, carried by the earth. The mound is a resonance chamber — the earth amplifies the 42Hz the way the Resonance Engine amplifies it in the Hollow Earth. The mound is the Hollow Earth's SURFACE EXPRESSION — the same frequency, surfacing through the earth, the way a spring surfaces through the ground. The Mound-keeper is the mound's frequency made into a figure — not a ghost, not an Echo, but a presence. The way the Inn has a presence. The mound has a presence.

**City 2: The River City** — the middle city. A city built along a river that no longer exists on the surface (the river was the ancient course of the Mississippi, before the river changed its path). The city's buildings are arranged along the riverbed — dry now, the river gone, but the buildings still FACE the river, the way riverside buildings always face the water. The River City's architecture is WOODEN — not stone, not earth. The wood is PETRIFIED — 1,500 years of mineral-rich water seeping through the wood turned the wood to stone. The petrified wooden city is the zone's thesis on transformation: the biological (wood) becoming the geological (stone) through the medium of time and water.

The River City's NPC: **The Ferryman** — not Charon (Charon is in the catacombs). This is an older ferryman — the one who ferried souls across the ancient river, the river that no longer exists. The Ferryman stands at the dry riverbed with a boat that is also petrified. The Ferryman is the pre-colonial Charon — the same archetype, 1,500 years earlier, the same function: carrying the dead across the water. The Ferryman doesn't charge obols. The Ferryman charges: a story. The traveler must tell the Ferryman a story — any story, a true story from their journey. The Ferryman listens. The Ferryman nods. The Ferryman steps aside.

**City 3: The Circle City** — the deepest city. A city built around a stone circle — not a European stone circle (not Stonehenge, not Avebury), but a NORTH AMERICAN stone circle. The stones are unmarked, uncarved, unhewn — natural stones placed in a circle by human hands. The circle is the city's center, and the city's buildings radiate outward from the circle the way the Inn's zones radiate from the main floor. The Circle City is the oldest expression of the Inn's architecture — the circular plan, the central gathering point, the stones as the anchor. The Circle City is the Inn's AMERICAN root — the proof that the Inn's architecture (tree, circle, center, radiating zones) was being built on this continent 1,500 years before the Inn existed.

The Circle City's NPC: **The Stone-singer** — a figure at the circle's center, hands on the largest stone. The Stone-singer is singing the 42Hz into the stone — the way Mona hums into the poplar, the way the gargoyles resonate at their frequencies. The Stone-singer is the bayou's version of Mona — the mother creation principle, expressed in a different medium (stone, not wood; earth, not paint). The Stone-singer's song is: the same song. The 42Hz. The Song. The mother, in a different form, in a different land, in a different millennium.

**Objective:** Visit all three cities. At each city, touch the central element (the mound, the petrified boat, the circle's largest stone). Each touch activates a 42Hz resonance in the buried city — the cities WAKE UP, the way the Hollow Earth wakes up when the crystal eye is placed.

**Reward:** The Bayou Stone — a piece of the Circle City's largest stone, small enough to carry. The stone hums at 42Hz, but the hum is DIFFERENT — it's the 42Hz filtered through earth and water and biological time, the way the Spanish moss filters the airborne Song. The Bayou Stone's 42Hz is: the murmur frequency. The quietest 42Hz in the Inn. The stone that says: the Song doesn't have to be loud to be present.

### Quest 4: "What Was There Before"
**Location:** All three layers — return to the colonial structure
**Giver:** Mama Coumine

The traveler returns to the colonial structure with the Bayou Stone. Mama Coumine is at the basement door.

**Dialogue:**
> "You found them. [She looks at the Bayou Stone.] The cities. The circle. The song in the stone."
> 
> "Now you know what was there before. Before the colony. Before the church. Before the name on the map. There was a mound, and a river, and a circle. And the people who built them were singing the same Song you've been carrying since you walked through the Inn's front door."
> 
> "The Song isn't European. The Song isn't African. The Song isn't Indigenous. The Song isn't anyone's. The Song is: the Song. The mother hummed it in the poplar. The stone-singer hummed it in the circle. The mound-keeper hummed it in the earth. Different voices. Same frequency. [She touches the Bayou Stone.] 42Hz. The Adequate."
> 
> "The colonial structure — this church — it's not the truth. The colonial structure is the DOOR. The door to the truth. I've been keeping the door because someone had to keep it, and because keeping is what I know how to do. [She adjusts the tignon.] But the door is not the truth. The cities are the truth. The circle is the truth. The song in the stone is the truth."
> 
> "Go. Take the Bayou Stone back to the Inn. The Crone will want to measure it. The Astronomer will want to calculate its age. The Firekeeper will want to hold it near the fire. [She pauses.] But the stone doesn't belong to any of them. The stone belongs to the swamp. The stone belongs to the circle. The stone belongs to what was there before."
> 
> "And what was there before... [She looks at the basement door, then at the swamp beyond the structure's broken walls.] ...is still here. Under the kudzu. Under the water. Under the name. Still singing."

**Reward:** The Bayou Stone (key item). Mama Coumine's Keeping (passive buff — the traveler's sanity drains 20% slower in all zones, because the Bayou's biological patience stays with the traveler). Access to the Circle City's stone circle as a fast-travel node (the circle connects to the Fae Kingdom's stone circles via the ley line network).
