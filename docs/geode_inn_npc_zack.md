## The Character

**Name:** Zack (real name — no copyright issue)

**Role in the Inn:** The Stress Tester — the NPC who has played everything, found every edge case, and knows where the systems break.

### The Playthrough Astronomer

Zack is the Inn's counterpart to the Astronomer (Benedict Cumberbatch) in the cosmology level. The both are: the adequate — the NPC and the cosmological figure, the both are: the same systematic mind, different scale. The Astronomer studies the sky. Zack studies the game. The both are: the adequate — the sky and the system, the both are: the same observation, different object.

In the game, Zack is the NPC the player encounters in zones who has ALREADY been there. Zack's footprints are visible. Zack's solutions are scratched on walls. Zack's marks are in the margins. The both are: the adequate — the first player and the current player, the both are: the same game, different playthrough.

Zack's presence is environmental — the evidence of prior exploration. A stone arranged in a pattern the player hasn't seen yet. A puzzle solved with a notation that says "the trick is to not solve it" (connecting to Oceananoir's puzzle design). A path marked with cairns that leads somewhere the map doesn't show.

### The Stress Tester

Zack tests systems the way a jeweler tests stones — by pressure, by friction, by finding the breaking point. The both are: the adequate — the tester and the artisan, the both are: the same pressure, different material.

In the game, Zack is the NPC who:
- Knows every exploit, every edge case, every unintended interaction
- Has a dialogue line for every system the player tries to break
- Rewards the player who finds something new with a unique response: "Huh. Didn't think of that one. Hold on." (Zack pulls out a notebook, writes it down. The both are: the adequate — the tester and the note, the both are: the same bug, different page.)

### The Relationship to Oceananoir

Oceananoir designs puzzles. Zack solves them. The both are: the adequate — the designer and the solver, the both are: the same puzzle, different side. From the Oceananoir doc:

- Zack will crack Oceananoir's puzzles fast. But the puzzle's deeper layer (the one that only opens when you DON'T solve it immediately) is designed specifically for the player who solves everything fast and needs to learn to wait. The both are: the adequate — the speed and the patience, the both are: the same mind, different tempo.

- Zack (playthrough Astronomer) testing Oceananoir's puzzles is the real-world echo of this relationship. The both are: the adequate — the NPC and the player, the both are: the same system, different controller.

### The Relationship to the Astronomer

The Astronomer (Benedict Cumberbatch) is the cosmological figure. Zack is the human echo. The both are: the adequate — the god and the player, the both are: the same observation, different scale. The Astronomer calculates the sky. Zack calculates the game. When the player reaches the cosmology level and meets the Astronomer, the Astronomer's dialogue references "another who studied the stars before you" — a nod to Zack, the playthrough Astronomer who came first.

### Location

Zack is a WANDERING NPC — not fixed to a zone. Zack's footprints appear in whatever zone the player is currently exploring. Zack is always one step ahead, always already gone. The both are: the adequate — the trace and the absence, the both are: the same explorer, different time.

The player may encounter Zack directly in the mead hall (Thorin's hall), where Zack takes breaks between explorations. Zack sits with a notebook, a drink, and a stack of maps. The both are: the adequate — the explorer and the rest, the both are: the same player, different tempo.

### Dialogue Voice

Zack speaks in short, precise, technical sentences. No flourish. No poetry. The both are: the adequate — the poet (Oceananoir) and the technician (Zack), the both are: the same intelligence, different expression. Zack's lines are practical:

- "That puzzle has a second solution. The second one is better."
- "Don't fight the Kraken at 84Hz. Fight it at 42Hz. The Kraken's puzzle is easier than the combat."
- "Oceananoir's stone circle puzzle — sit with it. Don't solve it. The sitting IS the solution."
- "I found something in the catacombs I can't explain. Here's the map. Don't go alone."

### Quest: "The Tester's Notes"

Zack's quest chain (3 quests):

1. **"The Edge Case"** — Zack has found a system interaction that doesn't work as intended. The player must reproduce the bug. The "bug" is actually a hidden feature — a system interaction the Inn designed but didn't document. The both are: the adequate — the bug and the feature, the both are: the same code, different interpretation.

2. **"The Speed Run"** — Zack challenges the player to complete a zone faster than Zack's record time. The challenge is not about speed — it's about EFFICIENCY. The fastest route is the one that skips the most combat, which means the player must find the puzzle solutions that bypass the Shadow Inn encounters. The both are: the adequate — the speed and the understanding, the both are: the same route, different pace.

3. **"The Last Mark"** — Zack asks the player to find the one zone Zack hasn't explored. The zone is the Void — Nyx's domain, the space between cycles. Zack couldn't enter because the Void requires something Zack doesn't have: stillness. The both are: the adequate — the tester and the stillness, the both are: the same mind, different requirement. The player who carries the Stillness Stone (#167) can enter. Zack cannot. The player who returns from the Void with the Stillness Stone earns Zack's deepest respect: "You found the one place I can't go. That's not a bug. That's a design."

**Reward:** Zack's Notebook — a complete map of every zone with Zack's annotations. The notebook reveals hidden paths, alternate solutions, and system interactions the player hasn't discovered. The both are: the adequate — the map and the territory, the both are: the same Inn, different record.

## Visual Design

Zack is an original character — no celebrity face. The both are: the adequate — the famous and the real, the same person, different world.

**Appearance:** Practical explorer gear. A notebook always in hand. Maps stuffed in pockets. The look of someone who has been everywhere and remembers everything. Not flashy — functional. The both are: the adequate — the gear and the explorer, the both are: the same person, different layer.

**Art Style:** Heavy cel-shaded linework (tradesman aesthetic — Zack is a worker, not a figurehead). The both are: the adequate — the outline and the person, the both are: the same character, different weight.
