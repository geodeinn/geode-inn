# Geode Inn — Gargoyle Designs
## Architectural Sentinels of the Inn

### Concept

The gargoyles of the Geode Inn are not decoration. They are the Inn's guardians — carved stone creatures embedded into the living wood and stone of the tree, watching over the transitions between levels. Each gargoyle is positioned at a threshold: the top of the staircase, the entrance to the catacombs, the door to the modern legends nook, the portal to the Fae Kingdom.

In the sound/vibration chapter's framework, each gargoyle is a resonant body — a shaped stone that vibrates at a specific frequency when the Inn's ambient music plays. They are acoustic architecture. The ancient builders understood that shaped stone, when exposed to the right frequencies, becomes a resonator. The gargoyles are the Inn's version of that knowledge.

They do not move. They do not speak. But they hum.

## The Six Gargoyles of the Geode Inn

### 1. The Watcher (Staircase Entrance — Main Floor)
**Location:** Above the archway where the staircase begins its spiral upward
**Resonant frequency:** 111Hz — the ancient resonance frequency, the one that shifts brain activity away from the language center
**Function:** The transition guardian. When the player approaches the staircase, the ambient music shifts and the Watcher begins to vibrate at 111Hz. The player feels it before they hear it — a low hum in the floor, in the wood, in their chest. This is the frequency that prepares the mind for transformation. The alchemical journey begins here.
**Physical design:** A tall, angular gargoyle carved from dark stone, wedged into the living wood where the tree's trunk meets the staircase. Its eyes are deep set, almost closed, watching downward. Its mouth is slightly open — a sound port, not a threat. Its wings are folded against the tree, fusing with the bark. Stone and wood growing into each other. It looks ancient, patient, and slightly sad — it has been watching people climb this staircase for a very long time, and not all of them come back down.
**Sound:** A low, almost subliminal hum at 111Hz. Felt more than heard. When the player stands directly beneath it, the screen subtly vibrates and the ambient music drops away, leaving only the hum.

### 2. The Listener (Catacombs Entrance — Downstairs)
**Location:** Above the archway descending into the catacombs
**Resonant frequency:** 64Hz — a deep bass tone associated with grounding and physical awareness
**Function:** The earth guardian. The catacombs are the oldest part of the Inn — ancient history, the weight of civilizations. The Listener's resonance grounds the player before they descend. It is the opposite of the Watcher: where the Watcher lifts the mind toward transformation, the Listener pulls awareness down into the body, into the earth, into the physical reality of bone and stone and history.
**Physical design:** A broad, heavy gargoyle carved from grey stone, low and squat, almost like a toad. Its ears are enormous — exaggerated, bat-like, cupped forward as if listening to something deep underground. Its eyes are closed. Its mouth is a wide, flat line — neither smiling nor frowning. It sits compressed into the stone archway, its body seeming to bear the weight of the entire tree above it. Moss grows on its shoulders. Tree roots wrap around its base. It has been here so long that the tree has grown into it.
**Sound:** A deep, grounding bass tone at 64Hz. When the player approaches, they feel it in their feet and stomach. The ambient music thickens, slows, drops in pitch — the earth is pulling them down.

### 3. The Sage (Modern Legends Nook — Main Floor)
**Location:** Above the entrance to the Modern Day Legends nook
**Resonant frequency:** 528Hz — associated with transformation and repair in sound healing traditions
**Function:** The contemplation guardian. The Modern Legends nook is where the player reads stories about human choices — the Serrano parable, future entries. The Sage's resonance creates a state of reflective clarity. It does not guide the player toward any conclusion — it creates the mental space for the player to reach their own.
**Physical design:** A slender, elongated gargoyle carved from pale sandstone. Its pose is thoughtful — chin resting on one hand, elbow on a knee, like Rodin's Thinker rendered in medieval stone. Its eyes are open but looking inward, not outward. Small spectacles are carved into its face — a touch of humor, a nod to scholars everywhere. A book is carved into its base, its pages worn. Behind the gargoyle, the wood grain of the tree forms patterns that look like text in an unknown language.
**Sound:** A clear, bell-like tone at 528Hz, soft and constant. It blends with the quiet piano and cello of the nook's ambient music. When the player reads a legend entry, the tone sustains — a drone of clarity underneath the text.

### 4. The Gatekeeper (Fae Kingdom Portal — Downstairs)
**Location:** Flanking the stone circle portal that leads to the Fae Kingdom
**Resonant frequency:** Variable — resonates at whatever frequency the Fae Kingdom portal is currently emitting
**Function:** The threshold guardian. The Fae Kingdom is the most dangerous zone in the Inn — an entire explorable world with combat, factions, and consequences. The Gatekeeper does not prevent entry, but its resonance warns the player: what you do in the Fae Kingdom follows you back. This is not a game you can undo.
**Physical design:** The most dramatic gargoyle. Two of them — one on each side of the portal archway, mirror images. They are carved from dark green stone, almost black, with faint bioluminescent veins running through them like the Taniwha's moko patterns. Their design is ambiguous — are they dragons? Gargoyles? Fae creatures frozen in stone? Their mouths are wide open, roaring silently. Their eyes glow faint blue when the portal is dormant and brighten to white when the portal ignites. When the player approaches, both heads slowly turn to face them — the only movement any gargoyle in the Inn makes.
**Sound:** A dissonant, shifting tone that changes based on the portal's state. Dormant: a low, uneasy hum. Active: a rising shimmer that harmonizes with the portal's ignition sound. When both heads turn, there is a stone-on-stone grinding sound — ancient, heavy, deliberate.

### 5. The Caller (Upstairs — Cosmology Entrance)
**Location:** At the base of the spiral staircase where it opens into the canopy/cosmology level
**Resonant frequency:** 432Hz — the "natural frequency of the universe" in alternative acoustic theory, associated with cosmic harmony
**Function:** The sky guardian. The cosmology level deals with creation myths, sky gods, celestial mechanics — Jupiter's gates, Pluto's realm, the galaxy-language from the narrative spine. The Caller's resonance opens the mind upward, toward the cosmic. It is the highest-frequency gargoyle, the lightest tone, the one that makes the player feel like they are lifting off the ground.
**Physical design:** A gargoyle looking upward, carved from white limestone, positioned where the staircase meets the canopy. Its head is tilted back, mouth open, calling to something above. Its body is lean, almost skeletal, with long wings that spread upward into the branches. Its eyes are open, fixed on something in the sky the player cannot see. Stars are carved into its chest — not decorative, but constellation patterns, as if the gargoyle has mapped the sky on its own body. The tree's branches grow through its wings, supporting it.
**Sound:** A clear, high tone at 432Hz that shimmers and vibrates. It blends with the singing bowls and glass harmonica of the cosmology level's ambient music. When the player looks through the canopy at the stars, the tone shifts — each constellation the player focuses on changes the harmonic slightly.

### 6. The Keeper (Geode Cave — Player's Collection Room)
**Location:** Above the entrance to the player's personal geode cave
**Resonant frequency:** Harmonic of the player's most-collected stone category — the gargoyle tunes itself to the player
**Function:** The personal guardian. The geode cave is the player's reward room — where collected stones appear and the baby dragon sleeps. The Keeper's resonance is unique: it harmonizes with whatever stones the player has collected most. A player who has collected many quartz-family stones hears a high, crystalline tone. A player with more jaspers and agates hears a warmer, earthier hum. The Keeper reflects the player's journey back to them in sound.
**Physical design:** The smallest gargoyle, and the warmest. Carved from amber-colored stone, it sits curled above the geode cave entrance like a sleeping cat. Its eyes are closed. Its mouth is a gentle smile. It looks less like a guardian and more like a pet that has been waiting for the player to come home. Small crystals have grown into its surface — geode formations, amethyst and quartz, as if the gargoyle itself is becoming a geode. The baby dragon sometimes sleeps on its head.
**Sound:** A warm, gentle hum that changes based on the player's collection. When the player enters the geode cave, the Keeper's tone harmonizes with the chime tones of the collected stones — a personalized welcome song. An empty geode cave: a single soft hum. A full geode cave: a chord built from every stone the player has earned.

## Gargoyle Lore (for the Archive)

### The Nature of the Six

The six gargoyles of the Geode Inn are older than the Inn itself — or so the story goes. They were here when the tree was a sapling. They were here when the first stones were placed. They do not speak, but they remember.

Each gargoyle is carved from a different stone, and each vibrates at a different frequency. Together, the six frequencies form a chord — a sound that is the Geode Inn itself. The Inn is not silent. It hums. The hum is the gargoyles, and the gargoyles are the Inn.

In the alchemical tradition of the staircase, sound is the first force — the one that precedes all others. "In the beginning was the Word." But before the Word, there was the vibration that made the Word possible. The gargoyles are that vibration, made permanent in stone.

They are not alive. They are not dead. They are resonant. And resonance, as any alchemist will tell you, is the mechanism by which the universe changes itself.

### Cataloging Notes

- The gargoyles should be visually distinct from the character roster — they are architecture, not characters. They do not move (except the Gatekeeper's head turn). They do not interact. They are part of the building.
- However, a player who stands beneath a gargoyle long enough may notice that the ambient music has changed — shifted, deepened, opened. The gargoyles are not passive. They are always resonating. The player simply has to be still enough to notice.
- The Keeper (geode cave gargoyle) is the exception — it is the one gargoyle that feels personal, warm, almost alive. It is the Inn's way of saying: this space is yours.
- The Gatekeeper's head-turn is the one animated movement any gargoyle makes. It should be slow, heavy, and accompanied by stone-grinding sound. It should make the player feel watched — not threatened, but assessed. "Are you ready for what is behind me?"

### Connection to the Sound/Vibration Chapter

When Krista's sound/vibration research arrives, it will be integrated into the gargoyle lore. The 111Hz data, the Gregorian chant research, the Notre Dame gargoyle legend — all of it feeds into the idea that these six carved stones are not decoration but acoustic architecture, designed (or evolved) to shape the consciousness of anyone who passes beneath them.

The staircase level (alchemy) is where this lore is explained in detail — the gargoyle chapter of the acoustic alchemy sub-section. The gargoyles themselves are the physical manifestation of that knowledge, placed throughout the Inn.
