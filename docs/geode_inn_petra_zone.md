# Geode Inn — Petra: "The Rose City"
## Zone: The Nabataean Crossroads (O'Keeffe aesthetic, Mediterranean extension of the Desert Bone)
## Access: Main Floor → World Map → Jordan pin → Petra
## Prerequisite: Desert Bone Quest Chain complete (Form Sight) + Petrified Forest Quest 3 (Spiral Key)

### Overview

Petra is the Subtractive Family's extrovert. Where the Cave City carves inward (the introvert — architecture hidden in the rock), Petra carves outward (the extrovert — architecture announced in the facade). Both are architecture indistinguishable from geology. Both are the Subtractive Family's philosophy: you don't build, you REVEAL. The stone was always there. You remove what isn't the building.

The Nabataeans didn't build Petra. They CARVED it — cut the city directly into the sandstone cliffs. The facades are ornate, sculpted, beautiful in a way that announces: this was carved, not built. This is the one place where the Subtractive Family's carving is DECORATIVE — the Petra facades are the family's public face, the extrovert who dresses for the audience.

The O'Keeffe aesthetic extends from the Desert Bone through the shared desert. Petra's sandstone is O'Keeffe's palette: ochre, rust, rose, bone-white, the colored shadow. But where the Desert Bone simplifies (strips the detail to find the form), Petra CELEBRATES (ornaments the form to honor the material). The Desert Bone is O'Keeffe's minimalism. Petra is O'Keeffe's maximalism — the same artist, different mood. O'Keeffe painted both: the spare desert floor AND the ornate skull with flowers.

Petra is the CROSSROADS. The Nabataeans controlled the trade routes — incense from Arabia, silk from China, spices from India. Petra was where the world met. The zone reflects this: Petra is the Inn's most multicultural zone, with architectural elements from Egypt, Greece, Rome, and the East, all carved into the same sandstone. The zone's thesis: the form is universal, but the ornament is local. The building is the same everywhere. The decoration tells you where you are.

## Key Locations

### 1. The Siq (The Approach)
A 1.2-kilometer narrow gorge — the only entrance to Petra. The walls rise 180 meters on either side. The passage is dark, cool, the sky a thin line above. The Siq is the zone's GATE — the long, patient approach that prepares the player for the revelation. The Siq is the Subtractive Family's hallway — carved by water over millions of years, not by hands. The Siq teaches: the most beautiful architecture is the one nobody designed.

In Form Sight: the Siq is two walls and a line of sky. The essential form — the passage — is visible without the geological detail. The Form Sight reveals the water's path: the crack in the rock that became the gorge, the fissure that became the door.

### 2. The Treasury (Al-Khazneh)
The facade that appears at the end of the Siq — the moment of revelation. The Treasury is Petra's most famous view: a monumental facade carved directly into the rose sandstone cliff, Hellenistic in style, ornate, enormous, glowing pink-gold in the morning light. The facade is the zone's O'Keeffe maximalism — the form (a rectangle with columns and a pediment) made ornate (sculpted figures, carved details, intricate friezes).

In Form Sight: the Treasury simplifies to its essential architecture — a rectangle, six columns, a pediment, a doorway. The ornament falls away. The form is Greek. The stone is Nabataean. The player sees: the architecture is borrowed, the stone is local. The form traveled. The material stayed.

### 3. The Monastery (Ad Deir)
Larger than the Treasury, less ornate, higher in the mountains. The Monastery is the Subtractive Family's most adequate work — the facade is simpler, the scale is bigger, the stone is more visible. The Monastery is the Cave City's cousin — the same principle (carving into rock) at a different scale. The Monastery is the introvert's extrovert moment — the quiet building that is also the biggest.

### 4. The Royal Tombs
A row of monumental tombs carved into the cliff face — the Nabataean kings' resting places. Each tomb has a different facade: Urn, Silk, Corinthian, Palace. The tombs are the zone's connection to the Desert Bone — the same culture of death and preservation, but expressed through FACADE, not burial. The Desert Bone hides its dead (underground tombs, sand-covered). Petra DISPLAYS its dead (cliff-face facades, visible from the valley). The philosophy: the Desert Bone preserves the body. Petra preserves the NAME.

### 5. The Colonnaded Street
The city's main street — Roman-era columns lining a cardo (north-south street). The street is where the trade happened: incense, silk, spices, stones. The Colonnaded Street is the zone's MARKET — the Inn's artisan market has a Petra branch here, with Nabataean-inspired stalls selling stones, incense, and carved objects. The street is multicultural: Roman columns, Nabataean carvings, Greek architectural elements, Eastern goods.

### 6. The High Place of Sacrifice
A mountain-top altar — the highest point in Petra. The view from the High Place shows the entire zone: the valley, the cliffs, the tombs, the street, the desert beyond. The High Place is the zone's COSMOLOGY moment — the elevated perspective that simplifies the landscape into forms. From here, in Form Sight, Petra is a handful of shapes in rose stone: rectangles (tombs), cylinders (columns), curves (the Siq), triangles (the mountains). The city simplifies to its forms. O'Keeffe's aerial view, made real.

## The Waterkeeper

Petra's secret is water. The Nabataeans built a water management system — cisterns, channels, dams, pipes — that turned a desert canyon into a lush city. The water system is the zone's INVISIBLE architecture: more impressive than the facades, less visible than the tombs, and more essential than both. Without the water, Petra is a canyon. With the water, Petra is a city.

The Waterkeeper maintains this system — a chibi NPC with a clay pipe in one hand and a water level in the other. The Waterkeeper is the zone's most practical resident — not impressed by the facades ("the stone carves itself, if you're patient"), but deeply proud of the cisterns ("the water doesn't wait. the water must be GUIDED").

**Voice:** Quick, practical, the cadence of flowing water. Sentences are short and connected — no pauses. The Waterkeeper doesn't pause because water doesn't pause.

**The Waterkeeper's lesson:** "The facades are beautiful. The facades are the face. The water is the body. The face lasts longer than the body — everyone can see the facades. No one can see the cisterns. But the cisterns MADE the facades possible. Without the water, there is no city. Without the city, there are no carvers. Without the carvers, there are no facades. The invisible made the visible possible. Remember that. The next time you see something beautiful, ask: what is carrying it that I can't see?"

## The Caravan Master

The Caravan Master is the zone's connection to the Wandering Program — the Inn's system for wandering NPCs and trade routes. The Caravan Master coordinates the incense road, the silk road, and the spice road. All three routes pass through Petra. The Caravan Master knows every stop, every water source, every safe camp, every bandit's territory.

**Voice:** Cosmopolitan — the accent shifts mid-sentence. Each word might carry a different language's weight. The Caravan Master speaks 12 languages, all of them with the same quiet confidence.

**Personality:** Weathered, warm, watchful. The Caravan Master has seen everything because the Caravan Master has been everywhere. Not jaded — appreciative. "The world is large. The roads connect it. I walk the roads. The roads walk me. We are the same movement."

## Quest Chain: "The Form That Traveled"

### Quest 1: "Through the Crack"
- **Giver:** The Waterkeeper, at the Siq entrance
- **Prerequisite:** Form Sight + Spiral Key
- **Objective:** Walk the Siq (1.2 km — a real-time walk, no fast travel). At three points along the Siq, activate Form Sight to see the water channels carved into the walls — the invisible architecture. The channels are the Waterkeeper's work, 2,000 years old, still functional. The player must follow the water channel from the Siq entrance to the Treasury, tracing the water's path.
- **Reward:** The Waterkeeper's first nod. Access to the cistern system (a mini-dungeon beneath Petra — the zone's hidden layer).

### Quest 2: "The Borrowed Form"
- **Giver:** The Facade Carver, at the Treasury
- **Prerequisite:** Quest 1 complete
- **Objective:** In Form Sight, examine the Treasury facade. The form is Greek (columns, pediment, entablature). The stone is Nabataean (rose sandstone). The Carver asks: "Where did the form come from?" The player must travel to the Aegean Light zone (Greece) and find the original Greek temple that inspired the Treasury's design. The form traveled from Greece to Petra via the trade routes. Return to the Carver with the answer: the form is Greek, the stone is local, the trade route is the bridge.
- **Reward:** The Carver's chisel — a tool that lets the player "reveal" hidden architecture in stone surfaces (a Form Sight upgrade: the player can now see what's BENEATH stone surfaces, not just what's simplified on top).

### Quest 3: "The Invisible Made Visible"
- **Giver:** The Waterkeeper, at the cistern entrance
- **Prerequisite:** Quest 2 complete
- **Objective:** Descend into the cistern system — Petra's hidden layer. The cisterns are carved into the rock beneath the city, a network of channels, dams, and reservoirs. In Form Sight, the water system is visible as a NETWORK — the same spiral pattern as the universal spiral. The water system follows the spiral. The spiral is the form of water. Navigate the cistern system by following the spiral water flow. At the deepest cistern, find the source: a natural spring, the water that made Petra possible. The spring resonates at 42Hz — the Inn's base frequency. The water was singing 42Hz before the stones learned it.
- **Reward:** The Spring Stone — a small, smooth, wet stone from the source spring. It's always slightly damp. Frequency: 42Hz (the source frequency — the water taught the stones). The stone connects to the Water Zone's Source (Layer 7), where the water also resonates at 42Hz. The Spring Stone is the physical link between Petra and the Water Zone.

### Quest 4: "The Name on the Cliff"
- **Giver:** The Caravan Master, at the Royal Tombs
- **Prerequisite:** Quest 3 complete
- **Objective:** The Royal Tombs display the dead — the names carved in stone, visible from the valley. The Desert Bone HIDES its dead (underground). Petra DISPLAYS its dead (cliff-face). The Caravan Master asks: "Which is braver? To hide what you've lost? Or to carve it where everyone can see?" The player must visit each of the four Royal Tombs facades and read the names. Each name has a story — the Caravan Master knows them all. The player must match each name to its story (four stories, four tombs, four connections to the trade routes that made Petra rich).
- **Reward:** The Caravan Master's map — the trade route map, showing all three roads (incense, silk, spice) and their connections to the Inn's world map. The map connects Petra to every zone the trade routes touch: Egypt (Desert Bone), Greece (Aegean Light), India (Sacred Scramble zone), and China (Wall Defense zone).

### Quest 5: "The Rose City's Form"
- **Giver:** The Waterkeeper, at the High Place of Sacrifice
- **Prerequisite:** Quest 4 complete
- **Objective:** Climb to the High Place of Sacrifice — the highest point in Petra. Activate Form Sight and look at the entire zone. In Form Sight, Petra simplifies to its essential forms: rectangles (tombs), cylinders (columns), curves (the Siq), triangles (mountains), and the spiral (the water system). The zone's form is the spiral — the water's form, the same universal spiral from the Petrified Forest. The spiral connects Petra to every zone in the Inn. The player must document the spiral's path: from the spring (42Hz) through the cisterns, through the Siq, through the city, and out to the desert — the water's journey, the form's journey. The documentation becomes the zone's archive entry — the player's record of Petra's form, added to the Inn's Living Book.
- **Reward:** The Rose Stone — Petra's capstone. A piece of the rose sandstone, carved with the spiral. Frequency: 528Hz (transformation — the stone that was carved, the form that traveled, the water that sang). The Rose Stone is the Subtractive Family's gift — the proof that the building was always in the stone, waiting to be revealed.

- **Base frequency:** 42Hz (the spring — the water that made Petra possible, the Inn's source frequency made local)
- **Ambient:** Desert wind through the Siq — a natural flute. The Siq's narrow passage creates a sustained tone when the wind blows through it. The tone changes pitch with wind speed. The Siq is the world's largest flute.
- **The Waterkeeper:** Water sounds — dripping, flowing, the practical music of plumbing. Processed through clay reverb (the cisterns' resonance).
- **The Caravan Master:** Oud (Arabic lute) + frame drum — the music of the trade routes. Multicultural: the oud shifts to Greek lyre when discussing Greece, to Chinese guqin when discussing China, to Indian sitar when discussing India. The Caravan Master's music IS the trade routes.
- **The cisterns:** Deep echo + water drips at 50 BPM (the Inn's heartbeat tempo — the water's pulse is the Inn's pulse)
- **Form Sight activation:** The bell, followed by the Siq's wind-tone — the sound of seeing the invisible architecture

- **The Living Book** — Quest 5's archive entry becomes part of the Living Book. The player's documentation of Petra's form is the player's first contribution to the Inn's archive — the first time the player writes an entry, not reads one.
