## SECTION 2: STORYLINE ADAPTATION

Original Characters Being Replaced:
- Simon Belmont -> The Nagi Warrior (a serpent-guardian wielding a stone-edged kris blade)
- Dracula -> The Sleeping Naga (a primordial serpent deity coiled beneath Angkor Wat, waking)
- Castlevania's monsters -> Temple guardians: stone apsaras (come alive), vine strangles, naga spawn, garuda scouts, spirit monks
- Castlevania's whip -> The Kris Blade (a serpentine dagger that extends on strike — upgradeable)
- Castlevania's sub-weapons -> Stone talismans: Cobra Strike (thrown serpent), Garuda Feather (glide), Lotus Bloom (heal), Apsara Veil (temporary invincibility)

New Narrative Frame:
- The Nagi Warrior descends into Angkor Wat's lower temples to prevent the Sleeping Naga from waking. The Naga is not evil — it is primordial. If it wakes, it will unmake the temple and everyone in it, not out of malice but out of scale. A waking god does not notice ants.
- Each stage is a level of the temple complex: outer galleries (carved bas-reliefs come alive), the Hall of Elephants, the terraces, the inner sanctuary, the subterranean serpent chamber.
- The Kris Blade is the signature weapon — a wavy-bladed dagger that extends like a serpent strike. Upgrades increase its range and add elemental properties: fire (amber), water (sapphire), earth (jade), and spirit (moonstone).
- The Zircon reward: found in the deepest chamber after defeating the Naga's echo (not the Naga itself — the player cannot kill a god). The Zircon is the oldest stone in Angkor's foundations — it holds the temple's original intention. Placing it back in the sanctuary floor lulls the Naga back to sleep.

Dialogue Requirements:
- NPC dialogue count: ~14 lines (The Apsara Guide — a temple spirit who appears at stage transitions)
- Voice profiles needed: Apsara Guide -> Khmer-inflected English, melodic, singsong cadence, airy register
- Whisper Layer presence: Yes — in the subterranean serpent chamber. The Whisper Layer manifests as Naga dreams — the walls pulse with visions of the world before temples. The archive shows what was here before humans built.

Frequency State:
- Living (42Hz): Standard gameplay. Temple is warm sandstone, golden hour light. Apsaras are graceful guides. The Naga sleeps peacefully.
- Shadow (84Hz): Temple goes to night. Stone apsaras become aggressive. Vine strangles grow faster. The Naga stirs — the floor vibrates. The music intensifies. The player must move faster.
- In-Between: Dawn/dusk transitions between stages. The temple shifts from warm to cool and back.

## SECTION 5: PROGRAMMING REQUIREMENTS

Engine: Godot 4.x | Offline-first

Controller Support:
- Move: Left stick / D-pad (left, right)
- Jump: A / face button
- Attack (Kris Blade): B / face button (or RT / R2)
- Crouch: Down on stick
- Climb: hold up + A near climbable vine
- Use talisman: X / face button (cycles through collected talismans)
- Special move: Y / face button (if kris upgraded to element level)
- Pause: Start / Options
- Bluetooth: auto-detect, prompt if no controller
- Touch: on-screen buttons (mobile — d-pad + action buttons)

Steam Integration:
- Achievements: "Serpent's Sleep" (beat game), "Kris Master" (fully upgrade Kris Blade), "Shadow Walker" (beat Shadow mode), "Untouched by Stone" (beat a stage without taking damage), "Zircon Keeper" (earn the Zircon), "Secrets of Angkor" (find all hidden rooms)
- Cloud saves: Sync PlayerProgress
- Leaderboard: "Fastest Clear" (speedrun)
- Overlay: Shift+Tab or LB+RB

Base44 API: On completion POST to PlayerProgress — add Zircon, increment gamesCompleted

Special Notes:
- The player does NOT kill the Naga. The final boss is the Naga's Echo — a projection of what the Naga would become if fully awake. Defeating the echo lulls the real Naga back to sleep. This respects the Inn's thesis: the primordial is not evil, it is simply beyond human scale.
- The Kris Blade's upgradeable elemental system adds replay value — different elements work better against different enemies.
- Angkor Wat's bas-reliefs coming alive (stone apsaras) is the signature visual moment. Moreau's symbolist style makes the carvings feel like they're emerging from the stone itself.
