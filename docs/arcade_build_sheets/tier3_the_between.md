## SECTION 2: STORYLINE ADAPTATION

The player falls through the cracks. Not dramatically — the way it actually happens: a step taken during a pause between two notes, a door opened in the second before it was a door. The world upstairs misplaces the player the way the world misplaces anything: nothing personal, the world is very large and you are very small, and things get lost between the two.

The player arrives in the Between: the shifting borderland between the Seelie summer and the Unseelie winter — the space neither court claims because neither court can hold it. Escher's territory. Stairs that ascend into their own beginnings. Corridors that loop without admitting it. A village under the staircase. A market in the seams. The hour between hours, which is a real hour — it just doesn't belong to a clock.

The quest: be found by the right thing. The player is invisible to the courts' regulars while between — but visible to the Displaced: everyone else the world misplaced. The way home is earned the way all doors in the Between open: by helping the misplaced find theirs. Every kindness anchors the player; every anchor makes the door clearer. The Bellringer keeps the ledger — everything misplaced ends up on a page eventually. The Norns keep the loom — and they have been expecting the player since before the player existed.

Core mechanics:

DOORWALKING — the Between is entered and traversed through seams: the space behind paintings, the underside of stairs, the pause between two notes, the gap where two rooms almost touch. In gameplay: any zone's environment shows faint seams when the player listens (audio cue: the beat frequency). Entering a seam drops the player into the margin-geography. Escher-impossible: the map loops; time navigation breaks the loop.

THE NORN LOOM — three weavers, three tenses. The same corridor can be walked as it WAS (Urd — past), as it IS (Verdandi — present), as it WILL BE (Skuld — future). Switching tense is switching the world: a door sealed in the present is ajar in the past; a bridge that won't hold now will hold after Skuld's thread is pulled. The skill curve: solving puzzles that exist across tenses, in the space between one moment's truth and the next.

THE HOUR BETWEEN HOURS — the Bellringer rings the bell in the blank space where an hour should be. When the bell rings, doors remember they are doors — margin-doors open for exactly one un-hour, and the player must choose which to walk before the world remembers its clock.

REMEMBRANCE (the only survival mechanic) — dwell too long unmoored and the world forgets you. Kindnesses anchor: every Displaced person helped, every misplaced thing returned, every bell rung for someone else's door adds weight to the player's page in the ledger. If Remembrance runs out, the game does not kill the player — the Between walks the player gently to the nearest crack home and the ledger stays open. (The Inn's no-kill-screen rule holds here too. Even forgetting is survivable. You just start again, still you.)

THE DISPLACED — the Between's population: everything the world misplaced. Homesick specters, ahead-of-schedule travelers, one entire village that got filed under the wrong staircase, the Loom's runaway threads. The player cannot tell which are homesick and which are ahead of schedule by looking. The difference is only ever revealed by kindness — the one diagnostic the Between trusts.

Score = anchors, not points. Leaderboard: Displaced reunited + margin-doors walked.

Frequency States (unique tri-mode — the zone IS the In-Between):
- The Between's own hum: the overlap of the two carriers — 42Hz and 84Hz sounding together. The interference pattern is the zone's music. The beat frequency is where the Between lives: the space between the beats is the door.
- Near the Seelie border: the zone warms, gold bleeds through (42Hz dominates the mix)
- Near the Unseelie border: the zone cools, frost-linework sharpens (84Hz dominates the mix)
- The gradient IS the navigation: the player reads warmth and cold the way sailors read stars

### Design Notes

THE ZONE WITH ALMOST NOTHING IN IT — was the point. The Between is the In-Between frequency made into geography: the space neither court claims, the hour that doesn't belong to a clock, the page you're on when the world misplaces you. Poetically accurate for two years; now it has a game layer because a Neverwhere-type world needs walkable cracks, not just the idea of them.

THE KINDNESS ENGINE. Every zone so far tests a skill: reflex (arcade), trust-reading (the Masquerade), patience (the Thread). The Between tests kindness — the only diagnostic it trusts. You cannot tell who is homesick and who is ahead of schedule by looking. You find out by helping. The door home opens by the weight of the anchors you make for others. The score is not what you took from the zone. It's who you brought back.

THE THREE-WAY PAIRING. The Thread, the Masquerade, the Between: three games that all refuse the kill screen. Catacomb Labyrinth chases you; the Thread seats you; the Masquerade reads you; the Between forgets you gently and lets you be found. Together: the Inn's complete thesis on what a monster is, what a choice is, and what a door is. Nothing in the Inn punishes you for being lost. Everything in the Inn lets you arrive.

### Aurora Falls — The In-Between Precedent

CORRECTION (2026-09-10, Krista-verified): the In-Between's native game already exists — the weightless grey Limbo-style platformer (docs/geode_inn_in_between_game.md), where Fizgig, the Duck (the shiny-collecting platypus), and Dobbie are stuck in the grey, and Brim goes full size — the one place, the mother, kneeling. THE definitive In-Between gameplay precedent. Disambiguation is now canonical: the IN-BETWEEN is the weightless grey interval between zones (the prose layer, the limbo game's home); the BETWEEN is the Fae Kingdom borderland (Escher's shifting marches — this sheet's game). Two spaces. The limbo game's Erasure, innocents, and Brim's one empathy belong to the In-Between; the margin-walk, Bellringer, and Norns belong to the Between. The Between build sheet borrows the precedent, not the territory.

Aurora Falls (game #60, Limbo-style) is the second component where the In-Between state appears in gameplay use: the real-time twilight-to-night aurora shift as the Seeker descends the geothermal valley. Wordless, HUD-less — the frequency state is told entirely in light.

The Between is that state made permanent geography. Aurora Falls visits the In-Between; the Between lives there.

The wire, three ways:
- THE GRADIENT IS THE AURORA, SLOWED. The Between's warm/cold border gradient (42Hz bleeding through near Seelie, 84Hz near Unseelie) is Aurora Falls' descent shift stretched to walking speed. A player who learned to read the Seeker's light can read the Between's borders by instinct — same language, different tempo.
- THE SPAR IS THE SEAM LENS. Icelandic Spar — Aurora Falls' reward, the Viking sunstone that shows direction when all other light is gone — becomes the Between's visible key. Held, it polarizes the gradient and makes seams glow readable. Players who earned it in Aurora Falls bring their own compass; players who haven't navigate by the beat-frequency audio cue alone (harder, fair, doable). Cross-game DNA per the integration map: the games hand each other tools, not just stones.
- THE WORDLESS ROOM. Aurora Falls proved the In-Between can be spoken without a single line of text. The Between keeps speech (the Bellringer, the Norns) — but its deepest point, the seam where the gradient balances perfectly, is silent: Aurora Falls' wordless rule, kept as a room.
