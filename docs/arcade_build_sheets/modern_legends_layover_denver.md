## SECTION 2: STORYLINE ADAPTATION

**The frame:** The player lands at the airport in the Modern Legends Nook. The connection is a layover. The departures board shows one flight: DELAYED. Between the seats and the vending machines, a floor tile shifts. The basement opens. The player descends — and finds the MIB's subterranean bureau: brass filing cabinets, memory-vault canisters, upside-down concourse signs, and three suits gliding in perfect synchrony. The basement is a giant chutes-and-ladders board, and the player is a piece on it.

**The board:** The basement plays as a vertical board — floors connected by CHUTES (baggage chutes, moving sidewalks that run downhill, trapdoors that slide the player DOWN a level) and LADDERS (maintenance ladders, service climbs that take the player UP a level). Up is toward the surface and escape. Down is toward the bureau's depths — where the deepest filing cabinets keep the collected memories (the ancient cities, the forgotten things, the "blue light stream" of the MIB prompt canon).

**The chase:** Three Mini MIB pursue. They do not run — they GLIDE (the eerie, perfectly synchronized, cold movement of the prompt canon). They do not speak. On a good roll they simply appear closer, nodding. THE MIB LAW (canon, from the Area 51 rule): CAUGHT DOESN'T KILL — THEY MAKE YOU FORGET. When the suits touch the player, a level's knowledge erases: the explored map fogs back in, unlocked doors re-lock, and the canisters hum a little fuller. The run continues; the MEMORY is the health bar. Run out of memories to lose and the player rides the last chute all the way down — waking in the terminal, with a ticket that says DELAYED and no memory of a basement that, the suits insist, doesn't exist.

**The goal:** Reach THE FILING CABINET (the deepest cabinet — the tie to the "Three Suits" festival achievement and its catacombs side quest): recover one canister of collected memory (an ancient city, returned) and ride the longest ladder to the surface. Delivering the canister to the Archive completes the game — the memory becomes a real Archive entry, readable in the library (the game feeds the real Book/Lore entities).

**The horse:** The final ladder is watched by the airport's resident legend: the blue horse with the glowing eyes (Inn-original rendering — the locals' nickname stays). It does not chase. It watches. If the player pauses at the top of the ladder, the horse looks at them — and the player's total score of remembered things briefly overlays the screen (the horse shows you what you're still carrying). The horse is the game's one moment of pure witness.

**The murals:** The basement walls carry the airport's famous murals — Inn-original modern legends art, each one a clue to a Modern Day Legends shelf entry. Reading the murals in order (across runs) unlocks the shelf's Denver entries.

**The narrator:** The game is narrated by the INTERCOM — the airport announcement voice, rendered as the Inn's calmest voice profile: the disembodied train-announcement cadence that comments on the chase with unbroken, schedule-obsessed serenity. ("The 3:15 to the surface is now boarding. The player is advised that the 3:15 has always been boarding.")

## SECTION 3: MECHANICS

| Mechanic | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| Movement | Frequency Dice (the Inn's D6 with frequency symbols — the tabletop system's dice as the board game's engine): roll, move. High rolls climb; chutes and ladders override. |
| Chutes | Slide down 1-3 floors toward the bureau's depths. Chutes are fast, dark, and the intercom announces them like gate changes. |
| Ladders | Climb 1-3 floors toward the surface. Ladders are slow, creaky, and the suits glide faster while the player climbs (climbing is exposure). |
| The Suits | Three pursuers with distinct glides: the First Suit follows the board's logic (predictable), the Second Suit follows the player's habits (learns the player's chute preferences), the Third Suit follows nothing (appears). |
| Forgetting | Capture = one floor's memory erased (map fog, re-locked doors). No death. The suits always leave the player at the top of a chute, minus what they took. |
| Murals | Each floor holds one mural fragment; collecting fragments across runs unlocks Modern Day Legends entries. |
| The canister run | The endgame: grab the deepest canister and climb without being touched — a perfect climb with zero forgetting awards the ammolite. Being touched means leaving the memory behind for the suits: the run ends, the city stays theirs. (Both outcomes are canon-complete: rescue the memory, or learn why the suits keep winning.) |

## SECTION 4: FREQUENCY STATE

- Living (42Hz): the terminal above — warm newsprint light, gate-change music, vending-machine hum. The basement at 42Hz is fluorescent but ordinary; the suits are merely unsettling.
- Shadow (84Hz): the basement's true state — the filing cabinets fully revealed, the memory canisters glowing, the murals animated, the suits' silhouettes crisper than the walls. Some canisters are readable ONLY at 84Hz (the Shadow preserves what motion and memory obscure).
- In-Between (the grey): the chutes and ladders are visible as the board's LOGIC — the grey shows the layout without the walls: the player sees the board as the suits see it, one glance, before the color returns.
