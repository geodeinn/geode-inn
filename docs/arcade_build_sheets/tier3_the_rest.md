## SECTION 2: STORYLINE ADAPTATION

The Void is where the Song goes when it isn't playing. 0Hz. The permanent dropout — the audiobook canon says the 50 BPM heartbeat cuts out at three key moments; the Void is where the cut lives. No Living state, no Shadow state, no In-Between. The Void predates the split.

The player is not dropped into the Void, does not fall into it, is not chased into it. The Void doesn't take the careless. The player is INVITED — a door that only opens for someone who has sat still somewhere in the Inn long enough to be noticed by silence. (The SIT threshold, canonized in the In-Between doc, becomes the Void's door key.)

Two residents. NYX — the silence between the notes, rendered as negative space: darkness shaped like a figure who has never needed a face. And THE GUEST — Death, on holiday, holding a music box that nobody may open. The music box contains the Song's final note. The Astronomer calculates the Song's end and doesn't want to; the Guest is the one keeping the end safe until it's needed. (Direct tie to the Ascension Canopy endgame: the Egg, the Violin. When the Inn's story finishes, the last note comes out of this box.)

The problem: the Guest is on holiday, and the endings are piling up. All across the cosmology layer, fragments of the Song have forgotten how to stop — stuck notes, unresolved chords, echoes that never got their ending. They ring on, exhausted, unable to finish. The Guest would normally hold them — giving each sound its ending is Death's actual job, and Death does it gently. But Death is resting.

The quest: the player takes the holiday shift. Not harvesting souls — holding endings. The player carries 0Hz: stand beside a stuck note, hold still, and the note finally gets to stop. The player is the period at the end of the sentence. The rest between the notes.

Core mechanics:

THE REST — the game's one verb. Puzzles have durations, not solutions. Stand in the place the stillness asks for, for the measured time — a whole rest (the two-minute SIT threshold, made a gameplay unit), a half rest, a quarter rest. What happens while the player stands still is the game.

THE UNPLAYED HARMONICA — the silver harmonica hangs in the Void. The one bright object in a dark world, the only specular highlight. Every instinct says pick it up and play it. The game is full of moments where playing/touching/acting is the obvious move, and restraint is the correct one. The temptation is the test. The harmonica is never played. Not by the player, not by the Guest, not by Nyx. It is the instrument that belongs to silence — the breath before the note, kept unspent.

THE STUCK NOTES — the game's enemies, if it had them: unresolved sounds rendered as pale gold filaments, hanging in the dark, ringing without end (Klimt-gold thread that forgot how to stop — the same thread language as the Weaver's Loom, but uncut). Each one is a real pitch. Ending them is the game's music: the player's victories are silences where sounds used to be. Every stuck note ended is a small kindness to a sound that got tired.

NYX'S LESSONS — Nyx speaks the silence between words. The player learns to hear what isn't said: instructions arrive as gaps, as the pause before a phrase, as the held breath in a sentence that means the opposite of its words. (Gaiman register — the mythic made personal; here the mythic is made of punctuation.) Nyx never says anything twice, because Nyx never says anything once.

THE MUSIC BOX — look, don't open. The player may look at it as long as they like. Looking is safe. The box is not a puzzle. The box is a promise the Inn is keeping.

THE ENDING — when every stuck note has its ending, the Guest returns to work: rested, grateful, and — for the first time in the history of the Inn — impressed. The Guest offers the silver harmonica. The player does not play it. That is the win state: the player carries the unplayed harmonica back into the Inn. The item is the choice not to use it — the Inn's quietest trophy, worn in the inventory like a held breath.

(There is no bad ending. A player who tries to play the harmonica finds it makes no sound — not because it's broken, but because the player isn't the one it's waiting for. The Guest takes it back gently: "Some other century." Try again.)

Score: stuck notes ended + rests held. The leaderboard, if we keep one, is LONGEST REST — hours, minutes, seconds of stillness, held. The only leaderboard in the world where the high score is measured in not moving.

Tri-mode note: the Void has no tri-mode. It is the permanent dropout — the state the other three states rest on. Standing in the Void, the player can FEEL the whole Inn's mix below them: 42Hz and 84Hz and 50 BPM, all of it, at once, at whisper volume — like hearing the party through the wall from the room where it's quiet.

## SECTION 3: VISUAL ASSETS NEEDED

- The Void proper: not black — the color of a held breath. Depth without fog (the In-Between used fog; the Void uses nothing). Things are at distances because they are at distances. Negative space as architecture.
- Nyx: no-outline linework (cosmological mode, per the Linework-as-UI canon). Rendered as negative space — the darkness shaped like a figure. Never a face.
- The Guest: Death on holiday — vacation attire over the velvet robe, like a professor in a borrowed cardigan; the music box under one arm. Soft features, warm posture. Hitchcock-cameo restraint: the less shown, the more felt.
- The silver harmonica: the one bright object in the world. The only specular highlight. (Never animated being played.)
- The stuck notes: pale gold filaments, ringing — thin, exhausted, beautiful. Each ending leaves a faint grey thread-mark where the sound was.
- The music box: dark wood, no visible seam, warm in the Guest's arm. Looks at home there.
- The door in: the invitation threshold — visible only to the player who has sat still long enough.
- UI: no HUD in the Void. The rest-duration indicator is the world itself: the stuck note dims as its ending approaches. Speech bubbles with ink outlines (Inn standard) — but Nyx's bubbles have no border.
- Stone reveal: Stillness Stone — a stone whose surface looks like held breath.
- Estimated total: 30-40 image assets

## SECTION 4: AUDIO ASSETS NEEDED

- The core paradox: a silence game with an audio budget. The mix is absence made audible: room tone at the edge of perception, the player's own breath, the world's held stillness.
- The 50 BPM heartbeat: present in the descent in, then the dropout — the Void is the permanent dropout, and the moment the heartbeat stops is the moment the player knows they've arrived.
- The stuck notes: real pitches that never resolve — each one a distinct tone (C, E, G — the unresolved triad of the Inn). Ending each is a silence with the shape of the note still in it.
- The harmonica: never heard. The mix keeps a harmonica-shaped hole. Players will report hearing it anyway. That's the intended effect.
- The whole-Inn whisper: the layer below everything — 42Hz, 84Hz, 50 BPM, all at whisper volume, heard through the wall.
- The ending: no fanfare. One sound: the music box lid, closing. Then the Stillness Stone's tone (Mohs-mapped, 12dB below narration) — the only new note the game ever adds.
- Voice lines: 8-12 (the Guest's holiday patter; Nyx renders as pauses in the mix, not words — though two or three almost-lines exist, each cut off exactly one word before meaning)
- Inn Mode DSP: the Void profile — infinite reverb time, gated to zero; the room that is the pause between all the Inn's other rooms
- Estimated total: 12-16 audio assets

## SECTION 5: PROGRAMMING REQUIREMENTS

Engine: Godot 4.x | Offline-first (all assets bundled locally)

Controller Support:
- Full Bluetooth controller mapping — deliberately minimal: one button (stand still / hold), stick (drift-walk), a trigger for "listen." The controller's vibration motor goes COMPLETELY silent in the Void — the dropout extends to haptics
- Touch support for mobile (press-and-hold as the rest)
- Valkyrie Shift compatible (TV): the ideal platform for the Void is actually the big screen in a dark room

Steam Integration:
- Achievements: Holiday Cover (all stuck notes ended), Tacet (finish the game having acted zero times where restraint was possible), Some Other Century (try to play the harmonica — everyone does), The Long Rest (hold one rest for 30 real minutes — the Inn's final patience test)
- Cloud saves: PlayerProgress sync
- Leaderboard: Longest Rest (hours:minutes:seconds of held stillness)
- Overlay: Shift+Tab or LB+RB (the overlay's chime is the loudest sound in the game — deliberately)

Base44 API: On completion, POST to PlayerProgress — add Stillness Stone, increment gamesCompleted, set flag `held_the_rest`

Dialogue hooks: `the_guest_001` (the holiday patter — the job, the rules, the music box) — seated in the Dialogue entity 2026-09-10. Nyx: deliberately UNSEATED. Nyx has no dialogue record. Nyx's record is the empty seat.

### Design Notes

THE TOP AND THE BOTTOM OF THE TREE. The gap scan found no game at the Stone Circle Chamber — the deepest tone the Inn ever produces. This sheet gives the Void its game — the only place with no tone at all. Top and bottom now mirror: the deepest point still waits (the natural capstone, saved for last on purpose), and the silence above the whole tree now has something to do. The two should ship as a pair eventually: the Rest and whatever the Stone Circle Chamber's game becomes. The Inn's first note and the Inn's last note, as games.

THE QUIET TRIPTYCH. The In-Between (the grey interval — kindness anchors), the Between (the shifting margin — the door home earned by helping), and the Rest (the held silence — the ending given gently). Three games that all refuse the kill screen and all reward stillness, presence, and care. The Inn's inner life, in playable form.

DEATH'S HOLIDAY IS THE INN'S KINDEST MOVE. The Guest is Death — and Death's job, in the Inn's cosmology, is not taking anything. It's HOLDING ENDINGS. Giving tired sounds their rest. The Void game lets the player do that job for one holiday shift: the player becomes, for two hours, the gentlest thing in the Inn. Nobody dies in the Rest. Things are finally allowed to finish.

THE HARMONICA IS THE INN'S SOVEREIGNTY, MINIATURE. The one object in the whole game the player is given and must never use — the item that is a choice. MP3 logic, Krista's soapbox: the file belongs to whoever holds it. The harmonica belongs to whoever doesn't play it.
