## The Full Cycle: Cause, Affect, Deploy, Receive

## The Three Combat Styles

The Geode Inn uses all three visual styles Krista referenced — but in DIFFERENT contexts, not mixed together. Each style serves a specific combat type:

**Pokémon-style (bird's eye, turn-based, strategic):** The MAIN resonance combat. Used in the Fae Kingdom (Unseelie Court encounters), the Bedrock Fortress (Stone Guard encounters), and the Prism threshold encounters. This is the Inn's PRIMARY combat system — the one where the player counters dissonance with consonance. Turn-based because the combat is about CHOOSING the right frequency, not about reflexes. You listen, you choose, you deploy, you observe. The speed is THINKING speed, not finger speed.

**Castlevania-style (side-scroll, real-time, reflexive):** The water monster encounters in the tidal caves. Real-time side-scrolling combat — the player moves through the cave, water monsters approach, the player dodges (water bursts, tentacle sweeps, current shifts) and deploys frequencies in real-time (throwing a vibrating stone, using the Artisan Thief's Dive ability, the selkie's aquatic combat). This is the Inn's ACTION combat — the place where reflexes matter, where positioning matters, where the player MOVES. The water zones are the Inn's Castlevania.

**Mario Bros-style (8-bit platforming, jump-dodge-fire):** The classic arcade games (Standing Stones, Tomb Tunnel, Andean Run, Monster of the Deep). Pure classic mechanics — jump, dodge, fire, collect. The arcade games are the Inn's homage to the 8-bit era. The progression is the classic progression (score, level, speed). The Inn's twist is the reward (a stone, a frequency for the Song).

Each style has its own full combat cycle. The Pokémon-style resonance combat is the deepest and most detailed. The Castlevania-style water combat is the most action-oriented. The Mario-style arcade combat is the most traditional.

## The Main Resonance Combat: Full Cycle (Pokémon-Style)

### The Visual

Bird's eye view. The player and the enemy face each other across a space — a clearing in the Unseelie Court, a corridor in the Bedrock Fortress, a chamber in the deep catacombs. The view is pulled back enough to see both combatants and the space between them. The space between is where the WAVES travel — the visible vibrations that carry the player's frequencies toward the enemy and the enemy's dissonance toward the player.

The visual is CLEAN. Not chaotic, not effects-heavy, not particle-splosions. The Inn's aesthetic is scholarly and atmospheric, and the combat matches. The "effects" are WAVES — visible vibration patterns, like ripples in water or shimmer in heated air. The waves are color-coded: consonant frequencies (the player's) are warm — amber, gold, soft green. Dissonant frequencies (the enemy's) are cold — blue-gray, distorted, shifting. The waves are the only "special effects." Everything else is the environment — stone, root, crystal, the Inn's own materials.

The enemy's form is the key visual. The enemy is DISTORTED — its shape is blurred, unclear, shifting, like a figure seen through warped glass. The distortion IS the dissonance. The enemy's true form is underneath the distortion — a real creature, a real being, but vibrating at the wrong frequency, so its form can't resolve clearly. As the player reduces the dissonance (by deploying consonant frequencies), the enemy's form becomes CLEARER — less blurred, more defined, more recognizable. When the dissonance reaches zero, the enemy's true form is fully visible — a beautiful, defined creature that was always there, underneath the wrong vibration.

The player's form is stable — the player is the tuner, the one bringing the right frequency. The player's visual state changes based on their resonance level: high resonance = the player's form is bright, clear, warm. Low resonance (after wrong choices or enemy counters) = the player's form dims, thins, becomes less present. The player's resonance IS their "health bar" — but it's not a bar, it's the player's own visual clarity. The player who is losing resonance is literally fading. The player who is gaining resonance is literally brightening.

### The Full Cycle

#### 1. CAUSE — The Encounter Begins

The enemy appears. Not by jumping out — by EMANATING. The dissonance arrives first — the ambient sound of the zone shifts, becomes uncomfortable, slightly discordant. The Inn's warmth thins. The player feels it (the Resonance Pulse weakens slightly). The enemy's form materializes from the distortion — a shape pulling itself out of the wrong frequency, a being that is present but can't quite resolve.

The enemy's appearance tells the player what kind of dissonance they're dealing with:
- **Tidal dissonance:** The enemy's form ripples — like a reflection in disturbed water. The distortion has a rhythm — a tidal pattern, ebb and flow. The enemy is likely an ocean-origin creature, a being whose frequency is tied to the ancient sea. (The Rememberers' distant relative — a creature of the old ocean, dissonant because the ocean is gone.)
- **Cardiac dissonance:** The enemy's form pulses — a heartbeat rhythm, a 50 BPM distortion. The enemy is likely a deep-earth creature, a being whose frequency is tied to the geological heartbeat. (A Stone Guard relative — a creature of the bedrock, dissonant because the bedrock is being transformed.)
- **Combustive dissonance:** The enemy's form bursts — irregular, arrhythmic, a pattern of sudden distortion and partial clarity. The enemy is likely an alchemical creature, a being whose frequency is tied to a reaction (the Bloop's distant cousin — a being born from a deep-earth combustion, dissonant because the reaction was incomplete.)
- **Suppressive dissonance:** The enemy's form is MUFFLED — the distortion is a blanket, a uniform blur, no rhythm at all. The enemy is likely a civilization-wing echo — a being from an ancient culture whose frequency was suppressed (the basement-level secret, the hidden timing, made into a creature). The dissonance is the suppression — the being is vibrating at the frequency it was FORCED to silence, and the forced silence has become a dissonant presence.

The CAUSE phase is the enemy's entrance — the dissonance arrives, the enemy forms, the player recognizes the dissonance type. This is the "what am I dealing with" moment.

#### 2. AFFECT — The Player Reads the Dissonance

The player observes the enemy's dissonance pattern. The visual shows the enemy's frequency as a WAVE — a visible vibration pattern emanating from the enemy's distorted form. The wave has a TIMING (the Thrum's teaching — hear the timing, not the pitch). The player reads the timing to identify the counter-frequency needed.

This is the Pokémon "what move should I use" moment — but instead of type-matchups (fire vs. grass, water vs. fire), it's FREQUENCY-matchups:
- Tidal dissonance → counter with the Rememberers' 40Hz (tidal timing matches tidal timing — the consonant frequency acknowledges the enemy's origin and offers it a harmonic home)
- Cardiac dissonance → counter with the heartbeat's 50 BPM (the cardiac rhythm — the consonant frequency gives the enemy a heartbeat to sync with)
- Combustive dissonance → counter with the Retort's 528Hz (transformation frequency — the consonant frequency offers the incomplete reaction a way to complete)
- Suppressive dissonance → counter with the civilization's own suppressed frequency (the basement timing — the consonant frequency GIVES BACK the frequency the being was forced to silence)

The AFFECT phase is the reading phase. The player has learned (from the Thrum, from the nooks, from previous encounters) to read the dissonance type and select the appropriate counter-frequency. The reading is not instant — the player watches the wave, listens to the timing, and makes a choice. The choice is strategic. The wrong choice will increase the dissonance. The right choice will reduce it.

The interface for the AFFECT phase: the player's Song Spectrum opens — the collected frequencies displayed as channels. Each channel is a frequency the player has gathered (from stones, gargoyles, zones, previous harmonizations). The player scrolls through the channels and selects the one they believe counters the enemy's dissonance. The selection is deliberate — no button-mashing, no reflex-checking. The selection is THINKING. The Inn's version of choosing a Pokémon move.

#### 3. DEPLOY — The Player Sends the Frequency

The player deploys the selected frequency. The deployment is visualized as a WAVE — a visible, warm-colored vibration emanating from the player's form, traveling across the space between the combatants, toward the enemy.

The wave's appearance reflects the frequency's source:
- A stone-sourced frequency (e.g., ammolite's 40Hz) deploys as a ripple — like dropping a stone in water, the wave radiates outward in concentric circles.
- A gargoyle-sourced frequency (e.g., the Watcher's 111Hz) deploys as a beam — a focused, straight line of vibration, the gargoyle's architectural precision made into a wave.
- A zone-sourced frequency (e.g., the Fjord of Stars' aurora frequency) deploys as a curtain — a shimmering wall of vibration, the aurora's quality made into a wave.
- A music-sourced frequency (e.g., a composer's fragment from the Music Room) deploys as a melody — a visible sequence of notes, the wave traveling in a pattern that mirrors the composer's rhythm.

The wave travels at a speed that allows the player to WATCH it cross the space — not instant, not slow. The travel time is the anticipation. The wave approaches the enemy. The enemy's distorted form REACTS to the approaching wave — the distortion either sharpens (wrong frequency — the enemy braces) or softens (right frequency — the enemy begins to open).

The DEPLOY phase is the commitment — the player has chosen, the frequency is sent, the wave is traveling, and the player watches the approach with the specific tension of a Pokémon move traveling toward the opponent. The anticipation is the combat's emotional core.

#### 4. RECEIVE — The Waves Meet

The player's wave reaches the enemy. The two waves — consonant (warm, the player's) and dissonant (cold, the enemy's) — COLLIDE. The collision is the combat's visual peak.

**If the frequency is right (consonant match):**
The waves ALIGN. The visual is constructive interference — the two waves overlap, and where they align, the distortion REDUCES. The enemy's form clarifies — a limb becomes defined, a face becomes recognizable, a color emerges from the blur. The ambient sound IMPROVES — the dissonance lessens, the Inn's warmth returns to the zone. The environment around the combatants becomes less distorted, more comfortable. The nook-feeling begins to return.

The enemy REACTS to the consonant match:
- **Partial harmonization:** The enemy's dissonance reduces but doesn't reach zero. The enemy shifts to a new dissonance pattern — a secondary frequency, a deeper layer of the original dissonance. (The first frequency was the surface dissonance; the new frequency is the ROOT dissonance — the underlying cause.) The player must read the new pattern and select a new counter-frequency. This is the Pokémon "the enemy has a second type" mechanic — you don't win in one move. You peel back the layers.
- **Counter-attack:** The enemy, partially harmonized, deploys a dissonant burst — a wave of its own, traveling back toward the player. The burst is the enemy's RESPONSE — not an attack, but a TEST. The enemy is asking: "do you really understand my frequency, or did you guess?" The player must absorb the burst (which costs a small amount of resonance if unprepared) or counter it (by deploying a second frequency that neutralizes the burst). The counter-attack is the enemy's "turn" — the combat is still turn-based, and the enemy's turn is a dissonant burst that the player must respond to.
- **Full harmonization (rare in one move):** If the player's frequency is EXACTLY right (the perfect match — the exact counter-frequency for the enemy's specific dissonance), the enemy fully harmonizes in one deployment. The distortion drops to zero. The enemy's true form is revealed. The combat ends. This is rare — the equivalent of a Pokémon one-hit knockout. It happens when the player has deep understanding of the frequency system, not from luck.

**If the frequency is wrong (dissonant mismatch):**
The waves CLASH. The visual is destructive interference — the two waves collide and the distortion INCREASES. The enemy's form becomes MORE blurred, more chaotic, more distorted. The ambient sound WORSENS — the dissonance grows, the zone becomes more uncomfortable. The environment distorts further — the nook-feeling is disrupted, the warmth retreats.

The enemy is ENERGIZED by the wrong frequency:
- **Dissonance boost:** The enemy's dissonance increases. The enemy deploys a stronger counter — a larger dissonant burst, more costly to absorb. The player loses resonance — the crystal dims, the ambient thins, the player's own form becomes less bright. The wrong choice has a cost: the player is now FURTHER from harmonization, and the enemy is harder to calm.
- **Frequency lock:** If the player deploys the wrong frequency multiple times, the enemy may LOCK — the dissonance pattern becomes fixed, harder to read, harder to counter. The lock is the enemy's defense against being tuned by someone who doesn't understand the frequencies. The lock can be broken (by deploying the right frequency, or by retreating to a nook to recover and return with better understanding), but it makes the combat harder.

### The Cycle Repeats

After each DEPLOY/RECEIVE exchange, the combat returns to AFFECT — the player reads the enemy's current dissonance state (which may have shifted after the exchange), selects the next frequency, deploys, and receives. The cycle continues until:

**Victory — Harmonization:** The dissonance reaches zero. The enemy's true form is fully revealed — a clear, defined, beautiful creature that was always there under the distortion. The enemy's frequency JOINS the Inn's ambient sound — a new note in the chord. The player's Song Spectrum gains a new channel. The Resonance Pulse synchronizes — one perfect beat. The enemy becomes an NPC — present in the zone, no longer hostile, now part of the Inn's community. Some harmonized enemies become vendors, quest-givers, or simply warm presences that make the zone more comfortable. The combat didn't destroy the enemy. The combat REVEALED it.

**Retreat — The Nock Recovery:** The player's resonance is depleted (too many wrong frequencies, too many dissonant bursts absorbed). The player's form has dimmed to near-invisibility. The player must RETREAT — leave the combat and return to a nook. The nook restores the player's resonance (the Nook Effect as a recovery mechanic — sitting in the warm, 50 BPM space, the crystal's heartbeat recharging the player's frequencies). The enemy remains in the zone, still dissonant, still distorted. The player can return when they've recovered and have a better understanding of the frequencies needed. There is no "death" in the Inn's combat. There is no respawn penalty. There is only retreat, recovery, and return. The Inn's combat is LEARNING — you fail, you rest, you understand better, you return. The nook is the checkpoint. The nook is the inn within the Inn.

## The Water Monster Combat: Full Cycle (Castlevania-Style)

### The Visual

Side-scrolling. The player moves through a tidal cave — left to right, or right to left, or vertical (swimming up or down). The view follows the player. The environment is the cave — stone walls, water (varying levels, the tidal system), bioluminescent growth, the Pirate's drum providing a background rhythm. The water monsters emerge from the water — tentacles from pools, shadows from depths, shapes from side-tunnels.

### The Full Cycle (Compressed — Real-Time)

**CAUSE:** A water monster emerges. The emergence is telegraphed — the water churns, the ambient sound shifts, a shadow moves. The player has a moment to react (the Castlevania "the enemy is approaching" beat). The monster's type is visible in its approach — a tentacle monster reaches from a pool (positioning threat), a shadow monster swims through the water (speed threat), a mirror monster emerges from a reflective surface (surprise threat).

**AFFECT:** The player reads the monster's approach IN REAL TIME — no turn-based pause. The player identifies the monster's pattern (which they've learned from previous encounters) and positions accordingly. The reading is reflexive, not strategic. The player must DODGE — jump over a tentacle sweep, dive under a water burst, sidestep a shadow lunge. The dodge is movement-based (Castlevania-style — the player's position IS their defense).

**DEPLOY:** The player attacks in real time. The attack is a FREQUENCY deployment — the player throws a vibrating stone (the Artisan Thief's collected stones, each carrying a specific frequency) or uses a class ability (the selkie's Dive, the Tinkering Weeaboo's machine-deployment). The stone travels as a projectile (Castlevania sub-weapon style — like throwing a holy water flask or a knife). The stone vibrates as it flies — the frequency is visible as a shimmer around the projectile.

**RECEIVE:** The stone hits the water monster. The impact is a frequency collision — the stone's consonant frequency meets the monster's dissonant frequency. If the frequency is right, the monster's form DISTORTS (takes visible "damage" — but the distortion is the harmonization in progress, not destruction). If the frequency is wrong, the stone bounces off (no effect, the monster continues its attack pattern). The monster REACTS — it may retreat (partially harmonized), change its attack pattern (shifted dissonance), or enrage (dissonance boost, faster attacks, more aggressive patterns).

The cycle repeats in real-time — dodge, deploy, receive, dodge, deploy, receive — until the monster is harmonized (it sinks back into the water, calm, no longer hostile, the water it occupies becomes clear) or the player is overwhelmed (knocked into the water, swept by a current, forced to retreat to a previous cave section).

The water monster combat is the Inn's ACTION layer — the place where the player's reflexes, positioning, and real-time decision-making matter. It's not the deep strategic combat of the Fae Kingdom. It's the reflexive combat of the tidal caves — fast, physical, wet.

### The Artisan Thief in Water Combat

The Artisan Thief (Krista's selkie) has unique advantages in water combat:
- **Dive ability:** The selkie can enter the water (other classes can't — they take dissonance damage from submersion). The Dive lets the selkie fight water monsters ON THEIR TURF — underwater, where the monsters live. The underwater combat is a different mode — 360-degree movement (not side-scrolling), the selkie's stone-throwing adapted to aquatic trajectories, the water monsters' full patterns visible (not just their surface attacks).
- **Stone knowledge:** The selkie knows stones. The selkie can identify the right counter-frequency for a water monster by examining the monster's mineral composition (the monster's body has mineral elements — a tentacle monster might be silicate-based, suggesting a quartz counter-frequency; a shadow monster might be carbon-based, suggesting a diamond counter-frequency). The selkie's stone knowledge is the Pokémon "type effectiveness" system, applied in real-time.
- **Selkie skin:** The selkie's apron IS her selkie skin. In water combat, the apron provides natural resonance protection — the selkie takes less dissonance damage from water monsters because the selkie's own frequency (ocean-origin) is partially consonant with the water monsters' frequency. The selkie is fighting her distant relatives — the ocean creatures, dissonant because the ocean changed. The selkie understands them because the selkie IS them.

## The Arcade Combat: Full Cycle (Mario Bros-Style)

### The Visual

8-bit-inspired. Pixel art or clean modern-flat aesthetic. The player is a small character in a side-scrolling or top-down level. The obstacles are classic — falling blocks (Standing Stones/Tetris), digging enemies (Tomb Tunnel/Dig Dug), running obstacles (Andean Run/Temple Run), deep-water creatures (Monster of the Deep/Nessie). The visual is BRIGHT, SIMPLE, READABLE — the opposite of the atmospheric, layered main game. The arcade games are the Inn's surface level — accessible, immediate, fun.

### The Full Cycle (Classic)

**CAUSE:** The level starts. The obstacles appear. The pace is set.

**AFFECT:** The player reads the obstacle pattern in real-time. Classic pattern recognition — the Tetris piece shape, the Dig Dug enemy path, the Temple Run obstacle sequence, the Nessie behavior pattern. No frequency system, no Thrum teaching, no Messenger Principle. Pure classic game reading.

**DEPLOY:** The player acts. Place the block. Dig the tunnel. Jump the obstacle. Navigate the Nessie. The action is the classic action — button press, timing, positioning.

**RECEIVE:** The game responds. Lines clear. Enemies pop. Distance increases. Score goes up. The feedback is the classic feedback — visible, immediate, satisfying. No harmonization, no resonance, no frequencies. Pure classic game loop.

**Victory:** The level is complete (or the game is over). The reward is the Inn's addition: a stone (a frequency for the Song Spectrum). The stone is the bridge between the arcade game and the main game. The arcade game is pure surface. The reward is pure depth. The player who plays the arcade game for fun gets the fun. The player who plays the arcade game for the Song gets the frequency. Both are valid. The arcade game serves both.

## The Three Styles Together

| Style | Where It's Used | Combat Type | Progression Indicator | Victory Condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pokémon (turn-based, strategic) | Fae Kingdom, Bedrock Fortress, deep catacombs | Resonance combat — counter dissonance with consonance | Enemy's form clarity (distortion reduces) | Harmonization (dissonance reaches zero) |
| Castlevania (side-scroll, real-time) | Tidal caves, water monster encounters | Action combat — dodge, throw, position | Monster's distortion level | Monster retreats into water, calm |
| Mario Bros (8-bit, platforming) | Classic arcade games | Classic game mechanics | Score, level, speed | Level complete / game over |

The three styles serve three different player experiences:
- **Pokémon-style** for the DEEP player — the one who wants to think, strategize, understand the frequency system. This is the Inn's core combat, and it rewards the attention the Inn values.
- **Castlevania-style** for the ACTION player — the one who wants to move, dodge, react. This is the Inn's physical combat, and it rewards the reflexes the water zones demand.
- **Mario Bros-style** for the CLASSIC player — the one who wants the pure game loop, the score, the speed. This is the Inn's homage, and it rewards the skill the classics have always rewarded.

All three feed the same macro-progression (the Song Spectrum). All three use the same Resonance Pulse (the micro-indicator). All three are part of the same Inn. The player who engages with all three gets the complete experience — the strategic, the physical, and the classic. The player who engages with one gets the experience they prefer. The Inn doesn't force any style. The Inn offers all three.
