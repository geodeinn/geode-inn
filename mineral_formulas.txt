Metals


Soft Metals (Mohs 0.2 to 2.0)These metals are highly reactive or highly ductile. They can easily be scratched by a fingernail (which is roughly 2.5 on the scale).Cesium (~0.2 Mohs): The softest known metal. It is a gold-colored alkali metal that reacts explosively with water and melts just above room temperature.Rubidium (~0.3 Mohs): A highly reactive, silvery-white alkali metal that ignites spontaneously in air.Potassium (~0.4 Mohs): A soft, silvery alkali metal that can be easily sliced with a butter knife.Sodium (~0.5 Mohs): Similar to potassium, a highly reactive, wax-like metal that must be stored under oil to prevent chemical reactions.Lithium (~0.6 Mohs): The least dense solid element on the periodic table. It is soft but slightly harder than other alkali metals.Indium (~1.2 Mohs): A post-transition metal with a bright luster. It remains incredibly malleable and soft even at sub-zero temperatures.Thallium (~1.2 Mohs): A malleable, bluish-gray metal that tarnishes rapidly. It is highly toxic and historically used in rodenticides.Lead (~1.5 Mohs): A heavy, dense post-transition metal. It is highly malleable and widely used in car batteries, radiation shielding, and weights.Tin (~1.5 to 1.8 Mohs): A silvery, malleable post-transition metal that resists corrosion. It is famously alloyed with copper to create bronze.Medium-Soft Metals (Mohs 2.0 to 4.0)These metals are highly workable and represent some of the most widely used materials for currency, wiring, and everyday jewelry.Zinc (~2.5 Mohs): A bluish-white, moderately brittle metal. It is primarily utilized to galvanize iron and steel to prevent rusting.Aluminum (~2.5 to 2.75 Mohs): A lightweight, silver-colored metal prized for its low density and corrosion resistance. It is a foundational element in aerospace engineering.Gold (~2.5 to 3.0 Mohs): One of the most ductile and malleable elements. Pure 24k gold is highly susceptible to scratching, which is why it is usually alloyed for jewelry.Silver (~2.5 to 3.0 Mohs): Features the highest electrical and thermal conductivity of any element. Like gold, it is relatively soft in its pure form.Copper (~3.0 Mohs): A reddish, ductile metal with exceptional electrical conductivity. It is widely used in plumbing pipes, electric motors, and power grids.Brass & Bronze Alloys (~3.0 to 4.0 Mohs): Copper-based alloys. Brass (copper-zinc) and bronze (copper-tin) are harder than pure copper and heavily utilized in marine fittings, musical instruments, and sculptures.Medium-Hard Metals (Mohs 4.0 to 6.0)Metals in this tier transition toward structural significance, offering standard resistance to surface damage.Pure Iron (~4.0 to 4.5 Mohs): The most common element on Earth by mass. Pure iron is moderately soft, but its properties change completely when alloyed into steel.Platinum (~4.0 to 4.5 Mohs): A dense, precious metal highly valued for jewelry and industrial catalysts due to its extreme resistance to chemical corrosion.Nickel (~4.0 to 5.0 Mohs): A silvery-white metal with a slight golden tinge. It is hard, ductile, and magnetic at room temperature, commonly used in stainless steel blends.Cast Iron (~4.5 to 5.0 Mohs): A brittle iron alloy with high carbon content. It possesses excellent wear resistance and thermal retention, making it ideal for engine blocks and cookware.Cobalt (~5.0 Mohs): A ferromagnetic metal used in high-strength superalloys, permanent magnets, and lithium-ion battery cathodes.Zirconium (~5.0 Mohs): Highly resistant to heat and corrosion. It is widely applied in nuclear reactors and chemical processing industries.Mild Steel (~5.0 to 5.5 Mohs): An alloy of iron and carbon. It serves as the bedrock of global structural engineering and construction.Stainless Steel (~5.5 to 6.0 Mohs): An iron alloy containing a minimum of 10.5% chromium. The addition of chromium dramatically improves scratch resistance and rust prevention.Hard Metals & Metalloids (Mohs 6.0 to 9.0+)These materials are extremely scratch-resistant, durable, and critical for high-stress industrial applications, cutting tools, and military hardware.Titanium (~6.0 Mohs): Highly renowned for having a high strength-to-weight ratio. It is deeply biocompatible, making it perfect for surgical implants and aerospace hulls.Manganese (~6.0 Mohs): A brittle, silvery metal. It is not used as a base metal but is critical in steel production to improve strength and resistance to impact.Ruthenium & Iridium (~6.5 Mohs): Rare platinum-group metals. They are incredibly dense, brittle, and possess high melting points. They are generally used to harden other platinum alloys.Tantalum (~6.5 Mohs): A heavy, hard, blue-gray metal. It is highly conductive of heat and electricity, making it vital for electronic capacitors in smartphones.Vanadium (~6.7 to 7.0 Mohs): A hard, silvery-gray transition metal rarely used raw. It is added to steel to manufacture ultra-strong, lightweight tools and car chassis elements.Osmium (~7.0 Mohs): The densest naturally occurring element. It is a hard, brittle, bluish-white metal often paired with iridium for fountain pen nibs and electrical contacts.Pure Tungsten (~7.5 Mohs): Boasts the highest melting point of all pure metals (3422°C). It is remarkably scratch-resistant, though brittle in its unrefined form.Hardened / Tool Steel (~7.0 to 8.0 Mohs): Carbon steel that has undergone rigorous heat-treating or quenching. It is hard enough to scratch standard glass and steel files.Chromium (~8.5 Mohs): The hardest pure elemental metal on Earth. Steels are chrome-plated to provide an ultra-hard, lustrous outer shield that resists scratching and corrosion.Tungsten Carbide (~9.0 Mohs): An engineered metalloid composite formed by bonding tungsten and carbon atoms. It approaches the hardness of diamond and is used for heavy-duty industrial drill bits, armor-piercing ammunition, and unscratchable wedding bands.
