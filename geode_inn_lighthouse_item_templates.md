# Geode Inn — Interactive Item Templates: Lighthouse Zone (The Vigil)
## Based on The Keeper's Chamber (Architectural Template)

### Design Philosophy
The Lighthouse is the last zone — and the loneliest. Where every other zone builds community, growth, or transformation, the Lighthouse builds ISOLATION. One tower. One light. One direction. One purpose. The Vigil: the light must not go out. The keeper doesn't live here for comfort — the keeper lives here because someone must, and the watching never stops. The Lighthouse teaches players that some spaces are not for living in. They are for holding. The light you maintain here doesn't illuminate your room — it illuminates the ocean. It shines for everyone except you.

### The Three-Tempo System
The Lighthouse operates on three simultaneous rhythms — the zone's core mechanic:

| Tempo | Name | Speed | Role | What It Governs |
|-------|------|-------|------|-----------------|
| SLOW | The Owl (Eternity) | Imperceptible | The watcher | Tidal cycles, deep time, what changes over hours |
| MEDIUM | Persephone (Cycle) | Slow (tide rhythm) | The cycle | Wave patterns, ocean states, what changes over minutes |
| FAST | The Pirate (Rhythm) | 50 BPM (heartbeat) | The worker | The lens rotation, the Vigil itself, what changes every second |

The three tempos run simultaneously. The Owl watches what Persephone cycles. Persephone cycles what the Pirate maintains. The keeper must hold all three rhythms at once — the deepest attention mechanic in the Inn.

## Lighting System (Lighthouse)
The Lighthouse light is about DIRECTION. Every other zone illuminates itself. The Lighthouse illuminates the ocean.

| Light Type | Source | Color | Range | Behavior |
|------------|--------|-------|-------|----------|
| The Beacon | The lens flame, focused by crystal | Warm amber-gold beam | Extreme (visible at horizon) | Sweeping. Rotating at 50 BPM. The primary light. Points outward, never inward. |
| Flame Spill | Unfocused light from the lens flame | Warm amber | Very short (2-3 blocks, interior only) | The light that accidentally illuminates the keeper's room — minimal, unintentional. |
| Moonlight | Through narrow windows | Cold silver-blue | Short (1-2 blocks) | Constant when moon is visible. Moves slowly across the room as the moon tracks. |
| Ocean Bioluminescence | Reflected from the water below | Faint blue-green | Very short (1 block, floor only) | The Water Zone's light, visible from above. The ocean glows — the lighthouse sees its own foundation. |
| Oil Lamp | Interior lamp (not the lens) | Warm yellow | Short (2-3 blocks) | For the keeper's needs — reading, logging, maintaining. Separate from the beacon. |
| Storm Light | Lightning during ocean storms | Bright white flash | Entire zone, momentary | Rare. Dramatic. During storms, the flashes illuminate the ocean in stutters — the keeper sees waves in freeze-frames. |

### Light Interaction Rule
The Beacon + ocean = the beam reveals what's in the water (ships, creatures, the Water Zone's depth from above). Flame spill + crystal lens fragments = small focused points of light in the room (the only "ambient" light the keeper gets — it comes from the lens's imperfections, not by design). Moonlight + salt crystals = the salt on the walls glows faint silver (the only beautiful light in the zone — accidental, like starlight on frost). The Lighthouse's light is functional. Beauty is incidental. The keeper doesn't light the room — the room is dark so the beam can be bright.

## Furniture Templates (Lighthouse)

### 1. The Great Lens
**Based on:** The lighthouse's centerpiece
**Description:** A massive crystal and brass lens mechanism that rotates at 50 BPM, sweeping a focused beam across the ocean. The heart of the Lighthouse. The Vigil itself.
**Materials:** Crystal disks, brass frame, oil flame
**Variants:**
- Full Lens (the template — the keeper's lens, complete and functional)
- Damaged Lens (cracked crystal — beam is split, less effective — must be repaired)
- Shard Lens (rebuilt from fragments — weaker beam, but the keeper's own work)
**Interactive Properties:**
- Rotates at 50 BPM (the sleeping heartbeat — the rhythm IS the Vigil)
- The beam reveals ocean content (ships, creatures, storms, the Water Zone from above)
- The flame requires oil refills (the most critical maintenance task — if the flame dies, the Vigil breaks)
- Damaged Lens: the beam splits, creating multiple weaker beams (less range but wider coverage — different strategy)
- Shard Lens: the beam is dim but unique — each shard has its own crystal frequency (the sonic system — the beam itself SINGS at different pitches as different shards pass the flame)
- The lens must be cleaned (salt crystals form on the surface — daily maintenance — the ocean never stops)

### 2. The Vigil Log
**Based on:** The keeper's logbook
**Description:** A heavy logbook recording every observation during the Vigil. Every ship. Every storm. Every night the light held.
**Materials:** Sea-stone cover, parchment pages, brass clasp
**Variants:**
- Active Log (current — the keeper is writing in it)
- Archived Log (full — stored in the tower's lower levels)
- Blank Log (new — the player starts their own Vigil record)
**Interactive Properties:**
- The player writes entries (time, weather, observations, ship sightings)
- The log is the Lighthouse's memory — old entries reveal patterns (shipping routes, creature migrations, storm cycles)
- Archived Logs: found in the lower tower, contain previous keepers' observations (historical data — connects to the archive system)
- The log cannot be faked — it records automatically when the beam detects something (the lens writes its own observations through the player)
- Completing a full Vigil cycle (one night) generates a log entry that can be shared with other players (the Lighthouse is a social zone through its RECORDS, not its presence)

### 3. The Swivel Telescope
**Based on:** The keeper's telescope
**Description:** A brass telescope on a swivel mount, aimed at the horizon. The keeper's primary tool for close observation — the lens sees broadly, the telescope sees deeply.
**Materials:** Brass tube, crystal lenses, swivel mount
**Variants:**
- Standard (the keeper's telescope — fixed magnification)
- Bifocal (the Bifocal Gaze — dual lenses that see above and below the water simultaneously)
- Star-Mounted (can rotate to face the sky — becomes a Cosmology zone tool when pointed up)
**Interactive Properties:**
- Standard: zoom in on distant objects (ships, horizon, weather formations)
- Bifocal: the Bifocal Gaze mechanic — see both the ocean surface AND what's beneath it at the same time (the only tool that reveals the Water Zone from the Lighthouse)
- Star-Mounted: connect the Lighthouse to Cosmology — the telescope becomes a bridge between the ocean and the stars
- The telescope requires calibration (brass contracts in cold, expands in heat — must be adjusted for temperature)
- Bifocal variant: requires both Seelie and Unseelie crystal lenses (the Fae Kingdom connection — the Bifocal Gaze sees both courts' influence in the water)

### 4. Keeper's Chair
**Based on:** The simple wooden chair facing the ocean
**Description:** A salt-bleached driftwood chair. Not comfortable. Not decorative. A post.
**Materials:** Driftwood, rope (repair)
**Variants:**
- Single Chair (the standard — one keeper, one view)
- Double Chair (two seats, facing opposite directions — for shared Vigils — two keepers watch both horizons)
- Rotating Chair (mounted on a swivel — the keeper rotates with the lens, always facing the beam's direction)
**Interactive Properties:**
- Sitting in the chair enters "Vigil mode" (the observation interface — the player watches the ocean through the lens beam)
- Single Chair: one direction (the keeper chooses which window to face — each window shows a different ocean region)
- Double Chair: two keepers can hold the Vigil together (shared Vigil — both must be present for the full cycle)
- Rotating Chair: the keeper's view rotates with the lens (always sees what the beam reveals — the most immersive but the most dizzying)

### 5. Oil Reservoir
**Based on:** The oil can and fuel system
**Description:** The oil storage that feeds the lens flame. The most critical infrastructure in the Lighthouse — without oil, the Vigil ends.
**Materials:** Brass tank, iron pipes, stone housing
**Variants:**
- Small Reservoir (holds one night's oil — must be refilled daily)
- Large Reservoir (holds a week's oil — the keeper's stockpile)
- Deep Reservoir (built into the tower's foundation — holds a season's oil — the ultimate security)
**Interactive Properties:**
- Oil level is visible (brass gauge — the most-watched instrument in the Lighthouse)
- When oil runs low, the flame dims (the beam weakens — the Vigil is failing — urgent)
- When oil runs out, the flame dies (the Vigil breaks — the ocean goes dark — consequences follow)
- Oil can be traded for, found, or produced (connects to other zones — the Lighthouse depends on the Inn's economy)
- Deep Reservoir: the most expensive construction in the zone, but the only one that allows the keeper to leave the Lighthouse (a short break from the Vigil)

### 6. Storm Shutters
**Based on:** The narrow windows and their protection
**Description:** Heavy brass shutters that close over the windows during ocean storms. The Lighthouse's armor against the sea.
**Materials:** Brass, iron hinges, sea-stone frame
**Variants:**
- Manual (hand-cranked — the keeper closes each one individually)
- Linked (connected by a chain — one lever closes all shutters simultaneously)
- Automatic (triggered by wind speed — the shutters close themselves when the storm hits)
**Interactive Properties:**
- Closed shutters block the view but protect the room (storm damage risk — open windows during a storm can crack the lens)
- Closed shutters also block the beam (the Vigil is paused — a choice: protect the lens or maintain the light)
- Automatic variant: the shutters close on their own but can be overridden (the keeper can force them open during a storm — risky but the Vigil continues)
- Storm Shutters also muffle the sound (the ocean's roar is reduced — a small mercy)

### 7. Tide Chart
**Based on:** The three-tempo system
**Description:** A carved stone chart on the wall showing the three rhythms — Owl (eternal), Persephone (tidal cycle), and Pirate (heartbeat). The keeper's clock.
**Materials:** Sea stone, brass inlay, crystal markers
**Variants:**
- Carved (static — shows the standard tide patterns)
- Mechanical (brass gears drive crystal markers — the chart moves with the actual tides)
- Resonant (connected to the 42Hz Song — the chart vibrates with the Inn's frequency, and the tides respond)
**Interactive Properties:**
- Shows the current state of all three tempos (Owl, Persephone, Pirate)
- The keeper reads the chart to predict ocean states (high tide, low tide, calm, storm, creature activity)
- Mechanical variant: the chart is always accurate (self-correcting — the brass gears track real conditions)
- Resonant variant: the chart reveals connections between the 42Hz Song and the ocean tides (the Song affects the tides — the Lighthouse is where this connection is visible)
- The chart IS the three-tempo system made physical — it's the zone's core mechanic rendered as a wall decoration

### 8. Signal Lamp
**Based on:** The communication system
**Description:** A smaller, directional lamp used to signal ships and other lighthouses. Not the beacon — this is the keeper's voice.
**Materials:** Brass, crystal lens (small), oil flame
**Variants:**
- Single Signal (one beam — basic communication)
- Coded Signal (shuttered — can send patterns — morse-like code for the ocean)
- Relay Signal (connects to other lighthouses — chains the signal across distances)
**Interactive Properties:**
- Used to communicate with ships seen through the telescope (warning, greeting, guidance)
- Coded Signal: sends specific patterns that other players can decode (the Lighthouse's social mechanic — communication at a distance)
- Relay Signal: connects multiple Lighthouses (if multiple players build Lighthouses, they can chain signals — a network of watchers)
- The signal is the only way the keeper "speaks" to the outside world — the Lighthouse is isolation, but the signal is connection

## Build Rules (Lighthouse)

1. **The light points outward.** Every design choice serves the beam. The room is dark so the beam can be bright. The windows are narrow so the walls can be strong. The keeper lives in shadow so others can see the light. Build for the ocean, not for yourself.
2. **The Vigil never stops.** The lens rotates at 50 BPM. The flame must be fed. The log must be kept. The telescope must be calibrated. If the keeper leaves, the Vigil breaks — unless a Deep Reservoir holds enough oil. The Lighthouse is the only zone that demands CONTINUOUS attention.
3. **Isolation is the purpose.** The Lighthouse is not a social zone. One keeper. One tower. One light. The keeper's social interaction is through the signal lamp and the Vigil Log — communication at a distance. You don't build community here. You build a post.
4. **The ocean is the zone.** The Lighthouse's content is the ocean — what the beam reveals, what the telescope finds, what the tides bring. The tower is small. The ocean is everything. The zone's content is OUTSIDE the structure.
5. **Three tempos, one keeper.** The Owl, Persephone, and Pirate rhythms run simultaneously. The keeper must hold all three at once — the deepest attention mechanic in the Inn. The Tide Chart is the tool that makes this possible. Without it, the tempos are invisible.
6. **Salt preserves.** Like the Catacombs' air, the Lighthouse's salt preserves items — but differently. Salt crystallizes on objects, creating a protective crust. Items in the Lighthouse don't age — they get saltier. The salt is the ocean's signature on everything the keeper owns.
7. **The Bifocal Gaze connects zones.** The Bifocal telescope sees both above and below the water. The Lighthouse is the only zone that can see the Water Zone and the Cosmology Zone simultaneously — it bridges the deepest and highest points of the Inn from the middle of the ocean.
8. **The Lighthouse is the last line.** The Vigil exists because something is out there. The beam looks for ships, for storms, for creatures — and for whatever the Owl tempo watches over the longest cycle. The Lighthouse is the Inn's warning system. The light doesn't just guide — it guards.

## Player Discovery Flow (Lighthouse)
1. Player discovers a path to the coast — a passage from the Water Zone or a bridge from the main floor
2. Sees the Lighthouse from a distance — a single light sweeping the dark ocean
3. Approaches — the tower is tall, dark, weathered, isolated on a black rock
4. Enters — the spiral staircase, the salt-encrusted walls, the sound of waves
5. Reaches the top — the keeper's chamber, the Great Lens, the narrow windows, the ocean in every direction
6. The lens is rotating at 50 BPM — the heartbeat rhythm is immediate and physical
7. The keeper's tools are present but the keeper is implied (the chair, the log, the coat — someone was just here)
8. Player sits in the Keeper's Chair — enters Vigil mode
9. The beam sweeps the ocean — the first revelation: the ocean is full of life, ship lights, creature movements, storm fronts
10. The oil gauge catches the eye — the flame needs fuel (the first responsibility)
11. The Tide Chart reveals the three tempos — the complexity of the zone becomes clear
12. The telescope reveals distant details — and the Bifocal variant reveals what's BENEATH the surface
13. A storm approaches — the shutters must be closed (protect the lens) or left open (maintain the Vigil) — the first real decision
14. The Vigil Log records the night — the player's observations become part of the Lighthouse's history
15. Morning comes — the lens keeps turning — the Vigil doesn't end with sunrise
16. Other players see the Lighthouse beam from the Water Zone, the Hearth, the Cosmology level — a single light in the darkness, always turning, always watching
17. The signal lamp allows the keeper to send a message — the only social interaction — communication at the distance of light

The ocean teaches by exposure. The player builds by watching, then holding.
