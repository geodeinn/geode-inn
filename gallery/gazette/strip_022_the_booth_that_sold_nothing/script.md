# The Geode Inn Gazette — Strip #022: "The Booth That Sold Nothing"

**Date:** 2026-10-02 | **Strip Number:** 22
**Zone:** The Fair's Edge — Jay's Tavern-Campgrounds
**Characters:** The Laughkeeper (the harlequin — unnamed, naming session owed)
**Art Style:** Mucha decorative borders with Royo warm amber — Gazette house style (jeweled beadwork costume, mineral-world harlequin, cirque-elegant-wrong register)
**Player Adventures:** None — NPC feature (The Laughkeeper; the harlequin's trust-ledger)

---

## Panel 1 — The Setup

The fair's edge at twilight. A small ornate booth where the fair thins toward Jay's tavern-campgrounds. Mucha decorative borders, deep amber and gold. The booth has a counter but no wares, no sign, no prices. The Laughkeeper stands behind it in full harlequin costume: black leather half-mask, agate-banded lozenges, opal-fire cabochons, diamond-pattern beadwork that reads as jewelry before it reads as circus. Still. Not performing. The slapstick hangs at the belt like a retired conductor's baton.

**Caption:** Every merchant at the fair had something to sell. Spices, stones, stories, rope. The booth at the edge had none of these. It had a counter, a costume, and the patience to wait for the one transaction the fair had never named: the deposit.

---

## Panel 2 — The Moment

A traveler approaches the booth. Not buying. Not browsing. They carry something invisible and heavy — recent, the shape of a loss. The Laughkeeper extends one gloved hand, palm up. The traveler opens their mouth, and what leaves isn't words. It's a laugh — small, startled, reluctant — offered the way you'd hand over a coat at a desk because your arms are full and you can't carry one more thing. The laugh settles into one diamond lozenge on the harlequin's chest and glows warm.

**Caption:** You couldn't carry your laugh anymore. It felt like betrayal — the one thing that wouldn't fit the grief. So you left it at the booth the way you'd leave a coat at the desk. The harlequin took it. Held it in a diamond. The costume that was born poor — a servant's patched rags, patched until the patches became the pattern — had become a trust-ledger, and every lozenge was a laugh kept warm for someone who wasn't ready yet.

---

## Panel 3 — The Twist

Months later. The same traveler returns. The Laughkeeper reaches for the mask's straps. The traveler freezes — the instinct of everyone who has watched a clown reach for its face. But the harlequin's hands stop at the buckle. Waiting. Not performing. The mask stays on until the traveler nods.

**Caption:** The return policy was the only one in the building with a face clause. You could not hand someone back their joy while wearing a costume. That was the law. The harlequin had worn the mask for months, held still for months, carried other people's laughter in diamonds for months — and the only audience that would ever see beneath it was the one coming to collect. The horror was not the mask. The horror was the trust fall.

---

## Panel 4 — The Button

The mask is off. The panel is framed from behind the Laughkeeper — the traveler's face is visible: not shock, not delight, just the quiet recognition of someone receiving back something they'd forgotten they owned. The returned laugh sits in the traveler's chest like a coal coming back to heat. One new joke — never heard, held warm the whole time — hangs in the air between them. The diamonds on the harlequin's chest are one fewer. Mucha borders settle into soft gold. The booth is still selling nothing, and the nothing is enough.

**Caption:** The laugh came back with interest. One joke the traveler had never heard, held warm the whole time. The first record to sell a million copies was a crying clown — Caruso, 1907, Pagliacci's aria. Grimaldi was grim all day making London laugh at night. The booth that sold nothing understood what the million-seller proved: the job was never the performance. The job was the keeping.
