# The Geode Inn Gazette — Strip #028: "The Helmets Are Honest"

**Date:** 2026-10-09 | **Strip Number:** 28
**Zone:** The Main Floor (near the fireplace)
**Characters:** A Viking Gnome, A Brownie
**Art Style:** Hobbit-hole warm palette (amber, gold, cream) — Gazette house style
**Player Adventures:** None — NPC feature (Viking Gnome; the Brownie constitutional canon, the dusting-rights incident)

---

## Panel 1 — The Setup

**Art:** The Main Floor of the Geode Inn. Warm amber firelight from the great hearth, hobbit-hole palette of gold and cream. Low wood-beamed ceilings, shelves lined with leather books and curios. In the doorway stands a Viking Gnome — eight inches tall, stocky, wearing a horned helmet with a small paper tag attached that reads "DECORATIVE." He carries a tiny axe with a tag reading "CEREMONIAL." He surveys the room with the calm authority of someone who has labeled himself accurately. The helmet's horns are clearly not real. The label is.

**Caption:** "The Inn had a new resident. He was eight inches tall, wore a horned helmet, and carried an axe that was honestly labeled ceremonial. He'd arrived that morning. He'd already filed his name."

---

## Panel 2 — The Moment

**Art:** Close on a wooden shelf near the fireplace. A small stone tablet leans against the shelf edge, carved with tiny runes reading "GNOME QUARTER — ESTABLISHED TODAY." The Viking Gnome stands beside it, arms crossed, helmet tag visible, radiating the quiet satisfaction of a territorial claim that is at least accurately dated. A Brownie — one foot tall, mushroom cap for a hat, dust cloth in hand — has rounded the shelf corner and stopped. The Brownie's mushroom cap tilts. Not with anger. With process. The Brownie reads the sign the way a zoning board reads an unpermitted structure.

**Caption:** "The Brownie read the sign. The sign was accurate — the gnome had established a quarter today. The Brownie's objection was not to the fact. It was to the absence of a motion, a second, and a vote."

---

## Panel 3 — The Twist

**Art:** Close confrontation on the shelf. Two tiny figures, two documents, one shelf. The Viking Gnome holds up his stone tablet: carved, direct, honestly labeled. The Brownie holds up the laminated mushroom cap constitution: cited, ratified, laminated in tree sap for durability. The scale difference is part of the joke — eight inches of honest-label philosophy meeting one foot of constitutional inevitability. The gnome's helmet tag reads "NOT ACTUALLY VIKING." The Brownie's constitution reads "ACTUALLY CONSTITUTIONAL."

**Gnome:** "The label is honest. The quarter is mine."
**Brownie:** "The label may be honest. The shelf is not on the agenda. ...We will need minutes."

**Caption:** "The gnome believed in the honest label: say what you are, claim what you've taken. The Brownie believed in the honest minute: say what you are, then file it, second it, and vote on it. The shelf held both documents. The shelf did not adjudicate."

---

## Panel 4 — The Button

**Art:** The Main Floor at evening. Warm firelight fading to soft amber. Both tiny documents sit side by side on the shelf — the carved stone tablet and the laminated mushroom cap. On one end, the Viking Gnome sits with his ceremonial axe across his lap, content. On the other end, the Brownie dusts methodically, untroubled. Neither has conceded. Neither has left. The firelight is warm on both of them.

**Caption:** "The cold war had begun. It would be fought with labels and minutes and the precise placement of dust cloths. The gnome would not invade the Brownies' rotation. The Brownies would not ratify the gnome's quarter. The Inn held them both the way it holds everything: without choosing, without asking either to be the other. The shelf was big enough. The Inn always is."

---

## Voice Notes

- **Rothfuss precision:** "The label is honest. The quarter is mine." — the gnome's philosophy distilled to two sentences. The taxonomy of two governance systems laid out by their founding documents (stone tablet vs. laminated mushroom cap).
- **Pratchett warmth:** "The Brownie's objection was not to the fact. It was to the absence of a motion, a second, and a vote." — the bureaucratic absurdity of a territorial dispute between figures under a foot tall. "We will need minutes." The cold war fought through dust cloth placement.
- **Gaiman mythic-made-personal:** "The Inn held them both the way it holds everything: without choosing, without asking either to be the other." — the Inn's theology of coexistence applied to a shelf. Two incompatible philosophies, one building, no adjudication. The shelf was big enough. The Inn always is.

## Canon Notes

**First strip featuring the Viking Gnomes as primary characters.** Canonizes:
- The honest-label helmet law: Viking gnomes label their gear honestly — helmet horns "decorative," axes "ceremonial," helmet itself "NOT ACTUALLY VIKING." The label is the identity. You are what you say you are, and you say it accurately.
- The brownie-gnome cold war: a territorial dispute fought through governance philosophy, not violence. The gnomes claim by honest labeling. The Brownies claim by constitutional process. Neither system acknowledges the other's legitimacy. Both stay anyway.
- Connects to strip #027's Brownie constitution (the laminated mushroom cap) — the Brownie's governance system now meets its first external challenge.
- The gnome's philosophy (honest labeling) vs. the Brownie's philosophy (constitutional process) — two valid systems, incompatible, coexisting. The Inn doesn't adjudicate. The shelf is big enough.
- The cold war as the Inn's theology of coexistence in miniature: the building doesn't ask anyone to agree. It asks everyone to stay.

**What the strip says about the Inn today:** The Inn's smallest residents have incompatible governance philosophies, and the building holds both. The cold war is not a failure of the Inn — it's the Inn working as designed. Two tiny civilizations, one shelf, no adjudication needed. The shelf was big enough. The Inn always is.
