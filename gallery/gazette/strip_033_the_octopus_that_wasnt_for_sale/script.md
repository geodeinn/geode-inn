# The Geode Inn Gazette — Strip #033: "The Octopus That Wasn't for Sale"

**Date:** 2026-10-14 | **Strip Number:** 33
**Zone:** The Faire — Vaulknut's Stall → The Waters (Water Zone)
**Characters:** Vaulknut (the Selkie / Artisan Thief), The Labradorite Octopus, A Traveler, Lozen (cameo)
**Art Style:** Gazette house style — Mucha decorative borders with Royo warm amber for the Faire panels, shifting to the Waters' gothic-anime deep blues (Vampire Hunter D register) for the second half. The style shift is the strip's visual joke: the faire asks, the Waters answers.
**Player Adventures:** None — NPC feature (Vaulknut; the GALLERY LAW and the labradorite octopus ruling, canonized from bench to Waters)

---

## Panel 1 — The Setup

**Art:** The Faire at golden hour. Rows of ornate stalls, each merchant performing — the spice man singing scales, the rope man rhyming, the map man making coastlines sound like marriage proposals. Mucha borders, warm amber and gold. Center: the selkie's stall, lit by one brass lamp, a jeweler's bench of finished silver-and-stone pieces with no signs and no prices, and on a velvet pad, a small carved labradorite octopus catching the low light with electric blue and peacock-green fire. Vaulknut behind the bench, still, watching the crowd the way water watches a shoreline. The queue at the silent stall is the longest at the Faire.

**Caption:** "The Faire had one universal language, and every stall spoke it loudly. The spice man sang. The rope man rhymed. The map man made coastlines sound like marriage proposals. Only the selkie's stall said nothing at all. It had a bench, a lamp, and an octopus cut from labradorite, and it let them do the talking. The queue at the silent stall was the longest at the Faire, which annoyed the rope man considerably."

## Panel 2 — The Moment

**Art:** Close on the counter. A faire-trained traveler, coin pouch already out, holds up the octopus and asks the only question the Faire knows. Vaulknut doesn't reach for the coin — she reaches for the octopus, gently, the way a maker takes back a thing someone is about to misread.

**Caption:** "'How much?' asked the traveler, because that is the one sentence the Faire trains into everybody. The selkie took the octopus back — gently, the way you take back a thing a person is about to misread. 'He's not stock,' she said. 'He's a delivery. He lives in the Waters now. I'm just walking him home.' The traveler, who had never heard a no that was also an invitation, followed."

## Panel 3 — The Twist

**Art:** The Waters — the style shifts from faire amber to deep blue, moonstone orbs glowing in iron sconces like held breath, the water still as glass. On a driftwood shelf above the carved labradorite stool, the octopus settles — and instantly looks ancestral, as though the room had been built with a hole exactly this shape. The traveler waits for the catch.

**Caption:** "The octopus took to the shelf the way a key takes to its own lock. Blue fire in the lamplight, eight arms at ease, looking as if the Waters had always been short exactly one octopus. 'There's a catch,' said the traveler, because faire instinct dies hard. The selkie nodded. 'He's labradorite. Labradorite tells. Everything that happens under these moonstones, he'll carry back to the doorkeeper.' The Waters' newest lamp was also its newest set of eyes."

## Panel 4 — The Button

**Art:** Wide, warm: the Waters at ease. Moonstones pulsing soft, the fae perched on the labradorite stool, the octopus settled and smug above them. The traveler sits on a bench, pouch fallen forgotten beside them, in no hurry to leave. In the far archway, Lozen leans on her staff and listens with the small knowing smile of someone who has just been told something worth knowing.

**Caption:** "The traveler forgot the question entirely. The pouch went back in the pocket unopened, the feet found a bench, and the visit ran long. That was the whole trick, and galleries had known it for eons before the Faire was a twinkle: never make the pitch — place the work where it belongs and let the frame do the selling. Beauty requires no cold call. The sale takes care of itself. And in this Inn, so does the news: the octopus watches, the octopus remembers, and the doorkeeper gets her report at dawn. One budget, two services."
