# The In-Between — Builder Spec (Condensed from full design doc)

## CONCEPT
A Limbo-style puzzle platformer in the Geode Inn. Pure greyscale, silhouette art, no HUD, no score, no tutorial, no text (except one moment). A silhouette figure moves through a weightless grey world, solving physics puzzles in near-silence. The In-Between is the space between zones — the pause between notes. The player plays the In-Between itself, not a game inside it.

## VISUAL
- Pure greyscale. No color. Charcoal, ash, fog, shadow. Variation is brightness only.
- Silhouette art style. Player is a pure black silhouette — no face, no details. A shape.
- Depth through fog. Distant = lighter. Near = darker. Fog IS the world.
- Light sources are rare and meaningful — a glow is a destination, a flicker is a threat.
- No sky. Vertical fog: lighter going up, darker going down. No horizon, no ceiling.

## AUDIO
- Near-silence. Ambient wind, water, footsteps on ash. Barely there.
- 42Hz hum is the only continuous tone — felt more than heard. The ground state.
- Action sounds are physical and dry — stone on stone, body displacing air.
- No music during puzzles. Only physics sounds.
- Puzzle completion = one pure note cuts through the fog. Each puzzle reveals a different frequency. All puzzles = the In-Between's hidden chord.

## THE FIGURE (Player Character)
- Small dark silhouette, 2D side-scrolling perspective.
- No name, no face, no backstory. The minimum viable self.
- Sparse physical animation. Walks, jumps, pushes, pulls, climbs.
- Weightless but the world is NOT — objects have mass. Things resist you. Things have consequence.
- Abilities: Push, Pull, Climb, Jump (short, slow drift — weightlessness means no momentum), Sink (in water, sinks slowly).

## SIX PUZZLE TYPES

### 1. Weight and Counterweight
Move heavy objects via pulley/lever/balance. Weightlessness means counterweights DRIFT — they move slowly, sideways, unpredictably. Weight is a tendency, not a force.

### 2. The Grey Water
Cross grey water by pushing stones in. Stones sink slowly, at different rates by size. Must place in right order with right timing before first stone sinks too deep. Connects to Water System Layers 4-5 (Charon's river, Deep Water) seen without color.

### 3. The Fog Door
Doors made of fog dense enough to block passage. Find the fog's source (a vent, crack, gap) and block it. Fog dissipates, door opens. Source is always small, hidden, where the player isn't looking first.

### 4. The Shadow Bridge
Shadows are SOLID in the In-Between. Move a light source (glowing stone, 42Hz crystal fragment) so an object's shadow falls across a gap. Shadow becomes the bridge. Move light = move bridge. Must hold light in position while crossing, or fix it in place. THESIS: the least real thing (a shadow) is what you can stand on.

### 5. The Echo
Sound echoes visibly — a ring of darker grey expanding from the source. Echo pushes fog, rattles stones, reveals hidden objects (same-grey objects become visible when vibration shakes them). Use echoes to find things, clear fog, trigger vibration-only mechanisms. This is acoustic alchemy's prototype — sound as tool, frequency as force.

### 6. The Erasure
The world is dissolving. Walls fade, floors thin. Stay too long and the figure fades too — silhouette lightens, edges blur, dissolves into fog. Must move through erasing areas quickly BUT puzzles here require careful deliberate movement. Tension: the erasure says hurry, the puzzle says be precise. Connects to the Erasure threat stage from Mim's Messes game.

## THE DESCENT — Five Sections

### Section 1: The Surface (Grey Ground)
The figure starts on grey ground. Fog everywhere. Teach push/pull basics. A stone blocks a path — push it. A gap — push a stone in to cross. No text. The world teaches through consequence. The first fog door introduces Puzzle Type 3.

### Section 2: The Caves (Darker Grey)
The figure descends into caves. Grey stone, narrow passages. Echo puzzle introduced. Weight/counterweight puzzles with drifting objects. The Fizgig encounter happens here (see below).

### Section 3: The River (Grey Water)
Charon's river in grey. The Duck encounter (see below). Sinking-stone puzzle. The Allegretto music section — the one moment of genuine gentleness.

### Section 4: The Catacomb Deep (Darkest)
The Erasure is most active. Walls fading, floor thinning. The Dobby encounter (see below). The figure must push stones against erasing walls to hold structures together — but the stones erase too. Constant, desperate maintenance. The Presto Agitato section.

### Section 5: The Crystal (Light)
The figure reaches a grey-white crystal. 42Hz becomes audible — finally SOUND, not just vibration. The figure touches the crystal. The silhouette fills with faint light — no longer pure dark. Dark and light. The one moment of text appears: "...You looked." The fog thins. The game ends. No victory screen. No score.

## COMPANION ENCOUNTERS (In the In-Between)

### Fizgig (Section 2 — The Caves)
Fizgig is sitting on a resonance stone in a fading cave. The stone's 42Hz is keeping the cave from erasing. The player needs the stone for a counterweight puzzle — but taking it means Fizgig fades and the cave erases. Only this specific stone works. The player takes it. Fizgig's mouth opens silently. The cave erases. When the player returns, Fizgig is still there — fainter, sitting on the stone's memory, the floor remembering the frequency. Place the stone back and Fizgig's fluff returns. LESSON: the innocent resist erasure by being too specific to generalize. Fizgig is all small strokes. The Erasure works in broad strokes.

### The Duck (Section 3 — The River)
The Duck is floating on its back in the grey river, eyes closed, at rest. No shinies to collect in the In-Between — everything is the same grey. The Duck found stillness. The Duck is floating directly above an underwater passage the player needs. The Duck's weight holds the passage open. The player must cross under the Duck without disturbing the water — move so slowly that ripples are smaller than fog. If the player stands on the bank and watches for 2 minutes (SIT threshold), the Duck opens one eye — grey — looks at the player, closes it. LESSON: the innocent resist by letting go. The Duck who held everything survives by releasing.

### Dobby (Section 4 — The Catacomb Deep)
Dobby is in a small nook that Dobby has cleaned and maintained — a folded cloth on the floor. The nook is erasing. The walls are closing in. The player pushes stones against the walls to hold them — but the stones erase too. The player can only build temporary structures. The player must leave to reach the crystal. Dobby grips the cloth. The player leaves. The nook erases. When the player returns: the nook is gone, the cloth is gone — but Dobby is still there. Faint. Sitting where the nook was. Holding the cloth's shape with empty hands. Dobby didn't survive by maintaining the nook. Dobby survived by maintaining the GESTURE of maintaining. The care outlasts the thing cared for.

## SOUNDTRACK — Moonlight Sonata (Soft Low Dub)

All three movements of Beethoven's Moonlight Sonata, transformed into a "soft low dub" — reverb-drenched, sub-bass heavy, no percussion, ambient.

### Movement I: Adagio Sostenuto (Sections 1-2)
50 BPM. Sub-bass drone. Reverb-drenched triplet arpeggios. No percussion. The descent. The fog. Drops to near-silence during Fizgig's cave. Ends in silence when the stone is taken.

### Movement II: Allegretto (Section 3)
~60 BPM. Key shifts from C-sharp minor toward D-flat major (enharmonic — same notes, different feeling). The river. The Duck. The ONE moment of genuine gentleness. Melody plays once through, then stops. Liszt called this movement "a flower between two abysses." The Duck is the flower.

### Movement III: Presto Agitato (Sections 4-5)
SLOWED to 42 BPM — the fastest movement becomes the heaviest. Rapid sixteenth notes stretched into relentless slow pulse. Sub-bass returns with force. Sforzandi become moments of sudden SILENCE (things disappearing). The Dobby section — the sound of maintaining something that is disappearing while you maintain it. The Presto erases ITSELF — arpeggios thin, bass flattens, becomes a single note approaching 42Hz. Then: true silence. Nyx's silence.

### The Resolution
The Adagio returns in MAJOR (C-sharp major). Same arpeggios, different light. The fog lifts. The melody plays fully, cleanly, without reverb. Resolves to one note: Middle C. The note all three movements orbit.

## THE REWARD
The player receives the **Grey Note** — the 42Hz Song in its In-Between form. Grey, diffuse, the Song without color, temperature, or pressure. The minimum viable Song.

Unique property: when played in the geode cave alongside other stones, the Grey Note harmonizes with EVERY frequency. It doesn't add a new note — it makes every existing note CLEARER. The silence that makes the music audible. The space between notes that makes notes music. This is Nyx's silence made playable and collectible.

## ACCESS
- No arcade cabinet. Not an arcade game.
- Accessed by entering the In-Between zone during travel between hubs — the platformer begins automatically.
- Also accessible voluntarily via "grey doors" — patches of wall slightly lighter than surrounding stone, visible only when the player stands still and watches for them. The In-Between doesn't advertise. The In-Between waits.

## THE UNDERCITY VERSION
- Accessed through "rib joints" — spaces between the sleeping giant's ribs where bone thins and grey shows through.
- Belladonna walks through casually. The player can follow.
- Same mechanics, but the architecture is BONE, not stone. Objects are bone fragments (lighter, drift more — "the dead are lighter than the living").
- Section 2 becomes the Marrow — hexagonal chambers, different echo acoustics.
- Section 3 is Charon's river from the dead side.
- Music: "Tuesday variant" — deeper bass (bone-deep), arpeggios out of phase from hexagonal chambers, Belladonna's footsteps added as soft low rhythm on each downbeat when her silhouette is visible.

## CONNECTIONS TO INN SYSTEMS
- **Water System:** Each section mirrors a water layer in silhouette. The In-Between is the sketch; the water system is the painting.
- **Nyx:** The Grey Note is Nyx's silence made playable. The In-Between is Nyx's territory.
- **Acoustic Alchemy:** The Echo puzzle is acoustic alchemy's prototype — the echo before it was carved into a gargoyle.
- **Magritte's Pipe:** The shadow-bridge puzzle is Magritte's thesis made playable: "This is not a bridge... but you can walk on it."
- **The Erasure:** Connects to the third threat stage from the Vampire Survivors-style game.
- **Einstein:** The In-Between exists because the figure is in it. When the figure leaves, it returns to fog. The observer makes it real.
- **Grit:** Grit doesn't appear here — Grit would dissolve into the fog (Grit IS the In-Between at a different density). The fog is the Song at its most patient.
- **The Vessel:** The one line of text ("...You looked.") echoes the Vessel's first words. Same recognition.

## BUILD PRIORITY
1. Core platformer engine: silhouette figure, 2D side-scroll, push/pull/climb/jump/sink mechanics
2. Greyscale rendering with fog depth system
3. Six puzzle types implemented as physics interactions
4. The five-section descent with companion encounters
5. The Moonlight Sonata soft low dub soundtrack (three movements)
6. The Grey Note reward + geode cave integration
7. Grey door access points between hubs
8. Undercity variant (rib joint access, bone architecture, Belladonna footsteps)
