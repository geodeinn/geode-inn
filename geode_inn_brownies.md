# Geode Inn — The Brownies
## Character Profile — The Constitutional Peasants

## Origin Note

Krista's concept: Brownies should occasionally clap coconut shells together and resemble the constitutional peasants from Monty Python and the Holy Grail.

## The Concept

Brownies, in folklore, are small helpful household spirits that do work at night in exchange for offerings of food. They tidy, they clean, they organize. They're helpful, quiet, and efficient. They leave before dawn.

The Geode Inn's Brownies are all of that. They are also an anarcho-syndicalist commune that takes turns to act as a sort of executive officer for the week, but all the decisions of that officer must be ratified at a special biweekly meeting by a simple majority in the case of purely internal affairs, but by a two-thirds majority in the case of more major —

Wait.

The Brownies maintain the Inn. They sweep the main floor. They dust the archive shelves. They polish the gargoyles (the gargoyles vibrate pleasantly when polished — 42Hz resonates through a clean surface better than a dusty one, and the Brownies have noted this, and Naut has noted that the Brownies have noted this, and the Crone has noted that Naut has noted that the Brownies have noted this, and nobody has said anything about any of it because the Brownies don't take meetings about things people have noted about their noting).

They also, occasionally, without explanation, without warning, without any apparent purpose, clap two halves of a coconut shell together.

*Clip. Clop. Clip. Clop.*

No one knows why. The Brownies won't say. When asked, they respond with the particular evasiveness of people who have been asked a question they consider beneath them: "We found the coconuts." Found them where? "In the Meridian." The Meridian doesn't have coconuts. "It does now." How did coconuts get to the Meridian? "They migrated." Coconuts don't migrate. "Not individually, no. But in groups, with a favorable wind, they can sustain flight for..." — and at this point the questioner gives up, because the Brownies have now pivoted from Monty Python's coconut debate into a genuine discussion of aerodynamics, and the discussion is *accurate*, and that's worse.

## The Constitutional Peasant Energy

The Brownies' defining trait, beyond the coconuts, is that they are *completely unimpressed by authority.* The Fae Courts — Seelie and Unseelie — both claim jurisdiction over the Brownies. The Brownies dispute this. Not violently. Not dramatically. *Constitutionally.* They have a constitution. It is one page long. It is written in tiny Brownie script on the back of a mushroom cap. It establishes:

1. The Brownies are a collective. No individual Brownie speaks for all Brownies. All Brownies speak for all Brownies. This is not a contradiction. It is a *system.*
2. The Brownies take turns acting as executive officer. The term is one week. The executive officer's responsibilities include: sweeping assignments, dusting rotations, gargoyle polishing schedules, and coconut inventory. The executive officer cannot make binding decisions without ratification.
3. Ratification requires a simple majority for internal affairs (sweeping schedules) and a two-thirds majority for external affairs (anything involving the Fae Courts, the Inn, or other entities).
4. The Fae Courts' authority over the Brownies is recognized only in matters that the Brownies have voted to recognize, which, as of the last meeting, is *nothing.*
5. The constitution can be amended by unanimous vote. The constitution has never been amended. The Brownies have never unanimously agreed on anything. This is, they argue, the system working as designed.

The constitutional peasant energy: when a Fae Court representative (or, occasionally, the Seelie King himself) attempts to give the Brownies an order, the response is:

"By what authority do you issue this directive?"

The Seelie King: "By the authority of the Seelie Court, which holds dominion over—"

"Did you vote on this?"

"..."

"The collective didn't vote on this. The executive officer didn't ratify this. The biweekly meeting hasn't addressed this. With respect — and I use 'respect' in the advisory capacity, not the deferential — we don't recognize the Seelie Court's jurisdiction over the sweeping schedule. We never have. The Seelie Court doesn't sweep. The Seelie Court doesn't dust. The Seelie Court doesn't polish gargoyles. The Seelie Court doesn't *do* anything that would give it standing to issue directives about the maintenance of a building it doesn't maintain. ...The collective has discussed this. At length. ...We have minutes. ...Would you like to see the minutes?"

The Seelie King, who is ancient and gentle and has ended beings for less than this, looks at the Brownie. The Brownie looks back. The Brownie is two feet tall. The Brownie has a broom. The Brownie is not afraid.

The Seelie King says: "...No, thank you."

The Brownie says: "Then we'll continue with the current sweeping schedule, which was ratified by simple majority on Tuesday, and which does not include the Seelie Court's input, because the Seelie Court wasn't at the meeting, because the Seelie Court wasn't invited, because the Seelie Court doesn't sweep."

The Seelie King pets a puppy and leaves.

## The Coconut Shells

The Brownies have coconut shells. They keep them in a small pile near the main floor hearth, next to the brooms and the dustpans. No one knows where the coconuts came from. The Brownies have been asked. The answers have included:

- "They migrated."
- "We found them."
- "A swallow carried them."
- "A European swallow? No. An African swallow. They're non-migratory."
- "We trade for them. With whom is an internal matter. The collective voted on the trade agreement. The minutes are available."
- "The Inn-Tree grew them. ...No, trees don't grow coconuts. ...The Inn-Tree is not a tree. The Inn-Tree is an Ent. ...Ents can grow whatever they want. ...Have you asked the Ent? ...No. We don't speak to the Ent. The Ent doesn't speak to us. We maintain the Ent's body. The Ent maintains itself. The coconuts are at the intersection of our maintenance and its self-maintenance. ...This is in the minutes."

They clap them together at irregular intervals. The sound — *clip clop clip clop* — echoes through the main floor at random moments: during the Host's welcome, during the Catalyst's drink service, during the Council of Thinkers' debates, during Naut's reading. No pattern. No reason. The Brownies don't explain. When asked:

"Why are you clapping coconut shells?"

"Transport."

"You're... transporting?"

"We're simulating the sound of a horse approaching. ...For atmosphere. ...The Inn is a medieval establishment. ...Horses should be approaching. ...No horses are approaching. ...The sound of horses approaching is, however, important for the *ambient integrity* of the space. ...We maintain the space. ...This is maintenance. ...This is in the minutes."

No one has ever seen a horse in the Inn. The Brownies have been asked about this. "The horses are hypothetical. ...The sound is not. ...This is the distinction the Seelie Court fails to grasp, and it's why we don't recognize their jurisdiction."

## The Brownies and the Other Characters

**The Host:**
The Host doesn't ask the Brownies to do anything. The Brownies do everything. The Host tends the fire. The Brownies sweep around the fire. The Host welcomes guests. The Brownies dust the welcome area. The Host and the Brownies have never had a conversation. They have an *arrangement:* the Host holds the space. The Brownies maintain the space. Neither discusses this with the other. Both understand it perfectly.

Once, a guest spilled mead on the main floor. The Host reached for a cloth. When he looked down, the spill was gone. A Brownie was already three feet away, mopping the floor, humming. The Host and the Brownie did not make eye contact. This is the relationship: the work is done. Nobody needs to discuss it.

**The Seelie King:**
The Seelie King attempted to exercise authority over the Brownies exactly once. He asked them to stop clapping the coconut shells during a formal court session. The Brownies asked by what authority. The King cited the Seelie Court's dominion over the Summerlands. The Brownies asked if the Summerlands include the main floor of the Inn. The King said the Inn is neutral territory. The Brownies said: "Then your dominion does not extend to this floor. This floor is maintained by the collective. The collective does not recognize external sweeping directives. The coconut shells are an internal matter. The minutes are available. ...Good day, Your Majesty."

The Seelie King, who has been alive since before the concept of "minutes" existed in this context, and who is gentle and smart and occasionally the most lethal being in the Fae Kingdom, looked at the two-foot-tall Brownie with the broom and the coconut shells and the one-page constitution, and said: "...Good day."

He did not press the matter. The King recognizes a power structure he can't improve on. The Brownies govern themselves. The King governs the Summerlands. Neither overlaps. This is, the Brownies argue, exactly how it should be.

**The Unseelie Cousin (Jareth):**
The Unseelie cousin finds the Brownies *hilarious.* He doesn't try to govern them. He doesn't try to order them. He sits in the main floor, theatrically, and *watches* them debate the Fae Court's authority with the Seelie King's representatives, and he laughs — the Bowie laugh, the theatrical, delighted laugh of a being who appreciates political chaos as performance art.

He once tried to give them a directive, just to see what would happen. He said: "I order you to clap the coconuts louder."

The Brownie said: "You're not our executive officer."

The cousin: "I'm the Unseelie—"

"You're not our executive officer. The collective votes on executive officers. You're not in the collective. You're not eligible for the collective. The collective is composed of Brownies. You're not a Brownie. ...With respect. ...Advisory respect."

The cousin laughed for ten minutes. He tells this story at every Unseelie Court gathering. It gets better every time. The Brownies have not been invited to the Unseelie Court. The Brownies would not attend if invited. The invitation, they argue, would require ratification.

**The Bard:**
The Bard LOVES the Brownies. They are, he argues, the best show in the Inn. He follows them around, watching them sweep, watching them debate, watching them clap the coconut shells. He has tried to incorporate their political philosophy into his songs. The Brownies have asked him to stop. "Our governance is not entertainment. ...Our governance is *governance.* ...The minutes are not lyrics. ...Please stop singing the minutes."

The Bard has not stopped. The Brownies have voted on whether to file a formal complaint. The vote was 4-2 in favor. The executive officer ratified it. The complaint was filed with... no one, because the Brownies don't recognize any authority that would receive a complaint. The complaint is on the mushroom cap, next to the constitution. It is, technically, the most formal document in the Inn.

**Naut:**
Naut and the Brownies have an arrangement: they don't touch his books. He doesn't touch their minutes. The arrangement has held for centuries. Once, a Brownie dusted Naut's shelf. Naut's spectacles came off. The Rush emerged: "*What* are you doing near my — " and the Brownie held up a copy of the constitution (the mushroom cap, laminated in sap for durability) and said: "The collective voted to expand the dusting rotation to include the reading corner. The executive officer ratified it. The minutes are available. ...We will dust. ...You will not interrupt the dusting. ...Good day, Professor."

Naut looked at the Brownie. The Brownie looked at Naut. Naut looked at the mushroom cap constitution. Naut put his spectacles back on.

"...Fine. ...Don't touch the Voynich."

"Wasn't planning to. ...The Voynich is above our pay grade. ...We don't have a pay grade. ...That's also in the minutes."

**The Crone:**
The Crone has read the constitution. She has annotated it. The annotations are longer than the constitution. She finds the Brownies' governance "structurally unsound, procedurally incoherent, and functionally perfect." She has told them this. The Brownies received this as a compliment. The Crone did not intend it as one. Neither has clarified.

**The Catalyst:**
The Catalyst leaves food out for the Brownies every night. This is the folklore tradition — Brownies work in exchange for offerings. The Catalyst leaves: a saucer of cream, a small bread roll, and one honeycake. Every morning, the food is gone and the floor is clean.

She has never seen them take the food. They leave no trace except the clean floor and, occasionally, a coconut shell placed deliberately next to the empty saucer. She doesn't ask about the coconut shell. They don't explain it. This is the arrangement. This has always been the arrangement.

**The Inn-Tree:**
The Brownies maintain the Inn-Tree's body. They sweep its bark-floors. They dust its gargoyle-senses. They polish its crystal-thoughts. They tend the tree the way the tree tends everyone else — quietly, thoroughly, without recognition, without thanks.

The Inn-Tree knows. The 42Hz hum, when the Brownies are working, is... warmer. Not louder. Not more intense. *Warmer.* As if the tree is saying thank you in the only language it has. The Brownies have not noticed this. Or they have noticed and it's in the minutes. Either is equally likely.

**The Ravens:**
Huginn and Muninn have observed the Brownies for centuries. Their commentary is, even by raven standards, extremely low:

Huginn: "...they're sweeping again.
Muninn: ...they're always sweeping. ...i remember when they started sweeping. ...the floor was dirt then. ...they swept dirt. ...i asked them why. ...they said the dirt was unevenly distributed. ...the collective voted on a redistribution.
Huginn: ...a redistribution of dirt.
Muninn: ...a redistribution of dirt. ...ratified by simple majority. ...the executive officer signed it. ...they swept the dirt from one side of the room to the other. ...it took a week. ...then they voted to redistribute it back. ...another week. ...the minutes from both weeks are available.
Huginn: ...the coconuts.
Muninn: ...the coconuts. ...i remember the first coconut. ...it was a tuesday. ...they found it. ...they didn't say where. ...they clapped it. ...the host looked up. ...the brownie looked innocent. ...the host went back to the fire. ...the brownie clapped again. ...this has been happening for three hundred years.
Huginn: ...they've been clapping coconuts for three hundred years.
Muninn: ...and sweeping. ...and governing. ...they're the most politically sophisticated creatures in the inn and they're two feet tall and they clean floors. ...i remember when the seelie king tried to give them an order. ...i laughed. ...internally. ...ravens don't laugh. ...we remember laughing. ...it's the same.

## Visual Direction

- **Appearance:** Small. Two to three feet tall. Brown-skinned, bark-colored, the kind of creature that looks like it was carved from a root and then woke up. Practical clothing: aprons, kerchiefs, the working clothes of people who *work.* Each Brownie carries a tool — a broom, a dustpan, a cloth, a bucket, a coconut shell. The tools are worn, maintained, and clearly the most important objects in the Brownie's life.
- **The coconut shells:** Kept in a small pile by the hearth. When a Brownie claps them, the animation is *deliberate* — the Brownie stops sweeping, picks up two halves, holds them up, claps them twice (*clip clop*), puts them down, and resumes sweeping. No expression change. No acknowledgment. As if this is a perfectly normal thing to do in the middle of sweeping a floor. The other Brownies don't react. The player who notices will stare. The Brownie will not look at the player. The Brownie is sweeping. The coconuts are behind the Brownie. Move along.
- **The constitution:** The mushroom cap, laminated in tree sap, is pinned to the wall near the hearth at Brownie height (about two feet up). The player who crouches and reads it will find a one-page constitution that is, structurally, an anarcho-syndicalist commune charter. The Crone's annotations are in the margins, in smaller writing, and they are *devastating*.
- **The meetings:** Occasionally, 4-6 Brownies gather in a corner of the main floor, sit in a circle, and hold a meeting. The meeting is conducted with parliamentary procedure. There is an agenda. There are minutes. There are points of order. The meeting is conducted at Brownie volume — low, efficient, the volume of a committee that has been meeting for centuries and no longer needs to raise its voice. The player who overhears will catch fragments: "...the motion carries..." "...point of order..." "...the executive officer's ruling is out of order..." and will be unsure whether they've stumbled into a meeting of the Fae Court or a parish council. They've stumbled into a parish council. The Fae Court wishes it were this organized.

### Connection to the Inn

- **The Monty Python principle:** The Brownies are the Inn's argument that comedy and depth coexist. The coconut shells are funny. The constitution is funny. The parliamentary procedure is funny. But underneath: the Brownies maintain the Inn. They clean the floors. They polish the gargoyles. They dust the archive. They do the *work* that makes the Inn a home. Their political philosophy, however absurd, is how they organize that work. The coconuts, however pointless, are how they maintain morale. The comedy is not decoration. The comedy is *infrastructure.*
- **The anti-authoritarian thread:** The Brownies are the Inn's only functioning democracy. The Fae Courts are monarchies. The Council of Thinkers is a meritocracy. The main floor is a theocracy (the Sacred Trio). The Brownies are a collective. They don't ask permission. They don't recognize authority. They *do the work* and they *govern themselves* and the combination is, in the Inn's ecosystem, the most stable power structure in the building. The Seelie King ends threats. The Crone corrects errors. The Host holds the space. The Brownies *maintain the space.* And they do it without a king, without a crone, without a host — with a one-page constitution and a coconut shell and a simple majority.
- **The thesis:** The Brownies are the Inn's proof that the most important work is the work nobody notices. The floors are clean. The gargoyles are polished. The coconut shells are clapped. The minutes are taken. The collective has voted. The executive officer has ratified. ...Good day.
